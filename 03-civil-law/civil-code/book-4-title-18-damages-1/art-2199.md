> ART. 2199. Except as provided by law or by stipulation, one is entitled to an adequate compensation only for such pecuniary loss suffered by him as he has duly proved. Such compensation is referred to as actual or compensatory damages.[^civil-code]

# Explanation

## Concept, Purpose and Historical Origin of Actual or Compensatory Damages

Article 2199 opens Chapter 2 of Title XVIII, Book IV of the Civil Code and governs the recovery of actual or compensatory damages across all obligations arising from law, contracts, quasi-contracts, crimes and quasi-delicts.[^civil-code]

- **Statutory definition and synonymy.** Under Article 2199, a claimant is entitled to adequate compensation only for such pecuniary loss suffered by him or her as has been duly proved, except as provided by law or by stipulation.[^civil-code] In *People v. Laceste* and *People v. Galo*, the Supreme Court clarified that under Chapter 2, Title XVIII, Book IV of the Civil Code, the terms "actual" and "compensatory" damages are synonymous and refer to the same pecuniary recompense.[^1] [^2] They are distinct from moral damages under Article 2217 and exemplary damages under Article 2229, which require no proof of pecuniary loss under Article 2216.[^civil-code] [^2]
- **Restorative purpose.** Actual or compensatory damages proceed from a sense of natural justice and are designed to repair the wrong that has been done, compensate for the injury inflicted and place the injured party in the position in which he or she had been before the injury.[^3] [^4] [^5] They are awarded in satisfaction of a real pecuniary loss and not to impose a penalty or enrich the claimant at the expense of the defendant.[^3] [^6]
- **Classification as economic damages.** In its November 25, 2025 En Banc ruling in *University of Southeastern Philippines v. Sarate*, the Supreme Court classified tort damages in Philippine law into four categories: (1) economic damages, consisting of actual or compensatory damages and temperate damages, (2) non-economic damages, consisting of moral damages and civil indemnity *ex delicto* or *ex quasi-delicto*, (3) punitive or exemplary damages and (4) nominal damages.[^7]
- **Historical origin as a new codal provision.** In *Basilan Lumber Co. v. Cagayan Timber Export Co.*, the Supreme Court explained that Article 2199 is a new provision that did not exist in the Spanish Civil Code of 1889.[^8] It was enacted specifically to deny the grant of speculative damages and damages not actually proved to have existed and to have been caused to the party claiming them.[^8] Accordingly, where a timber exporter's agent paid a foreign buyer's claim for short delivery, the exporter could not recover actual damages from its supplier under Article 2199 without proof that the exporter had already reimbursed the agent or had been required to pay the amount.[^8]
- **Prohibition against lumping actual damages with other awards.** Because actual damages require proof of specific pecuniary loss whereas moral damages compensate for mental anguish and physical suffering, trial courts cannot lump actual and moral damages into a single round sum.[^1] [^9] In *Malonzo v. Galang* and *People v. Laceste*, the Supreme Court struck down lumped awards that combined compensatory and moral damages without specifying the separate amount and factual basis for each item.[^1] [^9]

## Two Categories of Actual Damages: *Daño Emergente* and *Lucro Cesante*

Article 2199 is read together with Article 2200, which provides that indemnification for damages shall comprehend not only the value of the loss suffered, but also that of the profits which the obligee failed to obtain.[^civil-code] [^10] Jurisprudence divides actual or compensatory damages into two categories:

1. ***Daño emergente* (actual loss):** the loss of what a person already possesses, such as paid medical and hospital bills, funeral and wake expenses, cost of repair or replacement of damaged property and placement fees.[^10] [^11] [^12]
2. ***Lucro cesante* or *ganancias frustradas* (unrealized profits):** the failure to receive as a benefit that which would have pertained to the claimant had the wrong or breach not occurred.[^10] [^13] [^14]

- **Evidentiary standard for *lucro cesante*.** In *San Miguel Foods, Inc. v. Magtuto*, the Supreme Court held that while it is not necessary to prove the exact amount of *lucro cesante* with absolute certainty, unrealized profits cannot be granted on the basis of mere speculation, conjecture or surmise.[^10] Instead, the claimant must establish them by reference to a reasonably definite standard such as market value, established experience or direct inference from known circumstances, supported by the best evidence of which the case is susceptible.[^10] In *San Miguel Foods*, the poultry grower proved his unrealized income from a short delivery of 4,000 day-old chicks through flock records, liquidation statements, payment request memoranda, check vouchers and deposit slips establishing his average income over five growing periods.[^10]
- **Disallowed speculative claims for lost profits and rentals.** By contrast, where a claimant relies on bare, self-serving assertions of lost profits or rental value without documentary proof, no *lucro cesante* can be recovered under Article 2199:
  - In *Chingkoe v. Chingkoe*, the Supreme Court denied actual damages for a supposedly failed export business venture where the claimant presented only bare oral testimony without documentary proof of pecuniary loss.[^15]
  - In *Duenas v. Metropolitan Bank and Trust Co.* and *Princess Rachel Development Corp. v. Hillview Marketing Corp.*, the Supreme Court deleted awards of unrealized monthly rentals and construction losses that were anchored only on arbitrary estimates or self-serving allegations without receipts or lease contracts.[^16] [^17]
  - In *Kabisig Real Wealth Dev., Inc. v. Young Builders Corp.* and *Province of Bataan v. Escalada, Jr.*, the Supreme Court reiterated that actual damages under Articles 2199 and 2200 must rest on tangible documentary proof of the value of the work accomplished or the actual pecuniary injury sustained.[^11] [^13]
- **Foreseeable future earnings and fixed contractual salaries.** In *Cariaga v. Laguna Tayabas Bus Co.*, the Supreme Court awarded compensatory damages for the lost minimum income that a fourth-year medical student would have earned had he finished his studies on schedule, based on the uncontradicted testimony of a qualified physician establishing the prevailing minimum income of medical practitioners.[^18] In overseas employment cases such as *Santiago v. CF Sharp Crew Management, Inc.* and *Stolt-Nielsen Transportation Group, Inc. v. Medequillo, Jr.*, the Supreme Court held that when a recruitment agency and principal unreasonably fail to deploy a seafarer under a perfected POEA-approved contract, the seafarer is entitled to actual damages under Article 2199 equivalent to the fixed basic salary for the nine-month contractual period, but not to guaranteed overtime pay for work that was never rendered.[^19] [^20]
- **Loss of earning capacity in wrongful death.** In *University of Southeastern Philippines v. Sarate*, the Supreme Court emphasized that under paragraph 1 of Article 2206, compensation for loss of earning capacity is awarded not for loss of actual earnings at the time of death, but for the loss of capacity to earn money, which may be recovered even for deceased minor children if substantiated by documentary or testimonial evidence with a reasonable degree of certainty.[^7] However, where the parents prayed only for moral and exemplary damages and offered no evidence to substantiate actual damages or loss of earning capacity, actual damages could not be granted.[^7]

## Burden and Standard of Proof: Reasonable Certainty and Documentary Evidence

To recover actual damages under Article 2199, the injured party must plead and prove two essential requisites: (1) the fact of the injury or pecuniary loss and (2) the actual amount of the loss with a reasonable degree of certainty, premised upon competent proof and on the best evidence obtainable by the injured party.[^3] [^21] [^22]

- **Actual damages are never presumed.** Courts cannot rely on speculation, conjecture or guesswork as to the fact and amount of damages.[^3] [^23] In *Equitable PCIBank v. Spouses Lacson*, a bank sued its depositors for PHP 20,000,000.00 in actual damages arising from an alleged check-kiting scheme, even though the bank had dishonored the checks and no funds ever left its custody.[^3] The Supreme Court affirmed the dismissal of the claim, holding that because the checks were dishonored and no cash was disbursed, the bank suffered no actual pecuniary loss under Article 2199, which also defeated its accessory claims for exemplary damages and attorney's fees.[^3]
- **Official receipts and competent documentary proof.** In both civil and criminal cases, credence is given only to claims for expenses that are duly supported by official receipts or competent documentary evidence:
  - In *People v. Caluducan* (2026), the Supreme Court awarded actual damages under Article 2199 ordering the accused in a large-scale illegal recruitment case to reimburse the exact placement fees proved to have been paid by the complainants.[^12]
  - In *People v. Alvarez* (2024), the Supreme Court denied actual damages because the prosecution presented no receipts or other documentary evidence to substantiate the victim's hospital or medical expenses.[^24]
  - In *People v. Manansala* (2020), where the victim's family claimed PHP 201,500.00 in burial and funeral expenses by presenting an official receipt for PHP 30,000.00 alongside a handwritten list on a yellow pad for the remaining PHP 171,500.00, the Supreme Court held that a self-serving handwritten list is not competent proof under Article 2199 and recognized only the receipted PHP 30,000.00 (eventually awarding PHP 50,000.00 as temperate damages in accordance with prevailing criminal-indemnity guidelines).[^25]
  - In *Cirera v. People* (2014) and *People v. Tibon* (2010), the Supreme Court sustained actual damages strictly up to the amounts of hospital, medical and funeral expenses supported by official receipts on record.[^26] [^27]
- **Insufficiency of bare oral testimony in property and criminal reparation claims.** In *Viray v. People*, the Supreme Court deleted an award of PHP 296,000.00 as reparation for stolen jewelry and gadgets in a theft conviction because the private complainant relied solely on his uncorroborated oral testimony without presenting receipts or independent valuation evidence as required by Article 2199.[^28] Likewise, in *Tulfo v. People*, the Supreme Court deleted the award of actual damages in a libel case because the private complainant presented no documentary evidence of pecuniary loss resulting from the defamatory articles.[^29]
- **Constitutional mandate for distinct findings of fact and law.** Under Article VIII, Section 14 of the Constitution and Article 2199 of the Civil Code, a trial court decision awarding actual damages must state the specific evidentiary facts and itemized losses supporting the award.[^30] In *University of the Philippines v. Dizon*, the Regional Trial Court awarded PHP 5,716,729.00 in actual damages to a contractor based only on a terse statement that the contractor had been forced to re-mortgage his house and lot to pay bank interest and penalties.[^30] The Supreme Court annulled the award as a void judicial *ipse dixit* that contravened Article 2199 and never attained finality, even though the university's appeal had been dismissed as belated.[^30]

## Statutory Exceptions: Compensation Provided by Law or by Stipulation

Article 2199 begins with the qualifying clause, *"Except as provided by law or by stipulation."*[^civil-code] The requirement of proving actual pecuniary loss through official receipts or documentary evidence is therefore not absolute and yields in two situations:[^31]

1. **When provided by law.** Several provisions of the Civil Code and special laws authorize or fix monetary indemnity without requiring proof of actual pecuniary loss.[^civil-code] [^31] Under Article 2206, civil indemnity for death caused by a crime or quasi-delict is awarded automatically upon proof of the fact of death and the defendant's liability, without need of further proof of pecuniary loss.[^civil-code] [^7] In *University of Southeastern Philippines v. Sarate*, the Supreme Court increased this statutory wrongful-death indemnity floor for quasi-delicts to PHP 300,000.00.[^7] Similarly, Article 2216 provides that no proof of pecuniary loss is necessary to adjudicate moral, nominal, temperate, liquidated or exemplary damages.[^civil-code]
2. **When provided by stipulation of the parties.** Pursuant to the principle of autonomy of contracts under Article 1306 and the opening clause of Article 2199, parties may stipulateeither on the amount of damages (such as liquidated damages under Article 2226) or on the specific documentary requirements that will suffice to prove a claim without official receipts:[^civil-code] [^31]
   - **Contractual stipulation on documentary proof of loss.** In *Industrial Personnel and Management Services, Inc. v. Country Bankers Insurance Corp.*, a recruitment agency and a surety company executed a Memorandum of Agreement enumerating five specific documents (two demand letters, an affidavit of violation, a detailed statement of account and a transmittal claim letter) as the requirements for claiming reimbursement of nurse-processing expenses under a surety bond.[^31] The Court of Appeals held that the agency could not recover without presenting official receipts under Article 2199.[^31] The Supreme Court reversed, holding that under the express exception *"or by stipulation"* in Article 2199, contracting parties may validly stipulate that statements of account, demand letters and affidavits are sufficient proof of actual losses and thereby dispense with official receipts, particularly where foreign government agencies do not issue official receipts in the ordinary course of business.[^31]
   - **Judicial stipulation or admission during trial.** In *People v. Esoy*, defense counsel in a robbery with homicide trial stipulated with the prosecution that the victim's family incurred funeral expenses in the reduced amount of PHP 30,000.00, dispensing with the mother's testimony on those expenses.[^32] The Supreme Court held that because Article 2199 expressly recognizes exceptions *"by stipulation,"* the requirement of presenting receipts for actual proof was dispensed with and the PHP 30,000.00 award of actual damages was proper.[^32] Similarly, in *Marikina Auto Line Transport Corp. v. People*, where the bus company's own witness testified that the repair of the damaged commercial property would cost PHP 100,000.00, the Supreme Court awarded PHP 100,000.00 as actual damages on the basis of the defendant's own admission despite the complainant's failure to substantiate her higher PHP 171,088.46 claim with competent receipts.[^33]

## Award of Temperate or Nominal Damages When Actual Damages Are Not Proven

When a claimant fails to meet the strict evidentiary standard of Article 2199, the court's remedial response depends on whether the existence of pecuniary loss itself was established:

- **Temperate or moderate damages under Article 2224 when pecuniary loss is certain but the exact amount is unproven.** Under Article 2224, temperate or moderate damages, which are more than nominal but less than compensatory damages, may be recovered when the court finds that some pecuniary loss has been suffered, but its amount cannot, from the nature of the case, be proved with certainty.[^civil-code] [^21] The Supreme Court has consistently awarded temperate damages in lieu of actual damages across diverse causes of action where pecuniary injury is undeniable:
  - *Tortious interference with contract:* In *Tuna Processors, Inc. v. Frescomar Corp.* (2024), where a licensor proved that the respondent tortiously induced a licensee to stop paying patent royalties, but the exact number of tuna products produced during the license period was not proven in court, the Supreme Court deleted the baseless actual-damage claim and awarded PHP 1,000,000.00 as temperate damages anchored on the licensor's prior demand letter and shipment records.[^21]
  - *Copyright infringement:* In *COSAC, Inc. v. Filipino Society of Composers, Authors and Publishers, Inc.* (2023), where a restaurant infringed musical copyrights by playing licensed songs without a public performance license, the Supreme Court upheld PHP 300,000.00 as temperate damages because FILSCAP suffered a definite pecuniary loss of license fees whose exact amount could not be ascertained with certainty.[^4]
  - *Personal injury and property damage:* In *Aleta v. Sofitel Philippine Plaza Manila* (2023), *Dueñas v. Guce-Africa* (2009) and *De Guzman v. Tumolva* (2011), where claimants suffered physical injuries in a hotel pool or sustained damage to a building or collapsed perimeter fence, yet failed to present official receipts or presented only unverified estimates, the Supreme Court awarded temperate damages in lieu of actual damages.[^5] [^34] [^35]
  - *Breach of construction, carriage, utility and service contracts:* In *KLM Royal Dutch Airlines v. Tiongco* (2021) (undelivered luggage of an international speaker), *Manila Electric Co. v. AAA Cryogenics Philippines, Inc.* (2020) (damaged compressor caused by cut power lines), *Yamauchi v. Suñiga* (2018) (incomplete and defective house renovation), *Republic v. Looyuko* (2016), *Seven Brothers Shipping Corp. v. DMC-Construction Resources, Inc.* (2014) (cargo lost when a barge capsized), *Snow Mountain Dairy Corp. v. GMA Veterans Force, Inc.* (2014), *International Container Terminal Services, Inc. v. Chua* (2014) (personal effects destroyed in a warehouse fire) and *Oceaneering Contractors (Phils.), Inc. v. Barretto* (2011), the Supreme Court deleted unsupported awards of actual damages under Article 2199 and substituted reasonable temperate damages under Article 2224.[^6] [^23] [^36] [^37] [^38] [^39] [^40] [^41]
- **Nominal damages under Article 2221 when a legal right is violated without proof of pecuniary loss.** Under Article 2221, nominal damages are adjudicated in order that a right of the plaintiff, which has been violated or invaded by the defendant, may be vindicated or recognized, and not for the purpose of indemnifying the plaintiff for any loss suffered.[^civil-code] [^42] In *Pryce Properties Corp. v. Spouses Octobre* (2016) and *Cantemprate v. CRS Realty Development Corp.* (2009), where subdivision developers or depositaries breached their contractual obligation to deliver land titles on time, but the buyers failed to prove any actual pecuniary loss or unrealized profits under Article 2199, the Supreme Court deleted the awards of actual or moral damages and awarded nominal damages instead.[^14] [^42] Nominal damages cannot coexist with actual or temperate damages for the same breach, because actual and temperate damages indemnify for a pecuniary loss whereas nominal damages presuppose the absence of proven pecuniary loss.[^civil-code] [^42]

## Suggested Answers

**Q1: Hypothetical: A recruitment agency and a surety company stipulated in their Memorandum of Agreement that claims against a surety bond covering overseas nurse-processing costs would be payable upon submission of two demand letters, an affidavit of violation, a detailed statement of account and a transmittal letter. When the agency filed a claim with those five documents, the surety refused to pay and invoked Article 2199, arguing that actual damages cannot be recovered without official receipts. Is the surety correct?**
No, the surety is not correct.[^31] Under Article 2199 of the Civil Code, a claimant is limited to pecuniary losses duly proved by receipts or competent proof *"except as provided by law or by stipulation."*[^civil-code] [^31] In *Industrial Personnel and Management Services, Inc. v. Country Bankers Insurance Corp.*, the Supreme Court held that the prerequisite of presenting official receipts under Article 2199 is not absolute and may be dispensed with by stipulation of the parties pursuant to the autonomy of contracts.[^31] Because the parties expressly agreed in their Memorandum of Agreement that the five enumerated documents would suffice to substantiate a bond claim, the surety cannot require official receipts as an additional precondition.[^31]

**Q2: Hypothetical: A bank discovered that two depositors were engaging in a check-kiting scheme involving PHP 20,000,000.00 in checks drawn between their accounts. Exercising extraordinary diligence, the bank dishonored the checks before any funds were withdrawn or disbursed. The bank then sued the depositors and obtained a trial court judgment for PHP 20,000,000.00 in actual damages plus exemplary damages and attorney's fees. Should the award of damages be sustained?**
No, the awards of actual damages, exemplary damages and attorney's fees must be deleted.[^3] Under Article 2199 of the Civil Code and *Equitable PCIBank v. Spouses Lacson*, actual damages are awarded only to compensate for a pecuniary loss actually sustained and require proof of both the fact of injury and the actual amount of loss with reasonable certainty.[^3] Because the bank dishonored the checks and the PHP 20,000,000.00 never left the bank's custody or coffers, the bank suffered no actual pecuniary loss, and without an underlying entitlement to actual, moral, temperate or liquidated damages, exemplary damages and attorney's fees cannot be awarded either.[^3]

**Q3: Hypothetical: A poultry integrator breached its per-grow contract with a broiler grower by delivering only 32,000 day-old chicks instead of the agreed 36,000 heads for the fifth growing season. To prove his lost income on the 4,000-chick shortage, the grower presented flock records, liquidation statements, payment request memoranda, check vouchers and deposit slips from the preceding growing periods. The integrator objected that unrealized profits are speculative and cannot be awarded as actual damages under Article 2199. Rule on the objection.**
The integrator's objection is untenable and the grower is entitled to actual damages for his unrealized income on the 4,000-chick shortage.[^10] Under Articles 2199 and 2200 of the Civil Code, actual or compensatory damages include both actual loss (*daño emergente*) and unrealized profits (*lucro cesante*).[^civil-code] [^10] In *San Miguel Foods, Inc. v. Magtuto*, the Supreme Court ruled that while *lucro cesante* cannot be granted on mere conjecture, it need not be proved with absolute certainty and is recoverable when established by a reasonably definite standard such as historical average income supported by flock records, liquidation statements and payment vouchers.[^10]

**Q4: Hypothetical: A homeowner paid a contractor PHP 400,000.00 to renovate her house, but the contractor abandoned the project in a defective and unlivable state. In her suit for rescission and damages, the homeowner claimed PHP 800,000.00 in actual damages for renovation and demolition expenses, relying on an itemized summary of expenses without presenting official receipts from the suppliers or workers. May the court award the PHP 800,000.00 as actual damages, or must it dismiss her pecuniary claim entirely?**
The court cannot award the PHP 800,000.00 as actual damages under Article 2199, but it should award temperate damages under Article 2224 rather than dismiss her pecuniary claim entirely.[^36] Under *Yamauchi v. Suñiga*, actual damages under Article 2199 cannot rest on self-serving summaries or uncorroborated lists unsupported by official receipts.[^36] However, where the court finds that the claimant undeniably suffered pecuniary loss from the defective and abandoned renovation, yet the exact amount cannot be proved with certainty for lack of receipts, Article 2224 authorizes the award of reasonable temperate or moderate damages in lieu of actual damages.[^36]

**Q5: Hypothetical: A subdivision buyer fully paid for two residential lots, but the developerand its depositary bank delayed the delivery of the transfer certificates of title for several years. The buyer sued for breach of contract and prayed for actual damages for lost business opportunities, presenting only his own testimony that he had intended to use the titles as collateral for a commercial loan. What damages, if any, may the buyer recover?**
The buyer cannot recover actual damages under Article 2199, but he is entitled to nominal damages under Article 2221.[^42] In *Pryce Properties Corp. v. Spouses Octobre*, the Supreme Court held that bare testimonial allegations of missed business opportunities without competent documentary proof of pecuniary loss cannot support an award of actual or compensatory damages under Article 2199.[^42] Nevertheless, because the developer and the bank invaded the buyer's legal right to the timely delivery of the titles upon full payment, nominal damages are proper under Article 2221 to vindicate and recognize the violated right.[^42]

# Quotes

> "Our answer to the foregoing arguments of counsel for petitioner is the express provisions of Article 2199 of the Civil Code of the Philippines to the effect that damages must be "duly proved." This new provision, which did not exist in the Civil Code of Spain, denies the grant of speculative damages, damages not actually proved to have existed and to have been caused to the party claiming the same. In the case at bar the evidence shows that actual damage was caused to the agent through which petitioner sold the logs to a Japanese buyer, as said agent had already paid the same to the latter. However, there is no proof that respondent had already paid the agent said damages, or that it had already been required to pay the same, and while these have not happened the damage to the petitioner may not, under the above-cited article of the Civil Code, be deemed to have actually been caused to him."[^43]

> "The law is clear and unequivocal when it states that one is entitled to adequate compensation for pecuniary loss only for such losses as he has duly proved EXCEPT: (1) when the law provides otherwise, or (2) by stipulation of the parties. Otherwise stated, the amount of actual damages is limited to losses that were actually incurred and proven, except when the law provides otherwise, or when the parties stipulate that actual damages are not limited to the actual losses incurred or that actual damages are to be proven by specific documents agreed upon."[^44]

> "To reiterate, Article 2199 of the Civil Code explicitly provides that the prerequisite of proof for the recovery of actual damages is not absolute. This was illustrated in People of the Philippines v. Jonjie Eso y Hungoy, et al.,[38] wherein this Court held that the requirement of providing actual proof found under Article 2199 for the recovery of actual and compensatory damages (in that case, funeral expenses) may. be dispensed with, considering that there was a stipulation to that effect made by the parties."[^45]

> "An award of actual or compensatory damages requires proof of pecuniary loss. Under Articles 2199[19] and 2200[20] of the Civil Code, actual or compensatory damages are those awarded in satisfaction of or in recompense for loss or injury sustained. They proceed from a sense of natural justice and are designed to repair the wrong that has been done. In Terminal Facilities and Services Corporation v. Philippine Ports Authority,[21] we explained that there are two kinds of actual or compensatory damages: (1) the loss of what a person already possesses, and (2) the failure to receive as a benefit that which would have pertained to him. In the latter instance, the familiar rule is that damages consisting of unrealized profits, frequently referred to as ganacias frustradas or lucrum cessans, are not to be granted on the basis of mere speculation, conjecture, or surmise, but rather by reference to some reasonably definite standard such as market value, established experience, or direct inference from known circumstances. It is not necessary to prove with absolute certainty the amount of ganacias frustradas or lucrum cessans."[^46]

> "Time and again, this Court has declared that actual damages cannot be presumed. The claimant must prove the actual amount of loss with a reasonable degree of certainty premised upon competent proof and on the best evidence obtainable. Specific facts that could afford a basis for measuring whatever compensatory or actual damages are borne must be pointed out. Actual damages cannot be anchored on mere surmises, speculations, or conjectures.[36] Thus, it was held that before actual damages can be awarded, there must be competent proof of the actual amount of loss, and credence can be given only to claims which are duly supported by receipts.[37]"[^47]

> "Article 2199 of the Civil Code provides that a person is entitled to adequate compensation for a pecuniary loss that has been duly proved. Such actual or compensatory damages must be proved with a reasonable degree of certainty.[95] On the other hand. Article 2224 of the Civil Code allows the courts to grant temperate damages if a party suffered some pecuniary loss but its amount cannot, from the nature of the case, be proved with certainty. Thus, if there is no definitive proof of damages and a person still suffered a loss, temperate or moderate damages can be awarded. The computation of the amount of temperate or moderate damages is usually left to the court's discretion. In all cases, "the amount must be reasonable, bearing in mind that temperate damages should be more than nominal but less than compensatory."[96]"[^48]

# Tips

> [!TIP]
> **Bar Exam Focus:** Keep four core rules in mind whenever Article 2199 is tested:
> 1. **General Rule vs. Two Statutory Exceptions (*IPAMS v. Country Bankers*; *People v. Esoy*; *USEP v. Sarate*):** As a rule, actual or compensatory damages are recoverable only for pecuniary losses duly proved with a reasonable degree of certainty through official receipts or competent documentary evidence. However, Article 2199 expressly excepts cases **provided by law** (such as fixed civil indemnity for death under Article 2206, increased to PHP 300,000.00 for quasi-delicts in *USEP v. Sarate*) and cases **provided by stipulation** (such as a trial stipulation on funeral expenses in *People v. Esoy* or a contractual clause specifying that statements of account and affidavits suffice to prove bond claims without official receipts in *IPAMS v. Country Bankers*).
> 2. ***Daño Emergente* vs. *Lucro Cesante* (*San Miguel Foods v. Magtuto*; *Santiago v. CF Sharp*; *Stolt-Nielsen v. Medequillo*):** Read with Article 2200, actual damages cover both the loss of what a person already possesses (*daño emergente*) and unrealized profits (*lucro cesante*). While *lucro cesante* need not be proved with absolute certainty, it cannot rest on bare oral testimony or arbitrary rental estimates (*Chingkoe*; *Duenas*); it must be anchored on a reasonably definite standard such as historical average earnings proved by vouchers (*San Miguel Foods*) or a seafarer's fixed contractual basic salary upon non-deployment, excluding unworked overtime pay (*Santiago*; *Stolt-Nielsen*).
> 3. **Actual Damages (Art. 2199) vs. Temperate Damages (Art. 2224) vs. Nominal Damages (Art. 2221) (*Tuna Processors*; *Yamauchi*; *Pryce Properties*):** If the claimant proves both the fact of pecuniary loss and its exact amount by competent receipts, award **actual damages** (Art. 2199). If the claimant proves that some pecuniary loss was definitely suffered, but its exact amount cannot be proved with certainty, award **temperate damages** in lieu of actual damages (Art. 2224; *Tuna Processors*; *Yamauchi*). If a legal right was violated, but no pecuniary loss was suffered or proven at all, award **nominal damages** to vindicate the right (Art. 2221; *Pryce Properties*).
> 4. **No Actual Loss, No Accessory Exemplary Damages (*Equitable PCIBank v. Spouses Lacson*):** Where a claimant suffered no actual pecuniary disbursement (such as a bank that dishonored kited checks before any funds left its custody) and is not entitled to moral, temperate or liquidated damages, exemplary damages and attorney's fees grounded thereon cannot be awarded under Article 2234.

> [!NOTE]
> **Practical Application:** In commercial, tort and criminal litigation:
> - **Substantiate every item of expense with official receipts:** Never rely on handwritten summaries on yellow pad, self-serving expense tables or bare oral testimony to prove medical, burial, repair or property-loss claims. The Supreme Court consistently disallows unreceipted balances under Article 2199 (*People v. Manansala*; *Viray v. People*; *Yamauchi v. Suñiga*).
> - **Draft express proof-of-loss clauses in surety and commercial contracts:** When transactions involve foreign agencies or counterparties that do not issue Philippine-style official receipts, stipulate expressly in the contract which documents (such as billing statements, statements of account and sworn affidavits) will constitute sufficient proof of loss under the *"by stipulation"* exception of Article 2199 (*IPAMS v. Country Bankers*).
> - **Ensure trial court decisions itemize the factual basis of actual damages:** Prevailing counsel should verify that the body of the trial court decision sets out the specific documentary exhibits and arithmetic breakdown supporting any award of actual damages and attorney's fees; a conclusory award without distinct factual findings is a void *ipse dixit* that does not attain finality even if the opposing party's appeal is filed late (*University of the Philippines v. Dizon*).
