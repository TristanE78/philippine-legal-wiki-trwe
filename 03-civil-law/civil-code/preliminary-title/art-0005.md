> ART. 5. Acts executed against the provisions of mandatory or prohibitory laws shall be void, except when the law itself authorizes their validity. (4a)

[^civil-code]

# Explanation

Article 5 of the Civil Code establishes the general rule that acts executed against mandatory or prohibitory laws produce no legal effect. Whether an irregular act is void turns first on legislative intent as expressed in the violated statute, as explained in [*Far East Bank & Trust Co. v. Marquez*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46756).[^2] Where the law itself provides a specific annulment remedy or limits the sanction to penal fines without nullifying the transaction, the act falls outside absolute voidness under [*Cueno v. Bautista*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67306) [^3] and [*Moldex Realty, Inc. v. Saberon*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55856).[^4] Where no statutory validation exists, acts violating mandatory or prohibitory statutes remain legally inexistent from the beginning under [*Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. San Roque Power Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55538).[^5]

**In brief**

1. Identify a specific statutory command or prohibition that governs the transaction or proceeding rather than a merely directory rule.
2. Check whether the same statute or a companion provision supplies a limited annulment remedy or penal fine instead of absolute nullity.
3. Confirm that no curative statute, reliance exception, *res judicata* bar or procedural limit forecloses invoking nullity.
4. Determine the resulting relief, including cancellation, collateral attack, restitution or the *in pari delicto* bar.

The sections below examine how courts distinguish mandatory from directory laws, apply the statutory validation exception, enforce non-curability across judicial and executive acts, regulate specific legal domains, limit standing and remedies and trace the provision to the Spanish Civil Code.

## What Makes a Law Mandatory or Prohibitory

Article 5 operates only when an act actually contravenes a statute or valid regulation containing a command or prohibition. In [*Agga v. National Labor Relations Commission*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/36543),[^6] offshore oilrig workers invoked Articles 5 and 6 to assail their fixed lump-sum monthly salaries. The Supreme Court held that none of the cited laws and rules prohibited the payment scheme and affirmed the decision of the National Labor Relations Commission.[^7]

Courts distinguish mandatory or prohibitory provisions from directory ones by examining legislative intention rather than relying on mechanical formulas. Adopting Senator Arturo Tolentino's formulation in [*Far East Bank & Trust Co. v. Marquez*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46756),[^8] the Supreme Court explained that provisions dealing with convenience or unessential form are directory. Provisions affecting substantial rights or forming the essence of the thing required to be done are mandatory.[^9] Presidential Decree No. 957's protective purpose for innocent lot buyers therefore required construing Section 18 as prohibitory.[^10]

Even a prohibitory statutory command does not automatically void a private contract when Congress supplies an exclusive administrative or penal sanction. In [*Moldex Realty, Inc. v. Saberon*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55856),[^11] the Office of the President and the Court of Appeals relied on Article 5 to nullify a subdivision Contract to Sell executed before the developer obtained a License to Sell under Section 5 of Presidential Decree No. 957 or registered the contract under Section 17. Reversing the appellate court, the Supreme Court held that Sections 38 and 39 of Presidential Decree No. 957 impose general penalties without declaring unapproved contracts void.[^12] The Contract to Sell remained valid and was governed by the cash-surrender provisions of Republic Act No. 6552 upon buyer default. The Supreme Court declared the Contract to Sell cancelled and ordered the developer to refund PHP 187,647.75 as cash surrender value within 15 days from finality.[^13]

## Void Versus Voidable Acts Under the Statutory Exception

Article 5 expressly excepts cases where "the law itself authorizes their validity." In [*Cueno v. Bautista*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67306),[^14] the Supreme Court *En Banc* invoked this clause to resolve a long-standing doctrinal conflict over a husband's alienation of conjugal real property without his wife's consent under Article 166 of the Civil Code. In 1963, the husband was said to have sold a conjugal lot share to his father-in-law without his wife's consent. The spouses sued for nullity nearly forty-five years later in 2008.[^15]

Abandoning prior rulings in *De Leon* and *Bucoy* that had branded unconsented Article 166 sales void *ab initio* under Article 5, the Supreme Court *En Banc* held that Articles 166 and 173 must be read together.[^16] Article 173 gives the non-consenting wife a specialized action for annulment that must be filed during the marriage and within ten years from the questioned transaction.[^17] Categorizing such sales as void and imprescriptible would render Article 173 useless.[^18] Article 166 in relation to Article 173 therefore falls within Article 5's exception as a law that authorizes validity unless timely annulled, rendering the 1963 sale merely voidable and already prescribed. The Supreme Court denied the petition and affirmed the dismissal of the complaint.[^19]

By contrast, where a prohibitory statute contains no similar validation mechanism, the resulting act is void *ab initio* rather than voidable. In [*Geonanga v. Hodges*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1958/apr1958/gr_l-11323_1958.html),[^20] a creditor levied an attachment and had it annotated on land mortgaged to the Agricultural and Industrial Bank despite Section 26 of Commonwealth Act No. 459 declaring mortgaged properties exempt from attachment. The Supreme Court *En Banc* held that the annotation entry violated a prohibitory statute and was void under Article 5, not merely voidable.[^21] Unlike voidable acts, a void encumbrance may be assailed by any person adversely affected by it.[^22]

Separately, [*Philippine National Bank v. Lim*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55500) [^23] illustrates how *res judicata* can preserve a lower court's voidability holding between contracting parties even when a non-party buyer invokes voidness. In the first suit between developer Puerto Azul Land, Inc. and the bank, the trial court cited Article 5 and *Municipality of Camiling v. Lopez* to treat an unapproved mortgage under Section 18 of Presidential Decree No. 957 as merely voidable and estop the developer from questioning it.[^24] The Supreme Court denied review in a minute resolution that became final on September 10, 2004.[^25] When the developer later cited Article 5 and [*Far East Bank & Trust Co. v. Marquez*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46756) [^26] to re-litigate nullity in a buyer's suit, the Supreme Court held that the final minute resolution bound both contracting parties by *res judicata* regardless of whether it was erroneous.[^27]

## No Finality, Curability, or Estoppel Against Nullity

### Judicial, Quasi-Judicial and Tax Proceedings

Acts executed by courts, administrative tribunals or revenue officials in breach of mandatory jurisdictional or due process statutes are void and never acquire finality. In [*Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. G&W Architects, Engineers and Project Development Consultants Co.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70567),[^28] the Court of Tax Appeals *En Banc* issued two Amended Decisions after misapplying mandatory statutory voting rules under Section 2 of Republic Act No. 1125 and Section 3, Rule 2 of its Revised Rules. It first granted the taxpayer's motion for reconsideration on a tie vote and later denied the Commissioner's motion despite four affirmative simple-majority votes in a seven-member court.[^29] Applying the Article 5 rule, the Supreme Court declared both Amended Decisions void *ab initio*, incapable of attaining finality, susceptible to collateral attack and subject to reinstatement of the original March 21, 2018 Decision.[^30]

A judicial or quasi-judicial award rendered in defiance of a statutory stay order suffers the same fatal defect. In [*Lingkod Manggagawa sa Rubberworld, Adidas-Anglo v. Rubberworld (Phils.), Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/40799),[^31] a Labor Arbiter resolved an unfair labor practice complaint after the Securities and Exchange Commission had placed the employer under a management committee and issued a suspension order under Section 6(c) of Presidential Decree No. 902-A. Applying Article 5 language, the Supreme Court affirmed annulment of the Labor Arbiter's decision and the National Labor Relations Commission's writ, holding that a void judgment is non-existent and never attains finality even if the respondent failed to perfect an appeal bond.[^32]

Mandatory statutory waiting periods in tax refund litigation likewise trigger Article 5 nullity. In [*Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. San Roque Power Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55538),[^33] the taxpayer filed its judicial petition with the tax court only 13 days after submitting its amended administrative claim, violating the mandatory 120-day waiting period in Section 112(C) of the National Internal Revenue Code. The Supreme Court *En Banc* held that premature filing violated a mandatory jurisdictional provision, rendering the petition void under Article 5 and a mere scrap of paper incapable of conferring any right under Article 2254.[^34] Because no statute authorized the petition's validity, neither the tax court nor the Supreme Court could legitimize it even where the Commissioner did not dispute the numerical refund computation.[^35]

This jurisdictional defect cannot be waived by litigation conduct. In [*Team Sual Corp. v. Commissioner of Internal Revenue*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64167),[^36] the Supreme Court rejected the taxpayer's claim that the Commissioner had waived prematurity by allegedly failing to raise the 120-day defect early before the tax court division. A void petition under Article 5 confers no jurisdiction regardless of whether a party raises dismissal at the first instance.[^37] At the same time, [*Energy Development Corp. v. Commissioner of Internal Revenue*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67331) [^38] shows that taxpayers who filed premature refund petitions between December 10, 2003 and October 6, 2010 benefited from the *San Roque* exception allowing good-faith reliance on BIR Ruling No. DA-489-03 as a general interpretative rule.

Mandatory assessment safeguards protect taxpayers equally against revenue agencies. In [*Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. Reyes*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/40743),[^39] the revenue bureau issued an estate tax assessment notice and demand letter without stating in writing the factual and legal bases mandated by Section 228 of the Tax Code. Declaring that tax laws are civil in nature and that acts against mandatory laws are void under the Civil Code unless validated by statute, the Supreme Court held the assessment void and incapable of supporting a compromise.[^40] Extending this principle in [*Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. Unioil Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67609),[^41] the Supreme Court sustained cancellation of withholding tax assessments issued past the three-year prescriptive limit in Section 203 without proof of fraud or Preliminary Assessment Notice issuance and lacking Section 228 bases. Estoppel cannot excuse revenue officials from mandatory statutory mandates under Article 5.[^42]

### Non-Curability by Subsequent Acts, Approval, or Stipulations

An act void *ab initio* under Article 5 cannot be cured by subsequent enabling legislation, private performance, administrative approval, debt payment or litigation stipulations. In [*City of Pasig v. Manila Electric Company*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64064),[^43] Pasig passed a municipal franchise-tax ordinance in 1992 when Section 142 of the Local Government Code barred municipalities from levying franchise taxes. Converted into a city in 1995 by Republic Act No. 7829, Pasig sought to collect franchise taxes for 1996 to 1999 under Section 45 continuing existing ordinances. The Supreme Court affirmed nullification, ruling that the 1992 ordinance was void under Article 5 and that a cityhood law continuing existing ordinances presupposed valid enactments and could not revive a void measure.[^44]

Retroactive liberalizing laws also cannot save a void transaction once third-party vested rights intervene. In [*Nunga, Jr. v. Nunga III*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48276),[^45] a stockholder contracted in February 1996 to sell rural bank shares to a naturalized American citizen despite Section 4 of Republic Act No. 7353 restricting rural bank capital stock to Philippine citizens, then assigned the same shares to a qualified Filipino stockholder before Republic Act No. 8179 took effect in April 1996 granting former natural-born citizens investment rights. The Court of Appeals applied Article 5 to void the first sale. The Supreme Court affirmed *in toto*.[^46] Partial or full performance of a prohibited contract cannot make it operative. Republic Act No. 8179 could not retroact under Article 4 to impair vested rights already acquired by the qualified Filipino assignee.[^47]

Executive consent and private settlements are similarly powerless against Article 5 nullity:

- **Administrative approval:** In [*Abella v. Heirs of San Juan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/61711),[^48] a landowner and a farmer-beneficiary swapped a Presidential Decree No. 27 farm lot with approval from the Department of Agrarian Reform. Because Presidential Decree No. 27 prohibits transfers except to the government or hereditary heirs even before full amortization, no administrative approval could validate the void land exchange.[^49]
- **Full amortization and out-of-court settlements:** In [*Estate of the Late Encarnacion Vda. de Panlilio v. Dizon*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44473),[^50] harmonizing Presidential Decree No. 27 with Section 6 of Executive Order No. 228, the Supreme Court held that Section 6 must be read with Presidential Decree No. 27, so that even after full payment the land may pass only to the beneficiary's heirs or the Government. It declared transfers to non-qualified persons, if any, null and void and ordered the Department of Agrarian Reform to investigate them and coordinate the cancellation of the titles. It also agreed that no partial entry of judgment could be made for petitioners who relied on an out-of-court settlement.[^51]
- **Pre-trial stipulations:** In [*Spouses Terrado v. Court of Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1984/aug1984/gr_l-58794_1984.html),[^52] parties stipulated at pre-trial that a municipal fishery ordinance granting an exclusive 25-year concession without public bidding was legal. Because legality is a question of law, parties cannot stipulate validity over an ordinance and contract void under Act No. 4003, Act No. 4041 and Article 5.[^53]
- **Subsequent mortgage cancellation:** In [*Geonanga v. Hodges*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1958/apr1958/gr_l-11323_1958.html),[^54] settling and cancelling the Agricultural and Industrial Bank mortgage nearly a year after an illegal attachment entry did not retroactively validate the prohibited levy.[^55]
- **Judicial or agency approval of public-fund compromises:** In [*Strategic Alliance Development Corp. v. Radstock Securities Ltd.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/50847) [^56] the Supreme Court *En Banc* declared the compromise agreement between the Philippine National Construction Corporation and Radstock Securities Limited inexistent and void *ab initio*. Settling a PHP 6.185 billion liability needed an appropriation law. Article 1409 bars even the courts from ratifying such a contract.[^57] In [*Central Bay Reclamation and Development Corp. v. Commission on Audit*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68386),[^58] the Supreme Court *En Banc* dismissed the petition and affirmed the Commission on Audit's rejection of a compromise agreement that lacked congressional approval and appropriation.[^59]

## Substantive Domains Applying Article 5

Supreme Court decisions enforce Article 5 across diverse fields wherever Congress enacts mandatory form or public-interest prohibitions.

In government procurement, [*Miñao v. Office of the Ombudsman (Mindanao)*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68302) [^60] upheld the dismissal of a district engineer who split a PHP 5,500,000.00 allotment release for highway guardrails into eleven PHP 500,000.00 purchase orders to one supplier below his PHP 750,000.00 approval limit. Section 54.1 of the Implementing Rules of Republic Act No. 9184 prohibiting contract splitting to evade competitive bidding accords with Article 5.[^61] Financial loss to the government was immaterial. An acquittal in the parallel criminal case under the reasonable-doubt standard did not bar administrative dismissal proved by substantial evidence.[^62]

In succession and notarial practice, noncompliance with mandatory will formalities triggers Article 5 voidness. In [*Lee v. Tambago*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44758),[^63] a lawyer notarized a will attested by only two witnesses instead of the three required by Article 805 and omitted proper residence certificate notations under Article 806 and Section 251 of the old Notarial Law. Holding the will void under mandatory-law principles matching Article 5, the Supreme Court suspended the lawyer for one year, revoked his commission and perpetually disqualified him as notary public.[^64] Likewise, in [*Guerrero v. Bihis*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44409),[^65] a will acknowledged in Quezon City before a notary commissioned only for Caloocan City violated Article 806 and Section 240 of the Notarial Law. The acts of the testatrix, witnesses and notary were all completely void under Article 5, requiring denial of probate.[^66]

In civil status and marital disputes, mandatory registration and family-law rules restrict both private parties and courts. In [*Barcelote v. Republic*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63264),[^67] a married father registered two illegitimate children under his surname without the unmarried mother's signature. Applying Article 176 of the Family Code, Republic Act No. 9255 and Section 5 of Act No. 3753, the Supreme Court held that acts executed against mandatory laws are void and ordered the defective birth certificates cancelled.[^68] In [*Santos v. Santos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/65401),[^69] citing Article 2035(2) in relation to Article 5, the Supreme Court held that spouses cannot compromise the validity of their marriage. The appellate court therefore erred in terminating a wife's certiorari challenge in a nullity suit based solely on a property and custody settlement.[^70] Nevertheless, the compromise remained valid as a partial settlement of property relations under Article 2036. The petition was still denied because the wife's petition for relief failed to plead extrinsic fraud.[^71]

In land registration, [*Republic v. Court of Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1979/dec1979/gr_l-46626_27_1979.html) [^72] ordered cancellation of reconstituted titles issued over two Tala Estate lots whose original Commonwealth Torrens titles still existed in the registry. Because Republic Act No. 26 authorizes judicial reconstitution only for lost or destroyed certificates, proceedings covering subsisting titles are void under Article 5 and defeat a subsequent buyer who ignored the reconstituted-title warning on its face.[^73]

## Procedural Limits, Standing, and Restitution After Nullity

Article 5 does not confer roving standing or allow litigants to bypass procedural safeguards. In [*Ceruila v. Delantar*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/41563),[^74] petitioners obtained a trial court order under Rule 108 cancelling an allegedly falsified birth certificate without impleading the subject child or the Solicitor General. After the appellate court annulled that judgment under Rule 47 for lack of due process, petitioners invoked Article 5 to argue that falsity made the birth certificate void *ab initio* without Rule 108 compliance or allowed the appellate court to declare nullity peremptorily.[^75] Rejecting that contention, the Supreme Court held that Section 3 of Rule 108 strictly requires impleading affected parties and that Rule 47 annulment actions cannot substitute for trial on the merits.[^76]

Standing also limits the geographic or contractual reach of Article 5 relief. Under Section 18 of Presidential Decree No. 957, a developer's mortgage without written Housing and Land Use Regulatory Board approval violates a prohibitory law. In [*Far East Bank & Trust Co. v. Marquez*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46756),[^77] however, the buyer of one townhouse unit had actionable interest only over his own lot, so the Supreme Court restricted Article 5 nullification to Unit No. 10 rather than voiding the entire project mortgage.[^78] Following that limitation in [*Philippine National Bank v. Lim*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55500),[^79] the Supreme Court held that the mortgage over the project remained valid, without prejudice to the buyer's right under Section 25 of Presidential Decree No. 957. It partly granted the petition and affirmed the arbiter's decision except for the voiding of the mortgage and the award of damages against the bank.[^80]

Criminal prosecution over an allegedly illegal contract can likewise suspend civil recovery. In [*Republic v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/47492),[^81] a contractor sued for the unpaid balance of government dredging contracts that were simultaneously indicted before the Sandiganbayan under Section 3(g) of Republic Act No. 3019 as grossly disadvantageous. Rejecting an independent civil action under Article 31, the Supreme Court held that a contract executed against prohibitory laws is void and creates no valid obligation, requiring suspension of the civil suit under Section 2 of Rule 111 pending the criminal judgment.[^82]

Once a transaction is declared void under Article 5, remedies depend on whether both parties stand *in pari delicto* under Article 1411 or whether unjust enrichment and public-auditing rules allow documented restitution:

- **Barred by *in pari delicto*:** In [*Bank of the Philippine Islands v. Intermediate Appellate Court*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1988/aug1988/gr_l-66826_1988.html),[^83] a depositor who alleged Philippine residence delivered US$3,000.00 in cash to a bank for safekeeping instead of selling the foreign exchange to the Central Bank within one business day under Central Bank Circular No. 20 and Section 6 of Central Bank Circular No. 281. Because violating those circulars was a criminal offense and both parties were *in pari delicto*, the safekeeping agreement was void under Article 5 and Article 1411 barred the depositor from recovering the US$3,000.00. The Supreme Court modified the appellate decision. It ordered the bank to restore the separate US$1,000.00 unauthorized withdrawal to the depositor's dollar savings account with interest and sustained the PHP 8,000.00 award of damages.[^84]
- **Restitution to avoid unjust enrichment:** In [*Abella v. Heirs of San Juan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/61711),[^85] voiding the illegal Presidential Decree No. 27 land swap restored the farm lot to the tenant's heirs, so the Supreme Court ordered the heirs to return the Cararayan lot and PHP 5,250.00 with legal interest of six percent per annum from finality. Because the home lot had been resold to a non-party, it remanded the case to the trial court to determine the fair market value the heirs must pay.[^86]
- **Quantum meruit on void public contracts:** In [*Central Bay Reclamation and Development Corp. v. Commission on Audit*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68386),[^87] while voiding the unappropriated compromise agreement, the Supreme Court *En Banc* affirmed the audit commission's *quantum meruit* award of PHP 714,937,790.29 covering documented advance payments and project development costs.[^88]

## Historical Background and Spanish Civil Code Origin

Article 5 carries the "(4a)" code notation indicating its amendment of Article 4 of the Spanish Civil Code of 1889. Tracing that lineage in [*Cueno v. Bautista*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67306),[^89] the Supreme Court *En Banc* recalled its earlier interpretation in *Municipality of Camiling v. Lopez* explaining the Spanish term *"nulos"*. Acts contrary to law (*actos nulos*) were divided in Spanish commentary into two categories covering both *ipso facto* void acts and acts merely voidable at the option of the protected party.[^90]

This historical continuity explains both the inclusion of the exception clause in Article 5 and later legislative shifts toward stricter voidness in family property. Under the Civil Code, Articles 166 and 173 preserved voidability by limiting the wife's annulment remedy to ten years during marriage.[^91] When the Family Code took effect, Articles 96 and 124 omitted Article 173's remedy and expressly declared unconsented dispositions or encumbrances of community or conjugal property void, subject to acceptance as a continuing offer.[^92]

## Related Provisions

- [Civil Code, Article 4](art-0004.md): Establishes prospective operation of laws. Curative or liberalizing statutes such as Republic Act No. 8179 cannot apply retroactively to validate an acquisition void under Article 5 when third-party rights have already vested, as held in [*Nunga, Jr. v. Nunga III*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48276).[^93]
- [Civil Code, Article 6](art-0006.md): Bars waivers contrary to law or public policy. Invoked alongside Article 5 against challenged employment pay terms in [*Agga v. National Labor Relations Commission*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/36543),[^94] where the Supreme Court found that none of the cited laws and rules prohibited the scheme.
- [Family Code, Article 96](../../family-code/title-04-property-relations-between-husband-and-w/art-096.md) and [Family Code, Article 124](../../family-code/title-04-property-relations-between-husband-and-w/art-124.md): Declare unconsented spousal alienations void under the Family Code, contrasted with Civil Code Articles 166 and 173 in [*Cueno v. Bautista*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67306).[^95]
- [Civil Code, Article 166](../book-1-title-06-property-relations-between/art-0166.md) and [Civil Code, Article 173](../book-1-title-06-property-relations-between/art-0173.md): Governed conjugal real-property alienations prior to the Family Code and constitute a statutory validation exception under Article 5 making unconsented sales voidable within ten years, as settled in [*Cueno v. Bautista*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67306).[^96]
- [Family Code, Article 176](../../family-code/title-06-paternity-and-filiation/art-176.md): Mandates that illegitimate children use their mother's surname absent Republic Act No. 9255 compliance, voiding unauthorized birth certificates under Article 5 principles in [*Barcelote v. Republic*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63264).[^97]
- [Civil Code, Article 805](../book-3-title-04-succession/art-0805.md) and [Civil Code, Article 806](../book-3-title-04-succession/art-0806.md): Prescribe mandatory attestation and notarial acknowledgment rules whose violation voids a will under Article 5 in [*Lee v. Tambago*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44758) [^98] and [*Guerrero v. Bihis*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44409).[^99]
- [Civil Code, Article 1409](../book-4-title-02-contracts/art-1409.md): Declares contracts contrary to law or expressly prohibited by law inexistent and non-ratifiable, applied alongside Article 5 to unappropriated public compromises in [*Strategic Alliance Development Corp. v. Radstock Securities Ltd.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/50847).[^100]
- [Civil Code, Article 1411](../book-4-title-02-contracts/art-1411.md): Bars reciprocal causes of action when an Article 5-void contract involves a criminal offense and both parties are *in pari delicto*, applied in [*Bank of the Philippine Islands v. Intermediate Appellate Court*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1988/aug1988/gr_l-66826_1988.html).[^101]
- [Civil Code, Article 2035](../book-4-title-14-compromises-and-arbitrations/art-2035.md) and [Civil Code, Article 2036](../book-4-title-14-compromises-and-arbitrations/art-2036.md): Prohibit compromises on marital validity while limiting compromises to objects definitely stated, applied with Article 5 in [*Santos v. Santos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/65401).[^102]
- [Civil Code, Article 2254](../book-4-title-19-concurrence-and-preference-of-credits/art-2254.md): Confirms that no vested or acquired right arises from unlawful acts, reinforcing Article 5 against premature tax refund petitions in [*Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. San Roque Power Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55538).[^103]

## Key Cases

- [*Far East Bank & Trust Co. v. Marquez*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46756) [^104]: Adopts legislative intent and substance tests for mandatory vs. directory laws, treats Section 18 of Presidential Decree No. 957 as prohibitory under Article 5 and limits mortgage nullity to the buyer's lot.
- [*Cueno v. Bautista*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67306) [^105]: *En Banc* ruling abandoning *De Leon* and *Bucoy* and holding that Civil Code Articles 166 and 173 fall under Article 5's validation exception, making unconsented pre-Family Code conjugal sales voidable within ten years.
- [*Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. San Roque Power Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55538) [^106]: *En Banc* decision holding that premature judicial VAT refund claims violating the mandatory 120-day waiting period are void as a scrap of paper under Articles 5 and 2254 unless covered by the BIR Ruling No. DA-489-03 window.
- [*Moldex Realty, Inc. v. Saberon*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55856) [^107]: Reverses Article 5 nullification and holds that selling subdivision lots without a License to Sell under Section 5 of Presidential Decree No. 957 triggers statutory penalties rather than voiding the Contract to Sell.
- [*Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. G&W Architects, Engineers and Project Development Consultants Co.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70567) [^108]: Voided two Court of Tax Appeals *En Banc* Amended Decisions entered on tie and misapplied vote thresholds in breach of mandatory statutory deliberation rules.
- [*Lingkod Manggagawa sa Rubberworld, Adidas-Anglo v. Rubberworld (Phils.), Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/40799) [^109]: Annulled a Labor Arbiter decision rendered in defiance of a Securities and Exchange Commission suspension order under Section 6(c) of Presidential Decree No. 902-A regardless of appeal bond defects.
- [*Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. Reyes*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/40743) [^110] and [*Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. Unioil Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67609) [^111]: Established that tax assessments lacking written factual and legal bases under Section 228 or issued beyond Section 203 prescription are void without estoppel against taxpayers.
- [*City of Pasig v. Manila Electric Company*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64064) [^112]: Held that municipal conversion into a city and general ordinance-continuity clauses cannot cure or validate a local tax ordinance that was void *ab initio* when enacted.
- [*Strategic Alliance Development Corp. v. Radstock Securities Ltd.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/50847) [^113]: Declared the compromise agreement between a government corporation and a private creditor void *ab initio* for want of an appropriation law.
- [*Central Bay Reclamation and Development Corp. v. Commission on Audit*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68386) [^114]: Dismissed the petition and affirmed the Commission on Audit's rejection of a compromise agreement that lacked congressional approval and appropriation, while sustaining a *quantum meruit* award.
- [*Nunga, Jr. v. Nunga III*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48276) [^115]: Confirmed that full performance cannot validate a rural-bank share sale prohibited by foreign-ownership limits and subsequent liberalization cannot divest prior third-party rights.
- [*Guerrero v. Bihis*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44409) [^116] and [*Lee v. Tambago*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44758) [^117]: Enforced mandatory Civil Code will execution and Notarial Law territorial and witness requirements by voiding non-compliant wills and sanctioning erring notaries.
- [*Abella v. Heirs of San Juan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/61711) [^118] and [*Estate of the Late Encarnacion Vda. de Panlilio v. Dizon*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44473) [^119]: Abella voided a Presidential Decree No. 27 land swap despite Department of Agrarian Reform approval and ordered reciprocal restitution to avoid unjust enrichment. Panlilio declared transfers to non-qualified persons void even after full amortization and ordered the Department of Agrarian Reform to investigate them.
- [*Bank of the Philippine Islands v. Intermediate Appellate Court*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1988/aug1988/gr_l-66826_1988.html) [^120]: Applied Article 5 and the *in pari delicto* bar under Article 1411 to dismiss recovery of foreign currency deposited for safekeeping in criminal breach of Central Bank exchange controls.

## Case Discussions

### Non-Prohibition Threshold Against Article 5

Oilrig crewmen hired by Supply Oilfield Services, Inc. and Underseas Drilling, Inc. signed one-year contracts providing fixed monthly lump-sum pay covering basic wages, overtime, holiday pay and other statutory benefits. The Philippine Overseas Employment Administration dismissed their underpayment complaints on July 2, 1992. The National Labor Relations Commission affirmed dismissal on November 27, 1995. Elevating the dispute to the Supreme Court, petitioners invoked Articles 5 and 6 of the Civil Code alongside Labor Code provisions on overtime and differentials to argue that the lump-sum arrangement was illegal. The Supreme Court affirmed the decision of the National Labor Relations Commission. It held that none of the cited laws and rules prohibited the payment scheme. It described the cited Civil Code articles as merely providing that agreements violating law or public policy cannot be entered into and have no legal effect.[^121] It called the petitioners' stance bereft of legal support. The ruling shows that an Article 5 challenge fails when the challenger cannot point to a specific prohibition covering the arrangement.

### Non-Waivability of Premature VAT Refund Petitions

Team Sual filed two judicial refund claims before the Court of Tax Appeals covering taxable year 2001, one on March 31, 2003 for the first quarter and another on July 23, 2003 for the remaining three quarters. The tax court *En Banc* dismissed the March 31, 2003 claim as premature while awarding PHP 123,110,001.68 on the July 23, 2003 claim.[^122] Before the Supreme Court, Team Sual argued that pre-2010 rulings allowed immediate judicial filing and that the Commissioner waived the 120-day objection by raising it only in a motion for partial reconsideration.

Citing [*Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. San Roque Power Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55538),[^123] the Supreme Court affirmed *in toto* and held that a premature petition is void under Article 5 as a mere scrap of paper conferring no jurisdiction even if a respondent omitted early objection.[^124] The decision confirms that parties cannot waive Article 5 jurisdictional nullity by procedural silence.

### BIR Ruling No. DA-489-03 Reliance Window

Energy Development Corp. filed its administrative input VAT refund claim for 2007 on March 30, 2009 and elevated its judicial petition to the tax court just 25 days later on April 24, 2009. The tax court Second Division dismissed the claim as premature under *Aichi*. The tax court *En Banc* affirmed. Reversing both tax court rulings, the Supreme Court reiterated *San Roque*'s Article 5 doctrine on the mandatory 120-day waiting period while reinstating the 2009 petition under the *San Roque* exception recognizing taxpayers' valid reliance on BIR Ruling No. DA-489-03 from December 10, 2003 until October 6, 2010.[^125] The case clarifies that while premature petitions outside that reliance window are void under Article 5, timely reliance on that general interpretative ruling preserves jurisdiction.

### Void Execution Levy and Post-Payment Non-Curability

Two Iloilo lots mortgaged to the Agricultural and Industrial Bank were levied upon and annotated with an attachment by creditor C. N. Hodges on August 11, 1954 despite Section 26 of Commonwealth Act No. 459 declaring mortgaged properties exempt from attachment until bank debts were paid. After paying off the Bank and cancelling its mortgage on June 22, 1955, the property owners petitioned under Section 112 of Act No. 496 to cancel the attachment annotation, which the Court of First Instance granted on July 12, 1955. Rejecting Hodges's contention that later settlement of the bank loan cured the restriction and barred summary land-registration relief, the Supreme Court *En Banc* held that the entry violated a prohibitory law and was void under Article 5 rather than merely voidable.[^126] Subsequent cancellation of the Bank mortgage could not retroactively validate an annotation void at inception, allowing any adversely affected party to compel its removal.

### Procurement Contract Splitting

After receiving a PHP 5,500,000.00 special allotment release order for highway guardrails across eleven national road locations, an officer-in-charge district engineer split the procurement into eleven PHP 500,000.00 purchase orders awarded to one supplier without public bidding to fit within his PHP 750,000.00 approval cap. The Ombudsman-Mindanao dismissed him for grave misconduct, gross neglect of duty, serious dishonesty and conduct prejudicial to the service. The Court of Appeals affirmed despite a Regional Trial Court resolution acquitting him in the parallel criminal procurement case. Denying review, the Supreme Court held that Section 54.1 of the Implementing Rules of Republic Act No. 9184 prohibiting contract splitting accords with Article 5, actual government loss is immaterial and criminal acquittal under reasonable doubt does not defeat administrative findings supported by substantial evidence.[^127]

### Mandatory Notarial Will Formalities

In [*Guerrero v. Bihis*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44409),[^128] a daughter sought probate in the Quezon City Regional Trial Court of a will executed and acknowledged in Quezon City before a notary public commissioned solely for Caloocan City. Affirming denial of probate by both the trial court and the Court of Appeals, the Supreme Court held that extra-territorial acknowledgment breached Section 240 of the Notarial Law and mandatory Article 806 of the Civil Code, rendering the acts of the testatrix, witnesses and notary completely void under Article 5 and warranting administrative referral of the notary.[^129] In [*Lee v. Tambago*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44758),[^130] a complainant filed disbarment charges against Atty. Regino Tambago for notarizing a contested will attested by only two witnesses and lacking residence certificate notations. Adopting the Integrated Bar of the Philippines findings with modification, the Supreme Court declared a two-witness notarial will void under mandatory-law principles consistent with Article 5 and Article 805, suspending the lawyer for one year and perpetually disqualifying him as a notary.[^131]

### Illegitimate Child Birth Certificates Without Mother's Signature

Unmarried mother Jonna Karla Barcelote later found that the married father had unilaterally registered their two illegitimate children with the Davao City Local Civil Registrar using his surname without her signature. The Regional Trial Court of Davao City granted Barcelote's petition to cancel those Davao certificates, while the Court of Appeals reversed and treated them as voluntary recognition of filiation. Reversing the appellate court and reinstating the trial court order, the Supreme Court held that Article 176 of the Family Code and Section 5 of Act No. 3753 mandatorily require illegitimate children born after August 3, 1988 to bear the mother's surname absent Republic Act No. 9255 compliance and require the mother's signature on the birth certificate.[^132] Invoking Article 5's rule that acts against mandatory laws are void, the Supreme Court ordered both non-compliant birth certificates cancelled.

### Compromise Agreements During Marital Nullity Suits

After the Antipolo Regional Trial Court declared her marriage void for psychological incapacity under Article 36 of the Family Code, Dana Santos sought relief from judgment under Rule 38 and elevated its denial to the Court of Appeals via certiorari. During appellate proceedings, the spouses executed a compromise governing property relations and custody, prompting the Court of Appeals to declare the certiorari petition closed and terminated. The Supreme Court ruled that under Article 2035(2) in relation to Article 5, no compromise on marital validity is valid, making the appellate court's termination of a marital-validity suit erroneous.[^133] Nevertheless, the compromise remained valid as a partial property settlement under Article 2036. The Supreme Court affirmed dismissal because Dana's Rule 38 petition failed to plead extrinsic fraud by her former counsel.[^134]

### Subsisting Torrens Titles and Anti-Graft Suspensions

In the 1979 decision [*Republic v. Court of Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1979/dec1979/gr_l-46626_27_1979.html),[^135] private individuals obtained judicial reconstitution under Republic Act No. 26 over two Tala Estate lots even though original Torrens certificates of the Commonwealth remained on file. Both the trial court and the Court of Appeals denied cancellation. Reversing both courts, the Supreme Court held that reconstituting subsisting titles falls outside Republic Act No. 26 and is void under Article 5, cancelling the derivative titles and rejecting a transferee's good-faith defense where the face of the title showed it was reconstituted.[^136]

In the 2003 decision [*Republic v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/47492),[^137] a contractor sued the public works department in the Malabon Regional Trial Court to collect balances on four dredging contracts simultaneously prosecuted in the Sandiganbayan under Section 3(g) of Republic Act No. 3019. The lower courts refused consolidation. Modifying the appellate ruling, the Supreme Court ordered the civil trial suspended under Rule 111 because a contract entered against prohibitory anti-graft laws is void under Article 5 principles and cannot anchor an independent civil action under Article 31.[^138]

### Exclusive Municipal Fishery Concession Without Public Bidding

The Municipal Council of Bayambang enacted Ordinance No. 8 in 1974 appointing a private manager-administrator over the Mangabul Fisheries for 25 years renewable for another 25 years without competitive bidding, despite disapproval by the Agriculture Secretary under Act No. 4003. In subsequent execution disputes before the Pangasinan trial court and appellate court, the parties stipulated at pre-trial that Ordinance No. 8 was legal. Nullifying the alias writ of execution and possession, the Supreme Court held that Ordinance No. 8 violated the public-bidding mandate and five-year maximum limit of Act No. 4003 as well as forest-control rules under Act No. 4041.[^139] Parties cannot bind the court by stipulating legal validity at pre-trial. Both the ordinance and the management contract were void under Article 5.

### Procedural Due Process Boundaries in Rule 108 and Rule 47

Platon and Librada Ceruila secured a Rule 108 order from the Manila Regional Trial Court cancelling Rosilyn Delantar's birth certificate without notifying her, her guardian or the Solicitor General. The Court of Appeals annulled that order under Rule 47 for lack of jurisdiction. On review, petitioners argued that falsified entries rendered the certificate void *ab initio* under Article 5 and required the appellate court to declare nullity peremptorily to avoid multiplicity of suits. Denying the petition, the Supreme Court ruled that Article 5 cannot convert a Rule 108 special proceeding into an ordinary civil action excusing Section 3 indispensable-party notice, nor authorize an appellate court in a Rule 47 annulment petition to adjudicate the merits beyond extrinsic fraud or lack of jurisdiction.[^140]

## Suggested Answers

### 1. Pre-Family Code Versus Post-Family Code Unconsented Conjugal Sales

**Question:** A husband sold a conjugal parcel of land in 1963 without the written consent of his wife, who waited until 2008 to file an action to declare the deed void. Separately, a second husband sold another conjugal lot after the Family Code took effect without his wife's consent. Both wives invoke Article 5 of the Civil Code to annul the respective deeds. How should the court resolve each action?

**Answer:** The court should dismiss the action challenging the 1963 sale as time-barred under Article 173 of the Civil Code. Under [*Cueno v. Bautista*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67306),[^141] conjugal property alienations executed prior to the Family Code are governed by Articles 166 and 173 of the Civil Code, which fall within Article 5's exception as a law authorizing validity unless annulled by the wife during marriage and within ten years from the transaction. Because more than four decades had elapsed by 2008, her annulment action had prescribed.

By contrast, the second wife prevails regarding the post-Family Code sale because Articles 96 and 124 of the Family Code eliminated Article 173's prescriptive remedy and expressly declared unconsented spousal dispositions void, subject only to acceptance as a continuing offer prior to withdrawal.[^142]

### 2. Premature VAT Refund Petition, Waiver Argument and Defective Tax Assessment

**Question:** A corporate taxpayer filed an administrative claim for unutilized input VAT on April 2, 2012 and filed a petition for review with the Court of Tax Appeals 15 days later without awaiting the 120-day period. The Commissioner answered on the merits and raised prematurity only in a motion for reconsideration after losing at the division level. In a separate audit, the Commissioner issued a Final Assessment Notice for deficiency withholding tax beyond the three-year limit without proving fraud or stating the factual and legal bases under Section 228 of the Tax Code. Resolve both tax controversies under Article 5.

**Answer:** The taxpayer loses the premature VAT refund petition. Under [*Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. San Roque Power Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55538),[^143] failure to observe the mandatory 120-day waiting period under Section 112(C) of the Tax Code renders a judicial petition void under Article 5 as a mere scrap of paper conferring no right or jurisdiction. Because the filing fell outside the December 10, 2003 to October 6, 2010 reliance window on BIR Ruling No. DA-489-03 recognized in [*Energy Development Corp. v. Commissioner of Internal Revenue*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67331),[^144] no statutory exception saves it. Furthermore, [*Team Sual Corp. v. Commissioner of Internal Revenue*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64167) [^145] establishes that the Commissioner's failure to object early cannot waive Article 5 jurisdictional nullity.

Conversely, the separate withholding tax assessment must be cancelled. Under [*Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. Reyes*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/40743) [^146] and [*Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. Unioil Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67609),[^147] tax assessments violating mandatory due process notice rules in Section 228 or the three-year prescriptive limit in Section 203 without proven fraud are void under Article 5 and cannot be salvaged by estoppel.

### 3. Public-Fund Compromise Versus Unlicensed Subdivision Contract to Sell

**Question:** A government infrastructure authority signed a settlement agreeing to pay a private contractor PHP 500 million without congressional approval or an appropriation law, while the contractor proved PHP 200 million in documented actual project costs. Separately, a subdivision buyer who paid more than two years of installments seeks full nullification and 100 percent refund under Article 5 upon discovering that the developer lacked an HLURB License to Sell under Section 5 of Presidential Decree No. 957 when the Contract to Sell was signed. How should a court resolve both claims?

**Answer:** The settlement agreement is void *ab initio* with recovery limited to documented *quantum meruit*. Under [*Strategic Alliance Development Corp. v. Radstock Securities Ltd.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/50847) [^148] and [*Central Bay Reclamation and Development Corp. v. Commission on Audit*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68386),[^149] a government agency compromise committing public funds above PHP 100,000.00 without congressional approval and appropriation violates Article VI, Section 29(1) of the Constitution, Executive Order No. 292 and Presidential Decree No. 1445, rendering the contract void under Article 5. The second case nevertheless allowed *quantum meruit* reimbursement for substantiated costs.

In the subdivision dispute, the buyer cannot void the Contract to Sell under Article 5 and is entitled only to the statutory cash surrender value upon cancellation. Under [*Moldex Realty, Inc. v. Saberon*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55856),[^150] violations of Sections 4 and 5 of Presidential Decree No. 957 trigger administrative or penal sanctions under Sections 38 and 39 rather than contractual nullification, leaving a valid Contract to Sell governed by the 50 percent cash surrender value rule under Republic Act No. 6552.

# Quotes

> "Surely, acts executed against the provisions of mandatory or prohibitory laws shall be void, except when the law itself authorizes their validity. As such, the Amended Decisions of the CTA En Banc in this case are deemed void ab initio, and therefore, non-existent."[^151]

> "There is no well-defined rule by which a mandatory or prohibitory law may, in all circumstances, be distinguished from one which is directory, suppletory, or permissive. In the determination of this question, the prime object is to ascertain the legislative intention. Generally speaking, those provisions which are mere matter of form, or which are not material, do not affect any substantial right, and do not relate to the essence of the thing to be done, so that compliance is a matter of convenience rather that substance, are considered to be directory. On the other hand, statutory provisions which relate to matters of substance, affect substantial rights and are the very essence of the thing required to be done, are regarded as mandatory."[^152]

> "While Article 166 expressly requires the wife's consent, the remedies and limitations under Article 173 reveal the legislative intent to characterize these contracts as valid until annulled, i.e., voidable, and not void. Evidently, Article 166 in relation to Article 173 falls within the aforementioned exception as "a law that itself authorizes their validity.""[^153]

> "San Roque’s failure to comply with the 120-day mandatory period renders its petition for review with the CTA void. Article 5 of the Civil Code provides, “Acts executed against provisions of mandatory or prohibitory laws shall be void, except when the law itself authorizes their validity.” San Roque’s void petition for review cannot be legitimized by the CTA or this Court because Article 5 of the Civil Code states that such void petition cannot be legitimized “except when the law itself authorizes [its] validity.” There is no law authorizing the petition’s validity."[^154]

> "It is not disputed that at the time the ordinance in question was enacted in 1992, the local government of Pasig, then a municipality, had no authority to levy franchise tax. Article 5 of the Civil Code explicitly provides, "acts executed against the provisions of mandatory or prohibitory laws shall be void, except when the law itself authorizes their validity." Section 32 of Municipal Ordinance No. 25 is, thus, void for being in direct contravention with Section 142 of the LGC. Being void, it cannot be given any legal effect. An assessment and collection pursuant to the said ordinance is, perforce, legally infirm."[^155]

> "It would not matter that Gonzalez executed the Contract to Sell in favor of Francisco Jr. prior to the Deed of Assignment in favor of Francisco III. As established in the previous discussion, the Contract to Sell between Gonzalez and Francisco Jr. was void and without force and effect for being contrary to law. It intended to effect a transfer, which was prohibited by Republic Act No. 7353. It is even irrelevant that the terms of said Contract to Sell had been fully complied with and performed by the parties thereto, and that a Deed of Absolute Sale was already executed by Gonzalez in favor of Francisco Jr. A void agreement will not be rendered operative by the parties' alleged performance (partial or full) of their respective prestations. A contract that violates the law is null and void ab initio and vests no rights and creates no obligations. It produces no legal effect at all."[^156]

> "Tax laws are civil in nature. Under our Civil Code, acts executed against the mandatory provisions of law are void, except when the law itself authorizes the validity of those acts. Failure to comply with Section 228 does not only render the assessment void, but also finds no validation in any provision in the Tax Code. We cannot condone errant or enterprising tax officials, as they are expected to be vigilant and law-abiding."[^157]

> "Applying Section 29 (1), Article VI of the 1987 Constitution, as implanted in Sections 84 and 85 of the Government Auditing Code, a law must first be enacted by Congress appropriating P1,027,031,483.79 as compromise money before payment to Central Bay can be made. Otherwise, such payment violates a prohibitory law and thus void under Article 5 of the Civil Code which states that "[a]cts executed against the provisions of mandatory or prohibitory laws shall be void, except when the law itself authorizes their validity." Indisputably, without an appropriation law, PRA cannot lawfully pay the money claims to Central Bay. Any contract allowing such payment, like the Compromise Agreement, shall be void."[^158]

> "The violation of a mandatory or a prohibitory statute renders the act illegal and void unless the law itself declares its continuing validity. Here, mandatory and prohibitory statutes were transgressed in the execution of the alleged "acknowledgment." The compulsory language of Article 806 of the Civil Code was not complied with and the interdiction of Article 240 of the Notarial Law was breached. Ineluctably, the acts of the testatrix, her witnesses and Atty. Directo were all completely void."[^159]

> "Since the mere safekeeping of the greenbacks, without selling them to the Central Bank within one business day from receipt, is a transaction which is not authorized by CB Circular No. 20, it must be considered as one which falls under the general class of prohibited transactions. Hence, pursuant to Article 5 of the Civil Code, it is void, having been executed against the provisions of a mandatory/prohibitory law. More importantly, it affords neither of the parties a cause of action against the other."[^160]

> "Moreover, having been made against a "mandatory or prohibitory" provision of law, the aforemention entry was, and is, "void" (Article 5, Civil Code of the Philippines), not merely voidable, and may, accordingly, be assailed by any party adversely affected thereby, such as petitioners herein."[^161]

# Tips

### Practical Diagnostic Checklist

- Confirm first that an actual statutory or regulatory prohibition governs the challenged act, because [*Agga v. National Labor Relations Commission*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/36543) rejects Article 5 arguments when no statute bars the arrangement.
- Classify the violated rule using the legislative-intent test in [*Far East Bank & Trust Co. v. Marquez*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46756) to verify whether it affects substantial rights or mere convenience.
- Check whether the statute prescribes only administrative or penal fines, because [*Moldex Realty, Inc. v. Saberon*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55856) refuses to treat Sections 4, 5 and 17 of Presidential Decree No. 957 as rendering Contracts to Sell void.
- Determine the date of any conjugal property disposition: apply Civil Code Articles 166 and 173 voidability before August 3, 1988 under [*Cueno v. Bautista*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67306), or Family Code Articles 96 and 124 voidness from that date onward.
- Verify whether an express reliance exception or *res judicata* bar blocks relief before assuming an act can be attacked at any time.

### Common Traps

- **Confusing pre-Family Code conjugal sales with void acts:** Practitioners still cite older cases treating unconsented Civil Code Article 166 sales as void *ab initio*, but [*Cueno v. Bautista*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67306) *En Banc* expressly abandoned *De Leon* and *Bucoy* and held those pre-August 3, 1988 sales merely voidable within ten years.
- **Overstating standing in developer mortgages:** Even where a developer mortgaged an entire project without Housing and Land Use Regulatory Board approval under Section 18 of Presidential Decree No. 957, [*Far East Bank & Trust Co. v. Marquez*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46756) and [*Philippine National Bank v. Lim*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55500) restrict nullification or redemption relief to the complaining buyer's own lot or unit.
- **Invoking Article 5 to bypass procedural rules:** In [*Ceruila v. Delantar*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/41563), petitioners argued that an allegedly falsified birth certificate was void under Article 5 and needed no Rule 108 notice to affected parties. The Supreme Court rejected that shortcut and held that Rule 108 and Rule 47 jurisdictional requirements remain strictly enforceable.
- **Relying on silence, stipulations or later laws:** Litigants often assume that pre-trial stipulations in [*Spouses Terrado v. Court of Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1984/aug1984/gr_l-58794_1984.html), delayed objections in [*Team Sual Corp. v. Commissioner of Internal Revenue*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64167), municipal cityhood conversion in [*City of Pasig v. Manila Electric Company*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64064) or agrarian-agency consent in [*Abella v. Heirs of San Juan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/61711) cure an illegal act. Every one of those arguments failed.

> [!NOTE]
> Before relying on Article 5 to recover money paid under an illegal arrangement, check whether the transaction also constitutes a criminal offense. In [*Bank of the Philippine Islands v. Intermediate Appellate Court*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1988/aug1988/gr_l-66826_1988.html), *in pari delicto* under Article 1411 barred the depositor from recovering US$3,000.00 entrusted in breach of foreign-exchange circulars.

### Litigation Strategy: What Worked and What Failed

- **Challenges that succeeded:** Parties defeated tax assessments lacking written Section 228 bases or exceeding Section 203 limits in [*Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. Reyes*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/40743) and [*Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. Unioil Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67609), set aside tie-vote and miscounted tax court amended rulings in [*Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. G&W Architects, Engineers and Project Development Consultants Co.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70567) and voided unappropriated public-fund compromises in [*Strategic Alliance Development Corp. v. Radstock Securities Ltd.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/50847) and [*Central Bay Reclamation and Development Corp. v. Commission on Audit*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68386).
- **Challenges that failed:** Litigants failed when they ignored a final judgment between the same parties in [*Philippine National Bank v. Lim*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55500), filed outside the BIR Ruling No. DA-489-03 reliance window in [*Commissioner of Internal Revenue v. San Roque Power Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55538), sought contractual nullity over licensing violations in [*Moldex Realty, Inc. v. Saberon*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55856) or tried to validate a void foreign share sale retroactively in [*Nunga, Jr. v. Nunga III*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48276).

> [!TIP]
> When challenging a void government contract or agrarian land swap, pair your Article 5 nullity claim with an alternative claim for documented *quantum meruit* or reciprocal restitution, as applied in [*Central Bay Reclamation and Development Corp. v. Commission on Audit*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68386) and [*Abella v. Heirs of San Juan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/61711).
