> ART. 33. In cases of defamation, fraud, and physical injuries, a civil action for damages, entirely separate and distinct from the criminal action, may be brought by the injured party. Such civil action shall proceed independently of the criminal prosecution, and shall require only a preponderance of evidence.[^civil-code]

# Explanation

Article 33 of the Civil Code gives the injured party an independent action for damages in cases of defamation, fraud and physical injuries. The civil action proceeds on its own evidence and requires a preponderance of evidence. Its independence allows the injured party to seek compensation even when the same conduct is the subject of a criminal prosecution.[^2]

**In brief**

1. Identify the defamatory imputation, fraud or bodily injury on which the claim rests.
2. Determine the source of the civil liability alleged in the complaint.
3. Bring the independent action without reserving it in the criminal case and prove it by a preponderance of evidence.[^3]
4. Account for any civil award arising from the same act or omission before enforcing recovery.[^4]

The scope of each statutory ground comes first, followed by the rules on filing, proof and recovery.

## Scope of the independent action

### Defamation

A civil action alleging libel may proceed on allegations of a defamatory and malicious imputation that received publicity and identified the person defamed. In [*Trillanes IV v. Castillo-Marigomen*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63938), the allegations about statements made to the media outside legislative functions sufficed at the pleading stage. The defenses that depended on evidence remained for the trial court.[^5]

A publication is read as a whole. The headline and opening paragraphs can give one impression while the later paragraphs show that the item recounts an allegation made to the police. In [*Arafiles v. Philippine Journalists, Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46004), the Supreme Court took both parts of the report into account in finding the evidence insufficient.[^6]

Article 2219(7) permits a juridical person to claim moral damages for libel, slander or another form of defamation. In [*Filipinas Broadcasting Network, Inc. v. Ago Medical and Educational Center-Bicol Christian College of Medicine*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/42995), the broadcasts were libelous per se and the law implied damages. The extent of injury to reputation still mattered in setting the amount. The Supreme Court also required a basis in the evidence and the judgment for attorney's fees.[^7]

### Fraud

Fraud has its ordinary sense under Article 33. In [*Hao v. Lagahid*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70070), the scheme to obtain control of titles to the claimant's detriment supported a separate fraud action even though the civil liability arising from perjury was deemed instituted in the criminal action. The same complaint can therefore require separate analysis of the asserted sources of civil liability.[^8]

Fraud can also supply the basis for an action for damages for unfair competition. In [*Caterpillar, Inc. v. Samson*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/62685), the independent fraud action and the criminal prosecution could proceed separately. The question of lawful trademark registration in the civil action did not determine whether Samson passed off his goods as Caterpillar's in the criminal proceeding.[^9]

### Physical injuries

Physical injuries means bodily injury in its ordinary sense. In [*Kane v. Roggenkamp*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/66520), that meaning covered the bodily injury alleged in a prosecution under Section 5(a) of Republic Act No. 9262.[^10] In [*Dulay v. Court of Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1995/apr1995/gr_108017_1995.html), the Supreme Court explained that the term extends to bodily injuries causing death. It also held that the allegations of the fatal shooting sufficed to let the complaint proceed to trial.[^11]

## Filing, proof and recovery

### The source of the obligation controls

A single act or omission can give rise to civil liability arising from the offense and to independent civil liability. Rule 111, Section 1 generally deems the civil action arising from the offense instituted with the criminal action unless the offended party waives it, reserves it or files it first. Under Rule 111, Section 3, an Article 33 action proceeds on its own. The injured party may file the action before, during or after the criminal case without making a reservation.[^12]

A contract or quasi-delict can supply its own source of liability. [*Lim v. Co*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55074) distinguished an appeal on civil liability arising from estafa from an action alleging breach of contract and abuse of rights over the same cement transaction. Both could reach final adjudication subject to the bar on double recovery.[^13] [*Casupanan v. Laroya*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/51368) likewise distinguished quasi-delict from civil liability arising from the offense charged. Its interpretation of Rule 111 includes Article 33 among the independent actions whose prescriptive period continues to run despite the filing of a criminal action.[^14]

The action for the amount of a check included in a prosecution under Batas Pambansa Blg. 22 follows Rule 111, Section 1(b). [*Heirs of Simon v. Chan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/37623) distinguished that claim from an independent fraud action in an estafa setting. Allegations of fraud did not allow a separate collection action for the same check already at issue in the Batas Pambansa Blg. 22 prosecution.[^15]

### Proof after an acquittal

The civil claimant must establish the claim by a preponderance of evidence. If an acquittal under Section 5(a) of Republic Act No. 9262 rests on reasonable doubt and the criminal judgment contains no declaration that the facts giving rise to the offense did not exist, the acquittal does not bar an independent action for the bodily injury. In [*Kane v. Roggenkamp*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/66520), the two actions vindicated different rights. Finality of that acquittal did not supply identity of causes of action or make the damages suit forum shopping.[^16]

The evidence offered in the civil action still determines liability and relief. In [*Belamala v. Polinar*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1967/nov1967/gr_l-24098_1967.html), the Supreme Court found that the award appeared to rely on findings in criminal decisions that never became final. The claimant could pursue a separate action against the administrator of the deceased offender's estate.[^17]

### Prejudicial question and double recovery

An independent fraud action does not furnish a prejudicial question merely because the civil and criminal proceedings concern the same transaction. In [*People v. Consing, Jr.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/47841), the purchaser's entitlement to a refund and damages would not determine guilt of estafa through falsification of public document.[^18] In [*Domingo v. Singson*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63038), the civil action sought nullity of a sale and the authenticity of the signatures was determinative of the criminal charge. The Supreme Court upheld the suspension while that civil action was pending.[^19]

The injured party cannot recover twice for the same act or omission. In [*Supreme Transportation Liner, Inc. v. San Andres*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64620), the Supreme Court allowed evidence on a quasi-delict counterclaim on remand. It required the petitioners to show that the trial court had made no civil award in the concluded criminal case.[^20]

### Employer liability

The allegations and relief sought identify the capacity in which an employer is sued. In [*International Flavors and Fragrances (Phil.), Inc. v. Argos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/52734), a claim against an employer expressly based on subsidiary liability for its employee's alleged libel was premature before conviction of the employee. In that setting Article 33 allows the action against the employee in his primary civil liability.[^21] In [*Filipinas Broadcasting Network, Inc. v. Ago Medical and Educational Center-Bicol Christian College of Medicine*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/42995), the station's liability with its broadcasters rested on Articles 2176 and 2180. The broadcasters acted in their official duties and the station failed to show due diligence in their supervision.[^22]

## Case Discussions

### Fraud in obtaining duplicate titles

The Regional Trial Court awarded Hao damages after finding that Lagahid misrepresented herself and fraudulently sought duplicate titles, but the Court of Appeals reversed and dismissed the complaint as barred by res judicata or litis pendentia. The Supreme Court granted review because Lagahid's scheme supported an Article 33 fraud action. Hao could not set up a compulsory counterclaim in the title proceeding where he was not a party. The Supreme Court reversed and set aside the appellate decision and resolution and reinstated the Regional Trial Court judgment. It ordered Lagahid to pay Hao PHP 50,000 in actual damages, PHP 100,000 in attorney's fees, PHP 2,000 as appearance fee, PHP 50,000 in litigation expenses, PHP 200,000 in moral damages, PHP 100,000 in exemplary damages and costs of suit. All monetary awards bear 6% legal interest per annum from finality of the Supreme Court decision until full payment.[^23]

### Bodily injury after acquittal on reasonable doubt

After Kane's acquittal on reasonable doubt for violation of Section 5(a) of Republic Act No. 9262, the Regional Trial Court of Mandaluyong City, Branch 214 dismissed Roggenkamp's damages action on res judicata and jurisdiction grounds, but the Court of Appeals reversed and set aside the dismissal. The Supreme Court rejected res judicata and forum shopping because the bodily injury claim raised a distinct cause of action. It upheld venue in Mandaluyong City because the record did not disprove Roggenkamp's residence there when she filed the claim. It denied the petition for review on certiorari and affirmed the appellate decision and resolution. It directed the Regional Trial Court of Mandaluyong City, Branch 214 to reinstate Civil Case No. MC08-3871, continue the proceedings and resolve it with dispatch.[^24]

### Quasi-delict counterclaim and double recovery

After a bus collision the Regional Trial Court dismissed the complaint for failure to prove liability and dismissed the counterclaim for lack of reservation in the criminal case against San Andres's driver. The Court of Appeals dismissed the appeal, but the Supreme Court granted it, reversed and set aside the appellate decision and remanded Civil Case No. T-2240 to the Regional Trial Court in Tabaco City to receive evidence on the counterclaim subject to the prohibition on double recovery. The Supreme Court applied Article 2176 and Rule 111 and explained that the current rule dispenses with reservation for independent civil actions including those under Article 33.[^25]

### Civil defamation pleaded against a senator

The Regional Trial Court of Quezon City, Branch 101 denied dismissal of Tiu's Article 33 complaint alleging defamatory media interviews and denied reconsideration. On direct certiorari the Supreme Court held that the hierarchy of courts should have been observed and that the allegations sufficiently stated a cause of action. Parliamentary immunity did not cover the statements to the media outside legislative activity and the evidentiary defenses belonged in the trial court. The Supreme Court dismissed the petition and affirmed the two trial court orders insofar as consistent with its decision.[^26]

### Nullity of sale and authenticity of signatures

The Regional Trial Court suspended the prosecution for estafa through falsification of public documents while a prior suit to annul the sale put the authenticity of the same signatures in issue. It later dismissed the civil complaint with prejudice for repeated failures to appear at pretrial and the Court of Appeals sustained both rulings. The Supreme Court denied both petitions and affirmed the two appellate decisions because the nullity action presented a prejudicial question while pending and the civil dismissal was proper. It directed the Regional Trial Court of Pasig City, Branch 264 to proceed with Criminal Case No. 137867 with dispatch because the dismissal left the signature issue unresolved.[^27]

### Unfair competition and the limits of prejudicial questions

The Regional Trial Court in Muntinlupa City suspended proceedings for unfair competition pending resolution of the civil case for unfair competition, damages and cancellation of trademark. The Court of Appeals dismissed Caterpillar's certiorari petition. The Supreme Court granted the petition for review in G.R. No. 164352 because the civil fraud action could proceed independently under Article 33 and lawful registration did not determine the criminal charges. The Supreme Court explained that the cancellation case required a ruling on lawful registration. The criminal actions asked instead whether Samson gave his goods the general appearance of Caterpillar's goods with intent to deceive the public or defraud Caterpillar. Unfair competition does not depend on registration, so ownership of the trademark did not decide whether the prosecutions could proceed. It set aside the decision in CA-G.R. SP No. 75526 and directed the Regional Trial Court in Muntinlupa City to reinstate Criminal Cases Nos. Q-02-108043-44 and forthwith try and decide them without undue delay. In G.R. No. 205972 it denied review of the separate appellate ruling on the Department of Justice's determination of no probable cause because Rule 43 was the wrong remedy and Caterpillar failed to show grave abuse of discretion. It ordered Samson to pay costs of suit.[^28]

### Unicapital's action based on alleged fraud

The Regional Trial Court in Makati City deferred Consing's arraignment for estafa through falsification and the Court of Appeals initially sustained the deferment but reversed itself and directed the criminal hearing to proceed with dispatch. The Supreme Court affirmed the amended appellate decision with costs of suit against Consing because Unicapital's damages action alleging use of a spurious title was independent under Article 33 and determination of Consing's alleged agency in the Pasig action would not resolve criminal liability.[^29]

### Contract and abuse of rights alongside an estafa appeal

Co bought the cement withdrawal authorities for PHP 3.15 million. The information for estafa alleged receipt of PHP 2,380,800 from Lim and alleged conversion of PHP 2,300,800, a figure reproduced with a sic notation in the decision. The Regional Trial Court of Pasig City, Branch 154 acquitted Co for insufficiency of evidence and later held him free of civil liability arising from the offense. The Manila Regional Trial Court allowed Lim's separate suit alleging breach of a cement sale and abuse of rights to proceed. The Second Division of the Court of Appeals dismissed Lim's appeal on the civil aspect for forum shopping while the Seventeenth Division denied Co's petition and remanded the separate civil suit. The Supreme Court held that the proceedings had distinct causes of action and could proceed subject to Article 2177. It granted Lim's petition, reversed and set aside the Second Division's resolution, reinstated her appeal and directed the Court of Appeals to resolve it with deliberate dispatch. It denied Co's petition and affirmed the Seventeenth Division's decision in toto.[^30]

### Collection of the check in a Batas Pambansa Blg. 22 case

The Metropolitan Trial Court of Pasay City dismissed Chan's separate suit for the value of the same PHP 336,000 check involved in the Batas Pambansa Blg. 22 case on litis pendentia. The Regional Trial Court affirmed and the Court of Appeals reversed. The Supreme Court distinguished the separate fraud remedy available in an estafa setting because the Batas Pambansa Blg. 22 prosecution already included the civil claim for the same check. It granted the petition for review on certiorari, reversed and set aside the appellate decision and reinstated the decision of the Metropolitan Trial Court, Branch 45, in Pasay City. It ordered Chan to pay the costs of suit.[^31]

### Defamatory radio broadcasts

The Regional Trial Court of Legazpi City, Branch 10 held the station and Alegre solidarily liable for libelous broadcasts and awarded the school PHP 300,000 in moral damages, PHP 30,000 in attorney's fees and costs of suit. The Court of Appeals added Rima as a solidary obligor and denied Ago's claims for damages and attorney's fees because the broadcasts targeted the school. The Supreme Court denied the station's petition and affirmed the appellate decision and resolution with modifications because the broadcasts were libelous per se and the station was liable under Articles 2176 and 2180. The station, Alegre and Rima remained solidarily liable to the school for its costs of suit and moral damages reduced to PHP 150,000. The award of attorney's fees was deleted. The Supreme Court assessed costs against the station.[^32]

### Arraignment in an unfair competition prosecution

The Regional Trial Court of Quezon City, Branch 90 declined to quash the informations or defer arraignment. On the petition assailing the trial court's orders for grave abuse of discretion, the Supreme Court sustained Regional Trial Court jurisdiction and held that the civil unfair competition action under Article 33 raised no prejudicial question. Samson failed to establish a right to deferment pending Department of Justice review and to attach the pertinent order. The Supreme Court dismissed the petition.[^33]

### Proof of libel in a newspaper report

The Regional Trial Court of Quezon City, Branch 97 ordered the newspaper and its editors to pay PHP 1,000,000 in nominal damages, PHP 50,000 in exemplary damages, PHP 1,000,000 in moral damages and PHP 50,000 in attorney's fees over a news item on a complaint of abduction and rape made to the police. The Court of Appeals reversed and set aside the award and dismissed the complaint for insufficient proof of sinister intent. The Supreme Court denied Arafiles's petition because the full report conveyed that it was recounting the complainant's allegations and the case under Article 33 had not been established by a preponderance of evidence. The Supreme Court read the article as a whole including the headline. It acknowledged that the headline and the first seven paragraphs gave the impression that a director had committed the crimes. It held that the later paragraphs identified the complainant and showed that the item was an account of what she reported. It also found that her sworn statement supported the two incidents reported, so the charge of fabricated facts failed.[^34]

### Plus Builders' claim for damages and attachment

The Regional Trial Court of Imus, Cavite, Branch 21 denied deferment of Consing's arraignment for estafa through falsification of public document, but the Court of Appeals reversed and issued a permanent injunction. The Supreme Court granted review because neither the alleged agency nor Plus Builders' entitlement to a refund and damages would determine guilt and the purchaser's fraud claim fell under Article 33. It reversed and set aside the appellate decision, lifted the permanent injunction and ordered the Regional Trial Court of Imus, Cavite, Branch 21 to proceed with arraignment and trial in Criminal Case No. 7668-00.[^35]

### Collection after dismissal of estafa cases

The Regional Trial Court of Pampanga, Branch 49 dismissed Cancio's collection suit after the estafa cases over the checks were dismissed on the prosecution's motion without prejudice to refiling the civil aspect. On direct review of questions of law the Supreme Court held that the collection suit arose from contract, a distinct cause of action that could proceed without prior reservation and was free of the asserted bars of res judicata and forum-shopping. It granted the petition, reversed and set aside the two Regional Trial Court orders and remanded the case for further proceedings.[^36]

### Reservation under Rule 111

After a vehicular accident the Municipal Circuit Trial Court dismissed a separate quasi-delict action for forum shopping while the opposing driver pursued a criminal complaint. The Regional Trial Court of Capas, Tarlac, Branch 66 dismissed certiorari on the view that appeal was the proper remedy. The Supreme Court held that the dismissal was without prejudice and open to Rule 65 review and that the Article 2176 action could proceed separately under the revised procedural rules. It granted review, annulled the two Regional Trial Court resolutions and reinstated Civil Case No. 2089.[^37]

### Fraud suit without reservation

The Regional Trial Court of Misamis Oriental, Branch 20 recalled its dismissal of Villegas's civil complaint against the cooperative and its employee while the estafa case against the employee was pending. On direct certiorari the Supreme Court denied the petition and affirmed the order recalling dismissal with no costs. The certification requirement did not apply at the time of filing and the revised procedural rules allowed the pending Article 33 fraud action to proceed without reservation.[^38]

### Premature claim of subsidiary liability

The Regional Trial Court of Pasig, Branch 166 first dismissed the damages suit against the employer and its managing director while criminal libel cases against the director were pending but recalled the dismissal. The Court of Appeals dismissed the employer's petition for certiorari and prohibition. The Supreme Court granted review because the allegations and prayer sought to enforce the employer's subsidiary liability before conviction. It reversed and set aside the appellate decision and resolution, dismissed Civil Case No. 65026 as against the employer and assessed costs against the respondents. The ruling left open relief that the respondents might seek at the appropriate time.[^39]

### Civil proof of bodily injury despite acquittal

The Regional Trial Court, Branch XIV, Malolos, Bulacan credited Apolonio's account of a blow to the head and the Court of Appeals affirmed its award in full. Apolonio testified that Salao drove him away from a drinking session and hit him on the head with a gun as he left. The Municipal Trial Court of Obando, Bulacan acquitted Salao of serious physical injuries and grave threats on the same incident. He raised that acquittal for the first time in his reply before the Supreme Court. The Supreme Court held that the challenge to the damages and the effect of the acquittal had not been raised below and could not be raised for the first time on appeal. It added that the awards were supported. The actual damages rested on hospital bills and receipts that Apolonio identified and offered in evidence. Moral damages were within the trial court's discretion under Article 2219. The attorney's fees rested on the finding that Apolonio had to hire counsel because of the suit. The Supreme Court also held that Rule 111 on extinction of the civil action after acquittal refers to liability arising from the crime and not to the separate action allowed by Article 33. Acquittal of serious physical injuries and grave threats was therefore not conclusive of liability. The civil case needed only a preponderance of evidence. It affirmed with costs against Salao, leaving in place his obligation to pay Apolonio PHP 20,000 in actual damages, PHP 10,000 in moral damages and PHP 15,000 in attorney's fees.[^40]

### Fatal bodily injury and primary liability

The Regional Trial Court of Quezon City, Branch 84 dismissed the complaint as to the two alleged employers of the guard who allegedly shot Dulay. The Court of Appeals affirmed. The Supreme Court construed bodily injury under Article 33 to include injury causing death and found the allegations sufficient for trial under Articles 2176 and 2180. It granted review, reversed and set aside the appellate decision and the Regional Trial Court order and remanded Civil Case No. Q-89-1751 to the Regional Trial Court for trial on the merits. The decision was immediately executory.[^41]

### Separate action against an estate administrator

The Court of First Instance of Bohol allowed a claim for physical injuries against Polinar's estate after he died before the criminal judgment became final. The Court of Appeals certified the appeal to the Supreme Court on questions of law. The Supreme Court held that the Article 33 claim had to be brought as a separate action against the administrator and that the award appeared to rest on criminal decisions that never became final. It reversed and set aside the judgment without prejudice to Belamala's action against the administrator of Mauricio Polinar's estate and made no award of costs.[^42]

## Key Cases

- [*DMPI Employees Credit Cooperative, Inc. v. Velez*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/53056)[^50] affirmed the trial court's recall of dismissal and permitted Villegas's independent fraud suit to proceed without reservation during the estafa case. The Supreme Court denied certiorari with no costs.
- [*People v. Consing, Jr.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/47841)[^51] held that the purchaser's action based on alleged fraud was independent under Article 33. The Supreme Court reversed the appellate decision that displaced the trial court's refusal to defer arraignment, lifted the injunction and ordered arraignment and trial to proceed.
- [*Arafiles v. Philippine Journalists, Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46004)[^52] denied review after the Court of Appeals reversed the trial court's damages award. Read as a whole, the newspaper report recounted allegations made to the police and the evidence did not establish the civil libel claim by a preponderance.
- [*Heirs of Simon v. Chan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/37623)[^53] granted review and reinstated the trial court's dismissal of the separate collection action for the same check included in the Batas Pambansa Blg. 22 prosecution. The Supreme Court reversed and set aside the appellate decision and assessed costs against Chan.
- [*Kane v. Roggenkamp*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/66520)[^54] denied review and affirmed the appellate decision and resolution reversing the trial court's dismissal. Acquittal on reasonable doubt under Section 5(a) of Republic Act No. 9262 did not bar the bodily injury suit. The Supreme Court directed the trial court to reinstate the case, continue the proceedings and resolve it with dispatch.

## Suggested Answers

### Bodily injury after acquittal

**Question.** The Regional Trial Court acquits a defendant of physical violence under Section 5(a) of Republic Act No. 9262 on reasonable doubt. The judgment contains no declaration that the facts giving rise to the offense did not exist. May the injured party pursue a separate civil action for the bodily injury?

**Suggested answer.** Yes. Under Article 33 the injured party can pursue damages for bodily injury by a preponderance of evidence. The criminal acquittal in these circumstances does not bar the distinct cause of action. [*Kane v. Roggenkamp*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/66520).[^55]

### Fraud during a pending prosecution

**Question.** A depositor sues for damages after an employee allegedly fails to account for money entrusted for deposit. Estafa charges against the employee are pending. The depositor made no reservation. May the fraud suit proceed?

**Suggested answer.** Yes. If the complaint asserts an independent action for damages based on fraud, Rule 111, Section 3 permits the depositor to prosecute it without reserving it in the estafa case. The claimant must establish the fraud claim by a preponderance of evidence. [*DMPI Employees Credit Cooperative, Inc. v. Velez*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/53056).[^56]

### Subsidiary liability for alleged libel

**Question.** While criminal libel charges against an employee are pending, the complainants sue the employer expressly in its subsidiary capacity. Can their claim against the employer proceed under Article 33?

**Suggested answer.** The claim is premature. The civil action under Article 33 may proceed against the employee in his primary capacity. A claim to enforce the employer's subsidiary liability requires the employee's conviction for the wrongful act committed in the discharge of his duties. [*International Flavors and Fragrances (Phil.), Inc. v. Argos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/52734).[^57]

# Quotes

> "The civil action under Article 33 may be pursued before the filing of the criminal case, during the pendency of the criminal case, or even after the criminal case is resolved."[^58]

> "This independent civil action may be filed separately and prosecuted independently. Failure to make a reservation in the criminal action does not constitute a waiver of the right to file an independent civil action."[^59]

> "In actions for damages for libel, it is axiomatic that the published work alleged to contain libelous material must be examined and viewed as a whole."[^60]

# Tips

> [!TIP]
> **Start with the source of liability**
>
> - Identify the conduct alleged. Ask whether it is defamation, fraud or physical injuries.
> - Read the complaint and the prayer together. The Supreme Court looks at what the pleading seeks.
> - Check whether the claim rests on the crime or on the Civil Code. The answer decides whether reservation, subsidiary liability or double recovery is in play.

> [!NOTE]
> **Read the acquittal before relying on it**
>
> An acquittal does not end the civil case by itself. In [*Kane v. Roggenkamp*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/66520), the accused was acquitted on reasonable doubt. The Supreme Court rejected res judicata and forum shopping and returned the damages action to the trial court. In [*Salao v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/35461), acquittal of serious physical injuries and grave threats did not defeat the claim. The civil case needed only a preponderance of evidence. Look at the ground of acquittal and at what the civil complaint alleges.

> [!TIP]
> **Raise every issue in the trial court**
>
> In [*Salao v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/35461), the petitioner raised the acquittal only in his reply before the Supreme Court. The Supreme Court held that the point came too late. Plead the acquittal and the damages objections at the first opportunity.

> [!NOTE]
> **Pending criminal cases do not freeze the civil action**
>
> - In [*Caterpillar, Inc. v. Samson*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/62685), the civil case on trademark cancellation did not suspend the unfair competition prosecution. The criminal cases asked about intent to deceive and not about registration.
> - In [*Samson v. Daway*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/45792), the civil action for unfair competition raised no prejudicial question.
> - In [*Consing, Jr. v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/56111) and [*People v. Consing, Jr.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/47841), the alleged agency in the civil suit would not decide criminal liability, so arraignment could proceed.
> - In [*Domingo v. Singson*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63038), a pending suit on the authenticity of signatures did put the same issue in question. Compare the facts before arguing either way.

> [!TIP]
> **Check what the complaint seeks against an employer**
>
> In [*International Flavors and Fragrances (Phil.), Inc. v. Argos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/52734), the allegations and prayer sought to enforce the employer's subsidiary liability before any conviction. The Supreme Court dismissed the suit against the employer. In [*Dulay v. Court of Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1995/apr1995/gr_108017_1995.html), allegations that the employers were liable under Articles 2176 and 2180 were enough for trial. Plead the basis of the employer's liability with care.

> [!NOTE]
> **Avoid double recovery**
>
> In [*Supreme Transportation Liner, Inc. v. San Andres*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64620), the counterclaim went back to the trial court to receive evidence. The prohibition on double recovery still applied. The claimants had to show what the criminal trial court awarded. Mere assertion of non-recovery was insufficient to warrant an outright award.

> [!TIP]
> **Keep the Batas Pambansa Blg. 22 civil claim in mind**
>
> In [*Heirs of Simon v. Chan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/37623), the separate suit for the same check failed on litis pendentia because the Batas Pambansa Blg. 22 case already carried the civil claim. A separate suit works best when it rests on a distinct cause of action. In [*Cancio, Jr. v. Isip*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/51647), the collection suit arose from contract and could proceed without prior reservation.

> [!NOTE]
> **Prove libel from the whole publication**
>
> - In [*Arafiles v. Philippine Journalists, Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46004), the later paragraphs of the report showed that it was recounting what the complainant had reported to police. Read the passages that identify the source along with the headline and opening.
> - In [*Filipinas Broadcasting Network, Inc. v. Ago Medical and Educational Center-Bicol Christian College of Medicine*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/42995), the broadcasts were libelous per se and the station was liable under Articles 2176 and 2180.
> - In [*Trillanes IV v. Castillo-Marigomen*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63938), parliamentary immunity did not cover statements made to the media outside legislative activity.

> [!NOTE]
> **Choose the right remedy and the right forum**
>
> In [*Caterpillar, Inc. v. Samson*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/62685), the second petition failed because Rule 43 was the wrong remedy for reviewing the Department of Justice finding of no probable cause. In [*Cancio, Jr. v. Isip*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/51647), the petition went directly to the Supreme Court because it raised only questions of law. A defective route can cost the case even when the claim has merit.

> [!TIP]
> **Mind procedure and parties**
>
> - In [*Casupanan v. Laroya*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/51368), dismissal for forum shopping was without prejudice and open to review under Rule 65.
> - In [*DMPI Employees Credit Cooperative, Inc. v. Velez*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/53056), the fraud suit could proceed without reservation under the revised rules.
> - In [*Belamala v. Polinar*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1967/nov1967/gr_l-24098_1967.html), the claim after the accused died had to be brought against the administrator in a separate action.
> - In [*Hao v. Lagahid*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70070), a person who was not a party to the title proceeding could not set up a compulsory counterclaim there.
