> ART. 2. Laws shall take effect after fifteen days following the completion of their publication either in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation in the Philippines, unless it is otherwise provided.

[^civil-code]

# Explanation

## Concept

Publication is an indispensable requirement for the validity and enforceability of laws. A law takes effect fifteen days after the completion of its publication, unless the legislature fixes a different date. Publication is made in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation. The requirement rests on due process and on the public's right to know the laws that govern them.

## Publication and Effectivity of Laws

The clause unless it is otherwise provided in Article 2 refers to the date of effectivity. It does not refer to the requirement of publication itself, which cannot in any event be omitted. The legislature may not make a law effective immediately upon approval, or on any other date, without previous publication. Publication is indispensable in every case, but the legislature may shorten or extend the usual fifteen-day period.[^16]

Omitting publication would offend due process. It would deny the public knowledge of the laws that govern them. Persons unaware of a law would be prejudiced simply because they did not know it existed. This is not true only of penal laws. A non-penal measure, like a law on prescription, must also be communicated to the persons it may affect before it can begin to operate.[^17]

Every person is conclusively presumed to know the law. That presumption presupposes that the law has been published, if it is to have any legal justification at all. The Bill of Rights also recognizes the right of the people to information on matters of public concern. This applies especially to the legislative enactments of the government.[^18]

## Which Laws Must Be Published

The term laws refers to all laws and not only to those of general application. Strictly speaking, all laws relate to the people in general, although some do not apply to them directly. An example is a law granting citizenship to a particular individual. Such a law surely affects the public, although it does not apply directly to all the people. Its subject is a matter of public interest which any member of the body politic may question in the political forums or, if they are a proper party, in the courts of justice. A law without any bearing on the public would be invalid as an intrusion of privacy, as class legislation or as an ultra vires act of the legislature. To be valid, a law must affect the public interest, even if it is directly applicable only to one individual or to some of the people.[^19]

All statutes, including those of local application and private laws, shall be published as a condition for their effectivity, which shall begin fifteen days after publication unless a different effectivity date is fixed by the legislature. Presidential decrees and executive orders are covered when the President exercises legislative powers validly delegated by the legislature or, at present, directly conferred by the Constitution. Administrative rules and regulations must also be published if their purpose is to enforce or implement existing law pursuant to a valid delegation.[^20]

Even the charter of a city must be published, although it applies to only a portion of the national territory and directly affects only the inhabitants of that place. All presidential decrees must be published, including those naming a public place after a favored individual or exempting him from certain prohibitions or requirements. The circulars of the Monetary Board must be published if they are meant not merely to interpret but to fill in the details of the Central Bank Act which that body is supposed to enforce. Municipal ordinances are not covered by this rule but by the Local Government Code.[^21]

## Administrative Rules and Regulations

### Rules That Enforce a Law Must Be Published

An administrative rule is not exempt merely because it comes from an agency and not from Congress. In [*Commissioner of Civil Service v. Cruz*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1965/dec1965/gr_21026_1965.html) (1965), a rule was applied against a customs broker. The Court of Appeals held the rule invalid because it had not been published in the Official Gazette. The Supreme Court affirmed. It read Article 2 together with [*People v. Que Po Lay*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1954/mar1954/gr_l-6791_1954.html) (1954) and concluded that publication of the rule was necessary for its validity and application.[^22]

The same idea appears in [*Pesigan v. Angeles*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1984/apr1984/gr_l-64279_1984.html) (1984). Even bureau regulations and orders become effective only when approved by the Department Head and published in the Official Gazette or otherwise publicly promulgated. That statement rests on the Revised Administrative Code.[^23]

### Issuances Exempt from Publication

Interpretative regulations and those merely internal in nature need not be published. An internal regulation is one that regulates only the personnel of the administrative agency and not the public. Letters of instruction issued by administrative superiors concerning the rules or guidelines to be followed by their subordinates in the performance of their duties also need not be published.[^24]

[*Tañada v. Tuvera*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/dec1986/gr_l-63915_1986.html) (1986) gave these examples of issuances that need no publication: the instructions of the Minister of Social Welfare on the case studies to be made in petitions for adoption. They also included the rules laid down by the head of a government agency on the assignments or workload of his personnel or the wearing of office uniforms.[^25]

[*Villafuerte v. Cordial*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/66587) (2020) explains why. Interpretative regulations are merely annotative. Internal rules are directly related to the conduct of government personnel and not the public in general. The Supreme Court applied this to the rules of procedure on the investigation of administrative cases against elected municipal officials, issued by a provincial board. The rules were neither penal nor a tax measure. They merely interpreted the procedure for disciplinary action set out in the Local Government Code. They did not need to be published. Municipal ordinances and resolutions are governed by the Local Government Code (Sections 59, 188 and 511), not by the Civil Code.[^26]

[*La Suerte Cigar and Cigarette Factory v. Court of Tax Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1985/jan1985/gr_l-36130_1985.html) (1985), decided before [*Tañada v. Tuvera*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/dec1986/gr_l-63915_1986.html) (1986), shows the exemption at work. The petitioners assailed a revenue memorandum circular on tobacco inspection fees because it was not published. The Supreme Court described the circular as an opinion on how the law should be construed. It found no attempt to enlarge or restrict the meaning of the law. The petitioners had each been furnished a copy, so the purpose of publication had been substantially complied with. The circular needed no publication.[^27]

This ruling should be read with care today. It treats notice to the affected parties as substantial compliance. [*Tañada v. Tuvera*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/dec1986/gr_l-63915_1986.html) (1986) later held that publication in full cannot be replaced by lesser forms of notice. The [*La Suerte Cigar and Cigarette Factory v. Court of Tax Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1985/jan1985/gr_l-36130_1985.html) (1985) ruling is safest as an example of a purely interpretative and internal circular.

### Filing with the National Administrative Register

An administrative rule that affects the public must also be filed with the Office of the National Administrative Register of the University of the Philippines Law Center. [*Securities and Exchange Commission v. GMA Network, Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48403) (2008) shows both requirements.[^28]

The Securities and Exchange Commission had issued a memorandum circular fixing filing fees. The Court of Appeals held it invalid because it was not published. The Supreme Court agreed. The record did not show publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation. The circular had also not been filed with the Office of the National Administrative Register.[^29]

The Supreme Court rejected the argument that the circular was merely interpretative. The circular implemented a statute and regulated the public at large. It was therefore not a mere internal rule or an interpretation of the law. It was ineffective because it was neither published nor filed.[^30]

### A Pre-Tañada Ruling on Delayed Publication

[*Philippine Blooming Mills Co., Inc. v. Social Security System*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1966/aug1966/gr_l-21223_1966.html) (1966) involved amended rules of the Social Security System. The rules said they took effect on the date of approval by the President. The amendment was published in the Official Gazette about ten months after approval. The Supreme Court held that the delay did not affect the date of effectivity. It said the date of publication matters under Article 2 only if the statute itself does not provide otherwise.[^31]

That reasoning does not sit well with [*Tañada v. Tuvera*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/dec1986/gr_l-63915_1986.html) (1986), which holds that a law cannot take effect without publication. [*Pesigan v. Angeles*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1984/apr1984/gr_l-64279_1984.html) (1984), decided later than [*Philippine Blooming Mills Co., Inc. v. Social Security System*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1966/aug1966/gr_l-21223_1966.html) (1966) and earlier than [*Tañada v. Tuvera*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/dec1986/gr_l-63915_1986.html) (1986), did not apply it. It set the case aside because it did not involve the enforcement of a penal regulation.[^32] The archive contains no passage that expressly abandons [*Philippine Blooming Mills Co., Inc. v. Social Security System*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1966/aug1966/gr_l-21223_1966.html) (1966). It should be read as a pre-Tañada ruling on rules that were already published before they were enforced against the parties.

## Penal Circulars and Regulations

A circular implementing a law has the force and effect of law. A circular that prescribes a penalty for its violation should be published before it becomes effective. Before the public is bound by its contents, especially its penal provisions, the circular must first be published and the people officially and specifically informed of its contents and penalties.[^33]

The Supreme Court cited the Spanish authorities on the equivalent provision of the old Civil Code. Manresa, commenting on that provision, treated regulations, decrees, instructions and circulars issued in accordance with law as included under the generic term laws.[^34]

The word laws in Article 2 includes circulars and regulations which prescribe penalties. Publication is necessary to apprise the public of the contents of the regulations and make the penalties binding on the persons affected by them. The same rule applies to an executive order that prescribes a penal sanction. In [*Pesigan v. Angeles*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1984/apr1984/gr_l-64279_1984.html) (1984) the confiscation and forfeiture provision made the executive order a penal statute. Justice and fairness dictate that the public must be informed of such a provision by publication before violators can be bound by it.[^35]

Commonwealth Act No. 638 requires that all presidential executive orders of general applicability be published in the Official Gazette. It provides that every order or document which prescribes a penalty is deemed to have general applicability and legal effect. The practice has always been to publish executive orders in the Gazette.[^36]

## Rules of Legislative Inquiries

The Constitution itself requires publication of the rules of procedure for legislative inquiries in aid of legislation. Section 21, Article VI provides that the Senate or the House, or any of its committees, may conduct inquiries in accordance with its duly published rules of procedure. The requirement is intended to satisfy the basic requirements of due process. Publication is imperative, for it would be the height of injustice to punish or otherwise burden a citizen for the transgression of a law or rule of which he had no notice, not even a constructive one.[^37]

Each Senate must publish its rules of procedure because each Senate acts separately and independently of the Senate before it. The rules must be republished in each Congress, unless the published rules clearly state that they remain effective in subsequent Congresses until amended or repealed. The constitutional mandate to publish prevails over any custom, practice or tradition of the Senate.[^38]

Publication of the rules on the Senate website, or in pamphlet form available at the Senate, is not sufficient. [*Tañada v. Tuvera*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/dec1986/gr_l-63915_1986.html) (1986) requires publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation. The Electronic Commerce Act treats an electronic document as the functional equivalent of a written document only for evidentiary purposes. It does not make the internet a medium for publishing laws, rules and regulations. A publication made after the inquiry began did not cure the defect for the inquiry in question.[^39]

## How and Where to Publish

### Publication in Full

Publication must be in full or it is no publication at all, since its purpose is to inform the public of the contents of the laws. The mere mention of the number of the presidential decree, its title, its whereabouts, its supposed date of effectivity, or publication in a mere supplement of the Official Gazette cannot satisfy the requirement. This is not even substantial compliance.[^40]

[*Tañada v. Tuvera*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/dec1986/gr_l-63915_1986.html) (1986) also held that publication must be made forthwith, or at least as soon as possible.[^41]

### The Official Gazette and the Newspaper Option

Article 2 as first enacted named only the Official Gazette. [*People v. Que Po Lay*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1954/mar1954/gr_l-6791_1954.html) (1954) quoted the provision in that form.[^42] [*Commissioner of Civil Service v. Cruz*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1965/dec1965/gr_21026_1965.html) (1965) did the same.[^43]

[*Tañada v. Tuvera*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/dec1986/gr_l-63915_1986.html) (1986) held that the publication required by Article 2, as it then read, had to be made in the Official Gazette and not elsewhere.[^44]

Article 2 was later amended by Executive Order No. 200. Under it, laws take effect fifteen days after publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation in the Philippines. The Supreme Court applied the amended text in [*Umali v. Estanislao*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1992/may1992/gr_104037_1992.html) (1992).[^45] It also stated the amended rule in [*Garcillano v. House Committees*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48331) (2008)[^46] and in [*Fariñas v. Executive Secretary*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/47067) (2003).[^47]

## Effect of Non-Publication

A circular or regulation that requires publication does not bind anyone until it is published. In [*People v. Que Po Lay*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1954/mar1954/gr_l-6791_1954.html) (1954), the Central Bank circular was issued in 1949 but published only in November 1951, about three months after the accused's conviction. The circular, particularly its penal provision, had no legal effect and bound no one until its publication. The accused could not be held liable for violating it, because it was not binding when he was found to have failed to comply.[^48]

The question of non-publication is fundamental and decisive. If the circular had not been published as required by law before its violation, then in the eyes of the law there was no such circular to be violated. The accused committed no offense. The trial court may be said to have had no jurisdiction. The question may be raised at any stage of the proceeding, even for the first time on appeal.[^49]

The Supreme Court in [*Pesigan v. Angeles*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1984/apr1984/gr_l-64279_1984.html) (1984) restated this ruling. A person convicted of violating Central Bank Circular No. 20 was acquitted because the circular was published three months after his conviction. He was not bound by the circular.[^50]

Non-publication also defeats administrative charges. In [*Securities and Exchange Commission v. GMA Network, Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48403) (2008), the fee circular could not be used to collect the fees it prescribed, because it had been neither published nor filed.[^51]

Lack of publication does not, however, automatically strip a body of jurisdiction over a case. In [*Villafuerte v. Cordial*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/66587) (2020), jurisdiction over the subject matter is conferred by law and determined by the allegations in the complaint. Publication or its absence is relevant to whether due process was observed.[^52]

## Effectivity Clauses and the Date a Law Takes Effect

An effectivity clause that makes a law effective immediately upon approval is defective. The law takes effect fifteen days after its publication in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation. The defect in the clause does not invalidate the entire law.[^53]

The date of publication, not the date of enactment, controls the effectivity of a law. Presidential Decree No. 1073 was enacted on January 25, 1977 but published in the Official Gazette only on May 9, 1977. Although its Section 6 said it takes effect upon promulgation, the Supreme Court read it to mean that the decree took effect only upon its publication on May 9, 1977. That moved the cut-off date for applications for judicial confirmation of imperfect title from January 24, 1947 to May 8, 1947.[^54]

[*Umali v. Estanislao*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1992/may1992/gr_104037_1992.html) (1992) gives a worked example of the fifteen-day count. Republic Act No. 7167 was approved on December 19, 1991. It was published on January 14, 1992 in a newspaper of general circulation. The Supreme Court held that it took effect on January 30, 1992, which is after fifteen days following publication. The Supreme Court also set aside the provisions of the revenue regulations that applied them only from January 1, 1992. It held that they should apply from January 1, 1991.[^55]

## Related Point: Judicial Interpretation and Retroactivity

This point rests on Articles 4 and 8 of the Civil Code and not on Article 2. It is included because the date a law takes effect also fixes the date from which its judicial interpretation operates.

Judicial decisions applying or interpreting the laws or the Constitution form part of the legal system of the Philippines. They are not laws, but they evidence the meaning, breadth and scope of the laws. A judicial interpretation of a statute becomes part of the law as of the date the law was originally passed, since the Supreme Court's construction merely establishes the contemporaneous legislative intent. The exception is when a doctrine is overruled and a different view is adopted. The new doctrine is then applied prospectively and does not apply to parties who relied on the old doctrine in good faith.[^56]

## Key Cases

- **[*People v. Que Po Lay*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1954/mar1954/gr_l-6791_1954.html)** (G.R. No. L-6791, March 29, 1954) — A circular that prescribes a penalty for its violation must be published before it becomes effective. Before publication it binds no one. Non-publication may be raised at any stage.
- **[*Commissioner of Civil Service v. Cruz*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1965/dec1965/gr_21026_1965.html)** (G.R. No. L-21026, December 29, 1965) — Publication of an administrative rule in the Official Gazette was necessary for its validity and application.
- **[*Philippine Blooming Mills Co., Inc. v. Social Security System*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1966/aug1966/gr_l-21223_1966.html)** (G.R. No. L-21223, August 31, 1966) — Delayed publication did not change an effectivity date set by the rules themselves. Pre-Tañada. Read with care.
- **[*Pesigan v. Angeles*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1984/apr1984/gr_l-64279_1984.html)** (G.R. No. L-64279, April 30, 1984) — The word laws in Article 2 includes circulars and regulations which prescribe penalties. An executive order with a penal sanction must be published before it can be enforced.
- **[*La Suerte Cigar and Cigarette Factory v. Court of Tax Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1985/jan1985/gr_l-36130_1985.html)** (G.R. No. L-36130, January 17, 1985) — A purely interpretative and internal revenue memorandum circular needed no publication. Pre-Tañada.
- **[*Tañada v. Tuvera*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/dec1986/gr_l-63915_1986.html)** (G.R. No. L-63915, December 29, 1986) — All statutes, including those of local application and private laws, must be published as a condition for their effectivity. Publication must be in full. Interpretative and internal regulations and letters of instruction are exempt.
- **[*Umali v. Estanislao*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1992/may1992/gr_104037_1992.html)** (G.R. No. 104037, May 29, 1992) — Applying Article 2 as amended, a law published in a newspaper of general circulation took effect after fifteen days following publication.
- **[*Fariñas v. Executive Secretary*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/47067)** (G.R. No. 147387, December 10, 2003) — An effectivity clause making a law effective upon approval is defective. The law takes effect fifteen days after publication.
- **[*Securities and Exchange Commission v. GMA Network, Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48403)** (G.R. No. 164026, December 23, 2008) — A memorandum circular that regulates the public is ineffective if it is neither published nor filed with the National Administrative Register.
- **[*Garcillano v. House Committees*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48331)** (G.R. No. 170338, December 23, 2008) — Rules of legislative inquiry must be published in the Official Gazette or a newspaper of general circulation, in each Congress. Website or pamphlet publication is not enough.
- **[*Spouses Fortuna v. Republic*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/56655)** (G.R. No. 173423, March 5, 2014) — A decree that says it takes effect upon promulgation takes effect upon publication. The publication date fixes the cut-off date for the rights it affects.
- **[*Philippine International Trading Corporation v. Commission on Audit*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63590)** (G.R. No. 205837, November 21, 2017) — A judicial interpretation is part of the law as of the date the law was originally passed, unless an earlier doctrine is overruled and parties relied on it in good faith.
- **[*Villafuerte v. Cordial*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/66587)** (G.R. No. 222450, July 7, 2020) — Interpretative and internal rules need not be published. Local ordinances and resolutions are governed by the Local Government Code.

## Suggested Answers

**Q:** A law is signed on January 15, 2026. It says it takes effect immediately upon approval. Publication in the Official Gazette is completed on February 20, 2026. A person commits the act it penalizes on January 25, 2026. May the person be convicted?

**A:** No. The clause making the law effective upon approval does not dispense with publication. Publication is indispensable in every case. The law takes effect after fifteen days following publication, or on March 8, 2026.[^57] The act was committed before the law took effect. In the eyes of the law there was no such law to be violated. The question may be raised even on appeal.[^58]

**Q:** The head of a government agency issues rules on the assignments and workload of the agency's personnel. Are the rules effective without publication?

**A:** Yes. Rules that regulate only the personnel of the agency and not the public need not be published. The test is whether the issuance regulates the public or only the agency's own personnel.[^59] The result would differ if the rules imposed a penalty or filled in the details of a statute the agency enforces. Those must be published. An agency circular that fixes fees for the public must also be filed with the National Administrative Register.[^60]

**Q:** A Senate committee publishes its inquiry rules only on the Senate website and summons a witness. Is the inquiry valid?

**A:** No. Section 21, Article VI of the Constitution requires duly published rules of procedure. Publication must be in the Official Gazette or a newspaper of general circulation. Website or pamphlet publication is not enough.[^61]

# Quotes

In [*Tañada v. Tuvera*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/dec1986/gr_l-63915_1986.html) (1986), the Supreme Court stated the rule on publication as a condition for effectivity:

> "We hold therefore that all statutes, including those of local application and private laws, shall be published as a condition for their effectivity, which shall begin fifteen days after publication unless a different effectivity date is fixed by the legislature."[^2]

In the same case, the Supreme Court identified the issuances exempt from publication:

> "Interpretative regulations and those merely internal in nature, that is, regulating only the personnel of the administrative agency and not the public, need not be published. Neither is publication required of the so-called letters of instructions issued by administrative superiors concerning the rules or guidelines to be followed by their subordinates in the performance of their duties."[^3]

In the same case, the Supreme Court held that publication must be in full:

> "We agree that publication must be in full or it is no publication at all since its purpose is to inform the public of the contents of the laws."[^4]

In [*People v. Que Po Lay*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1954/mar1954/gr_l-6791_1954.html) (1954), the Supreme Court explained why an unpublished circular binds no one:

> "It is clear that said circular, particularly its penal provision, did not have any legal effect and bound no one until its publication in the Official Gazzette or after November 1951. In other words, appellant could not be held liable for its violation, for it was not binding at the time he was found to have failed to sell the foreign exchange in his possession thereof."[^5]

In the same case, the Supreme Court treated non-publication as a fundamental question:

> "But the question of non-publication is fundamental and decisive."[^6]

In [*Pesigan v. Angeles*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1984/apr1984/gr_l-64279_1984.html) (1984), the Supreme Court held that penal circulars and regulations are laws within Article 2:

> "The word "laws" in article 2 (article 1 of the old Civil Code) includes circulars and regulations which prescribe penalties."[^7]

In [*Commissioner of Civil Service v. Cruz*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1965/dec1965/gr_21026_1965.html) (1965), the Supreme Court held that publication of an administrative rule was necessary:

> "From the cases of Ting and Po Lay, supra, and from the provisions of Art. 2, Civil Code and Sec. 511, Administrative Code, it is clear that publication in the Official Gazette of questioned Rule 30 is necessary for its validity and application."[^8]

In [*La Suerte Cigar and Cigarette Factory v. Court of Tax Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1985/jan1985/gr_l-36130_1985.html) (1985), the Supreme Court found substantial compliance with the purpose of publication:

> "As admitted by counsel for petitioners, the latter were each furnished with a copy of the Revenue Memorandum Circular in question and the purpose of the law, that is to inform or notify those who may be affected, has been substantially complied with."[^9]

In [*Securities and Exchange Commission v. GMA Network, Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48403) (2008), the Supreme Court held the circular invalid for lack of publication:

> "However, we agree with the Court of Appeals that the questioned memorandum circular is invalid as it does not appear from the records that it has been published in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation."[^10]

In the same case, the Supreme Court noted the lack of filing with the National Administrative Register:

> "The questioned memorandum circular, furthermore, has not been filed with the Office of the National Administrative Register of the University of the Philippines Law Center"[^11]

In the same case, the Supreme Court explained why the circular was not merely interpretative:

> "It cannot, therefore, be considered a mere internal rule or regulation, nor an interpretation of the law, but a rule which must be declared ineffective as it was neither published nor filed with the Office of the National Administrative Register."[^12]

In [*Umali v. Estanislao*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1992/may1992/gr_104037_1992.html) (1992), the Supreme Court fixed the date a law took effect:

> "Accordingly, the Court rules that Rep. Act 7167 took effect on 30 January 1992, which is after fifteen (15) days following its publication on 14 January 1992 in the "Malaya.""[^13]

In [*Garcillano v. House Committees*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48331) (2008), the Supreme Court held that website publication is not enough:

> "The publication of the Rules of Procedure in the website of the Senate, or in pamphlet form available at the Senate, is not sufficient under the Tañada v. Tuvera ruling which requires publication either in the Official Gazette or in a newspaper of general circulation."[^14]

In [*Fariñas v. Executive Secretary*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/47067) (2003), the Supreme Court restated the effect of the proviso:

> "the clause "unless it is otherwise provided" refers to the date of effectivity and not to the requirement of publication itself, which cannot in any event be omitted. This clause does not mean that the legislator may make the law effective immediately upon approval, or on any other date without its previous publication."[^15]

# Tips

> [!TIP]
> **Quick Diagnostic Checklist for Article 2 Problems.** Before deciding whether an issuance binds anyone, ask four questions in sequence:
> 1. **What kind of issuance is it?** A statute, a decree or an executive order that exercises legislative power must be published ([*Tañada v. Tuvera*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/dec1986/gr_l-63915_1986.html) (1986)). A rule that enforces or implements a law must also be published ([*Tañada v. Tuvera*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/dec1986/gr_l-63915_1986.html) (1986), [*Commissioner of Civil Service v. Cruz*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1965/dec1965/gr_21026_1965.html) (1965)). An internal rule for agency personnel and a purely interpretative circular are exempt ([*Tañada v. Tuvera*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/dec1986/gr_l-63915_1986.html) (1986), [*Villafuerte v. Cordial*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/66587) (2020), [*La Suerte Cigar and Cigarette Factory v. Court of Tax Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1985/jan1985/gr_l-36130_1985.html) (1985)). Local ordinances follow the Local Government Code and not Article 2 ([*Villafuerte v. Cordial*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/66587) (2020)).
> 2. **Does it carry a penalty, a fee or a burden on the public?** If yes, it cannot be treated as internal or interpretative. A penal circular or executive order needs publication before it binds anyone ([*People v. Que Po Lay*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1954/mar1954/gr_l-6791_1954.html) (1954), [*Pesigan v. Angeles*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1984/apr1984/gr_l-64279_1984.html) (1984)). A circular that fixes fees for the public needs publication and filing ([*Securities and Exchange Commission v. GMA Network, Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48403) (2008)).
> 3. **Was it published in full in an authorized medium?** The medium is the Official Gazette or a newspaper of general circulation ([*Umali v. Estanislao*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1992/may1992/gr_104037_1992.html) (1992), [*Fariñas v. Executive Secretary*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/47067) (2003)). Publication of a number, a title or a supplement is not enough ([*Tañada v. Tuvera*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/dec1986/gr_l-63915_1986.html) (1986)). Posting on a website or in a pamphlet is not enough ([*Garcillano v. House Committees*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48331) (2008)).
> 4. **Did fifteen days pass after publication?** The law takes effect on the sixteenth day unless the legislature fixed another date ([*Umali v. Estanislao*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1992/may1992/gr_104037_1992.html) (1992), [*Tañada v. Tuvera*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/dec1986/gr_l-63915_1986.html) (1986)). An effectivity clause that says upon approval does not skip publication ([*Fariñas v. Executive Secretary*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/47067) (2003)).

### Counting the Effectivity Date

> [!TIP]
> **Bar Exam Focus.** Keep four rules in view when a problem gives dates:
> 1. **Publication date controls, not the date of signing ([*Spouses Fortuna v. Republic*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/56655) (2014)).** A decree said it took effect upon promulgation. It took effect only upon publication. That moved the cut-off date for the rights it affected.
> 2. **Count from the completion of publication ([*Umali v. Estanislao*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1992/may1992/gr_104037_1992.html) (1992)).** A law published on January 14 took effect on January 30. That is the sixteenth day.
> 3. **A defective clause does not kill the law ([*Fariñas v. Executive Secretary*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/47067) (2003)).** An upon-approval clause is defective. The law still takes effect fifteen days after publication.
> 4. **Only the legislature may shorten or extend the period ([*Tañada v. Tuvera*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/dec1986/gr_l-63915_1986.html) (1986)).** It may not remove publication itself.

### Publication Medium and Completeness

> [!NOTE]
> **What Counts as Publication.** Check the following whenever the facts mention how an issuance was made known:
> - **Full text ([*Tañada v. Tuvera*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/dec1986/gr_l-63915_1986.html) (1986)).** Publication must be in full. Mentioning the decree number, the title or its whereabouts is not even substantial compliance.
> - **Prompt publication ([*Tañada v. Tuvera*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/dec1986/gr_l-63915_1986.html) (1986)).** Publication must be made forthwith, or at least as soon as possible.
> - **Electronic posting ([*Garcillano v. House Committees*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48331) (2008)).** A website or a pamphlet available at the agency does not satisfy Article 2. The Electronic Commerce Act does not turn the internet into a medium for publishing laws.
> - **Late cure ([*Garcillano v. House Committees*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48331) (2008)).** A publication made after the inquiry began did not cure the defect for that inquiry.
> - **Each Congress ([*Garcillano v. House Committees*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48331) (2008)).** Senate inquiry rules must be republished in each Congress unless the published rules state that they continue.

### Administrative Issuances: Publish, File or Exempt

> [!TIP]
> **Sorting an Agency Issuance.** Use this order of questions:
> - **Does it implement a law and affect the public?** Publish it ([*Tañada v. Tuvera*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/dec1986/gr_l-63915_1986.html) (1986), [*Commissioner of Civil Service v. Cruz*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1965/dec1965/gr_21026_1965.html) (1965)). In [*Commissioner of Civil Service v. Cruz*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1965/dec1965/gr_21026_1965.html) (1965) the Supreme Court held that publication of the rule in the Official Gazette was necessary for its validity and application.
> - **Does it also fix fees or regulate the public at large?** Publish it and file it with the Office of the National Administrative Register ([*Securities and Exchange Commission v. GMA Network, Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48403) (2008)). The Supreme Court rejected the claim that such a circular was merely interpretative.
> - **Does it regulate only agency personnel?** It is exempt ([*Tañada v. Tuvera*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/dec1986/gr_l-63915_1986.html) (1986), [*Villafuerte v. Cordial*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/66587) (2020)). Examples are rules on assignments, workload and office uniforms.
> - **Is it a local ordinance or resolution?** Article 2 does not govern. The Local Government Code does ([*Villafuerte v. Cordial*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/66587) (2020)).
> - **Does it predate [*Tañada v. Tuvera*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/dec1986/gr_l-63915_1986.html) (1986)?** Read it with care. [*La Suerte Cigar and Cigarette Factory v. Court of Tax Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1985/jan1985/gr_l-36130_1985.html) (1985) found substantial compliance because the affected parties received copies. [*Philippine Blooming Mills Co., Inc. v. Social Security System*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1966/aug1966/gr_l-21223_1966.html) (1966) held that delayed publication did not change an effectivity date fixed by the rules. Neither should be stated as current law without checking [*Tañada v. Tuvera*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/dec1986/gr_l-63915_1986.html) (1986).

### Non-Publication as a Defense

> [!NOTE]
> **Raising the Defense.** When a client is charged under an unpublished circular or order:
> - **Raise it at any stage ([*People v. Que Po Lay*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1954/mar1954/gr_l-6791_1954.html) (1954)).** The question is fundamental and decisive. It may be raised for the first time on appeal.
> - **Frame it as ineffectivity ([*People v. Que Po Lay*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1954/mar1954/gr_l-6791_1954.html) (1954)).** Without publication there was no circular to violate.
> - **Do not plead it as lack of subject-matter jurisdiction ([*Villafuerte v. Cordial*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/66587) (2020)).** The Supreme Court said jurisdiction comes from law and from the allegations in the complaint. Publication goes to due process. [*People v. Que Po Lay*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1954/mar1954/gr_l-6791_1954.html) (1954) did describe the trial court as lacking jurisdiction, so state the point as ineffectivity and due process to be safe.
> - **Check for a penal sanction ([*Pesigan v. Angeles*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1984/apr1984/gr_l-64279_1984.html) (1984)).** A confiscation and forfeiture clause made an executive order a penal statute. It could not be enforced before publication.
> - **Check the date of the act ([*People v. Que Po Lay*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1954/mar1954/gr_l-6791_1954.html) (1954)).** The circular was published about three months after the conviction. The accused was acquitted.

### Related Doctrines Often Tested With Article 2

> [!NOTE]
> **Connecting Points.**
> - **Presumption of knowledge of law.** Everyone is presumed to know the law, but the presumption presupposes publication ([*Tañada v. Tuvera*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/dec1986/gr_l-63915_1986.html) (1986)).
> - **Judicial interpretation.** A construction of a statute becomes part of the law as of the date the law was passed. A new doctrine that overrules an old one applies prospectively to parties who relied on the old one in good faith ([*Philippine International Trading Corporation v. Commission on Audit*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63590) (2017)). This rests on Articles 4 and 8 and not on Article 2.

> [!NOTE]
> **Practical Application.**
> - In any case on a penalty or a fee, ask first for the publication date and the medium.
> - Obtain the actual issue of the Official Gazette or the newspaper. Do not rely on an agency website.
> - For an agency circular that affects the public, ask for proof of filing with the National Administrative Register.
> - Compute the effectivity date from the completion of publication and not from the date of approval.
> - When a client cites a pre-Tañada case on notice or delayed publication, check whether it still fits [*Tañada v. Tuvera*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/dec1986/gr_l-63915_1986.html) (1986) before relying on it.
