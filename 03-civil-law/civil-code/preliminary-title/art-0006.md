> ART. 6. Rights may be waived, unless the waiver is contrary to law, public order, public policy, morals, or good customs, or prejudicial to a third person with a right recognized by law. (4a)

[^civil-code]

# Explanation

Article 6 establishes the general rule that a person may voluntarily surrender or relinquish an existing right, privilege or benefit. That freedom ends at two statutory boundaries. A waiver is void when it runs counter to law, public order, public policy, morals or good customs, or when it prejudices a third person whose right the law recognizes.

**In brief:** To test whether a waiver holds under Article 6, courts follow four steps:
1. Confirm that an actual right exists and belongs to the party waiving it.
2. Verify clear, unequivocal, voluntary and knowing intent to relinquish that right without unilateral imposition.
3. Check whether surrendering the right violates law, public order, public policy, morals or good customs.
4. Determine whether the waiver prejudices a third person who holds a law-recognized right.

This page explains the scope and elements of waiver, how courts test clear intent and conduct, the five public-interest bars and third-person limits, labor quitclaims and compromises, constitutional and procedural waivers and the provision's Spanish Civil Code origin.

## Scope and Elements of Waiver

The Supreme Court defines waiver as a voluntary and intentional relinquishment or abandonment of a known existing legal right, advantage, benefit, claim or privilege.[^2] That rule spans private agreements, statutory benefits and personal constitutional guarantees, as recognized in [*People v. Donato*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/jun1991/gr_79269_1991.html)[^3] and [*Concerned Organization of PASAR Progressive Employees for Reform (COPPER) v. Philippine Associated Smelting and Refining Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70547).[^4] Three essential requisites must concur for a valid waiver under [*COPPER*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70547).[^5] First, the right must actually exist. Second, the holder must know of its existence. Third, the holder must intend to relinquish it.

Not every legal interest stands on equal footing when renounced. Distinguishing waivable from non-waivable rights in [*Valenzuela Hardwood and Industrial Supply, Inc. v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/34833), the Supreme Court held that patrimonial rights may generally be waived as opposed to rights to personality and family rights.[^6] A party may also renounce a statutory protection enacted solely for personal benefit. In [*Mabugay-Otamias v. Republic*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/62002), the Supreme Court held that a retired military officer validly waived his statutory exemption of retirement benefits from execution by assigning half of his pension for the support of his wife and five children.[^7] In [*Albay Electric Cooperative, Inc. v. Santelices*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/47188), the Supreme Court likewise held that a respondent validly waived pre-trial transportation and appearance awards granted exclusively for its own benefit.[^8] Conversely, Article 6 does not invalidate a contract when no statute or public policy forbids the challenged arrangement, as the Supreme Court held in [*Agga v. National Labor Relations Commission*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/36543) in upholding fixed lump-sum offshore compensation contracts where no underpayment occurred.[^9]

## Clear Intent, Voluntariness and Conduct

Courts never lightly infer the surrender of a legal right. In [*Gatchalian v. Delim*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/oct1991/gr_56487_1991.html), the Supreme Court emphasized that a valid waiver must be couched in clear and unequivocal terms that leave no doubt of the person's intention to give up a right.[^10] Applying the same strict standard in [*Premiere Development Bank v. Central Surety & Insurance Co., Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48855), the Supreme Court held that courts must indulge every reasonable presumption against waiver and require persuasive proof of actual intent to relinquish rather than mere silence.[^11] In [*Premiere Development Bank*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48855), the Supreme Court added that a collection letter did not waive the bank's right to apply payments across obligations, especially given an express non-waiver clause in the loan instruments.[^12] Similarly, a party cannot manufacture a waiver by imposing a unilateral notice on another. In [*Bank of the Philippine Islands v. Casa Montessori Internationale*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46619), the Supreme Court ruled under Article 6 that a bank had no right to print a unilateral ten-day error-reporting condition on monthly statements and treat a depositor's silence as a waiver or renunciation of rights it had not possessed.[^13]

A waiver may nonetheless be implied when unequivocal voluntary conduct proves relinquishment. In [*F.F. Cruz & Co., Inc. v. HR Construction Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/24219), the Supreme Court affirmed an arbitral ruling under Article 6 that a contractor's repeated payment of monthly progress billings without requiring joint measurement with the public works department waived its contractual right to demand joint measurement later.[^14] In [*F.F. Cruz*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/24219), the Supreme Court also held that the subcontractor waived extrajudicial rescission by agreeing to Article 11.2 of the subcontract, so its work stoppage was unjustified.[^15] Voluntary acceptance of an outcome without coercion operates identically. In [*University of the East v. Masangkay*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64125), the Supreme Court held under Article 6 that two university professors and associate deans who collected their benefits and did not contest their dismissal for almost three years voluntarily waived their right to challenge it.[^16]

Conduct embedded in formal instruments carries the same binding force. In [*Esguerra v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/34493), the Supreme Court held under Article 6 that a spouse waived any alleged right of first refusal by signing a judicially approved compromise agreement that authorized a corporate property sale without reserving it.[^17] In [*Estate of the Late Encarnacion Vda. de Panlilio v. Dizon*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44473), the Supreme Court held under Article 6 that a landowner's intentional and voluntary surrender of agricultural holdings through a sworn affidavit waived her statutory exemption for sugarland and validly gave rise to beneficiaries' ownership under Presidential Decree No. 27.[^18] In litigation as well, a party who signs an explicit factual admission at pre-trial waives a contradictory defense under Article 6, as held in [*Alano v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/35392),[^19] and repeated unjustified postponements waive trial presence and defense opportunities, as held in [*Sayson v. People*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1988/oct1988/gr_l-51745_1988.html)[^20] and [*People v. Serzo, Jr.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/34896).[^21]

## Public-Interest Limits and Third-Person Prejudice

Article 6 voids any waiver that contravenes law, public order, public policy, morals or good customs, even if freely signed. In [*Son v. University of Santo Tomas*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64107), the Supreme Court held that a university could not waive the statutory master's degree requirement under CHED Memorandum Order No. 40-08 through a collective bargaining tenure-by-default clause because doing so violated law and prejudiced students and the general public.[^22] Applying [*Son*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64107) in [*Brazil v. STI Education Services Group, Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64817), the Supreme Court rejected part-time instructors' estoppel claims because no waiver or estoppel can arise from an illegal employment arrangement lacking mandatory qualifications.[^23] Agrarian reform protections enjoy the same non-waivable shield. In [*Siacor v. Gigantana*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/50693), the Supreme Court added that even if a farmer-beneficiary's written waiver had covered his Certificate of Land Transfer parcel, it would be void under Article 6 as contrary to law and public policy.[^24]

Public policy bars waivers across executive privilege, foreclosure notices, carrier diligence, negligence and social welfare laws. In [*Department of Foreign Affairs v. BCA International Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63486), the Supreme Court held under Article 6 that executive deliberative process privilege rests on the public policy of candid administrative discussion and cannot be waived by contract clauses.[^25] In [*Philippine National Bank v. Nepomuceno Productions, Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/51783), the Supreme Court invalidated a mortgagor's adhesion waiver of republication and reposting under Act No. 3135 because foreclosure notices protect the general public and third-party bidders rather than the debtor alone.[^26] In [*Gatchalian*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/oct1991/gr_56487_1991.html), the Supreme Court held that a hospital affidavit signed by a dizzy injured bus passenger violated public policy by diluting a common carrier's extraordinary diligence under Articles 1733 and 1755.[^27] By contrast, in [*Valenzuela Hardwood*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/34833), the Supreme Court upheld a cargo-damage exemption in a private charter party because private carriage affects only contracting parties and implicates neither public policy nor third-person rights.[^28] Outside private carriage, contractual immunity for one's own fault fails, as held in [*Pleasantville Development Corp. v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/33514), where the Supreme Court ruled under Article 6 that waiving damages for a seller's or agent's negligence violates public policy.[^29]

Social survival statutes and judicial integrity likewise fall inside the public-policy and good-morals bars. In [*Maniego v. Castelo*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1957/apr1957/gr_l-9855_1957.html), the En Banc Supreme Court held under Article 6 that a tenant could not waive his 25 percent statutory palay harvest exemption because waiving family sustenance from harvest to harvest amounts to waiving the tenant's right to live.[^30] In [*Sorreda v. Cambridge Electronics Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/53613), after ruling that the labor arbiter lacked jurisdiction over a claimed civil promise, the Supreme Court observed *arguendo* under Article 6 that an employer cannot grant perpetual employment for life because that waiver of management prerogative offends public policy and good customs.[^31] Bypassing opposing counsel of record after losing a lawsuit violates good morals and public order as well, as held in [*Pampanga Sugar Development Co., Inc. v. Court of Industrial Relations*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1982/jun1982/gr_l-39387_1982.html).[^32] Conversely, waivers that infringe no third person's legal right stand, as illustrated in [*Mabugay-Otamias*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/62002)[^33] (supporting a spouse and children under the Constitution and Family Code) and [*Albay Electric Cooperative*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/47188)[^34] (renouncing personal monetary awards).

## Labor Quitclaims, Settlements and Compromises

The Supreme Court scrutinizes labor waivers closely because employees and employers do not bargain on equal terms. Under [*Pampanga Sugar Development Co., Inc. v. Court of Industrial Relations*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1982/jun1982/gr_l-39387_1982.html), post-judgment quitclaims that workers signed individually without union counsel were void *ab initio* under Articles 6 and 22 because they gave up adjudged differentials while exempting the employer from liability.[^35] The Supreme Court refused to treat them as compromises under Articles 2028 and 2040.[^36] In [*Mercury Drug Co., Inc. v. Dayao*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1982/sep1982/gr_l-30452_1982.html), the Supreme Court held under Article 6 that additional compensation for nighttime work rests on public policy and cannot be waived. The Supreme Court also held that express contractual waivers of Sunday and holiday pay were invalid where employees lacked equal freedom to decline onerous terms.[^37] Unconscionably low payouts are likewise void. In [*Principe v. Philippine-Singapore Transport Services, Inc.*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1989/aug1989/gr_80918_1989.html), the Supreme Court held under Article 6 that a bereaved widow's receipt of PHP 7,000.00 against a S$100,800 death benefit claim was scandalously low, inequitable and null and void as contrary to public policy.[^38]

The burden of proving a valid labor quitclaim falls squarely on the employer. Applying [*Land and Housing Development Corp. v. Esquillo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/42348),[^39] which quoted [*Marcos v. National Labor Relations Commission*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1995/sep1995/gr_111744_1995.html)[^40] quoting [*Veloso v. Department of Labor and Employment*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/aug1991/gr_87297_1991.html)[^41] on Articles 6 and 22, the Supreme Court in [*Naldo, Jr. v. Corporate Protection Services, Phils., Inc.*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2024/apr2024/gr_243139_2024.html) held that the employer must prove that a quitclaim is a credible and reasonable settlement executed voluntarily with full understanding and free of fraud.[^42] Quitclaims failed that test across four distinct factual patterns:

- **Deceit and Misrepresentation:** In [*Naldo*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2024/apr2024/gr_243139_2024.html), management tricked security guards during conciliation into signing quitclaims and resignations in exchange for their own trust-fund savings and cash bonds under a false promise of salary reconciliation.[^43]
- **Unpaid Unexpired Contract Terms:** In [*Esquillo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/42348), a Jeddah overseas engineer's release covered only earned amounts in Saudi riyals and excluded US$9,447 in unexpired contract salaries.[^44]
- **Written Protest:** In [*Marcos*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1995/sep1995/gr_111744_1995.html), redundant employees expressly wrote reservations claiming earned service awards and bonuses directly on their quitclaims, negating voluntariness.[^45]
- **Sweeping General Clauses:** In [*Dela Rosa Liner, Inc. v. Borela*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/60782), a compromise clause releasing "known or unknown, foreseen or unforeseen" claims settled only an earlier illegal dismissal suit and did not waive separate statutory wage differentials without appropriate consideration.[^46]

Valid labor settlements nevertheless stand when genuinely knowing, fair and free of coercion. In [*Veloso*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/aug1991/gr_87297_1991.html), the Supreme Court distinguished [*Pampanga Sugar*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1982/jun1982/gr_l-39387_1982.html)[^47] and upheld counsel-assisted, labor-department-approved compromises under Article 227 of the Labor Code because claimants failed to prove trickery or unconscionably low consideration beyond bare assertions of "dire necessity."[^48] In [*COPPER*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70547), a union knowingly waived its Perfect Attendance Bonus claim under Article 6 when management explicitly rejected the union's proposed attendance-credit clause during strike-settlement bargaining and executed a final memorandum declaring unapproved proposals withdrawn.[^49] In [*Masangkay*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64125), university professors who accepted termination and collected their benefits waived any illegal-dismissal challenge under Article 6.[^50]

## Constitutional and Procedural Rights

Personal constitutional protections may be waived under Article 6 when neither the Constitution nor statute forbids renunciation and no third person is prejudiced. In [*Donato*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/jun1991/gr_79269_1991.html), the En Banc Supreme Court held that the constitutional right to bail under Article III, Section 13 is a personal right whose waiver violates none of Article 6's prohibitions, reversing the grant of bail after an accused had solemnly agreed in a *habeas corpus* joint manifestation to remain in custody and face trial.[^51] The privilege against self-incrimination under Article III, Section 17 is likewise waivable in government or private investigations if unequivocal and willing, as noted in [*Casa Montessori*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46619).[^52]

Where the Constitution prescribes a mandatory mode of waiver, compliance with that form is indispensable. Both [*Donato*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/jun1991/gr_79269_1991.html)[^53] and [*People v. Tulin*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/52498)[^54] emphasize that under Article III, Section 12(1), the right to counsel during custodial investigation cannot be waived except in writing and in the presence of counsel. Outside custodial interrogation, trial-stage counsel rights are waivable under Article 6 when clear and uncoerced. In [*Tulin*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/52498), pirates initially defended by a non-lawyer validly waived that early representation defect when they later confirmed in open court, with full assistance of a bona fide lawyer, an exhibit adopting prior trial evidence.[^55] Similarly, in [*Serzo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/34896), a murder accused who delayed trial for nearly two years without hiring counsel *de parte* and refused to cooperate with three appointed *de oficio* lawyers validly waived counsel *de parte* under Article 6.[^56] In [*Sayson*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1988/oct1988/gr_l-51745_1988.html), obtaining seven postponements and then appearing without counsel waived the right to present defense evidence under Article 6.[^57]

Procedural rules and litigation privileges follow the same private-benefit versus public-policy division. In [*Villalon, Jr. v. Intermediate Appellate Court*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/sep1986/gr_l-73751_1986.html), the Supreme Court held under Article 6 that confidentiality of disbarment proceedings under Rule 139, Section 10 protects the respondent lawyer's personal and professional reputation, allowing that lawyer to waive confidentiality to impeach adverse witnesses in a related civil case.[^58] In [*Alano*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/35392), signing a criminal pre-trial stipulation admitting a deed signature validly waived suspension for an alleged civil prejudicial question under Article 6.[^59] In [*Siacor*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/50693), failing to plead the non-jurisdictional lack of a Barangay Agrarian Reform Committee certification in the Answer waived that procedural objection.[^60]

## Origin in the Spanish Civil Code

The parenthetical "(4a)" appended to Article 6 records that the provision was adapted with amendment from Article 4 of the Spanish Civil Code of 1889. As interpreted by the Supreme Court under Republic Act No. 386, the codal rule preserves the general power to renounce rights while conditioning every waiver on strict conformity with law, public order, public policy, morals, good customs and third-person legal rights.

## Key Cases

- [*Concerned Organization of PASAR Progressive Employees for Reform (COPPER) v. Philippine Associated Smelting and Refining Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70547)[^73]: CBA rights may be waived under Article 6 when the right exists, the holder knows it and bargaining history proves intentional relinquishment.
- [*Naldo, Jr. v. Corporate Protection Services, Phils., Inc.*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2024/apr2024/gr_243139_2024.html)[^74]: Employer bears the burden to prove a labor quitclaim is a credible, reasonable and voluntary settlement, so quitclaims procured through deceit are void.
- [*Brazil v. STI Education Services Group, Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64817)[^75]: Academic institutions cannot waive CHED master's degree requirements by practice or estoppel without violating law and prejudicing students.
- [*University of the East v. Masangkay*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64125)[^76]: Employees who accept dismissal voluntarily and with full understanding, collect their benefits and sue years later waive their challenge under Article 6.
- [*Son v. University of Santo Tomas*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64107)[^77]: Collective bargaining tenure-by-default clause cannot waive statutory faculty master's degree mandates because doing so violates law and prejudices students.
- [*Department of Foreign Affairs v. BCA International Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63486)[^78]: Executive deliberative process privilege involves public policy and cannot be waived under Article 6.
- [*Mabugay-Otamias v. Republic*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/62002)[^79]: Statutory exemption of military retirement benefits from execution is a personal right validly waived by assigning pension benefits for family support.
- [*Dela Rosa Liner, Inc. v. Borela*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/60782)[^80]: A broad release covering unknown or unforeseen claims in an earlier dismissal case cannot waive separate statutory labor-standards pay without appropriate consideration.
- [*F.F. Cruz & Co., Inc. v. HR Construction Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/24219)[^81]: Repeated voluntary progress payments without demanding joint measurement waive that contractual condition by conduct under Article 6, just as contractual terms may waive extrajudicial rescission.
- [*Sorreda v. Cambridge Electronics Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/53613)[^82]: Even *arguendo*, management cannot waive prerogatives under Article 6 to grant perpetual employment for life because doing so offends public policy and good customs.
- [*Albay Electric Cooperative, Inc. v. Santelices*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/47188)[^83]: Monetary pre-trial awards wholly for a party's personal benefit may be waived under Article 6 without prejudicing third persons.
- [*Premiere Development Bank v. Central Surety & Insurance Co., Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48855)[^84]: Directory debtor payment-application rights are waivable under Article 6, while creditor waiver is never presumed from silence or a demand letter.
- [*Estate of the Late Encarnacion Vda. de Panlilio v. Dizon*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44473)[^85]: Intentional and voluntary written surrender of a landowner's sugarland exemption under Article 6 gives rise to valid beneficiary ownership under Presidential Decree No. 27.
- [*Land and Housing Development Corp. v. Esquillo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/42348)[^86]: An overseas worker's quitclaim covering only earned items and omitting unexpired contract salaries is not a reasonable settlement and must be set aside.
- [*Bank of the Philippine Islands v. Casa Montessori Internationale*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46619)[^87]: A bank cannot unilaterally impose a ten-day reporting clause on monthly statements and treat depositor silence as a waiver under Article 6.
- [*Philippine National Bank v. Nepomuceno Productions, Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/51783)[^88]: Statutory foreclosure posting and publication protect third-person bidders and the general public and cannot be waived by the mortgagor.
- [*Siacor v. Gigantana*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/50693)[^89]: Any waiver of Presidential Decree No. 27 agrarian tenancy or ownership rights is void as contrary to law and public policy under Article 6 without *in pari delicto*.
- [*People v. Tulin*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/52498)[^90]: Trial representation defects may be waived knowingly with bona fide counsel under Article 6, whereas custodial counsel waivers require writing and counsel's presence.
- [*Agga v. National Labor Relations Commission*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/36543)[^91]: Articles 5 and 6 invalidate only agreements violating law or public policy, leaving lawful fixed lump-sum contracts valid.
- [*Alano v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/35392)[^92]: A signed criminal pre-trial factual stipulation validly waives a forgery claim and civil-prejudicial-question suspension under Article 6.
- [*Valenzuela Hardwood and Industrial Supply, Inc. v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/34833)[^93]: Patrimonial rights like cargo-damage recourse in a private carriage charter may be waived under Article 6 without violating public policy.
- [*People v. Serzo, Jr.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/34896)[^94]: Personal right to counsel *de parte* may be waived under Article 6 through persistent dilatory refusal while represented *de oficio*.
- [*Esguerra v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/34493)[^95]: Entering into a judicially approved compromise authorizing property sale without reserving a right of first refusal waives that alleged right under Article 6.
- [*Pleasantville Development Corp. v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/33514)[^96]: A buyer cannot contractually waive recovery of damages arising from the seller's or agent's negligence because that violates public policy under Article 6.
- [*Marcos v. National Labor Relations Commission*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1995/sep1995/gr_111744_1995.html)[^97]: Written reservations claiming service awards on a quitclaim negate total voluntariness and preserve unpaid earned benefits.
- [*Gatchalian v. Delim*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/oct1991/gr_56487_1991.html)[^98]: Waiver must be clear and unequivocal. Passenger waivers against common carriers offend public policy under Article 6.
- [*Veloso v. Department of Labor and Employment*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/aug1991/gr_87297_1991.html)[^99]: Voluntary, counsel-assisted and agency-approved compromises of disputed awards cannot be annulled on bare claims of "dire necessity."
- [*People v. Donato*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/jun1991/gr_79269_1991.html)[^100]: Constitutional and statutory rights of any character may be waived under Article 6 unless contrary to its limits, including the personal right to bail.
- [*Principe v. Philippine-Singapore Transport Services, Inc.*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1989/aug1989/gr_80918_1989.html)[^101]: A widow's release for PHP 7,000.00 against a S$100,800 death benefit claim is scandalously low, inequitable and void as contrary to public policy under Article 6.
- [*Sayson v. People*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1988/oct1988/gr_l-51745_1988.html)[^102]: Accused's right to be heard by counsel and present defense evidence is subject to waiver under Article 6 through dilatory conduct.
- [*Villalon, Jr. v. Intermediate Appellate Court*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/sep1986/gr_l-73751_1986.html)[^103]: Confidentiality of disbarment proceedings vests for the lawyer's reputational protection and may be waived by that lawyer under Article 6.
- [*Mercury Drug Co., Inc. v. Dayao*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1982/sep1982/gr_l-30452_1982.html)[^104]: Nighttime differential pay rests on public policy and cannot be waived under Article 6, nor can forced contractual waivers defeat Sunday and holiday pay.
- [*Pampanga Sugar Development Co., Inc. v. Court of Industrial Relations*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1982/jun1982/gr_l-39387_1982.html)[^105]: Post-judgment individual quitclaims bypassing union counsel and exempting employer liability are void *ab initio* under Articles 6 and 22.
- [*Maniego v. Castelo*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1957/apr1957/gr_l-9855_1957.html)[^106]: A tenant's statutory 25 percent harvest exemption cannot be waived under Article 6 because doing so waives the tenant's right to live.

## Case Discussions

### [*Concerned Organization of PASAR Progressive Employees for Reform (COPPER) v. Philippine Associated Smelting and Refining Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70547): Collective Bargaining Waiver

COPPER is the sole bargaining representative of the rank and file employees of the Philippine Associated Smelting and Refining Corporation, or PASAR. Failed talks on their 2017 to 2022 collective bargaining agreement led it to strike from August 3 to 18, 2020.[^107] On August 19, 2020 the parties signed a memorandum of agreement that settled all issues raised in the talks and treated unapproved union demands as withdrawn and waived.[^108] PASAR later deducted two days of rest day pay for the strike period and refused the Perfect Attendance Bonus for November 1, 2019 to October 31, 2020.[^109] The Voluntary Arbitrator dismissed both claims. He reasoned that rest day pay presupposes six days of work and that the memorandum waived the bonus claim.[^110] The Court of Appeals affirmed in CA-G.R. SP No. 14340.[^111] The Supreme Court ruled that the rest day pay clause is unconditional and that absence from a legal strike does not forfeit it.[^112] It found the bonus also covered justified absences but held that COPPER waived it.[^113] COPPER had proposed that strike absences would not count against the employees. PASAR rejected that proposal and the memorandum withdrew every disapproved demand. The Supreme Court applied Article 6 and its three requisites of a right, knowledge of it and intent to relinquish it.[^114] It partly granted the petition, affirmed the Court of Appeals with modification and ordered PASAR to pay two days of rest day pay for August 3 to 18, 2020 with six percent annual interest from finality.[^115] It remanded the computation to the Voluntary Arbitrator of the National Conciliation and Mediation Board in Tacloban City.[^116] The case shows that a union gives up a benefit when its own rejected proposal is swept into a final settlement.[^117]

### [*Naldo, Jr. v. Corporate Protection Services, Phils., Inc.*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2024/apr2024/gr_243139_2024.html): Quitclaims Procured by Deceit

Seven security guards demanded all their money claims at a conciliation conference. On March 10, 2015 the agency's representative told them that checks would cover those claims if they first signed resignation letters and quitclaims.[^118] The checks paid only their trust fund savings and cash bonds. The guards were then barred from work.[^119] The labor arbiter dismissed their complaints on August 28, 2015 and held that the resignations and quitclaims were voluntary.[^120] The National Labor Relations Commission set that aside on December 29, 2015. It found no intent to resign and no dismissal and remanded the money claims.[^121] The Court of Appeals affirmed in CA-G.R. SP Nos. 144925 and 145329.[^122] The Supreme Court first found no forum shopping or *res judicata* because conciliation produces no judgment on the merits.[^123] Quoting [*Esquillo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/42348), whose cited authority includes Articles 6 and 22 of the Civil Code, it held the quitclaims void for fraud.[^124] Both sides knew the checks left other claims pending and the employer never meant to pay them.[^125] The resignations were likewise induced by deceit, so the guards were constructively dismissed. It set aside the Court of Appeals rulings in the same dockets.[^126] It ordered reinstatement and backwages with 13th month pay from March 10, 2015 until finality. It added the December 27, 2016 labor arbiter awards unless already paid, PHP 50,000 moral damages and PHP 20,000 exemplary damages each and ten percent attorney's fees with six percent annual interest from finality.[^127] The labor arbiter must prepare a revised computation.[^128] The case shows that a quitclaim procured by deceit binds no one.[^129]

### [*Brazil v. STI Education Services Group, Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64817): Non-Waivable Faculty Qualifications

Luningning Brazil, Salvacion Garcera and Rita De Mesa joined STI College-Legazpi as part-time faculty on June 3, 1997 and in June 2000 and June 2001.[^130] None held a master's degree, yet the school treated them as regular faculty for years. On June 3, 2011 it offered them part-time or probationary contracts under the 2008 Manual of Regulations for Private Higher Education. They refused to sign, were replaced and sued for constructive dismissal.[^131] The labor arbiter ruled for them on December 16, 2011.[^132] The National Labor Relations Commission first partly upheld that ruling but dismissed the complaints in a December 27, 2013 resolution. The Court of Appeals affirmed on November 9, 2016 in CA-G.R. SP No. 134584.[^133] The Supreme Court took the regular status as given and still denied relief. Equity cannot override the Manual and a teacher without all the minimum qualifications is a part-time faculty member on a fixed term.[^134] It treated [*Son*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64107) as on all fours. Estoppel and a school's grant of regular status cannot waive a qualification set by law. Son applied Article 6 on that point.[^135] The Supreme Court saw no material difference between a collective agreement and an employment contract. It also found no bad faith in hiring replacements who met the standards.[^136] It denied the petition and affirmed the Court of Appeals Decision of November 9, 2016 and Resolution of June 30, 2017.[^137] The decision shows that estoppel cannot confer a status the law withholds.[^138]

### [*University of the East v. Masangkay*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64125): Voluntary Acceptance of Dismissal

Veronica Masangkay and Gertrudo Regondola were associate professors and associate deans of the University of the East in Caloocan. They had certified that their engineering mechanics manual was original. The university approved it for student use.[^139] The heirs of two textbook authors then complained that the manual copied the books. After an investigation in which the two professors took part, the Board of Trustees dismissed them effective November 26, 2007.[^140] A colleague named Rocamora asked for reconsideration of her own dismissal and later won in the Supreme Court. Masangkay and Regondola did not appeal and instead collected their accrued benefits and signed vouchers and pay slips. Masangkay even asked that part of the money be applied to her car loan.[^141] They sued for illegal dismissal on July 20, 2010, almost three years later.[^142] The labor arbiter ruled for them on February 28, 2011. The National Labor Relations Commission reversed on June 29, 2012 and the Court of Appeals reinstated the arbiter's decision on February 19, 2016.[^143] The Supreme Court restated Article 6 and the rule that a quitclaim binds when it is voluntary and made with full understanding and credible consideration.[^144] It found no sign of coercion and no unwillingness in how the two professors behaved after the dismissal. Their later complaint was an afterthought inspired by their colleague's victory, so Rocamora was not a precedent.[^145] The Supreme Court reversed the February 19, 2016 Decision and August 26, 2016 Resolution in CA-G.R. SP No. 132774 and dismissed the complaint for lack of merit.[^146] The case shows that conduct after a dismissal can waive the right to contest it.[^147]

### [*Son v. University of Santo Tomas*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64107): Collective Bargaining Tenure Waiver

Three full time professors of the University of Santo Tomas had been hired in 2004 and 2005 on probationary status that required a master's degree before the probationary period ended.[^148] They never finished the degree. After the Commission on Higher Education ordered strict enforcement of CHED Memorandum Order No. 40-08 in 2010, the university stopped reappointing them and sent termination letters on June 11, 2010.[^149] The professors claimed tenure by default under Article XV of the faculty collective bargaining agreement. The labor arbiter and the National Labor Relations Commission first ruled for them in 2011. The commission's Special Division reversed on March 26, 2012 and its Second Division restored the arbiter's ruling on October 30, 2012.[^150] The Court of Appeals set aside the pro-tenure commission rulings and reinstated the March 26, 2012 decision in CA-G.R. SP No. 128666.[^151] The Supreme Court held that a master's degree had been required since the 1992 Revised Manual of Regulations for Private Schools. The tenure clause was therefore void as contrary to law.[^152] Both sides violated the requirement, so they stood *in pari delicto* under Articles 1411 and 1412 and neither could recover from the other.[^153] The university did not waive the requirement by signing the agreement. Citing Article 6, the Supreme Court said such a waiver is contrary to law and prejudices students and the public.[^154] It denied the petition and affirmed the September 27, 2013 Decision and January 29, 2014 Resolution *in toto*.[^155] The case shows that a collective agreement cannot waive a regulatory qualification set for the public interest.[^156]

### [*Department of Foreign Affairs v. BCA International Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63486): Deliberative Process Privilege

The Department of Foreign Affairs awarded the Machine Readable Passport and Visa Project to BCA International under an Amended Build-Operate-Transfer Agreement dated April 5, 2002. When the Department sought to terminate it, BCA took the dispute to an ad hoc arbitral tribunal under the UNCITRAL Rules.[^157] On May 16, 2013 BCA asked the Regional Trial Court of Makati City, Branch 146 for subpoenas against Department officials and records.[^158] The trial court granted the petition on September 2, 2013. It held that the deliberative process privilege no longer applied because the Department had reached a definite proposition and signed a contract.[^159] It denied the motion to quash on October 11, 2013 and the motion for reconsideration on January 8, 2014.[^160] The Supreme Court corrected that reading of *Chavez v. Public Estates Authority*. The privilege survives a definite proposition and a perfected contract. An arbitration under Republic Act No. 9285 can invoke it.[^161] The Supreme Court read the confidentiality and disclosure clauses of the agreement and found no express waiver. It held that the privilege cannot be waived because Article 6 bars waiver of a right grounded in public policy and the policy is open and frank discussion within government.[^162] It added that the privilege is qualified. The agency must prove that the material is predecisional and deliberative and the requesting party may overcome it by showing a greater need.[^163] Both sides' claims were too broad to decide. The Supreme Court partially granted the petition and remanded to Branch 146 to determine which documents are protected. Its decision superseded the April 2, 2014 temporary restraining order.[^164] The ruling shows that a privilege resting on public policy cannot be bargained away in a contract.[^165]

### [*Mabugay-Otamias v. Republic*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/62002): Waiver of Pension Exemption

Retired army colonel Francisco Otamias signed a Deed of Assignment on February 26, 2003 that gave his wife Edna and their five children fifty percent of his retirement benefits.[^166] He retired on April 1, 2003 and the Armed Forces of the Philippines Pension and Gratuity Management Center honored the deed until January 6, 2006.[^167] It then asked for a court order, so Edna sued for support in the Regional Trial Court of Cagayan de Oro. The colonel was declared in default and the trial court ordered automatic deduction of the support from his pension.[^168] The Court of Appeals granted the Center's petition for certiorari and partly nullified that decision because Section 31 of Presidential Decree No. 1638 exempts pensions from execution.[^169] The Supreme Court reversed. It quoted Article 6 and a definition of waiver, then held that the colonel waived the exemption by signing the deed.[^170] The exemption belonged to him, the waiver harmed no third person and it protected his family's right to support under Article XV of the Constitution and the Family Code.[^171] The Supreme Court added that the Center itself accepted similar waivers from other retirees.[^172] It expressly left the wider conflict between the support provisions and Section 31 for a more appropriate case.[^173] The Supreme Court reversed the May 22, 2009 Decision and August 11, 2009 Resolution in CA-G.R. SP No. 02555-MIN and reinstated the February 27, 2007 trial court decision.[^174] The case shows that a personal statutory exemption may be waived in favor of lawful family support.[^175]

### [*Dela Rosa Liner, Inc. v. Borela*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/60782): General Clause in Compromise

Bus driver Calixto Borela and conductor Estelo Amarille settled their first illegal dismissal case before the Court of Appeals for PHP 350,000 and PHP 150,000 under a compromise that covered all claims known or unknown, foreseen or unforeseen.[^176] On September 23, 2011 they filed a second complaint for underpaid salaries, holiday pay, overtime, service incentive leave, 13th month pay and wage order violations.[^177] The labor arbiter dismissed it for forum shopping on November 24, 2011, the National Labor Relations Commission reinstated it on July 31, 2012 and the Court of Appeals upheld that ruling.[^178] The Supreme Court found no forum shopping or *res judicata* because the first case concerned illegal dismissal and the second concerned labor standards benefits.[^179] Citing [*Pampanga Sugar*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1982/jun1982/gr_l-39387_1982.html) and Article 6, it held that a sweeping general waiver over unknown claims without appropriate consideration settles only the earlier dispute and cannot waive statutory labor-standards pay.[^180] Dismissing the petition for lack of merit, the Supreme Court affirmed the Court of Appeals decision and resolution.[^181] The ruling establishes that catch-all compromise wording never surrenders unconsidered statutory benefits.[^182]

### [*F.F. Cruz & Co., Inc. v. HR Construction Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/24219): Implied Waiver by Voluntary Payment

F.F. Cruz & Co. subcontracted part of a DPWH viaduct project to HR Construction for PHP 31,293,532.72 on August 9, 2004. The subcontract tied payment to progress billings and a joint measurement of completed works with the DPWH.[^183] F.F. Cruz paid three amounts without any joint measurement, the last on March 11, 2005. It refused the rest of the billings.[^184] HR Construction stopped work after December 18, 2004 and sued before the Construction Industry Arbitration Commission.[^185] The Commission awarded it PHP 2,239,452.63 plus costs of arbitration. It held that F.F. Cruz had waived the joint measurement by paying without it, citing Article 6.[^186] The Court of Appeals affirmed in CA-G.R. SP No. 91860.[^187] The Supreme Court quoted [*People v. Donato*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/jun1991/gr_79269_1991.html) on waiver, including the inference of relinquishment from conduct. It held that the repeated payments without insisting on joint measurement waived that right.[^188] F.F. Cruz therefore could not dispute the valuation in the billings. The Supreme Court parted ways with the lower tribunals on the work stoppage. Article 11.2 of the subcontract made HR Construction continue performing during any dispute, so it waived extrajudicial rescission and had no right to stop.[^189] It affirmed the February 6, 2009 Decision and April 13, 2009 Resolution with the modification that the parties share the arbitration costs equally.[^190] The decision shows that waiver may arise from conduct as well as from express terms.[^191]

### [*Sorreda v. Cambridge Electronics Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/53613): Perpetual Employment Claims

Ronilo Sorreda was hired on May 8, 1999 as a technician for five months. On June 15, 1999 a machine crushed his left arm so that it had to be amputated.[^192] He claimed that company officers later assured him of regular employment for as long as the company existed. He filed a complaint on November 16, 1999 after the company refused to rehire him.[^193] The labor arbiter ruled for him on March 9, 2001, but the National Labor Relations Commission found that he was not a regular employee and that the dispute fell outside its jurisdiction.[^194] The Court of Appeals agreed that a claimed second contract of perpetual employment was cognizable by regular courts.[^195] Holding primarily that the labor arbiter lacked jurisdiction over an alleged post-expiration civil contract, the Supreme Court added *arguendo* under Article 6 that waiving management prerogative to promise perpetual employment for life violates public policy and good customs.[^196] It found no concrete proof of the promise beyond his own self-serving claim.[^197] The Supreme Court denied the petition with costs against petitioner.[^198] The case illustrates both judicial refusal to enforce lifetime-tenure waivers and rejection of unsubstantiated party claims.[^199]

### [*Albay Electric Cooperative, Inc. v. Santelices*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/47188): Waiver of Personal Fee Awards

Albay Electric Cooperative went directly to the Supreme Court on certiorari against pre-trial orders in Mayon International Hotel's damages suit over an electric disconnection.[^200] The orders made the cooperative pay the hotel's counsel PHP 2,500 for transportation and PHP 3,000 for appearance after the last-minute cancellation of the November 12, 1997 pre-trial.[^201] The trial court decided the main case on August 7, 2000. The Court of Appeals affirmed with modification on July 30, 2007 in a decision that became final.[^202] On December 20, 2006 the hotel filed a manifestation waiving any claim under the orders and joining the cooperative's prayer to nullify them.[^203] Applying Article 6 directly, the Supreme Court held that the fee awards existed solely for the hotel's benefit and could be waived without prejudicing anyone.[^204] The final merits judgment mooted the challenge to the setting of the pre-trial. The waiver mooted the challenge to the fee award.[^205] The Supreme Court also noted that certiorari against interlocutory orders was precipitate and should have been filed first in the Court of Appeals.[^206] Dismissing the petition as moot and academic, the Supreme Court made no pronouncement as to costs.[^207] The decision confirms that a prevailing party may freely renounce monetary awards granted exclusively to it.[^208]

### [*Premiere Development Bank v. Central Surety & Insurance Co., Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48855): Presumption Against Creditor Waiver

Central Surety borrowed PHP 6,000,000.00 from Premiere Development Bank on August 20, 1999 under Promissory Note No. 714-Y secured by a pledge of its Wack Wack membership. It separately owed the bank PHP 40,898,000.00 under another note.[^209] After the bank's August 22, 2000 demand letter on the PHP 6,000,000.00 loan, Central Surety issued a PHP 6,000,000.00 check marked full payment of that loan.[^210] The bank returned the check and demanded both loans on September 28, 2000, then later accepted it with a second check and applied the PHP 8,600,000.00 total across four accounts.[^211] Only PHP 1,619,187.22 of that total went to Promissory Note No. 714-Y.[^212] The Makati Regional Trial Court Branch 132 dismissed Central Surety's complaint on July 12, 2005 and awarded PHP 100,000.00 attorney's fees. The Court of Appeals reversed because the demand letter waived the bank's right to apply payments.[^213] Holding that Article 1252 payment-application rights are directory and waivable under Article 6, the Supreme Court reversed the Court of Appeals because neither the demand letter nor silence proved actual relinquishment of the bank's authority, especially given an express non-waiver clause.[^214] The notes empowered the bank to apply payments as it saw fit and all of the debts were due.[^215] Partially granting the petition, the Supreme Court reversed and set aside the July 31, 2006 Decision and January 4, 2007 Resolution in CA-G.R. CV No. 85930 and reinstated the July 12, 2005 Regional Trial Court decision in Civil Case No. 00-1536 while deleting the award of attorney's fees, with no pronouncement as to costs.[^216] It found no malice in Central Surety's suit.[^217] The case reinforces the strong presumption against waiver and the force of non-waiver clauses.[^218]

### [*Estate of the Late Encarnacion Vda. de Panlilio v. Dizon*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44473): Voluntary Surrender Under Agrarian Reform

Encarnacion Panlilio owned the 115.41 hectare Hacienda Masamat in Mexico, Pampanga. About 65.19 hectares were planted with sugar cane, which would otherwise have been outside Presidential Decree No. 27.[^219] On January 12, 1977 she signed an affidavit placing her entire property under Operation Land Transfer in favor of her tenants.[^220] George Lizares, the executor of her estate, later relied on a February 3, 1977 affidavit that revoked the first one. It first surfaced in 1994 and 1995.[^221] The Provincial Adjudicator dismissed his complaints on November 14, 1995 and the Agrarian Reform Adjudication Board affirmed on August 7, 1997.[^222] The Court of Appeals first reversed on April 11, 2000 and then reversed itself in its November 29, 2000 Amended Decision in CA-G.R. SP No. 47502.[^223] The Supreme Court held that the second affidavit was not genuine. Panlilio never gave it to the Department of Agrarian Reform or to her lessee during her nine remaining years.[^224] It also found a valid waiver through the first affidavit, which did not defeat the purpose of the decree. It also applied laches against her successors.[^225] It cited Article 6 for the rule that an intentional and voluntary surrender of a right can give valid title to another, so the disposition under Operation Land Transfer was legal.[^226] It dealt separately with transfers by the beneficiaries. Transfers to non-qualified persons violate Presidential Decree No. 27 and Executive Order No. 228 and are void under Article 5 of the Civil Code.[^227] It partly granted G.R. No. 148777 and dismissed G.R. No. 157598. It affirmed the Amended Decision and declared void any transfers to non-qualified persons. It ordered the Department of Agrarian Reform to investigate and to coordinate title cancellation with the Pampanga Register of Deeds.[^228] The case shows that a landowner's voluntary surrender of exempt land binds her successors while the transferee restrictions of the decree stay intact.[^229]

### [*Land and Housing Development Corp. v. Esquillo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/42348): Overseas Worker Release Excluding Unexpired Pay

Marianito Esquillo, a structural engineer hired by ABV Rock Group in Jeddah, was dismissed under a November 17, 1994 notice citing reduction of force even though his contract ran until July 26, 1995.[^230] On November 29, 1994 he signed an employer-prepared release and quitclaim for SR23,153, which covered only overtime, vacation pay, indemnity, contract reward and notice pay already due to him.[^231] The labor arbiter awarded him US$9,447 in unexpired salaries plus ten percent attorney's fees on February 27, 1997. The National Labor Relations Commission reversed on May 30, 1997.[^232] The Court of Appeals reinstated the labor arbiter's decision on July 27, 2001.[^233] Quoting [*Marcos*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1995/sep1995/gr_111744_1995.html) on Articles 6 and 22, the Supreme Court set aside the quitclaim because a payout covering only earned dues for far less than legal entitlement is not a reasonable settlement.[^234] It added that being a professional engineer did not make him less vulnerable to disadvantageous offers while facing unemployment abroad.[^235] Denying the petition, the Supreme Court affirmed the July 27, 2001 Decision and January 29, 2002 Resolution of the Court of Appeals with costs against petitioners.[^236] The case shows that partial payment of undisputed dues cannot extinguish excluded contractual claims.[^237]

### [*Bank of the Philippine Islands v. Casa Montessori Internationale*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46619): Unilateral Statement Notice and Self-Incrimination

Leonardo T. Yabut, the external auditor of Casa Montessori Internationale, forged its president's signature on checks drawn on its current account and encashed them at the Bank of the Philippine Islands under the fictitious name Sonny D. Santos.[^238] Casa sued the bank on March 4, 1991. The Regional Trial Court ruled for Casa on February 16, 1999.[^239] The bank invoked a monthly statement notice treating balances as correct unless an error was reported within ten days.[^240] The Court of Appeals split liability equally between the bank and Casa for Casa's contributory negligence.[^241] The Supreme Court held that Yabut's private affidavit admitting the forgery was admissible because it was not made under custodial investigation.[^242] The Supreme Court noted that the privilege against self-incrimination under Article III, Section 17 may be waived unequivocally in private or public inquiries.[^243] It then held under Article 6 that the bank could not unilaterally impose a ten-day condition on bank statements and treat failure to object as a waiver of rights never possessed.[^244] Denying G.R. No. 149454 and partly granting G.R. No. 149507, the Supreme Court modified the Court of Appeals decision by holding BPI liable for PHP 547,115 less amounts already recovered from Leonardo T. Yabut, plus six percent annual legal interest compounded annually from complaint filing until full payment and ten percent attorney's fees, subject to reimbursement from Yabut except attorney's fees, furnishing a copy to the Professional Regulation Commission Board of Accountancy with no costs.[^245] The case distinguishes waivable self-incrimination privileges from invalid unilateral notice waivers.[^246]

### [*Philippine National Bank v. Nepomuceno Productions, Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/51783): Waiver of Foreclosure Publication

The Philippine National Bank granted a PHP 4,000,000.00 credit line on November 28, 1973 to finance the film Pacific Connection, secured by mortgages on a Makati lot, a Forbes Park lot and film equipment.[^247] After the borrower defaulted, the auction sale was rescheduled several times without republication under a bank-drafted Agreement to Postpone Sale that waived republication and reposting.[^248] The bank bought the properties at the December 20, 1976 auction as highest bidder for PHP 10,432,776.97.[^249] The Pasig Regional Trial Court Branch 155 annulled the sale for lack of publication and the Court of Appeals affirmed.[^250] Applying [*Donato*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/jun1991/gr_79269_1991.html) and Article 6, the Supreme Court held that posting and publication protect the general public and third-person bidders to attract fair bids rather than the mortgagor's sole interest, rendering any waiver void.[^251] It added that the borrower committed no act of estoppel because requesting a postponement is different from requesting one without republication.[^252] It also called the agreement a contract of adhesion prepared solely by the bank.[^253] The Supreme Court affirmed the December 10, 1998 Decision in CA-G.R. CV No. 47500 with modification reducing attorney's fees from PHP 100,000.00 to PHP 25,000.00 and made no pronouncement as to costs.[^254] The decision demonstrates that third-person statutory safeguards cannot be signed away.[^255]

### [*Siacor v. Gigantana*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/50693): Waiver of Agrarian Tenancy Rights

Fernando Siacor was a farmer-beneficiary under Presidential Decree No. 27 who held Certificate of Land Transfer No. 0-050555 issued on July 20, 1983 over Lot No. 00202, a 1.0043-hectare parcel in Sillon, Bantayan, Cebu.[^256] Two heirs of the former owner sold their shares to the Gigantana spouses on June 6, 1986. The sale included his parcel.[^257] He was ejected on February 11, 1992 and sued to annul the sale.[^258] The Agrarian Reform Adjudicator dismissed his complaint on April 21, 1994. The Department of Agrarian Reform Adjudication Board annulled the sale as to his parcel on January 11, 2000.[^259] The Court of Appeals reversed on March 15, 2001 because it treated the land sold as Lot No. 4610 in Kangkaibe, over which he had waived his tenancy rights.[^260] The Supreme Court first held that lack of a barangay agrarian reform committee certification is not fatal and that respondents waived the objection by failing to raise it in their answer.[^261] It found that the land sold included Lot No. 00202 and that his waiver covered only Lot No. 4610.[^262] It added that even if the August 8, 1986 waiver referred to the land-transfer parcel, the waiver would be void under Article 6 as contrary to law and public policy. *In pari delicto* would not apply.[^263] It also held the sale void for violating Presidential Decree No. 27 and Executive Order No. 228 and ruled that an action to declare a contract inexistent does not prescribe.[^264] Reversing and setting aside the March 15, 2001 Court of Appeals decision, the Supreme Court reinstated the January 11, 2000 Decision of the Department of Agrarian Reform Adjudication Board.[^265] The case separates waivable pleading objections from non-waivable social-justice titles.[^266]

### [*People v. Tulin*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/52498): Trial Representation and Custodial Counsel

Seven armed pirates seized the M/T Tabangao off Mindoro on March 2, 1991. Roger Tulin, Virgilio Loyola, Andres Infante, Jr. and Cecilio Changco were tried in the Manila Regional Trial Court Branch 49 for qualified piracy under Presidential Decree No. 532.[^267] Their first representative, Tomas Posadas, turned out not to be a lawyer after he had examined seven defense witnesses.[^268] The four accused then adopted that evidence in a manifestation made with the assistance of bona fide counsel Atty. Abdul Basar.[^269] The trial court convicted them as principals and imposed *reclusion perpetua*. It convicted Cheong San Hiong as an accomplice.[^270] Applying Article 6, [*Serzo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/34896) and [*Sayson*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1988/oct1988/gr_l-51745_1988.html), the Supreme Court held that the accused validly waived early trial representation defects knowingly and with bona fide counsel.[^271] It held the uncounselled custodial confessions inadmissible under Article III, Section 12(1) because custodial counsel cannot be waived without writing and counsel's presence.[^272] Finding eyewitness proof sufficient, the Supreme Court affirmed the trial court conviction *in toto*.[^273] The case marks the constitutional divide between informal custodial waivers and counsel-assisted trial waivers.[^274]

### [*Agga v. National Labor Relations Commission*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/36543): Lawful Lump-Sum Employment Contracts

Offshore oilrig workers hired by Supply Oilfield Services, Inc. for the drillship SEDCO/BP 471 served under one-year contracts giving them two months off with pay for every two months on duty and a fixed monthly compensation covering their basic rate, allowances and benefits.[^275] They sued before the Philippine Overseas Employment Administration for overtime, holiday, rest day, 13th month and night shift pay and invoked Articles 5 and 6 against the lump-sum arrangement.[^276] The agency dismissed the complaint on July 2, 1992. The National Labor Relations Commission dismissed their appeal on November 27, 1995.[^277] The Supreme Court held that Articles 5 and 6 void only agreements violating law or public policy. Because neither labor statutes nor overseas employment rules barred the lump-sum arrangement where no underpayment was shown, the contracts stood valid.[^278] It also found that their insurance coverage exceeded the legal minimum, treated them as land-based workers and denied damages for lack of bad faith.[^279] The Supreme Court affirmed the November 27, 1995 Decision of the National Labor Relations Commission with no costs.[^280] The ruling confirms that Article 6 applies only where a legal or public-policy prohibition exists.[^281]

### [*Alano v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/35392): Waiver via Pre-Trial Stipulation

An estafa information alleged that on June 10, 1986 Arturo Alano sold a 1,172-square-meter lot in Bicutan, Taguig a second time to Erlinda Dandoy for PHP 87,900 after selling it to Roberto Carlos for PHP 30,000.[^282] Alano moved to suspend the criminal case because Carlos's civil action in the Pasig Regional Trial Court to annul the second sale raised his defense that his signature on the first deed was forged.[^283] He had signed an October 5, 1990 pre-trial order stipulating the validity of his signature on the first deed and acknowledging his signatures on 23 cash vouchers.[^284] The Manila Regional Trial Court Branch 37 denied suspension on October 3, 1991. The Court of Appeals dismissed his petition on July 26, 1993.[^285] The Supreme Court conceded that a prejudicial question would ordinarily warrant suspension.[^286] It held under Article 6 that the rights to present evidence and seek suspension for a prejudicial question may be waived expressly or impliedly. Signing the pre-trial stipulation validly waived the forgery claim without violating statutory limits.[^287] Affirming the July 26, 1993 Court of Appeals decision, the Supreme Court adjudged costs against petitioner.[^288] The case shows that pre-trial stipulations operate as binding procedural waivers.[^289]

### [*Valenzuela Hardwood and Industrial Supply, Inc. v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/34833): Cargo Liability in Private Carriage

Valenzuela Hardwood chartered the M/V Seven Ambassador on January 16, 1984 to carry 940 lauan round logs from Maconacon, Isabela to Manila under a charter party stating that the owners would not be responsible for any loss or damage to the cargo.[^290] The vessel sank on January 25, 1984 through the captain's negligence in stowing and securing the logs. The Valenzuela Regional Trial Court held the shipowner and the cargo insurer liable for PHP 2,000,000 in the alternative.[^291] The Court of Appeals sustained the insurer's liability but cleared the shipowner because it acted as a private carrier.[^292] Upholding the exemption under Article 6, the Supreme Court held that Valenzuela's rights under Articles 586 and 587 of the Code of Commerce were patrimonial and were waived in the charter party.[^293] It also held that private carriage affects only the contracting parties and that neither public policy nor Articles 1170 and 1173 bar the stipulation.[^294] Denying the petition, the Supreme Court affirmed the October 15, 1991 Court of Appeals decision.[^295] The decision contrasts waivable patrimonial rights in private carriage with non-waivable carrier duties in public transport.[^296]

### [*People v. Serzo, Jr.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/34896): Waiver of Counsel de Parte

Mario Serzo, Jr. was charged with murder for stabbing Alfredo Alcantara at the back on August 22, 1990 in Antipolo, Rizal.[^297] At his February 11, 1991 arraignment he had no private lawyer, so the trial court assisted him with Atty. Wilfredo Lina-ac as counsel *de oficio*.[^298] Over almost two years he kept asking for resets to find counsel *de parte*. The court later appointed Atty. Bella Antonano and Atty. Bonifacia Garcia of the Public Attorney's Office.[^299] He refused to cooperate with his appointed counsel and declined to testify on November 5, 1992, so the defense rested.[^300] The Supreme Court held under Article 6 that the personal right to counsel *de parte* is waivable when unequivocally, knowingly and intelligently surrendered without offending statutory limits, which occurred through persistent dilatory conduct while adequately represented *de oficio*.[^301] It noted that he never showed how his appointed counsel were unsatisfactory.[^302] Affirming the conviction, the Supreme Court deleted the PHP 25,000 moral damages for lack of proof and ordered PHP 50,000.00 civil indemnity and PHP 2,000.00 actual burial damages.[^303] The case demonstrates that protracted delay by an accused forfeits choice of private counsel.[^304]

### [*Esguerra v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/34493): Unreserved Right of First Refusal

Julieta Esguerra and her husband settled her property case through a compromise agreement that the court approved by partial judgment on January 11, 1990.[^305] The agreement authorized the family corporation VECCI to sell listed properties and to pay her half of the net proceeds.[^306] When VECCI sold Esguerra Building II to Sureste Properties, Inc., she moved to nullify the sale because she was not consulted and her offer to buy her half was ignored.[^307] The Makati trial court held the sale valid as to half and ineffectual as to the other half. The Court of Appeals set that order aside on May 31, 1994.[^308] The Supreme Court held that the compromise authorized the sale without any consultation requirement and that she could not repudiate a voluntary agreement.[^309] It held under Article 6 that the alleged right of first refusal was waivable and was waived because she did not expressly reserve it in the compromise.[^310] Denying the petition for lack of merit, the Supreme Court affirmed *in toto* the May 31, 1994 Court of Appeals decision with costs against petitioner.[^311] The ruling confirms that an unreserved compromise waives the ancillary right of first refusal.[^312]

### [*Pleasantville Development Corp. v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/33514): Negligence Liability Clause

Wilson Kee bought Lot 8 of Pleasantville Subdivision in Bacolod City on installment and built improvements on Lot 9 after the developer's exclusive sales agent C.T. Torres Enterprises, Inc. delivered the wrong lot to him.[^313] Eldred Jardinico, who owned Lot 9, found the improvements after getting his title on December 19, 1978 and sued Kee for ejectment. Kee filed a third-party complaint against the developer and its agent.[^314] The Regional Trial Court dismissed that complaint for lack of proof of negligence, but the Court of Appeals declared Kee a builder in good faith and held the developer and agent liable.[^315] The developer invoked Clause 13 of the contract to sell on installment as a waiver of damages. The Supreme Court held under Article 6 that the clause addressed only changes due to erosion and that waiving liability for negligence would contravene public policy.[^316] It held the developer liable as principal under Articles 1909 and 1910 and ruled that Jardinico's July 24, 1987 sale of Lot 9 to Kee pending appeal did not affect that liability.[^317] Partially granting the petition, the Supreme Court modified the Court of Appeals decision to declare Kee a builder in good faith and hold Pleasantville Development Corporation and C.T. Torres Enterprises, Inc. solidarily liable for negligence damages left unquantified for lack of trial proof.[^318] It ordered solidary payment of PHP 3,000.00 attorney's fees plus litigation expenses to Jardinico and dispensed with rentals.[^319] The case illustrates both the public-policy bar against negligence waivers and strict proof requirements for damages.[^320]

### [*Marcos v. National Labor Relations Commission*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1995/sep1995/gr_111744_1995.html): Quitclaim Executed Under Written Protest

Insular Life Assurance Co., Ltd. declared the positions of four employees with 20 to 30 years of service redundant and dismissed them on November 1, 1990 with a redundancy benefit package.[^321] They questioned the package in an October 23, 1990 letter and signed the required release and quitclaim only with a written protest reiterating their claim to service awards.[^322] The labor arbiter awarded PHP 144,579.00 in service awards, anniversary bonus and prorated performance bonus plus ten percent attorney's fees, but the National Labor Relations Commission upheld the quitclaim and set the award aside.[^323] Quoting [*Veloso*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/aug1991/gr_87297_1991.html) on Articles 6 and 22, the Supreme Court held that the written protest negated total voluntariness.[^324] It found that the redundancy package did not cover the awards because the company had first denied owing them. It ordered the bonuses prorated to the months actually served in 1990.[^325] Setting aside the May 31, 1992 Decision and August 27, 1993 Resolution of the National Labor Relations Commission, the Supreme Court reinstated Labor Arbiter Alex Arcadio Lopez's decision.[^326] The decision confirms that express written reservations defeat any claim of knowing waiver.[^327]

### [*Gatchalian v. Delim*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/oct1991/gr_56487_1991.html): Common-Carrier Passenger Waiver

A paying passenger was injured on July 11, 1973 when a mini-bus in La Union went off the road and turned turtle after a snapping sound.[^328] Three days later, while she was still in the hospital, the owner's wife had the injured passengers sign a prepared joint affidavit saying they no longer wished to sue and gave her PHP 12.00 for her fare home.[^329] The trial court dismissed her damages action because of that affidavit. The Court of Appeals rejected the waiver but still denied damages.[^330] The Supreme Court held under Article 6 that waivers must be couched in clear and unequivocal terms and that she signed while still dizzy from the accident.[^331] It also held that passenger waivers against common carriers offend public policy by eroding extraordinary diligence under Articles 1733 and 1755.[^332] The carrier proved no extraordinary diligence. The driver had brushed off the snapping sound as normal, so the Supreme Court found gross negligence.[^333] The Supreme Court reversed the October 24, 1980 Court of Appeals decision and the December 4, 1975 trial court decision. It ordered Delim to pay PHP 15,000.00 actual damages for plastic surgery on the forehead scar, PHP 30,000.00 moral damages and PHP 1,000.00 attorney's fees.[^334] It added six percent annual legal interest from promulgation and costs.[^335] The case pairs strict waiver-clarity rules with public-policy protection of common-carrier passengers.[^336]

### [*Veloso v. Department of Labor and Employment*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/aug1991/gr_87297_1991.html): Voluntary Agency-Approved Quitclaim

Workers who won a labor decision on October 6, 1987 for unfair labor practice and unpaid overtime and holiday benefits saw the employer move for reconsideration and recomputation of the PHP 46,267.92 award per claimant.[^337] While that motion was pending, Alfredo Veloso signed a quitclaim for PHP 25,000 on April 15, 1988 and Edito Liguaton signed one for PHP 20,000 on July 19, 1988.[^338] Counsel assisted both workers, a labor department officer attested Veloso's quitclaim and the Labor Undersecretary approved both settlements on December 16, 1988.[^339] The workers then sought annulment of the quitclaims for alleged dire necessity. The Supreme Court distinguished [*Pampanga Sugar*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1982/jun1982/gr_l-39387_1982.html) because those quitclaims were obtained after judgment without leave and rejected earlier by the Supreme Court.[^340] The Supreme Court held under Labor Code Article 227 that dire necessity alone does not invalidate a voluntary compromise absent proven deceit, coercion or unconscionably low consideration.[^341] Dismissing the petition, the Supreme Court imposed costs against petitioners.[^342] The ruling clarifies that fair, counsel-assisted compromises of disputed awards bind workers.[^343]

### [*People v. Donato*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/jun1991/gr_79269_1991.html): Constitutional Waiver of Bail

Rodolfo Salas, alias Commander Bilog, was charged with rebellion under Article 134 in relation to Article 135 of the Revised Penal Code in an information filed on October 2, 1986.[^344] On October 16, 1986 the Supreme Court dismissed his *habeas corpus* petition on the parties' joint manifestation that he would remain in legal custody and face trial.[^345] After Executive Order No. 187 of June 5, 1987 restored the non-capital penalty of *prision mayor*, the Manila trial court granted bail of PHP 30,000.00 on July 7, 1987.[^346] Its July 30, 1987 order raised the bail to PHP 50,000.00 but denied the prosecution's request to present evidence against bail.[^347] Ruling En Banc under Article 6, the Supreme Court held that contractual, statutory and constitutional rights may all be waived unless barred by statutory limits.[^348] It distinguished personal privileges like bail under Article III, Section 13 from custodial counsel waivers requiring written counsel-assisted compliance under Article III, Section 12(1).[^349] Setting aside the trial judge's July 7, 1987 and July 30, 1987 orders in Criminal Case No. 86-48926, the Supreme Court enforced the accused's binding waiver.[^350] The landmark decision maps Article 6 across constitutional rights.[^351]

### [*Principe v. Philippine-Singapore Transport Services, Inc.*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1989/aug1989/gr_80918_1989.html): Scandalously Low Death-Benefit Quitclaim

Abelardo Principe, a chief engineer of a Singapore-registered vessel, fell ill and died on September 15, 1982 while on duty off Palawan under a contract paying S$2,800 monthly.[^352] His widow sued the local agent PSTSI before the Philippine Overseas Employment Administration on July 5, 1983 and signed a release and quitclaim on December 22, 1983 for PHP 7,000.00 plus hospital and burial expenses.[^353] The agency dismissed that complaint with prejudice against PSTSI, but the motion to dismiss stated that the release was without prejudice to the foreign principal Chuan Hup.[^354] When she filed a second claim in 1986 that included Chuan Hup, the agency dismissed it as barred by prior judgment and the National Labor Relations Commission dismissed her appeal on September 25, 1987.[^355] The Supreme Court held that the release covered only PSTSI and that her acceptance of PHP 7,000.00 while in dire need of money did not waive her husband's death benefits.[^356] It ruled under Article 6 that a consideration far below the amount claimed is null and void as contrary to public policy. It called the payment scandalously low and inequitable.[^357] The Supreme Court blamed PSTSI for the delay in the second claim because it failed to keep its promise to help her recover the benefits.[^358] It then computed 36 months of wages under Singapore maritime law at S$100,800.[^359] Granting the petition and setting aside the September 25, 1987 resolution, the Supreme Court ordered PSTSI and Chuan Hup Agencies, Pte. Ltd. jointly and severally to pay the peso equivalent of S$100,800.00 and declared the decision immediately executory.[^360] The ruling establishes that grossly disproportionate consideration voids a quitclaim under Article 6.[^361]

### [*Sayson v. People*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1988/oct1988/gr_l-51745_1988.html): Dilatory Waiver of Defense Rights

Ramon Sayson was charged in a March 25, 1972 information with estafa through falsification of a commercial document for passing a forged Bank of America dollar draft to Mever Films, Inc.[^362] After seven postponements secured at his own initiative, he appeared alone on December 9, 1974 when the defense was to present evidence and said his lawyer had another case.[^363] The trial court denied a further postponement and submitted the case for decision, then convicted him on January 30, 1975.[^364] The Court of Appeals affirmed but found only attempted estafa and imposed six months of *arresto mayor* without a fine.[^365] The Supreme Court held under Article 6 that neither the Constitution nor statute forbids waiving the right to be heard by counsel and to present defense evidence, so protracted dilatory conduct without a meritorious defense validly waived those rights.[^366] It added that the trial court had no duty to appoint a counsel *de oficio* because that duty is mandatory only at arraignment and Sayson had filed his own pleadings as a self-described lawyer.[^367] Denying the petition, the Supreme Court affirmed *in toto* the Court of Appeals decision with costs against appellant.[^368] The case illustrates forfeiture of procedural due-process complaints through deliberate delay.[^369]

### [*Villalon, Jr. v. Intermediate Appellate Court*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1986/sep1986/gr_l-73751_1986.html): Lawyer's Waiver of Disbarment Confidentiality

Atty. Roman Villalon, Jr. was sued in Civil Case No. 2799 on May 16, 1979 for annulment of a deed of sale over land in San Juan, La Union that was also the subject of Administrative Case No. 1488, a disbarment complaint filed on July 22, 1975.[^370] He introduced the disbarment testimonies of three adverse witnesses to impeach them. The trial court struck every reference to the disbarment proceedings on September 20, 1985 under Rule 139, Section 10.[^371] The Intermediate Appellate Court dismissed his petition on February 3, 1986 because the order was interlocutory.[^372] Reversing both courts under Article 6, the Supreme Court held that Rule 139, Section 10 confidentiality is a privilege vested solely to protect the respondent lawyer's personal and professional reputation and may therefore be waived by that lawyer without violating public policy.[^373] It found that certiorari lay despite the interlocutory character of the order because the trial court patently abused its discretion and a later offer of evidence would be inadequate.[^374] Setting aside the February 3, 1986 Decision and February 19, 1986 Resolution, the Supreme Court lifted its restraining order and directed the trial court to retain the testimonies and references in Civil Case No. 2799.[^375] The ruling separates personal protective privileges from public-policy secrecy rules.[^376]

### [*Mercury Drug Co., Inc. v. Dayao*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1982/sep1982/gr_l-30452_1982.html): Contractual Waivers of Night and Holiday Pay

Nardo Dayao and 70 others sued Mercury Drug in the Court of Industrial Relations for unpaid additional pay for Sunday, legal holiday and night work despite employment contracts waiving extra compensation for Sundays and holidays.[^377] The industrial court ruled on March 30, 1968 that Mercury Drug must pay 69 petitioners 25 percent of basic salary for Sunday and holiday work and another 25 percent for night work from March 20, 1961 to June 30, 1962.[^378] It denied their claim for the first four hours of work on every other Sunday and on legal holidays.[^379] Applying Article 6 directly, the Supreme Court held that nighttime compensation rests on public policy and cannot be waived.[^380] The Supreme Court also treated the express waivers of Sunday and holiday pay as ineffective because workers cannot freely decline onerous or illegal terms.[^381] Dismissing the petition, the Supreme Court affirmed the March 30, 1968 Decision and July 6, 1968 En Banc Resolution with costs against petitioner.[^382] The case affirms that unequal bargaining power cannot sustain contractual waivers of statutory wage protections.[^383]

### [*Pampanga Sugar Development Co., Inc. v. Court of Industrial Relations*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1982/jun1982/gr_l-39387_1982.html): Post-Judgment Bypassing of Union Counsel

The Court of Industrial Relations found the employer guilty of unfair labor practice in its December 4, 1972 decision and ordered wage and fringe benefit differentials for 88 readmitted workers except 7.[^384] On December 27, 1972 the employer bypassed union counsel and obtained 53 individual quitclaims from the workers while its petition for review was pending.[^385] The industrial court later ordered 20 percent attorney's fees on the benefits of those 53 workers and a computation for the 28 who signed nothing.[^386] Applying Articles 6 and 22 of the Civil Code, the Supreme Court held the quitclaims void *ab initio* because they bound the workers to give up statutory benefits while exempting the employer from any liability.[^387] It also held that the employer used its moral ascendancy to procure them and that a cause of action already in court cannot be compromised without leave of court. Dealing with workers individually behind union counsel also violated good morals.[^388] The Supreme Court rejected the employer's reliance on Articles 2028 and 2040 because the quitclaims were separate renunciations by the workers and not a compromise with mutual concessions.[^389] Dismissing the petition, the Supreme Court directed the industrial court's successor to implement its June 6, 1974 Order with costs against petitioner.[^390] The landmark decision guards both statutory labor awards and judicial authority against post-judgment circumvention.[^391]

### [*Maniego v. Castelo*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1957/apr1957/gr_l-9855_1957.html): Tenant's Statutory Harvest Exemption

A landlord won a March 11, 1954 judgment against his tenant Daniel Jaime in the justice of the peace court of San Jose, Nueva Ecija, then bought at the execution sale as highest bidder all 145 cavans and 11 kilos of the tenant's palay for PHP 835.80.[^392] The provincial sheriff agreed to deliver the palay only after deducting 25 percent as exempt under Republic Act No. 1199, Section 19.[^393] On February 16, 1955 the justice of the peace court ordered that portion released to the tenant.[^394] The landlord sought mandamus to compel full delivery. The Court of First Instance of Nueva Ecija denied it because the tenant's late claim could not amount to a waiver.[^395] Rejecting the landlord's theory that delayed assertion waived the exemption, the En Banc Supreme Court held under Article 6 that the 25 percent harvest exemption safeguards family sustenance from harvest to harvest, so waiving it amounts to waiving the right to live in violation of public policy.[^396] The Supreme Court added that the landlord could not plead ignorance of the exempt portion because the palay was harvested on his own land.[^397] Holding the execution levy and sale over the exempt portion illegal, the Supreme Court affirmed the appealed judgment with costs against petitioner.[^398] The decision remains an early authority barring waivers that threaten basic subsistence.[^399]

## Suggested Answers

### Scenario 1: Mortgagor's Waiver of Foreclosure Notice

A borrower signs a bank's printed postponement agreement waiving republication and reposting of an extrajudicial foreclosure auction under Act No. 3135. When the bank purchases the property without new notices, may the borrower still annul the foreclosure sale?

Yes, the borrower may annul the foreclosure sale because the waiver is void under Article 6 of the Civil Code. Under [*Philippine National Bank v. Nepomuceno Productions, Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/51783), statutory posting and publication under Act No. 3135, Section 3 are mandated to protect the general public and third-person bidders so the auction attracts fair competition rather than merely to benefit the mortgagor.[^400] Waiving those notices contravenes public policy and prejudices third persons, creating neither a valid waiver nor estoppel against the borrower.

### Scenario 2: Deceitful Labor Quitclaims During Conciliation

During labor conciliation, a security agency hands seven guards checks representing only their own trust-fund savings and cash bonds, promising to reconcile unpaid money claims later if they sign quitclaims and resignation letters. Does executing those quitclaims bar their subsequent claims for constructive dismissal and statutory benefits?

No, the quitclaims do not bar the guards' claims and must be declared void. Under [*Naldo, Jr. v. Corporate Protection Services, Phils., Inc.*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2024/apr2024/gr_243139_2024.html), the employer bears the burden of proving that a quitclaim constitutes a credible, reasonable settlement executed voluntarily and with full understanding.[^401] Extracting quitclaims through misrepresentation over checks covering only the employees' own deposits negates voluntariness and consideration, leaving constructive dismissal and unpaid statutory claims enforceable.

### Scenario 3: Assignment of Military Pension for Family Support

A retired soldier executes a notarized Deed of Assignment directing payment of half of his military retirement pension to his separated spouse and minor children for support, then later invokes Section 31 of Presidential Decree No. 1638 exempting military pensions from execution. May the pension exemption be enforced to defeat the support assignment?

No, the pension exemption cannot defeat the assignment because the soldier validly waived it under Article 6. Under [*Mabugay-Otamias v. Republic*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/62002), the retiree owns the right to his pension and may waive its exemption from execution.[^402] Assigning half of that pension fulfills legal support duties under Article XV of the Constitution and Article 194 of the Family Code. The deed was not contrary to law and prejudiced no third person's recognized right. The Supreme Court left the wider conflict between the support provisions and Section 31 for a more appropriate case.

> "Such a waiver is precisely contrary to law. Moreover, a waiver would prejudice the rights of the students and the public, who have a right to expect that UST is acting within the bounds of the law, and provides quality education by hiring only qualified teaching personnel."[^418]

[*Son v. University of Santo Tomas*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64107)

> "In sum, We are of the view that their acceptance of UE's decision is voluntary and with full understanding thereof, tantamount to a waiver of their right to question the management's decision to terminate their services for academic dishonesty."[^419]

[*University of the East v. Masangkay*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64125)

# Tips

Treat Article 6 as a structured validity filter rather than an automatic defense whenever a signed release or disclaimer appears in a problem. Even an unambiguous document fails if the waiving party lacked knowledge, acted under deceit or surrendered a protection enacted for public welfare or third persons.

## Waiver Validity Checklist

Apply these five checks in sequence when analyzing any express or implied waiver question:

1. **Identify an existing personal right.** Confirm under [*People v. Donato*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/jun1991/gr_79269_1991.html) and [*Concerned Organization of PASAR Progressive Employees for Reform (COPPER) v. Philippine Associated Smelting and Refining Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70547) that a contractual, statutory or constitutional right actually existed, belonged to the party waiving it and was known to that holder.
2. **Test clarity and voluntariness.** Require clear and unequivocal terms or unequivocal voluntary conduct under [*Gatchalian v. Delim*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/oct1991/gr_56487_1991.html) and [*Premiere Development Bank v. Central Surety & Insurance Co., Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48855). Silence alone, unaccepted bank notices under [*Bank of the Philippine Islands v. Casa Montessori Internationale*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46619) or express written reservations under [*Marcos v. National Labor Relations Commission*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1995/sep1995/gr_111744_1995.html) defeat waiver.
3. **Check formal mode limits.** Verify whether the Constitution or statute mandates a special manner of waiver, such as the written and counsel-assisted safeguard required for custodial interrogation rights under [*People v. Tulin*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/52498) or leave of court before compromising a claim already pending in court under [*Pampanga Sugar Development Co., Inc. v. Court of Industrial Relations*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1982/jun1982/gr_l-39387_1982.html).
4. **Screen against the five public-interest bars.** Strike down waivers that contravene mandatory law, public order, public policy, morals or good customs. Regulatory qualifications under [*Son v. University of Santo Tomas*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64107), deliberative privilege under [*Department of Foreign Affairs v. BCA International Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63486), common-carrier diligence under [*Gatchalian v. Delim*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/oct1991/gr_56487_1991.html), seller negligence liability under [*Pleasantville Development Corp. v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/33514) and tenant subsistence exemptions under [*Maniego v. Castelo*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1957/apr1957/gr_l-9855_1957.html) all fail this bar.
5. **Check third-person prejudice.** Determine whether outsiders hold a recognized interest in the duty being waived. Foreclosure posting and publication under [*Philippine National Bank v. Nepomuceno Productions, Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/51783) protect third-party bidders and cannot be waived, whereas assigning one's own military pension for family support under [*Mabugay-Otamias v. Republic*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/62002) prejudices nobody.

> [!TIP]
> Watch for three classic bar-exam traps under Article 6:
> - **Private versus common carriers:** A shipper may waive cargo-loss liability in a private charter party under [*Valenzuela Hardwood and Industrial Supply, Inc. v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/34833), whereas a paying passenger cannot waive extraordinary diligence against a common carrier under [*Gatchalian v. Delim*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/oct1991/gr_56487_1991.html).
> - **Sweeping release clauses:** A compromise releasing "known or unknown, foreseen or unforeseen" claims settles only the pending dispute under [*Dela Rosa Liner, Inc. v. Borela*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/60782) and never bars unrelated statutory wage claims lacking separate consideration.
> - **Equal footing versus distress:** Counsel-assisted compromises of disputed awards stand when trickery and gross inadequacy are unproven under [*Veloso v. Department of Labor and Employment*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/aug1991/gr_87297_1991.html), while a payout accepted in dire need and found scandalously low fails under [*Principe v. Philippine-Singapore Transport Services, Inc.*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1989/aug1989/gr_80918_1989.html).

> [!NOTE]
> Remember the allocation of proof in practice: the party asserting a contractual or procedural waiver must show clear relinquishment against a strong presumption to the contrary under [*Premiere Development Bank v. Central Surety & Insurance Co., Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48855), and an employer invoking a labor quitclaim bears the affirmative burden of proving reasonable consideration and voluntary execution under [*Naldo, Jr. v. Corporate Protection Services, Phils., Inc.*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2024/apr2024/gr_243139_2024.html).

## High-Yield Bar Distinctions

When an exam fact pattern tests Article 6, contrast three recurring factual pairings to state both the rule and its statutory boundary precisely:

1. **Private benefit versus third-person statutory safeguards:** Distinguish a military retiree assigning half of his pension for family support under [*Mabugay-Otamias v. Republic*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/62002) from a mortgagor waiving extrajudicial foreclosure posting and publication under [*Philippine National Bank v. Nepomuceno Productions, Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/51783). The pension exemption exists solely for the retiree and prejudices nobody, whereas foreclosure notices attract third-party bidders and protect the public.
2. **Written reservation or deceit versus equal-footing acceptance:** Distinguish an employee writing an express claim reservation on a redundancy quitclaim under [*Marcos v. National Labor Relations Commission*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1995/sep1995/gr_111744_1995.html) or signing under deceit over trust-fund savings under [*Naldo, Jr. v. Corporate Protection Services, Phils., Inc.*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2024/apr2024/gr_243139_2024.html) from university professors who accepted their dismissal, collected their benefits and waited almost three years before suing under [*University of the East v. Masangkay*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64125).
3. **Trial representation or bail versus custodial investigation:** Distinguish waiving the personal constitutional right to bail under [*People v. Donato*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/jun1991/gr_79269_1991.html) or ratifying earlier trial representation with a bona fide lawyer in open court under [*People v. Tulin*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/52498) from uncounselled confessions during custodial interrogation, which remain inadmissible unless waived in writing in counsel's presence under [*People v. Tulin*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/52498).

Remember also that a waiver's legal effect follows conduct across civil litigation as well as labor disputes. Repeated voluntary progress payments without demanding joint works measurement waived that condition under [*F.F. Cruz & Co., Inc. v. HR Construction Corp.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/24219), signing a judicial compromise authorizing corporate asset sale without reserving pre-emption waived an alleged right of first refusal under [*Esguerra v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/34493) and signing a pre-trial factual stipulation waived a forgery claim under [*Alano v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/35392). By contrast, a bank's unilateral ten-day error notice printed on monthly statements cannot create a waiver under [*Bank of the Philippine Islands v. Casa Montessori Internationale*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46619).
