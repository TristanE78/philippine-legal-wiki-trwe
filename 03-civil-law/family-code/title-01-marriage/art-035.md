> Art. 35. The following marriages shall be void from the beginning:
>
> (1) Those contracted by any party below eighteen years of age even with the consent of parents or guardians;
>
> (2) Those solemnized by any person not legally authorized to perform marriages unless such marriages were contracted with either or both parties believing in good faith that the solemnizing officer had the legal authority to do so;
>
> (3) Those solemnized without license, except those covered the preceding Chapter;
>
> (4) Those bigamous or polygamous marriages not failing under Article 41;
>
> (5) Those contracted through mistake of one contracting party as to the identity of the other; and
>
> (6) Those subsequent marriages that are void under Article 53.

[^family-code]

# Explanation

## Scope and Architecture of Article 35

In [*Pulido v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67720), the Supreme Court named Articles 35, 36, 37, 38, 44 and 53 of the Family Code as the provisions that make a marriage void from the beginning.[^2] Unlike voidable marriages under Article 45 that remain valid and produce civil effects until set aside by a competent court in a direct action, void marriages are inexistent from the very beginning so that in legal contemplation no marriage ever took place.[^3] Under Article 26, paragraph 1 of the Family Code, *lex loci celebrationis* governs the extrinsic validity or formalities of a marriage celebrated abroad, whereas intrinsic validity remains bounded by Philippine public policy exceptions, expressly including Articles 35(1), 35(4), 35(5) and 35(6), alongside Civil Code Article 15 binding Filipino citizens anywhere in the world.[^4]

## Underage Parties: Article 35(1)

Article 35(1) declares void from the beginning any marriage contracted by a party below 18 years of age even with the consent of parents or guardians.[^family-code] None of the curated Supreme Court decisions in the local archive directly apply Article 35(1) to void an underage marriage. The En Banc decision in [*Pulido v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67720) involved a husband who married at age 16 on September 5, 1983.[^5] The Supreme Court did not apply Article 35(1) to that marriage. It acquitted him of bigamy because the prosecution failed to prove a prior valid marriage.[^6] [*Maliwat-Melad v. Melad*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69969) quotes all six paragraphs of Article 35, but its ruling turns on paragraph (2).[^7] [*De Munari v. Asprec*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2025/apr2025/gr_262831_2025.html) mentions Article 35(1) only when it quotes Article 26(1) of the Family Code.[^8]

## Lack of Authority of the Solemnizing Officer: Article 35(2)

Articles 3(1) and 4 of the Family Code establish the authority of the solemnizing officer under Article 7 as a formal requisite whose absence renders the marriage void *ab initio* except as provided in Article 35(2).[^9] Under Article 35(2), a marriage solemnized by an unauthorized person remains valid if either or both contracting parties believed in good faith that the solemnizing officer possessed legal authority to officiate.[^10]

### Presumption of Regularity and the Good-Faith Exception

In [*Maliwat-Melad v. Melad*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69969), the wife petitioned to nullify her March 23, 1990 Tarlac City wedding after her lawyer, Atty. Eduardo Cunanan, examined wedding photographs in 2017 and claimed the officiant was not Judge Conrado De Gracia, whose name appeared on the marriage contract, but Rotary Club co-member Rosalio Florendo.[^11] The Supreme Court dismissed the petition on two distinct grounds. First, petitioner and her witnesses failed to establish the identities of either Judge De Gracia or Florendo by clear and convincing evidence, leaving intact both the *prima facie* weight of the marriage contract as a public document and the presumption that the named judge possessed legal authority under Article 7(1).[^12] Second, even assuming someone other than Judge De Gracia solemnized the ceremony, the marriage remained valid under Article 35(2) because petitioner genuinely believed in good faith from 1990 until 2017 that an incumbent judge officiated her marriage.[^13]

### Proof Burden and Criminal Consequences When Authority Is Rebutted

In [*Genio v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69309), the accused was charged with bigamy for a September 7, 2013 wedding while still married to his May 20, 2006 spouse. His second wife and two witnesses testified that the Municipal Mayor never appeared and that the parties did not declare themselves husband and wife before an authorized officer.[^14] The Supreme Court applied Section 6, Rule 131 of the Rules of Court retroactively. It held that substantial evidence is enough for an accused to rebut the presumption in the Marriage Certificate. Estoppel could not replace the requisites that the Family Code sets for a valid marriage.[^15] The Supreme Court acquitted him of bigamy under Article 349 of the Revised Penal Code because the prosecution failed to prove every element beyond reasonable doubt. It added that nothing in that record showed the good faith exception under Article 35(2). It then convicted him under the variance doctrine of knowingly contracting a marriage against provisions of laws under Article 350 of the Revised Penal Code. He had signed the Marriage Certificate and gone through a sham marriage while knowing that his first marriage was never dissolved or declared void under Article 40.[^16]

## Absence of a Valid Marriage License: Article 35(3)

Under Article 35(3), marriages solemnized without a license are void from the beginning unless covered by the exemptions in Chapter 2, Title I of the Family Code.[^17] In [*Bounsit-Torralba v. Torralba*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68706), the Supreme Court reinstated a nullity decree for a January 26, 1996 civil wedding. The certificate marked the Article 34 box, but nothing showed an executed affidavit. The record showed the couple became sweethearts only in December 1995.[^18] In [*Go-Bangayan v. Bangayan, Jr.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55960), the purported marriage took place on March 7, 1982. The Supreme Court applied Article 35(3) because the Pasig City registrar certified that License No. N-07568 was not among the numbers 6648100 to 6648150 issued for February 1982. It also applied Article 1409 of the Civil Code to a marriage that the trial court described as "made only in jest". The marriage was void from the beginning and also non-existent. The couple's property relations fell under Article 148.[^19]

### Pleading Sufficiency Versus Trial Proof

At the pleading stage, a petitioner need not prove the evidentiary weight of civil registry records to survive dismissal. In [*Moncayo v. Republic*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70019), the husband sued in 2018 under Article 35(3) regarding a December 11, 1997 civil wedding and October 17, 1998 church wedding bearing Marriage License No. 2889833, attaching an August 22, 2017 Imus City Civil Registrar certification that the parties never applied for a license and alleging that no Chapter 2 exemption applied.[^20] The Supreme Court held that the complaint sufficiently stated a cause of action under Article 35(3) to proceed to trial. It further held that serving the Office of the Solicitor General and City Prosecutor past the five-day window in Section 5(4) of A.M. No. 02-11-10-SC does not compel automatic dismissal when service was eventually effected.[^21]

### Proving Non-Issuance Through Local Civil Registrar Certifications

Under Section 28, Rule 132 of the Rules of Court and the disputable presumption of regularity in Rule 131, Section 3(m), a local civil registrar's written certification is competent proof that no marriage license was issued. In [*Abbas v. Abbas*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55503), the Supreme Court held that omitting the words "despite diligent search" does not invalidate the certification where the registrar verifies the office records and shows the license number entered on the marriage contract was actually issued to a different couple.[^22] There, the Carmona Municipal Civil Registrar certified that Marriage License No. 9969967 listed in the 1993 marriage contract was issued to Arlindo Getalado and Myra Mabilangan, shifting to the opposing spouse the burden to produce the license or the person named Qualin who had secured it. She failed to discharge that burden.[^23] Clarifying a holistic approach that considers statutory registration duties under Act No. 3753 and attendant evidence, [*Cariaga v. Republic*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67964) reiterated that omitting "despite diligent search" does not diminish probative value and nullified a November 10, 2000 marriage where two Quezon City Civil Registry certifications confirmed Marriage License No. 131078 was issued to Mamerto O. Yambao and Amelia B. Parado rather than the parties while the Republic failed to call the certifying officer to prove its typographical-error theory.[^24]

### Rejection of Unclean-Hands Bars and Judicial Accountability

Neither questionable litigation motives nor party acquiescence validates a marriage celebrated without a license. In [*Abbas v. Abbas*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55503), the Supreme Court held that wedding photographs, cohabitation, a common child, or the husband's motive to evade a 2003 bigamy information could not cure the missing marriage license.[^25] Similarly, [*Cariaga v. Republic*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67964) declined to apply the clean-hands doctrine against a spouse who acquiesced in parents arranging documents, holding that any irregularity liability belongs in a separate proceeding and refusing nullity would validate a union the law declares void.[^26] Solemnizing officers who disregard the license requirement also face administrative sanctions: in [*Moreno v. Bernabe*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1995/jul1995/am_mtj-94-963_1995.html), the Supreme Court fined a judge P10,000.00 with a stern warning for misconduct after he admitted solemnizing a marriage without a license, ruling that Christian motives only mitigate liability and the complainant's later desistance does not divest disciplinary jurisdiction.[^27]

## Bigamous and Polygamous Marriages: Article 35(4)

Article 35(4) voids from the beginning bigamous or polygamous marriages not falling under Article 41 and functions alongside Article 40 requiring a final judicial declaration of nullity of a prior void marriage before remarrying.[^28] In [*Macalinao v. Macalinao*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2024/apr2024/gr_250613_2024.html), a seafarer married in 1981 entered a second marriage in 1990 while separated in fact and lived with the second partner for 25 years until his death. The Supreme Court ruled that the 1990 marriage was void *ab initio* under Article 35(4) in relation to Article 40, holding that the second partner's claimed ignorance of the prior marriage or the legal wife's own bigamous marriage could not cure it. It added that collateral nullity could be declared strictly to apportion PHP 4,506,309.52 in death benefits (awarding one-fourth to the legal wife, one-half to the legitimate child and one-eighth each to the two illegitimate children) outside A.M. No. 02-11-10-SC.[^29] Evidentiary proof of bigamy is likewise simplified by Article 410 of the Civil Code: in [*Iwasawa v. Gangan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/56188), self-authenticating National Statistics Office certificates of the prior 1994 marriage, the 2002 subsequent marriage and the prior husband's 2009 death, corroborated by the wife's admission, proved a void bigamous marriage under Article 35(4) without needing the records custodian to testify.[^30]

### Interplay with Article 41 Presumptive Death and Extrinsic Fraud

Bigamous unions not strictly compliant with Article 41 fall squarely under Article 35(4). In [*Navarro v. Domagtoy*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/33979), a municipal circuit trial judge solemnized a 1994 wedding relying on a joint affidavit of seven years' separation without an Article 41 judicial declaration of presumptive death (and performed another marriage outside his court's territorial jurisdiction contrary to Articles 7(1) and 8), resulting in a void bigamous marriage under Article 35(4) and a six-month suspension for gross ignorance of the law.[^31] In [*Santos v. Santos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/58366), a husband obtained a July 27, 2007 decree declaring his wife presumptively dead and remarried on September 17, 2008. The wife alleged that she never left their Quezon City home and that he falsely claimed she had been absent for 12 years.[^32] The Supreme Court remanded the case to determine extrinsic fraud. It explained that a subsequent marriage contracted in bad faith lacks the well-founded belief that Article 41 requires. Such a marriage is void for being bigamous under Article 35(4).[^33] An affidavit of reappearance under Article 42 would not suffice, because it ends the subsequent marriage without nullifying its effects on the children or on criminal liability for bigamy. A petition for declaration of nullity was not open to her, because Section 2(a) of A.M. No. 02-11-10-SC allows only the husband or the wife to file it. An action to annul the judgment declaring her presumptively dead therefore lay.[^34]

### Conversion to Islam Under the Muslim Code

Subsequent conversion to Islam does not excuse bigamy when the first marriage was celebrated outside Muslim law. In [*Malaki v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67896), a husband married under Iglesia ni Cristo rites in 1988 converted to Islam and wed another woman before a judge in 2005. The Supreme Court affirmed both spouses' convictions for bigamy under Article 349 of the Revised Penal Code, holding that Article 13(2) of Presidential Decree No. 1083 excludes prior non-Muslim marriages from Muslim law and that noncompliance with Article 162's written Shari'a Circuit Court notice and prior wife's consent in any event renders the subsequent marriage bigamous, void from the beginning under the Family Code and outside Article 180's exculpatory clause.[^35]

### Void Marriages as a Defense in Criminal Bigamy After Pulido

Sitting En Banc, [*Pulido v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67720) abandoned earlier jurisprudence that barred an accused in bigamy from invoking the nullity of a prior or subsequent marriage without a prior judicial declaration under Article 40.[^36] Harmonizing Article 40's phrase "for purposes of remarriage" with Article 349 of the Revised Penal Code and the strict construction of penal statutes, the Supreme Court held for both Civil Code and Family Code marriages that an accused may establish via competent evidence (or via a subsequent decree obtained during trial) that the prior marriage was void *ab initio* (such as Pulido's 1983 marriage lacking a marriage license) or that the second marriage was void on non-bigamous grounds. This defense warrants acquittal.[^37] By contrast, a subsequent marriage declared void solely because it is bigamous cannot exculpate the accused. Knowingly contracting a void marriage in disregard of legal requisites remains punishable under Article 350 of the Revised Penal Code.[^38]

### Standing to Sue and Recognition of Foreign Nullity Decrees

Recognizing Article 35(4) as the civil counterpart of Article 349 of the Revised Penal Code, [*Fujiki v. Marinay*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55916) held that "the husband or the wife" under Section 2(a) of A.M. No. 02-11-10-SC refers to the spouse of the prior subsisting marriage, empowering a prior Japanese husband to file a Rule 108 and Rule 39, Section 48(b) petition to recognize a Japanese Family Court judgment nullifying his Filipino wife's subsequent bigamous marriage without prejudice to bigamy prosecution.[^39] The posture in [*Santos v. Santos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/58366) was different, since the wife there had been declared presumptively dead. In [*Fujiki*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55916) and [*De Munari*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2025/apr2025/gr_262831_2025.html), the Supreme Court upheld the standing of the prior spouse where the ground was a bigamous subsequent marriage.[^40] Under the same rule, [*De Munari v. Asprec*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2025/apr2025/gr_262831_2025.html) held that Philippine courts have jurisdiction over a bigamous marriage celebrated abroad between an Italian citizen and a Filipino spouse with prior subsisting marriages and recognized that the deceived foreign spouse has standing under Section 2(a) to file a direct nullity suit.[^41] Conversely, [*Quirit-Figarido v. Figarido*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69745) held that the spouse who contracted a subsequent marriage while her first marriage still subsisted (before her first foreign spouse obtained a divorce four years later) is not an "aggrieved or injured spouse" and lacks legal personality to sue for nullity of her own bigamous union, though dismissing her petition neither legalizes the union nor prevents criminal liability or independent actions on heirship, legitimacy, estate settlement or property dissolution.[^42]

## Mistake as to Identity and Article 53 Noncompliance: Articles 35(5) and 35(6)

Article 35(5) renders void from the beginning marriages contracted through mistake of one contracting party as to the identity of the other. Article 35(6) voids subsequent marriages declared void under Article 53 after noncompliance with the recording requirements of Article 52 following a prior judgment of annulment or absolute nullity.[^family-code] None of the curated Supreme Court decisions in the local archive directly apply Article 35(5) or Article 35(6) to resolve a controversy. Instead, [*De Munari v. Asprec*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2025/apr2025/gr_262831_2025.html) mentions both provisions only within its quotation of Article 26(1) of the Family Code.[^43]

## Decisions Examined

### [*Bounsit-Torralba v. Torralba*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68706) Examined

Sue Ann Bounsit-Torralba married Joseph Torralba in a civil ceremony on January 26, 1996 without a marriage license.[^44] She petitioned for nullity on the ground of psychological incapacity. She also alleged the lack of a license.[^45] The Court of Appeals reversed the trial court on the first ground and never ruled on the second.[^46] The Supreme Court held that she failed to prove psychological incapacity. The report rested on her own interview, her sister's comments and court records. No independent source supported it.[^47] The Supreme Court then took up the license ground. It found no affidavit and no five years of cohabitation before the wedding, so Article 34 did not apply. It held the marriage void from the beginning under Article 35 and reinstated the trial court decree.[^48] The case shows that a petition pleading two grounds can fail on one and succeed on the other.

### [*Go-Bangayan v. Bangayan, Jr.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55960) Examined

Benjamin Bangayan, Jr. and Sally Go-Bangayan signed a purported marriage contract dated March 7, 1982 that bore Marriage License No. N-07568. Benjamin had married Azucena Alegre in 1973. He later petitioned for a declaration of a non-existent marriage or of its nullity.[^49] The Pasig City registrar testified that only License Nos. 6648100 to 6648150 were issued for February 1982. The marriage was also not recorded with the registrar or the National Statistics Office.[^50] The Supreme Court saw no inconsistency in calling the marriage both void under Article 35 and non-existent.[^51] It also held that the marriage was not bigamous. A second marriage that is void for lack of a license is not a subsequent marriage at all, so no bigamy was committed.[^52] The case shows that a missing license and a subsisting prior marriage are separate questions with separate consequences.

### [*Santos v. Santos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/58366) Examined

Celerina Santos was declared presumptively dead on July 27, 2007 on her husband Ricardo's petition. He remarried on September 17, 2008.[^53] She filed a petition to annul the judgment on November 17, 2008 and alleged extrinsic fraud and lack of jurisdiction.[^54] The Court of Appeals dismissed it as a wrong remedy and said she should have recorded an affidavit of reappearance under Article 42.[^55] The Supreme Court disagreed. An affidavit of reappearance admits that the marriage ended with the declaration of absence. She denied ever being absent.[^56] It remanded the case for the Court of Appeals to determine extrinsic fraud.[^57] The case shows that the choice of remedy decides whether the effects of a presumptive death decree can be undone.

### [*Quirit-Figarido v. Figarido*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69745) Examined

Maria Lina Quirit-Figarido married Ho Kar Wai in 1989 and again in 1994. She married Edwin Figarido in 2003 while the first marriage subsisted. Ho Kar Wai obtained a divorce decree four years later. A Philippine court recognized it in 2009.[^58] In 2017 she petitioned to declare her marriage to Edwin void for being bigamous and to confirm her capacity to remarry.[^59] The Supreme Court denied the petition. She was not an aggrieved or injured innocent spouse. Ho Kar Wai lost that status once the divorce ended the prior marriage.[^60] The denial did not legalize the marriage. Erring parties may still be held civilly and criminally liable. The nullity of a void marriage may also be raised for other purposes without a judicial action.[^61] The case shows that standing under Section 2(a) turns on who was injured by the bigamy.

### [*Navarro v. Domagtoy*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/33979) Examined

The Municipal Mayor of Dapa charged Judge Hernando Domagtoy with two acts. On September 27, 1994 he solemnized a wedding for a groom who was only separated from his first wife. On October 27, 1994 he solemnized another wedding at his residence in Dapa, outside his court's jurisdiction.[^62] The judge relied on a joint affidavit of almost seven years of separation. The Supreme Court held that Article 41 requires a summary proceeding to declare presumptive death. The groom had not instituted one. The resulting marriage was bigamous and void.[^63] On the second wedding, it held that Article 8 is directory and refers only to venue. A judge appointed to specific municipalities may officiate only within them.[^64] It suspended the judge for six months for gross ignorance of the law.[^65] The case shows how a solemnizing officer's errors can produce a void marriage and administrative liability at once.

### [*Fujiki v. Marinay*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55916) Examined

Minoru Fujiki helped Maria Paz Marinay obtain a 2010 Japanese family court judgment declaring void her marriage to Shinichi Maekara on the ground of bigamy. On January 14, 2011 he petitioned the Regional Trial Court to recognize the judgment.[^66] The trial court dismissed the petition under Sections 2(a) and 4 of A.M. No. 02-11-10-SC.[^67] The Supreme Court held that the Rule does not apply to a petition to recognize a foreign judgment on the status of a marriage with a foreign party. The petitioner needs only to prove the judgment as a fact.[^68] It also held that Section 2(a) refers to the husband or the wife of the subsisting marriage, so Fujiki had personality to sue.[^69] Recognition of the judgment is without prejudice to prosecution for bigamy.[^70] The case shows that a prior spouse can reach a foreign bigamy-nullity judgment through Rule 108 without filing a nullity action.

## Suggested Answers

**Question 1.** A wife seeks a declaration of nullity of her 1990 civil marriage at a municipal hall. Their marriage contract names an incumbent municipal judge as the solemnizing officer. In 2017, her lawyer inspects wedding photographs and testifies that the man standing at the ceremony was a civic-club acquaintance rather than the named judge, although neither the wife nor any wedding witness knew what either man looked like. Should the petition be granted?

**Answer.** No. Under [*Maliwat-Melad v. Melad*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69969), self-serving and uncorroborated testimony failing to identify either the named judge or the alleged impostor cannot overcome the *prima facie* evidentiary value of the marriage contract or the presumption that the solemnizing officer held legal authority under Article 7(1).[^103] Moreover, even if an unauthorized person had actually officiated, the marriage remains valid under the Article 35(2) exception because the wife genuinely believed in good faith since the ceremony in 1990 until consulting her lawyer in 2017 that an incumbent judge solemnized the union.[^104]

**Question 2.** A couple allowed the bride's parents and a family friend to arrange the documentary requirements for their civil wedding in 2000. Their marriage contract bears Marriage License No. 131078 issued the day before the wedding. Years later, the wife obtains two local civil registrar certifications stating that the registry has no marriage license application in the spouses' names and that Marriage License No. 131078 was actually issued to another couple. The Office of the Solicitor General opposes nullity on the grounds that the certification omits the phrase "despite diligent search," that another license number might have been issued in error and that the wife had unclean hands by leaving paperwork to third persons. Resolve the petition.

**Answer.** The marriage must be declared void *ab initio* under Article 35(3). Applying the holistic evidentiary rule in [*Cariaga v. Republic*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67964), a civil registrar's certification under Section 28, Rule 132 need not recite the words "despite diligent search" when it affirmatively proves no application was recorded and shows the listed serial number belonged to a different couple, shifting the burden to the Republic to prove a valid license was in fact issued.[^105] Nor does the doctrine of unclean hands prevent declaring an unlicensed marriage void under Article 35(3), as any liability for irregularity must be resolved in a separate proceeding rather than by validating a marriage the Family Code declares void.[^106]

**Question 3.** While validly married to a foreign spouse, a Filipino citizen contracts a second marriage in the Philippines in 2003. Four years later, the foreign first spouse obtains a divorce decree abroad, which a Philippine trial court judicially recognizes. May the Filipino spouse subsequently file a direct petition under A.M. No. 02-11-10-SC to declare her 2003 marriage void *ab initio* for being bigamous under Article 35(4)?

**Answer.** No. As held in [*Quirit-Figarido v. Figarido*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69745), Section 2(a) of A.M. No. 02-11-10-SC and its Rationale limit standing to file a petition for absolute nullity of a bigamous marriage under Article 35(4) to the aggrieved or injured innocent spouse of either marriage. The party who contracted the bigamous union is excluded.[^107] Nevertheless, dismissing her direct petition does not legalize the 2003 marriage or foreclose criminal liability or collateral civil proceedings involving heirship, legitimacy, estate settlement or dissolution of property regime.[^108]

# Quotes

## Solemnizing Officer Without Authority: Article 35(2)

> "In any case, even assuming, for the sake of argument, that the person who solemnized the marriage was not Judge De Gracia, the marriage is still valid because the case falls under Article 35(2) of the Family Code."[^109]

> "Thus, petitioner's case falls under the exception of void marriages as provided in Article 35(2) of the Family Code. Petitioner's marriage with private respondent is valid owing to her genuine belief, in good faith, that the solemnizing officer had the legal authority to officiate her marriage with private respondent."[^110]

> "Meanwhile, as to the absence of the authority of the solemnizing officer, the general rule is that the marriage is void ab initio, unless the good faith exception in Article 35(2) of the Family Code applies, which has not been shown to exist in the case at bar."[^111]

> "Simply, the law acknowledges that while a second marriage may be void ab initio due to the existence of a legal impediment or absence of any of the essential requisites for a valid marriage, it may still produce legal consequences in that the parties thereto may incur criminal liabilities for either Article 349 or Article 350 of the RPC."[^112]

## Lack of a Marriage License: Article 35(3)

> "Respondent's petition clearly states the ultimate facts on which he bases his claim. It cites the legal requirement to get married under a valid license and discusses how their marriage failed to comply with that requirement. In filing his petition, respondent need not yet prove the facts he is alleging. The evidence is meant to be presented during trial on the merits. His petition need only show that a cause of action exists so that trial can ensue."[^113]

> "Since the exceptional case under Article 34 of the Family Code does not apply, the requisite of a valid marriage license is lacking. Hence, pursuant to Article 35 of the said law, the marriage between Sue Ann and Joseph is void from the beginning."[^114]

> "Hence, lest there be any confusion, the Court here clarifies that in cases where the absence of a marriage license is sought to be established through a certification issued by the local civil registrar, courts must take a holistic approach in resolving the case."[^115]

> "As already held in Abbas, the absence of the words "despite diligent search" in the certification of the local civil registrar does not, on its own, diminish the probative value of the certification."[^116]

> "That said, the Court also recognizes that in petitions to declare the absolute nullity of marriage based on the absence of a valid marriage license, testimony of this nature should not ipso facto preclude a finding of nullity on the ground that parties who come to court must do so with clean hands."[^117]

> "We see no inconsistency in finding the marriage between Benjamin and Sally null and void ab initio and, at the same time, non-existent. Under Article 35 of the Family Code, a marriage solemnized without a license, except those covered by Article 34 where no license is necessary, “shall be void from the beginning.”"[^118]

> "All the evidence cited by the CA to show that a wedding ceremony was conducted and a marriage contract was signed does not operate to cure the absence of a valid marriage license. Article 4 of the Family Code is clear when it says, “The absence of any of the essential or formal requisites shall render the marriage void ab initio, except as stated in Article 35(2).” Article 35(3) of the Family Code also provides that a marriage solemnized without a license is void from the beginning, except those exempt from the license requirement under Articles 27 to 34, Chapter 2, Title I of the same Code."[^119]

> "Respondent cannot hide behind his claim of good faith and Christian motives which, at most, would serve only to mitigate his liability but not exonerate him completely. Good intentions could never justify violation of the law."[^120]

## Bigamous Marriages, Criminal Liability, and Standing: Article 35(4)

> "After a careful consideration, this Court is constrained to abandon our earlier rulings that a judicial declaration of absolute nullity of the first, and/or second marriages cannot be raised as a defense by the accused in a criminal prosecution for bigamy. We hold that a judicial declaration of absolute nullity is not necessary to prove a void ab initio prior and subsequent marriages in a bigamy case. Consequently, a judicial declaration of absolute nullity of the first and/or second marriages presented by the accused in the prosecution for bigamy is a valid defense, irrespective of the time within which they are secured."[^121]

> "In view of the foregoing, it is evident that Philippine courts have jurisdiction to nullify a bigamous marriage involving a foreigner and a Filipino, even if the marriage was celebrated abroad. This aligns with the principle that the intrinsic validity of a marriage, including its legality, must adhere to Philippine law when one or both of parties are Filipino citizens."[^122]

> "First, with respect to her belief that the denial of her petition has the general effect of legalizing bigamous marriages, We hold that this is definitely not the import of this Decision. The denial of Maria Lina's petition is not a refusal to declare her bigamous marriage void, but rather a repudiation of Maria Lina's legal personality to file the said petition."[^123]

> "In law and in fact, the bigamous marriage entered into by one spouse with another is completely distinct from and irrelevant to the bigamous marriage which the other spouse may have also entered into. The bigamous nature of the marriage of Pedrito and Elenita cannot be cured by any successful imputation of fault or bad faith on the part of Cerena in herself contracting a bigamous marriage."[^124]

> "If, as Celerina contends, Ricardo was in bad faith when he filed his petition to declare her presumptively dead and when he contracted the subsequent marriage, such marriage would be considered void for being bigamous under Article 35(4) of the Family Code."[^125]

> "This Court has consistently held that a judicial declaration of nullity is required before a valid subsequent marriage can be contracted; or else, what transpires is a bigamous marriage, which is void from the beginning as provided in Article 35(4) of the Family Code of the Philippines."[^126]

> "Article 35(4) of the Family Code, which declares bigamous marriages void from the beginning, is the civil aspect of Article 349 of the Revised Penal Code, which penalizes bigamy. Bigamy is a public crime. Thus, anyone can initiate prosecution for bigamy because any citizen has an interest in the prosecution and prevention of crimes."[^127]

> "Whether petitioner Francis converted to Islam before or after his marriage with petitioner Jacqueline, the subsequent marriage consummated the crime of bigamy. He cannot successfully invoke the exculpatory clause in Article 180, considering that the Muslim Code finds no application in his then subsisting marriage with Nerrian, the marriage recognized by law that bars and penalizes a subsequent marriage."[^128]

> "In the case at bar, Gaspar Tagadan did not institute a summary proceeding for the declaration of his first wife's presumptive death. Absent this judicial declaration, he remains married to Ida Peñaranda. Whether wittingly, or unwittingly, it was manifest error on the part of respondent judge to have accepted the joint affidavit submitted by the groom. Such neglect or ignorance of the law has resulted in a bigamous, and therefore void, marriage."[^129]

# Tips

## Diagnostic Checklist Under Article 35

- **First, verify the governing law:** Check the date of celebration to confirm whether the Family Code or the Civil Code governs the marriage (*Pulido v. People*). Also check whether a prior non-Muslim wedding excludes Muslim Code coverage (*Malaki v. People*).
- **Second, match the defect to the exact paragraph of Article 35:** Identify whether the ground is underage capacity under Article 35(1), unauthorized officiant under Article 35(2), missing marriage license under Article 35(3), subsisting prior marriage outside Article 41 under Article 35(4), identity mistake under Article 35(5), or Article 53 noncompliance under Article 35(6).
- **Third, test exceptions and good faith strictly:** For Article 35(2), determine whether either or both parties genuinely believed in good faith that the officiant held authority; for Article 35(3), check whether an exemption such as Article 34 is actually met by an affidavit and five years of cohabitation; for Article 35(4), verify whether an Article 41 summary decree rested on well-founded belief without extrinsic fraud.
- **Fourth, verify party standing and criminal exposure:** Distinguish the injured prior spouse or deceived subsequent spouse (who has standing) from the guilty bigamous spouse (who lacks standing under Section 2(a) of A.M. No. 02-11-10-SC), and assess separate exposure under Articles 349 and 350 of the Revised Penal Code.

## Article 35(2): Verifying Authority and Good Faith

> [!TIP]
> **Bar Exam Focus:** Impersonation or lack of solemnizing authority does not automatically void a marriage under Article 35(2). Check whether either party genuinely believed in good faith at the wedding that the officiant held legal authority under Article 7 (*Maliwat-Melad v. Melad*), and do not treat failure of proof by witnesses unfamiliar with the named officer as affirmative proof of impersonation.

> [!NOTE]
> **Criminal Exposure Distinguished:** An acquittal for bigamy because the prosecution failed to prove the second marriage does not shield a defendant who knowingly went through a sham marriage during a subsisting prior union. A conviction under Article 350 of the Revised Penal Code remains available under the variance doctrine (*Genio v. People*).

## Article 35(3): Challenging or Defending the Marriage License

> [!TIP]
> **Bar Exam Focus:** Do not stop at a checked Article 34 exemption box on the marriage contract. A missing affidavit or proof that the parties became sweethearts less than five years before the wedding defeats Article 34 and voids the marriage under Article 35(3) (*Bounsit-Torralba v. Torralba*).

> [!NOTE]
> **Civil Registrar Certifications and Clean Hands:** Under the holistic standard in *Cariaga v. Republic* and *Abbas v. Abbas*, a local civil registrar certification need not use the exact formula "despite diligent search" if it states no license record exists for the spouses and shows the cited license number was issued to another couple. Alleged unclean hands or impure motives do not bar nullity under Article 35(3), and judges who solemnize marriages without a license face administrative sanctions despite Christian motives or complainant desistance (*Moreno v. Bernabe*).

## Article 35(4): Bigamy, Standing, and Overlapping Proceedings

> [!TIP]
> **Bar Exam Focus:** Under *Pulido v. People*, an accused charged with bigamy may raise the absolute nullity of a void *ab initio* first marriage or a non-bigamous void second marriage without first securing a separate Article 40 decree. However, if the second marriage is void solely because it is bigamous, that nullity is no defense to bigamy.

> [!NOTE]
> **Standing and Foreign Judgments:** The prior spouse may sue directly under Section 2(a) of A.M. No. 02-11-10-SC (*Fujiki v. Marinay*, *De Munari v. Asprec*). So may a foreign subsequent spouse (*De Munari v. Asprec*). The spouse who contracted the bigamous union cannot file a nullity suit (*Quirit-Figarido v. Figarido*). Collateral proceedings such as death benefit distribution (*Macalinao v. Macalinao*) or annulment of a presumptive death decree allegedly obtained by fraud (*Santos v. Santos*) remain distinct remedies.

## Pack Gaps: Articles 35(1), 35(5), and 35(6)

> [!NOTE]
> **Absence of Direct Jurisprudence in Archive:** None of the curated decisions in the local archive directly apply Article 35(1) on underage marriage, Article 35(5) on mistake as to identity, or Article 35(6) on subsequent marriages void under Article 53. Practitioners addressing those paragraphs should anchor arguments directly in the statutory text of Article 35, read with Article 26(1) and Articles 52 and 53 of the Family Code.
