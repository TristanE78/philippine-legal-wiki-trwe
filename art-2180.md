> ART. 2180. The obligation imposed by article 2176 is demandable not only for one’s own acts or omissions, but also for those of persons for whom one is responsible.
>
> The father and, in case of his death or incapacity, the mother, are responsible for the damages caused by the minor children who live in their company.
>
> Guardians are liable for damages caused by the minors or incapacitated persons who are under their authority and live in their company.
>
> The owners and managers of an establishment or enterprise are likewise responsible for damages caused by their employees in the service of the branches in which the latter are employed or on the occasion of their functions.
>
> Employers shall be liable for the damages caused by their employees and household helpers acting within the scope of their assigned tasks, even though the former are not engaged in any business or industry.
>
> The State is responsible in like manner when it acts through a special agent; but not when the damage has been caused by the official to whom the task done properly pertains, in which case what is provided in article 2176 shall be applicable.
>
> Lastly, teachers or heads of establishments of arts and trades shall be liable for damages caused by their pupils and students or apprentices, so long as they remain in their custody.
>
> The responsibility treated of in this article shall cease when the persons herein mentioned prove that they observed all the diligence of a good father of a family to prevent damage. (1903a)[^civil-code]

# Explanation

## Concept, Rationale and Scope of Vicarious Liability

Article 2180 establishes the rule of vicarious or imputed liability (*responsabilidad indirecta*) in Philippine quasi-delict law.[^civil-code] Under [Article 2176](art-2176.md), a person is ordinarily responsible only for his or her own fault or negligence.[^civil-code] The first paragraph of Article 2180 expands that rule by declaring that the obligation imposed by Article 2176 is demandable not only for one's own acts or omissions, but also for those of persons for whom one is responsible.[^civil-code] [^2] Although the injury is physically inflicted by another person, the law imputes the negligence to the person who exercises authority, supervision or control over the actor because of the supervisor's own failure to exercise the diligence of a good father of a family (*culpa in eligendo* or *culpa in vigilando*).[^3] [^4]

The Supreme Court has explained that the underlying basis of vicarious liability in Article 2180 (derived from Article 1903 of the Spanish Civil Code of 1889) is the Roman law principle of *patria potestas* or *paterfamilias*.[^5] [^6] Unlike the Anglo-American doctrine of *respondeat superior*, where the negligence of the employee is conclusively presumed to be the negligence of the employer, the Philippine civil law theory of *bonus paterfamilias* rests on the non-performance of duties of precaution and prudence imposed upon the person who is united to the actor by a civil bond of authority.[^7] [^8] [^9] In commercial and transport enterprises, the Supreme Court (quoting Prosser and Keeton on the Law of Torts in [*Metro Manila Transit Corp. v. Court of Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1998/nov1998/gr_116617_1998.html) (1998) and [*Victory Liner, Inc. v. Heirs of Andres Malecdan*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2002/dec2002/gr_154278_2002.html) (2002)) has also observed that vicarious liability reflects a deliberate policy allocation of risk, placing the losses caused by employee torts upon the enterprise that profits from the activity and has the greatest incentive to select, instruct and supervise its personnel carefully.[^10] [^11]

Because Article 2180 is an extension of Article 2176, its operation presupposes an extra-contractual claim founded on **quasi-delict (*culpa aquiliana*)** rather than breach of contract (*culpa contractual*).[^12] [^13] In [*Government Service Insurance System v. Spouses Labung-Deang*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2001/sep2001/gr_135644_2001.html) (2001), where the GSIS lost the borrowers' owner's duplicate title after their mortgage loan had been fully paid, the Supreme Court held that the obligation arose from a pre-existing mortgage contract rather than a quasi-delict, so GSIS could not invoke the sixth paragraph of Article 2180 to evade contractual liability.[^14] Conversely, where union officers or striking workers commit illegal acts during a strike, or where a strike leader is sued by a third party who does not employ the strikers, the Supreme Court held in [*Central Azucarera De San Antonio, Inc. v. CASA Sugar Mill Workers Union-FFW*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2026/jan2026/gr_259683_2026.html) (2026) and [*Universal Aquarius, Inc. v. Q.C. Human Resources Management Corp.*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2007/sep2007/gr_155990_2007.html) (2007) that Article 2180 does not create collective or vicarious liability for acts governed by Articles 19 and 20 of the Civil Code outside the relationships enumerated in Article 2180.[^15] [^16]

## Nature of Liability: Direct, Primary and Solidary Responsibility

The liability imposed by Article 2180 is **direct, primary and immediate**, not subsidiary.[^17] [^18] Under [Article 2194](art-2194.md) of the Civil Code, the responsibility of two or more persons who are liable for a quasi-delict is solidary.[^civil-code] Read together, Articles 2180 and 2194 render the person vicariously responsible and the actual tortfeasor **joint and solidary debtors** toward the injured party.[^19] [^20]

This direct and solidary nature produces four major procedural and substantive consequences that distinguish a Civil Code quasi-delict action under Article 2180 from subsidiary civil liability under Article 103 of the Revised Penal Code:[^revised-penal-code]

1. **No requirement of criminal conviction or employee insolvency.** Subsidiary liability under Article 103 of the Revised Penal Code attaches only when the employer is engaged in an industry, the employee has been convicted of a felony committed in the discharge of duties and the employee is proven insolvent upon execution.[^revised-penal-code] [^21] [^22] By contrast, under Article 2180 of the Civil Code, the injured party may enforce the entire obligation directly against the parent, guardian, employer or school without first suing the employee or exhausting the employee's properties.[^23]
2. **Independence from criminal proceedings and acquittal.** Because a quasi-delict under Articles 2176 and 2180 is an independent civil action under [Article 2177](art-2177.md), the death of the employee before final judgment, the dismissal of the criminal case, or the acquittal of the driver on reasonable doubt does not extinguish the employer's primary civil liability under Article 2180.[^24] [^25] [^26] [^27]
3. **Tortfeasor is not an indispensable party.** Because solidary creditors may proceed against any one of the solidary debtors or some or all of them simultaneously, the negligent employee, child or student is **not an indispensable party** in a suit against the vicariously liable defendant.[^28] [^29] [^30]
4. **Availability of the defense of due diligence.** In a proceeding to enforce subsidiary liability under Article 103 of the Revised Penal Code, the employer is conclusively bound by the criminal conviction of the employee (absent collusion) and cannot invoke the defense of due diligence in selection and supervision.[^31] [^32] In an action under Article 2180 of the Civil Code, by contrast, the person held vicariously responsible may escape liability by proving that he or she observed all the diligence of a good father of a family to prevent the damage.[^civil-code]

Under [Article 2181](art-2181.md) of the Civil Code, whoever pays for the damage caused by his or her dependents or employees may recover from the latter what he or she has paid or delivered.[^civil-code] The Supreme Court has explained, however, that Article 2181 does not make the employer's liability subsidiary to the employee's, nor does it authorize a court to summarily order the co-defendant employee to reimburse the employer unless the employer asserted that right by filing a **cross-claim** or separate action against the employee.[^33] [^34]

## Parents and Guardians (Paragraphs 2 and 3)

### Basis in Parental Authority and Modification by the Family Code

Under the second paragraph of Article 2180, the father and in case of his death or incapacity the mother, are responsible for the damages caused by the minor children who live in their company.[^civil-code] Under the third paragraph, guardians are liable for damages caused by the minors or incapacitated persons who are under their authority and live in their company.[^civil-code] The Supreme Court has explained that parental liability is a natural and logical consequence of the parental authority (*patria potestas*) vested in parents, which carries the legal duty to support, keep in their company, educate, instruct, control and discipline their minor children.[^35] [^36]

Under the original text of the second paragraph of Article 2180, the mother's liability was alternate and secondary, attaching only in case of the death or incapacity of the father.[^civil-code] [^37] [^38] That alternate qualification was superseded by the **Family Code of the Philippines** (Executive Order No. 209), under which the father and the mother jointly exercise parental authority.[^39] Specifically, **Article 221 of the Family Code** provides that parents and other persons exercising parental authority shall be civilly liable for the injuries and damages caused by the acts or omissions of their unemancipated children living in their company and under their parental authority, subject to the appropriate defenses provided by law.[^family-code] [^40] [^41]

### Primary Liability for Both Delicts and Quasi-Delicts of Minors

A major doctrinal question resolved by the Supreme Court was whether Article 2180 of the Civil Code applies when a minor child living in the parents' company commits a **deliberate crime (delict)** with discernment rather than a mere negligent act.[^42] [^43] In [*Salen v. Balce*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1960/apr1960/gr_l-14414_1960.html) (1960) and [*Fuellas v. Cadano*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1961/oct1961/gr_l-14409_1961.html) (1961), the Supreme Court held that because Article 101 of the Revised Penal Code expressly addresses only minors under nine or minors between nine and fifteen who act *without* discernment while leaving a statutory void for minors who act *with* discernment, that void is filled by the second paragraph of Article 2180 of the Civil Code.[^44] Subsequently, in the *En Banc*-level doctrinal synthesis in [*Spouses Libi v. Intermediate Appellate Court*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1992/sep1992/gr_70890_1992.html) (1992), reiterated in [*CICL XXX v. People*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2019/aug2019/gr_237334_2019.html) (2019), the Supreme Court resolved earlier inconsistencies in the case law and definitively ruled that the civil liability of parents for **both quasi-delicts and criminal offenses** committed by their minor children living in their company is **direct and primary, not subsidiary**, subject to the defense that the parents exercised the diligence of a good father of a family to prevent the damage.[^45] [^46]

### The *Libi* Doctrine: Primary Liability of Parents and the Diligence Defense

[*Spouses Libi v. Intermediate Appellate Court*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1992/sep1992/gr_70890_1992.html) arose from the deaths of two minors who had been sweethearts. Julie Ann Gotiong and Wendell Libi each died of a single gunshot wound from a revolver licensed to Wendell's father.[^47] The parents of Julie Ann sued the parents of Wendell for damages under Article 2180. The Court of Appeals reversed the trial court's dismissal and held the Libi spouses liable.[^48] The Supreme Court found that the overwhelming evidence pointed to Wendell as the assailant and rejected the theory that an unknown third person had shot both.[^49] The question that mattered for the doctrine was whether the liability of parents for the criminal act of a minor child is subsidiary, as the Court of Appeals had described it, or direct and primary.[^50]

The Supreme Court held that the civil liability of parents for the quasi-delicts of their minor children under Article 2180 is primary and not subsidiary. It reasoned that Article 2194 makes the persons responsible for an act or omission solidarily liable and that the last paragraph of Article 2180 lets parents escape liability by proving the diligence of a good father of a family.[^51] It held that the liability of parents for felonies of their minor children is likewise primary, because Article 101 of the Revised Penal Code makes the civil liability devolve on those who have the minor under their authority unless there was no fault or negligence on their part.[^52] The minor answers with his own property only when he has no parents or guardian or when they are insolvent, as Article 2182 of the Civil Code and Article 101 of the Revised Penal Code provide.[^53] The Supreme Court therefore ruled that parents are primarily liable for the civil liability arising from criminal offenses of their minor children who are under their legal authority or live in their company, unless they prove the diligence of a good father of a family.[^54] Article 101 governs that liability for children nine years of age or under and for those over nine but under fifteen who acted without discernment. Article 2180 governs it for children over nine but under fifteen who acted with discernment and for those fifteen or over but under twenty-one.[^55]

The Supreme Court then found the defense unproven. The revolver was kept in a safety deposit box in the parents' bedroom, each parent held a key and Wendell knew of both, yet the revolver was no longer in the box on the night of the deaths.[^56] The Supreme Court held that the diligence required of parents consists largely of the instruction and supervision of the child. It found the parents gravely remiss because they discovered only after Wendell's death that he worked as an informer for the Constabulary Anti-Narcotics Unit and that the revolver was missing.[^57] It also corrected the Court of Appeals on its reliance on [*Fuellas v. Cadano*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1961/oct1961/gr_l-14409_1961.html). The word subsidiary came from the syllabus in the law report of that case and does not appear in the text of the decision.[^58] Finally, the Supreme Court noted that under the Family Code this civil liability is now, without the alternative order found in the Child and Youth Welfare Code, the responsibility of the parents and of those who exercise parental authority over the minor.[^59]

### Requirement of Actual Physical Custody ("Living in Their Company")

For parental liability to attach under the second paragraph of Article 2180 (and Article 221 of the Family Code), the minor child must **live in the company** (that is, be in the actual physical custody and control) of the parents sought to be held liable at the time the tort is committed.[^60] In [*Tamargo v. Court of Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1992/jun1992/gr_85044_1992.html) (1992), a ten-year-old boy shot a girl to death with an air rifle while he was still in the actual physical custody of his natural parents in Ilocos Sur, although a petition for his adoption had already been filed prior to the shooting and the decree of adoption was granted after the shooting.[^61] The Supreme Court held that the natural parents were the parties answerable under Article 2180 and that the retroactive effect of an adoption decree back to the filing of the petition under Article 36 of the Child and Youth Welfare Code applies only when essential to permit the accrual of a benefit or advantage in favor of the adopted child and cannot be used to impose vicarious liability upon adopting parents for a tort they could neither foresee nor prevent.[^62] Similarly, when a parent places a minor child under the effective authority and custody of a school and its teacher during school hours or a school-supervised activity, the Supreme Court held in [*Cuadra v. Monfort*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1970/sep1970/gr_24101_1970.html) (1970) and [*Apolinario v. Heirs of Francisco de los Santos*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2024/nov2024/gr_219686_2024.html) (2024) that the parent is not primarily liable for an unforeseeable prank at school or a tort committed under the direct supervision and instruction of the teacher-in-charge.[^63] [^64]

## Owners, Managers and Employers (Paragraphs 4 and 5)

### Distinction Between Paragraph 4 and Paragraph 5 and Meaning of "Managers"

Article 2180 contains two paragraphs governing employment relations.[^civil-code] The **fourth paragraph** provides that the owners and managers of an establishment or enterprise are likewise responsible for damages caused by their employees in the service of the branches in which the latter are employed or on the occasion of their functions.[^civil-code] The **fifth paragraph** provides that employers shall be liable for the damages caused by their employees and household helpers acting within the scope of their assigned tasks, even though the former are not engaged in any business or industry.[^civil-code]

In [*Castilex Industrial Corp. v. Vasquez, Jr.*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1999/dec1999/gr_132266_1999.html) (1999), the Supreme Court explained that the fifth paragraph of Article 2180 applies to employers **whether or not** they are engaged in any business or industry, covers both employees and household helpers and expands the fourth paragraph in both coverage and application.[^65] [^66]

Crucially, in [*Philippine Rabbit Bus Lines, Inc. v. Phil-American Forwarders, Inc.*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1975/mar1975/gr_25142_1975.html) (1975) and [*Metro Manila Transit Corp. v. Court of Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1998/nov1998/gr_116617_1998.html) (1998), the Supreme Court held that the term **"managers"** in the fourth paragraph of Article 2180 (*directores de un establecimiento o empresa* in the Spanish Civil Code) is used in the sense of **"employers"** and **does not include the manager or officer of a corporation** who is himself merely an employee of the owner-corporation.[^67] [^68] For the same reason, in [*Spouses Jayme v. Apostol*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2008/nov2008/gr_163609_2008.html) (2008), the Supreme Court held that a municipal mayor who was riding as a passenger in a vehicle driven by a driver employed by the Municipality of Koronadal could not be held vicariously liable under Article 2180 because the municipality (not the mayor personally) was the driver's employer and a fellow employee or official supervisor who merely occupies the vehicle is not an employer under Article 2180.[^69]

### Prerequisite Elements and Burden of Proof

Except where the registered-owner rule creates a disputable presumption in motor vehicle cases, the plaintiff who sues an employer under Article 2180 bears the initial burden of proving three foundational facts under the principle *ei incumbit probatio qui dicit, non qui negat* ("he who asserts, not he who denies, must prove"):[^70] [^71]

1. **Actionable negligence of the employee and proximate causation.** Because the wrongful or negligent act of the employee creates the *vinculum juris*, the presumption of employer negligence under Article 2180 never arises if the plaintiff fails to prove that the employee was negligent and that such negligence was the proximate cause of the injury.[^72] [^73]
2. **Existence of an employer-employee relationship.** The plaintiff must establish that the tortfeasor was chosen or employed by the defendant and subject to the defendant's power of control.[^74] In [*Filamer Christian Institute v. Intermediate Appellate Court*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1992/aug1992/gr_75112_1992.html) (1992), the Supreme Court held on reconsideration that an implementing rule of the Labor Code excluding working scholars from the labor-standards definition of employees cannot be used as a shield to evade primary civil liability under the substantive provisions of Article 2180 of the Civil Code and held the school solidarily liable where a working scholar who swept school corridors in exchange for free tuition drove the school service jeep for the benefit of the school.[^75] Likewise, in [*Carticiano v. Nuval*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2000/sep2000/gr_138054_2000.html) (2000), the Supreme Court rejected the vehicle owner's uncorroborated claim that he had already fired his driver before the collision, noting that this claim was inconsistent with his simultaneous defense of due diligence in selection and supervision and was contradicted by the fact that the car keys were left accessible to the driver.[^76]
3. **Performance within the scope of assigned tasks.** An act is deemed within the scope of assigned tasks if it is done by an employee in furtherance of the interests of the employer or for the account of the employer at the time of the infliction of the injury.[^77] Thus, in [*Castilex Industrial Corp. v. Vasquez, Jr.*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1999/dec1999/gr_132266_1999.html) (1999), the Supreme Court absolved the corporate employer where its manager used a company-assigned pick-up truck at 2:00 a.m. outside office hours at a restaurant for personal socializing unrelated to the company's furniture business.[^78] Similarly, in [*Greenstar Express, Inc. v. Universal Robina Corp.*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2016/oct2016/gr_205090_2016.html) (2016), the Supreme Court absolved the registered owner and employer where its operations manager drove a company van not assigned to him on a declared national holiday while traveling home to his family in Quezon province outside his assigned plant in Cavite.[^79]

### Independent Contractors, Security Agencies, Principals, Pilots, Hospitals and Banks

Because vicarious liability under the fourth and fifth paragraphs of Article 2180 presupposes an employer-employee relationship and the power of control, the Supreme Court has established specific rules for recurring multiparty and institutional settings:

- **Independent contractors vs. labor-only contractors.** A client or general contractor is not vicariously liable under Article 2180 for the negligence of an independent contractor or the latter's employees when no employer-employee relationship exists between them.[^80] [^81] Conversely, in [*National Power Corp. v. Court of Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1998/aug1998/gr_119121_1998.html) (1998), the Supreme Court held that where the arrangement between a principal (NPC) and a nominal contractor (PHESCO) is **labor-only contracting**, an employer-employee relationship is created by operation of law between the principal employer and the workers supplied by the labor-only contractor, making the principal directly, primarily and solidarily liable under Article 2180 for damages caused to third persons by those workers.[^82]
- **Security agencies and their clients.** Where a security agency recruits, hires and assigns security guards to a client under a service contract, the **security agency (not the client) is the employer** of the guards.[^83] [^84] [^85] [^86] However, in [*Roque, Jr. v. Torres*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2006/dec2006/gr_157632_2006.html) (2006), the Supreme Court held that while a property claimant who hired security guards through an agency is not the guards' employer under Article 2180, he may still be held **directly liable under Article 2176** when he acted in bad faith by posting armed guards on land he knew was titled in another's name to forcibly bar the true owner's administrator, thereby paving the way for the shooting.[^87]
- **Principal-agent relationship vs. employer-employee relationship.** In [*Spouses Viloria v. Continental Airlines, Inc.*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2012/jan2012/gr_188288_2012.html) (2012), the Supreme Court held that Article 2180 does not make a principal vicariously liable for a quasi-delict committed by the employee of its independent travel agent because there is no *vinculum juris* or power of control between the principal airline and the travel agency's employee, although the principal is bound under the law on agency when its agent commits fraud or misrepresentation within the scope of authority.[^88]
- **Compulsory pilotage and pilot associations.** In [*Far Eastern Shipping Co. v. Court of Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1998/oct1998/gr_130068_1998.html) (1998), the Supreme Court held that no employer-employee relationship exists between a pilots' association (Manila Pilots' Association) and its member harbor pilots, rendering Article 2180 inapplicable to the association, although the association was held solidarily liable with the negligent pilot and vessel owner by force of Customs Administrative Order No. 15-65 and its corporate by-laws.[^89]
- **Hospitals, visiting consultants, resident physicians and nurses.** In its 2002 resolution on reconsideration in [*Ramos v. Court of Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2002/apr2002/gr_124354_2002.html), the Supreme Court held that private visiting consultants accredited by a hospital are not employees of the hospital under the control test and therefore absolved the hospital of vicarious liability under Article 2180.[^90] In [*Professional Services, Inc. v. Court of Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2008/feb2008/gr_126297_2008.html) (2008) and the subsequent *En Banc* resolution in [*Professional Services, Inc. v. Court of Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2010/feb2010/gr_126297_2010.html) (2010), the Supreme Court clarified the three distinct bases of hospital liability: (1) vicarious liability as an employer under **Article 2180** when an employment relationship with the physician or nurse is established under the control test, (2) vicarious liability as a principal under **ostensible agency (apparent authority)** pursuant to Articles 1431 and 1869 when the hospital holds out an independent-contractor consultant as its agent and the patient relies on that manifestation and (3) direct institutional liability for **corporate negligence** under Article 2176 when the hospital breaches its own non-delegable duty to monitor operations and review surgical discrepancies (such as missing surgical gauzes).[^91] [^92] [^93] By contrast, for **regular hospital nurses**, the Supreme Court held in [*Our Lady of Lourdes Hospital v. Spouses Capanzana*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2017/mar2017/gr_189218_2017.html) (2017) that nurses are employees of the hospital, making the hospital directly and solidarily liable under Article 2180 when its nurses negligently delayed administering oxygen to a post-operation patient and the hospital failed to prove actual floor supervision.[^94]
- **Banking institutions.** Because the banking industry is impressed with public interest and rests on a fiduciary relationship with depositors, the Supreme Court has held in [*Producers Bank of the Philippines v. Court of Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2003/feb2003/gr_115324_2003.html) (2003), [*Philippine Savings Bank v. Chowking Food Corp.*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2008/jul2008/gr_177526_2008.html) (2008), [*Oliver v. Philippine Savings Bank*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2016/apr2016/gr_214567_2016.html) (2016) and [*Gumabon v. Philippine National Bank*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2016/jul2016/gr_202514_2016.html) (2016) that banks are held to the **highest degree of diligence** (higher than that of a good father of a family) in selecting and supervising their branch managers and employees under Article 2180.[^95] [^96] [^97] [^98]

## Motor Vehicle Mishaps: The Registered-Owner Rule and Article 2184

### The Registered-Owner Rule as a Legal Fiction and Disputable Presumption

In motor vehicle quasi-delict litigation, the Supreme Court has read Article 2180 together with **Section 5 of Republic Act No. 4136** (the Land Transportation and Traffic Code)[^ra-4136-1964] under the **registered-owner rule**.[^99] Because the primary aim of motor vehicle registration is to identify the owner immediately so that if any injury or damage is caused by the vehicle on public highways, responsibility can be fixed on a definite individual, the registered owner is considered in contemplation of law to be the **employer of the driver** (with the actual operator or lessee treated merely as the registered owner's agent) and is **directly and primarily liable** to injured third persons under Articles 2176 and 2180.[^100] [^101] [^102] [^103] [^104]

In [*Caravan Travel and Tours International, Inc. v. Abejar*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2016/feb2016/gr_170631_2016.html) (2016), [*Greenstar Express, Inc. v. Universal Robina Corp.*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2016/oct2016/gr_205090_2016.html) (2016), [*De Belen v. Fuchs*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2023/oct2023/gr_258557_2023.html) (2023) and [*Laza v. Standard Insurance Co., Inc.*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2026/jun2026/gr_279772_2026.html) (2026), the Supreme Court harmonized the registered-owner rule with the fifth paragraph of Article 2180 so that neither rule is discarded.[^105] [^106] [^107] [^108] Under this harmonized framework:

1. **Plaintiff's initial burden.** In cases where both the registered-owner rule and Article 2180 apply, the injured plaintiff need only prove that the defendant is the **registered owner** of the vehicle involved in the mishap (for example, by presenting the Certificate of Registration or an admission of registration) and that the driver was negligent.[^109]
2. **Shift of the burden of evidence and the three defenses.** Once registration is proven, a **disputable presumption** arises that the driver was the registered owner's employee acting within the scope of his or her assigned tasks and the burden of evidence shifts to the registered owner to establish that it incurred no liability under Article 2180 by proving any of the following three defenses: (a) that it had **no employment relationship** with the driver (coupled with proof that the vehicle was stolen or operated without authorization), (b) that the driver **acted outside the scope of his or her assigned tasks**, or (c) that the owner **exercised the diligence of a good father of a family** in the selection and supervision of the driver.[^110]

### Solidary Liability of Both Registered Owner and Actual Operator and Right of Reimbursement

When the registered owner of the offending motor vehicle is different from the actual owner or operator who employs the driver, **both the registered owner and the actual operator/employer may be held jointly and severally (solidarily) liable** with the negligent driver toward the injured third party.[^111] [^112] To prevent unjust enrichment, the registered owner who is compelled to pay damages to the victim has a **right to full reimbursement** from the actual owner, transferee or employer of the driver, which may be enforced by way of a **cross-claim, third-party complaint or independent action**.[^113] [^114] [^115]

### Distinction Between Article 2180 and Article 2184

[Article 2184](art-2184.md) of the Civil Code establishes a critical bifurcation in motor vehicle mishaps depending on whether the owner was physically riding inside the vehicle at the time of the accident:[^civil-code]

- **Owner is NOT in the motor vehicle (Article 2180 governs).** By the express command of the second paragraph of Article 2184, if the owner was **not** in the motor vehicle at the time of the mishap, the provisions of **Article 2180** are applicable.[^civil-code] In that situation, once the driver's negligence and the employment or registration are established, the owner is immediately presumed negligent in the selection and supervision of the driver.[^116] [^117]
- **Owner IS in the motor vehicle (Article 2184 governs).** If the owner **was** in the motor vehicle at the time of the mishap, the first paragraph of **Article 2184** governs and the owner is solidarily liable with the driver if the owner could have, by the use of due diligence, prevented the misfortune.[^civil-code] Thus, in [*De Belen v. Fuchs*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2023/oct2023/gr_258557_2023.html) (2023), where the jeepney owner was riding in the front seat beside his employee-driver when the driver recklessly overtook another vehicle on a curved highway and killed a motorist, the Supreme Court clarified that the owner's solidary liability rested on **Article 2184** rather than Article 2180.[^118]

## The State and Government Instrumentalities (Paragraph 6)

Under the sixth paragraph of Article 2180, the State is responsible in like manner when it acts through a **special agent**, but not when the damage has been caused by the official to whom the task done properly pertains, in which case what is provided in Article 2176 shall be applicable.[^civil-code]

### Governmental vs. Proprietary Functions and the Meaning of "Special Agent"

In [*Spouses Fontanilla v. Maliaman*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1989/dec1989/gr_55963_1989.html) (1989), [*National Irrigation Administration v. Intermediate Appellate Court*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1992/sep1992/gr_73919_1992.html) (1992) and [*Bank of the Philippine Islands v. Central Bank of the Philippines*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2020/oct2020/gr_197593_2020.html) (2020), the Supreme Court held that the test of the State's vicarious liability depends on whether the government entity and its employees were performing **governmental (sovereign)** functions or **proprietary (corporate/business)** functions:[^119] [^120]

1. **Governmental functions and the "special agent" rule (Paragraph 6).** When the State or a government instrumentality performs a **governmental function** (such as the Central Bank operating clearing house facilities for member banks as part of its monetary authority in [*Bank of the Philippine Islands v. Central Bank of the Philippines*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2020/oct2020/gr_197593_2020.html) (2020)), it is liable only when it acts through a **special agent**.[^121] A **special agent** is defined as a public official who receives a **definite and fixed order or commission, foreign to the exercise of the regular duties of his or her office**, or a private individual specially commissioned by the government to perform a special governmental task.[^122] When the damage is caused by a regular official or employee performing the tasks that naturally pertain to his or her office, the State is **not** vicariously liable under paragraph 6 of Article 2180 and only the erring official personally is liable under Article 2176.[^123]
2. **Proprietary functions of government corporations (Paragraph 5).** When a government agency or government-owned or controlled corporation has a separate corporate personality and performs **proprietary functions** (such as the National Irrigation Administration constructing and operating irrigation systems for a fee in [*Spouses Fontanilla v. Maliaman*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1989/dec1989/gr_55963_1989.html) (1989) and [*National Irrigation Administration v. Intermediate Appellate Court*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1992/sep1992/gr_73919_1992.html) (1992)), it stands on the same footing as an **ordinary private employer** under the fifth paragraph of Article 2180 and is directly and solidarily liable for the torts of its regular employees unless it proves the diligence of a good father of a family.[^124] [^125]

## Teachers and Heads of Establishments of Arts and Trades (Paragraph 7)

Under the seventh paragraph of Article 2180, **teachers or heads of establishments of arts and trades** shall be liable for damages caused by their pupils and students or apprentices, so long as they remain in their custody.[^civil-code]

### Doctrinal Evolution and the *Reddendo Singula Singulis* Rule

The construction of the seventh paragraph of Article 2180 evolved through three landmark decisions of the Supreme Court before being supplemented by the Family Code:

1. **[*Mercado v. Court of Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1960/may1960/gr_l-14342_1960.html) (1960) and *Exconde v. Capuno* (as cited in [*Mercado*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1960/may1960/gr_l-14342_1960.html)).** In [*Mercado*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1960/may1960/gr_l-14342_1960.html), where a nine-year-old grade-school pupil at Lourdes Catholic School cut a classmate with a razor blade during recess and the culprit's father argued that liability should pass to the school, the Supreme Court (citing *Exconde v. Capuno*, as cited in [*Mercado*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1960/may1960/gr_l-14342_1960.html) and [*Spouses Palisoc v. Brillantes*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1971/oct1971/gr_29025_1971.html)) stated in *obiter dictum* that the seventh paragraph of Article 2180 applied only to an institution of arts and trades and not to an academic educational institution and that the clause "so long as they remain in their custody" contemplated a situation where the pupil **lives and boards** with the teacher.[^126]
2. **[*Spouses Palisoc v. Brillantes*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1971/oct1971/gr_29025_1971.html) (1971).** In [*Palisoc*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1971/oct1971/gr_29025_1971.html), where a sixteen-year-old student at the Manila Technical Institute (a technical and vocational school) was killed by fist blows inflicted in the laboratory room during recess by a classmate who was already of legal age, the Supreme Court **expressly set aside the *Mercado* and *Exconde* boarding dictum** and held that the phrase "so long as they remain in their custody" means the **protective and supervisory custody that the school and its heads and teachers exercise over the pupils and students for as long as they are at attendance in the school, including recess time**, without any requirement that the pupil live and board in the school.[^127]
3. **[*Amadora v. Court of Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1988/apr1988/gr_47745_1988.html) (1988).** In the landmark *En Banc* decision in [*Amadora*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1988/apr1988/gr_47745_1988.html), where a graduating high school student at Colegio de San Jose-Recoletos (an academic school) was fatally shot by a classmate in the school auditorium three days after the semester ended while he was on campus to submit a physics report, the Supreme Court harmonized the seventh paragraph of Article 2180 by applying the canon of ***reddendo singula singulis***:[^128]
  - The provision applies to **all educational institutions, academic as well as non-academic (schools of arts and trades)**.[^129]
  - Where the school is **academic**, responsibility for the tort committed by a **pupil or student** attaches to the **teacher** (specifically the **teacher-in-charge** designated to exercise supervision over the specific class or section) and not to the head of the academic school.[^130]
  - Where the school is an **establishment of arts and trades** (technical or vocational school), responsibility for the tort committed by an **apprentice** attaches to the **head** of the establishment.[^131]
  - Under the seventh paragraph of Article 2180 of the Civil Code (unlike parental liability under the second paragraph or special parental authority under the Family Code), the teacher or head of the school of arts and trades is answerable **regardless of the age of the offending student or apprentice**, even if the student is already of legal age.[^132] [^133]

### Meaning of "Custody," Authorized Activities, Outsiders and Religious Catechists

Under [*Amadora*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1988/apr1988/gr_47745_1988.html) (1988) and [*Apolinario v. Heirs of Francisco de los Santos*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2024/nov2024/gr_219686_2024.html) (2024), a student is deemed in the **custody** of the school authorities for as long as the student is under the control and influence of the school and within its premises in pursuance of a legitimate student objective, right or privilege, whether the semester has not yet begun or has already ended and even if the teacher-in-charge is not physically present at the exact moment of the injury.[^134] [^135]

Subsequent decisions have further defined the limits of school and teacher responsibility under Article 2180:

- **Student acting under military ROTC orders rather than academic attendance.** In [*Salvosa v. Intermediate Appellate Court*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1988/oct1988/gr_70458_1988.html) (1988), where an ROTC student who was employed by the Armed Forces of the Philippines as a military armorer under the direct orders of the AFP ROTC Commandant left the armory inside the Baguio Colleges Foundation campus and shot a student in the parking lot after dismissal, the Supreme Court reversed the appellate court and absolved the college and its president under Article 2180 because mere enrollment or presence on campus without being "at attendance in the school" does not constitute school custody.[^136]
- **Unsanctioned off-campus class picnics.** In [*St. Francis High School v. Court of Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/feb1991/gr_82465_1991.html) (1991), where a thirteen-year-old student drowned at Talaan Beach during a purely private class picnic that was not sanctioned by the school and was not an extracurricular activity, the Supreme Court reversed the appellate court and absolved the school, the principal and the teachers, holding that mere knowledge by the principal of the planned picnic did not constitute school acquiescence under the fourth or fifth paragraph of Article 2180 and that the teachers who attended had exercised due diligence.[^137]
- **Torts committed by outsiders.** In [*Philippine School of Business Administration v. Court of Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1992/feb1992/gr_84698_1992.html) (1992), where a student was stabbed to death on the second floor of the PSBA campus by assailants who were **outsiders and not students of the school**, the Supreme Court held that Article 2180 did not apply because the seventh paragraph plainly requires that the damage be inflicted by pupils or students of the educational institution, although the school could still be held liable for breach of its implied academic contract to provide a safe learning environment.[^138]
- **Outside catechists designated by a religious congregation.** In [*Aquinas School v. Spouses Inton*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2011/jan2011/gr_184202_2011.html) (2011), where a catechist sent by a religious congregation slapped and kicked a grade-three pupil, the Supreme Court absolved Aquinas School under Article 2180 because the catechist was chosen and controlled by her religious congregation rather than employed by the school.[^139]
- **Special parental authority under Articles 218 and 219 of the Family Code and Republic Act No. 11053.** With respect to **unemancipated minor** students, Articles 218 and 219 of the Family Code confer special parental authority and responsibility upon the school, its administrators and teachers while the minor is under their supervision, instruction or custody (including authorized activities whether inside or outside the premises of the school) and make them **principally and solidarily liable** for damages caused by the acts or omissions of the unemancipated minor, while the parents or guardians are made subsidiarily liable.[^family-code] [^140] Furthermore, in [*University of Southeastern Philippines v.Hidalgo*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2025/nov2025/gr_247617_2025.html) (2025), where an engineering student died during an off-campus fraternity hazing initiation, the Supreme Court held the state university and its officials solidarily liable for quasi-delict under Articles 2176 and 2180 for failing to exercise the diligence of a good father of a family in implementing anti-hazing regulations and monitoring student organizations.[^141]

## Defense of Diligence of a Good Father of a Family (Paragraph 8)

### The *Juris Tantum* Presumption and Conjunctive Burden of Proof

Under the final paragraph of Article 2180, the responsibility treated of in the article shall cease when the persons therein mentioned prove that they observed **all the diligence of a good father of a family** (*diligentissimi patris familias*) to prevent the damage.[^civil-code] Whenever an employee's negligence causes damage to another while performing assigned duties, a disputable (*juris tantum*) presumption of law instantly arises that the employer was negligent either in the **selection** of the employee (*culpa in eligendo*) or in the **supervision** over the employee after selection (*culpa in vigilando*), or both.[^142] [^143] [^144] Moreover, because the employer's negligence is presumed by operation of law once the employee's negligence is alleged and proven, the Supreme Court held in [*Viron Transportation Co., Inc. v. Delos Santos*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2000/nov2000/gr_138296_2000.html) (2000) that the injured claimant's complaint or counterclaim need not even expressly allege that the employer failed to exercise due diligence in selection and supervision.[^145]

To overcome this presumption, the employer must prove **both** due diligence in selection **and** due diligence in supervision conjunctively. Proving careful selection without proving continuous supervision (or vice versa) is insufficient to exonerate the employer.[^146] [^147] [^148]

### Concrete Evidentiary Requirements for Selection and Supervision

In [*Metro Manila Transit Corp. v. Court of Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1993/jun1993/gr_104408_1993.html) (1993) and [*Metro Manila Transit Corp. v. Court of Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1998/nov1998/gr_116617_1998.html) (1998), the Supreme Court established the strict evidentiary rule that **bare oral testimony is insufficient**. To fend off vicarious liability under Article 2180, the employer must submit **concrete proof, including object and documentary evidence**, to obviate the biased nature of employer and witness testimony:[^149] [^150] [^151]

1. **Diligence in selection (*culpa in eligendo*).** The employer cannot be satisfied with the applicant's mere possession of a professional driver's license, nor can the employer delegate vetting to a spouse who conducts a single informal test drive.[^152] [^153] Instead, the employer must carefully examine the applicant's qualifications, work experience, driving history and service records, require NBI, police and barangay clearances and administer written, practical, medical, psychological and drug examinations, backed by the actual documentary records.[^154] [^155] [^156] [^157]
2. **Diligence in supervision (*culpa in vigilando*).** Supervision requires formulating standard operating procedures and safety rules, actively monitoring consistent compliance through dependable field supervisors, inspectors or speed-monitoring devices and imposing disciplinary sanctions for infractions.[^158] [^159] [^160] By contrast, in [*Reyes v. Doctolero*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2017/aug2017/gr_185597_2017.html) (2017), the Supreme Court **exonerated** the security agency (Grandeur Security and Services Corporation) under the last paragraph of Article 2180 because the agency corroborated its Human Resources head's testimony with extensive **documentary evidence** (including government clearances, medical and psychiatric test results, training certificates, operational manuals and regular on-site inspection reports) proving both careful selection and diligent supervision.[^161]
3. **Heightened diligence for gun stores and dangerous weapons.** In [*Pacis v. Morales*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2010/feb2010/gr_169467_2010.html) (2010), where a gun store owner kept a customer's loaded defective pistol inside a table drawer whose key was left accessible to untrained sales employees who accidentally shot a seventeen-year-old student to death, the Supreme Court held that a **higher degree of care** is required of someone dealing with dangerous weapons and held the gun shop owner liable under Articles 2176 and 2180.[^162]

## Related Provisions

- **[Article 2176](art-2176.md)** defines the primary obligation arising from a quasi-delict (*culpa aquiliana*), which Article 2180 extends to persons for whom one is vicariously responsible.[^civil-code]
- **[Article 2177](art-2177.md)** establishes that civil responsibility for a quasi-delict under Articles 2176 and 2180 is entirely separate and distinct from the civil liability arising from negligence under the Revised Penal Code, subject to the bar against recovering damages twice for the same negligent act or omission.[^civil-code]
- **[Article 2179](art-2179.md)** bars recovery when the plaintiff's own negligence was the immediate and proximate cause of the injury and mitigates damages when the plaintiff's negligence was only contributory.[^civil-code]
- **[Article 2181](art-2181.md)** grants any person who pays for the damage caused by his or her dependents or employees under Article 2180 the right to recover from the latter what he or she has paid or delivered.[^civil-code]
- **[Article 2182](art-2182.md)** provides that if the minor or insane person causing damage has no parents or guardian, the minor or insane person shall be answerable with his or her own property in an action against a guardian *ad litem*.[^civil-code]
- **[Article 2184](art-2184.md)** governs motor vehicle mishaps when the owner is inside the vehicle at the time of the accident, while expressly directing that Article 2180 applies if the owner was not in the motor vehicle.[^civil-code]
- **[Article 2185](art-2185.md)** creates a disputable presumption that a person driving a motor vehicle was negligent if at the time of the mishap he or she was violating any traffic regulation.[^civil-code]
- **[Article 2194](art-2194.md)** declares that the responsibility of two or more persons who are liable for a quasi-delict is solidary, rendering the person vicariously liable under Article 2180 and the actual tortfeasor solidary debtors.[^civil-code]
- **Articles 216, 218, 219, 221 and 236 of the Family Code (Executive Order No. 209, as amended by Republic Act No. 6809)** modify the second and seventh paragraphs of Article 2180 with respect to unemancipated minors by making both parents jointly and primarily liable for the acts or omissions of unemancipated children living in their company and under their parental authority and by vesting special parental authority in schools, administrators and teachers (making them principally and solidarily liable and the parents subsidiarily liable while the minor is under school supervision, instruction or custody).[^family-code]
- **Articles 100, 101, 102 and 103 of the Revised Penal Code (Act No. 3815)** govern civil liability *ex delicto*, including the direct liability of parents or guardians for acts of insane persons or minors acting without discernment under Article 101 and the subsidiary civil liability of employers engaged in industry for felonies committed by insolvent employees in the discharge of their duties under Article 103.[^revised-penal-code]
- **Section 5 of the Land Transportation and Traffic Code (Republic Act No. 4136)** requires the compulsory registration of motor vehicles, which forms the statutory foundation for the registered-owner rule integrated by jurisprudence into Article 2180.[^ra-4136-1964]

## Key Cases

- **[*Laza v. Standard Insurance Co., Inc.*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70498)** (G.R. No. 279772, June 29, 2026) — Affirmed the subrogation award in favor of the insurer against the negligent driver under Article 2176 and against the registered owner of the Toyota Innova under Article 2180 after the driver violated traffic rules and the registered owner failed to rebut the presumption of employer negligence.[^163]
- **[*Central Azucarera De San Antonio, Inc. v. CASA Sugar Mill Workers Union-FFW*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70125)** (G.R. No. 259683, January 28, 2026) — Held that while Article 2180 establishes vicarious liability for quasi-delicts under Article 2176, Philippine labor and civil law reject collective or vicarious union liability for illegal strike acts governed by Articles 19 and 20, limiting liability to union officers or members who knowingly participated in the illegal acts.[^164]
- **[*Soriano v. Intestate Estate of Bryan G. Diano*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70091)** (G.R. No. 277496, January 20, 2026) — Held the owner-operator of an Isuzu passenger vehicle and the registered owner of a colliding Honda Civic solidarily liable under Articles 2180 and 2194 for the concurrent negligence of their drivers, while affirming the registered owner's cross-claim for full reimbursement against the actual owner of the Honda Civic.[^165]
- **[*Mother Goose Special School System, Inc. v. Spouses Palaganas*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69882)** (G.R. No. 267331, January 20, 2025) — Held that a school's failure to protect a pupil from a classroom punching incident and its mishandling of the incident breached its pre-existing academic contract (*culpa contractual*), so the school could not invoke the defense of due diligence in the selection and supervision of employees under Article 2180.[^166]
- **[*Apolinario v. Heirs of Francisco de los Santos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69815)** (G.R. No. 219686, November 27, 2024) — Held a school principal primarily and vicariously liable as the teacher-in-charge under Article 2180 of the Civil Code and Articles 218 and 219 of the Family Code after he directed a minor pupil during a school *pintakasi* to cut down a banana plant beside the highway without early warning devices or adult assistance, resulting in the death of a passing motorcyclist.[^167]
- **[*De Belen v. Fuchs*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69244)** (G.R. No. 258557, October 23, 2023) — Synthesized the distinctions among Article 2180, the registered-owner rule, Article 103 of the Revised Penal Code and Article 2184 and held that because the employer-owner was riding inside the passenger jeepney with his employee-driver at the time of the fatal collision, his solidary liability was governed by Article 2184 rather than Article 2180.[^168]
- **[*Bank of the Philippine Islands v. Central Bank of the Philippines*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2020/oct2020/gr_197593_2020.html)** (G.R. No. 197593, October 12, 2020) — Absolved the Central Bank of vicarious liability under paragraphs 5 and 6 of Article 2180 for the pilferage and check-clearing fraud committed by its bookkeeper and janitor-messenger, holding that operating clearing house facilities is a governmental function and that regular civil service employees performing tasks pertaining to their offices are not "special agents."[^169]
- **[*Reyes v. Doctolero*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2017/aug2017/gr_185597_2017.html)** (G.R. No. 185597, August 2, 2017) — Absolved the client mall owner (Makati Cinema Square) for lack of an employer-employee relationship with the security guards and exonerated the security agency (Grandeur Security and Services Corporation) under the last paragraph of Article 2180 after it presented comprehensive documentary proof of clearances, psychological and medical evaluations, training certificates and field supervision.[^170]
- **[*Caravan Travel and Tours International, Inc. v. Abejar*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2016/feb2016/gr_170631_2016.html)** (G.R. No. 170631, February 10, 2016) — Harmonized the registered-owner rule with Article 2180 by ruling that once the plaintiff proves that the defendant is the registered owner of the offending vehicle, a disputable presumption arises that the requirements of Article 2180 are satisfied, shifting the burden of evidence to the registered owner to prove absence of employment, acting outside assigned tasks, or due diligence in selection and supervision.[^171]
- **[*Professional Services, Inc. v. Court of Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2010/feb2010/gr_126297_2010.html)** (G.R. No. 126297, February 2, 2010, *En Banc*) — Clarified that while a hospital is not the employer of an independent-contractor visiting consultant under Article 2180 when control over the details of treatment is absent, the hospital may still be held vicariously liable under ostensible agency (Articles 1431 and 1869) and directly liable for corporate negligence (Article 2176).[^172]
- **[*Cerezo v. Tuazon*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2004/mar2004/gr_141538_2004.html)** (G.R. No. 141538, March 23, 2004) — Held that an employer's liability under Article 2180 in relation to Article 2194 is direct, primary and solidary, so the negligent driver is not an indispensable party and the trial court can render a valid judgment against the employer alone even if summons was never served on the driver.[^173]
- **[*Castilex Industrial Corp. v. Vasquez, Jr.*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1999/dec1999/gr_132266_1999.html)** (G.R. No. 132266, December 21, 1999) — Distinguished the fourth and fifth paragraphs of Article 2180 and held that the plaintiff bears the burden of proving that the employee was acting within the scope of his assigned tasks, absolving the employer where its manager drove a company pick-up truck at 2:00 a.m. outside office hours from a restaurant for personal socializing.[^174]
- **[*Spouses Libi v. Intermediate Appellate Court*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1992/sep1992/gr_70890_1992.html)** (G.R. No. 70890, September 18, 1992) — Resolved prior jurisprudential conflicts and ruled that the civil liability of parents for both quasi-delicts and criminal offenses committed by their minor children living in their company is **direct and primary, not subsidiary**, subject to the defense of diligence of a good father of a family.[^175]
- **[*Amadora v. Court of Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1988/apr1988/gr_47745_1988.html)** (G.R. No. L-47745, April 15, 1988, *En Banc*) — Construed the seventh paragraph of Article 2180 *reddendo singula singulis* to hold that it applies to all schools (with teachers-in-charge liable for pupils and students in academic schools and heads liable for apprentices in schools of arts and trades) regardless of the student's age, for as long as the student is in the school's protective and supervisory custody.[^176]
- **[*Philippine Rabbit Bus Lines, Inc. v. Phil-American Forwarders, Inc.*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1975/mar1975/gr_25142_1975.html)** (G.R. No. L-25142, March 25, 1975) — Held that the term "managers" in the fourth paragraph of Article 2180 is used in the sense of "employers" and does not make a corporate manager personally liable for the quasi-delict of a subordinate employee of the corporation.[^177]

## Suggested Answers

**Q1: Hypothetical: A delivery van registered in the name of X Corp. and driven by its employee D ran a red light during a morning delivery route and struck pedestrian P, causing severe fractures. P filed a civil action for damages based on quasi-delict against X Corp. and D. Summons was served on X Corp., but D had gone into hiding and was never served with summons. X Corp. moved to dismiss the complaint on two grounds: (1) D is an indispensable party without whom the trial court cannot proceed and P must first convict D and prove D's insolvency and (2) X Corp.'s human resources manager testified on the witness stand that X Corp. always checks its drivers' licenses and verbally reminds them to drive safely, although X Corp. did not offer D's personnel file or test records in evidence. Rule on X Corp.'s defenses.**
Both defenses of X Corp. are untenable and X Corp. should be held directly and solidarily liable to P. Under Articles 2180 and 2194 of the Civil Code, the liability of an employer for the quasi-delict of its employee acting within the scope of his or her assigned tasks is direct, primary and solidary, not subsidiary.[^civil-code] As held in [*Cerezo v. Tuazon*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2004/mar2004/gr_141538_2004.html) (2004) and [*Caravan Travel and Tours International, Inc. v. Abejar*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2016/feb2016/gr_170631_2016.html) (2016), the negligent driver is not an indispensable party in a quasi-delict action against the employer and enforcement of the employer's primary liability is not conditioned upon a prior criminal conviction or proof of the employee's insolvency.[^178] [^179] Furthermore, under [*Metro Manila Transit Corp. v. Court of Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1998/nov1998/gr_116617_1998.html) (1998) and [*Syki v. Begasa*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2003/oct2003/gr_149149_2003.html) (2003), once the employee's negligence is established, a *juris tantum* presumption arises that the employer was negligent in selecting and supervising the employee, which can be rebutted only by concrete proof (including documentary evidence) of both careful selection and continuous supervision.[^180] [^181] Here, P's action against X Corp. is anchored on quasi-delict under Articles 2176 and 2180 of the Civil Code rather than subsidiary liability under Article 103 of the Revised Penal Code.[^civil-code] [^revised-penal-code] Because X Corp. is a solidary debtor, the non-service of summons on D does not deprive the trial court of jurisdiction to render judgment against X Corp. alone. Moreover, the uncorroborated oral testimony of X Corp.'s manager that the company checks driver's licenses and gives verbal reminders (without producing D's personnel records, clearances, test results or supervision logs) falls short of the concrete documentary proof required to establish the diligence of a good father of a family under the last paragraph of Article 2180.

**Q2: Hypothetical: A service van registered in the name of U Corp. was assigned to its Cavite plant logistics officer. On a declared national holiday when the Cavite plant was closed, B, another employee of U Corp., took the van without authorization to visit his relatives in Quezon province, where U Corp. had no business operations. Along the highway in Quezon, B negligently swerved into the opposite lane and collided with a bus owned by G Inc. G Inc. sued U Corp. for damages under Article 2180, arguing that as the registered owner of the van, U Corp. is conclusively liable and cannot invoke the defense that B was acting outside the scope of his assigned tasks. Is G Inc. correct?**
No, G Inc. is incorrect and U Corp. should be absolved of vicarious liability. Under the fifth paragraph of Article 2180 of the Civil Code, employers are liable for damages caused by their employees acting within the scope of their assigned tasks.[^civil-code] As harmonized by the Supreme Court in [*Caravan Travel and Tours International, Inc. v. Abejar*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2016/feb2016/gr_170631_2016.html) (2016) and [*Greenstar Express, Inc. v. Universal Robina Corp.*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2016/oct2016/gr_205090_2016.html) (2016), when the plaintiff proves that the defendant is the registered owner of the offending vehicle, a disputable presumption arises that the driver was the owner's employee acting within the scope of his assigned tasks.[^182] However, the registered owner may overturn that presumption and avoid liability under Article 2180 by proving any of three defenses: (1) that it had no employment relationship with the driver, (2) that the employee acted outside the scope of his or her assigned tasks or (3) that it exercised the diligence of a good father of a family in selecting and supervising the driver.[^183] [^184] Here, while G Inc.'s proof that U Corp. is the registered owner of the van triggered the disputable presumption under Article 2180, that presumption is *juris tantum*, not conclusive. U Corp. established that the collision occurred on a declared national holiday when there was no work, that the van was not assigned to B and that B was traveling to his family home in Quezon province where U Corp. had no plant or business operations. Because B was not performing any assigned task or acting in furtherance of U Corp.'s business, U Corp. incurred no vicarious liability under Article 2180.

**Q3: Hypothetical: During a school-organized campus clean-up (*pintakasi*) at a public school, Principal A directed R, a fifteen-year-old student, to cut down a tall banana plant standing at the edge of the school perimeter beside a national highway. Principal A neither assigned an adult to assist R nor installed early warning devices along the highway. When R cut the banana plant, it fell onto the highway and struck passing motorcyclist M, causing M's death. M's heirs sued Principal A and R's mother, T (without impleading R), praying that T be held primarily liable as R's parent and that Principal A be absolved. Decide the respective liabilities of Principal A and T.**
Principal A is primarily and solidarily liable for M's death, whereas R's mother, T, is only subsidiarily liable and cannot be held primarily liable. Under the seventh paragraph of Article 2180 of the Civil Code, teachers or heads of establishments of arts and trades are liable for damages caused by their pupils and students or apprentices so long as they remain in their custody, unless they prove that they observed all the diligence of a good father of a family to prevent damage.[^civil-code] Complementing this provision for unemancipated minors, Articles 218 and 219 of the Family Code vest special parental authority in the school, its administrators and teachers while the minor child is under their supervision, instruction or custody and render them **principally and solidarily liable** for damages caused by the acts or omissions of the unemancipated minor, while the parents are made **subsidiarily liable**.[^family-code] As held in [*Apolinario v. Heirs of Francisco de los Santos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69815) (2024) and [*Amadora v. Court of Appeals*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1988/apr1988/gr_47745_1988.html) (1988), when a parent places a minor child under the effective authority and supervision of the school and its teacher-in-charge, the teacher-in-charge (and not the parent) is primarily answerable for the tort committed by the pupil while under school custody.[^185] [^186] Here, the cutting of the banana plant occurred during an official school *pintakasi* while R, a minor pupil, was under the direct custody, instruction and supervision of Principal A. Principal A failed to exercise the diligence of a good father of a family when he ordered a minor student to fell a plant beside a public highway without installing early warning devices or providing adult supervision. Because R was under Principal A's special parental authority and custody at the time of the incident, Principal A is principally and solidarily liable for the damages arising from M's death even though R was not impleaded as a defendant. Conversely, R's mother, T, had no authority to interfere with the school's supervision during the activity and cannot be held primarily liable.

# Quotes

> "Therefore, the appropriate approach is that in cases where both the registered-owner rule and Article 2180 apply, the plaintiff must first establish that the employer is the registered owner of the vehicle in question. Once the plaintiff successfully proves ownership, there arises a disputable presumption that the requirements of Article 2180 have been proven. As a consequence, the burden of proof shifts to the defendant to show that no liability under Article 2180 has arisen."[^187]

> "Moreover, an employer’s liability based on a quasi-delict is primary and direct, while the employer’s liability based on a delict is merely subsidiary.[43] The words “primary and direct,” as contrasted with “subsidiary,” refer to the remedy provided by law for enforcing the obligation rather than to the character and limits of the obligation.[44] Although liability under Article 2180 originates from the negligent act of the employee, the aggrieved party may sue the employer directly. When an employee causes damage, the law presumes that the employer has himself committed an act of negligence in not preventing or avoiding the damage. This is the fault that the law condemns. While the employer is civilly liable in a subsidiary capacity for the employee’s criminal negligence, the employer is also civilly liable directly and separately for his own civil negligence in failing to exercise due diligence in selecting and supervising his employee. The idea that the employer’s liability is solely subsidiary is wrong.[45]"[^188]

> "In the selection of prospective employees, employers are required to examine them as to their qualifications, experience, and service records.[17] On the other hand, with respect to the supervision of employees, employers should formulate standard operating procedures, monitor their implementation, and impose disciplinary measures for breaches thereof.[18] To establish these factors in a trial involving the issue of vicarious liability, employers must submit concrete proof, including documentary evidence.[19]"[^189]

> "Under the foregoing considerations, therefore, we hereby rule that the parents are and should be held primarily liable for the civil liability arising from criminal offenses committed by their minor children under their legal authority or control, or who live in their company, unless it is proven that the former acted with the diligence of a good father of a family to prevent such damages. That primary liability is premised on the provisions of Article 101 of the Revised Penal Code with respect to damages ex delicto caused by their children 9 years of age or under, or over 9 but under 15 years of age who acted without discernment; and, with regard to their children over 9 but under 15 years of age who acted with discernment, or 15 years or over but under 21 years of age, such primary liability shall be imposed pursuant to Article 2180 of the Civil Code.31"[^190]

> "During all these occasions, it is obviously the teacher-in-charge who must answer for his students' torts, in practically the same way that the parents are responsible for the child when he is in their custody. The teacher-in-charge is the one designated by the dean, principal, or other administrative superior to exercise supervision over the pupils in the specific classes or sections to which they are assigned. It is not necessary that at the time of the injury, the teacher be physically present and in a position to prevent it. Custody does not connote immediate and actual physical control but refers more to the influence exerted on the child and the discipline instilled in him as a result of such influence. Thus, for the injuries caused by the student, the teacher and not the parent shag be held responsible if the tort was committed within the premises of the school at any time when its authority could be validly exercised over him."[^191]

> "To reiterate, CBP's establishment of clearing house facilities for its member banks to which Valentino and Estacio were assigned as Bookkeeper and Janitor-Messenger, respectively, is a governmental function. As such, the State or CBP in this case, is liable only for the torts committed by its employee when the latter acts as a special agent but not when the said employee or official performs his or her functions that naturally pertain to his or her office. A special agent is defined as one who receives a definite and fixed order or commission, foreign to the exercise of the duties of his office.[43] Evidently, both Valentino and Estacio are not considered as special agents of CBP during their commission of the fraudulent acts against petitioner BPI as they were regular employees performing tasks pertaining to their offices, namely, bookkeeping and janitorial-messenger. Thus, CBP cannot be held liable for any damage caused to petitioner BPI by reason of Valentino and Estacio's unlawful acts."[^192]

> "We are of the opinion that those terms do not include the manager of a corporation. It may be gathered from the context of article 2180 that the term "manager" ("director" in the Spanish version) is used in the sense of "employer"."[^193]

# Tips

> [!TIP]
> **Quick Diagnostic Checklist for Article 2180 Claims.** Before analyzing the defense of diligence of a good father of a family under the last paragraph of Article 2180, verify four threshold questions in sequence:
> 1. **Is the cause of action extra-contractual?** If the plaintiff is a passenger suing a common carrier, a student suing a school for breach of its academic safety undertaking or a depositor suing a bank on a deposit or mortgage contract, the action is *culpa contractual* and the defense of due diligence in selecting and supervising employees under Article 2180 cannot defeat contractual liability.
> 2. **Was the subordinate actor negligent?** Vicarious liability under Article 2180 is derivative of an underlying tort. If the driver, guard or pupil was not negligent or if the victim's own negligence was the sole proximate cause of the injury, the presumption of negligence under Article 2180 never arises.
> 3. **Does one of the statutory relationships in Paragraphs 2 to 7 exist?** Identify the exact relationship (parent, guardian, owner or manager of an establishment, employer, State acting through a special agent, or teacher and head of an establishment of arts and trades) and verify whether special rules (such as the registered-owner rule, Article 2184 when the owner is inside the vehicle, or Articles 218, 219 and 221 of the Family Code for minors) apply.
> 4. **Did the defendant present concrete documentary proof of both selection and supervision?** Once the presumption of negligence attaches, bare oral testimony of hiring checks and verbal safety reminders is never enough. The defendant must produce documentary records proving both careful selection (*culpa in eligendo*) and continuous supervision (*culpa in vigilando*).

### Pleading and Litigating Direct vs. Subsidiary Liability

> [!NOTE]
> **Choosing Between Article 2180 of the Civil Code and Article 103 of the Revised Penal Code.** When an employee's reckless driving or shooting causes injury or death, the offended party must keep the two statutory tracks distinct:
> - **Independent civil action under Articles 2176, 2180 and 2194 (Civil Code):** Liability is **direct, primary and solidary**. The plaintiff may sue the employer or registered owner directly without impleading or serving summons on the driver, without waiting for the criminal case to finish and without proving the driver's insolvency. Even the driver's death or acquittal on reasonable doubt does not extinguish the civil action. However, the employer may invoke the defense of diligence of a good father of a family in selection and supervision.
> - **Subsidiary civil liability under Article 103 (Revised Penal Code):** Liability is **subsidiary** and can be enforced only inside the criminal case upon execution after (1) the employer is shown to be engaged in an industry, (2) the employee is convicted by final judgment of a felony committed in the discharge of duties and (3) the employee is proven insolvent by a return of *nulla bona*. In this proceeding, the criminal conviction is conclusive upon the employer (absent collusion) and the defense of due diligence in selection and supervision is **not available**. Conversely, a trial court cannot hold an employer solidarily liable under Article 2180 inside a criminal prosecution where the employer is not a party.

### Motor Vehicle Mishaps and Triangular Relationships

> [!TIP]
> **Spotting the Registered-Owner Rule, Article 2184 and Independent-Contractor Defenses.** Bar and litigation problems frequently test who bears liability when multiple parties are connected to a motor vehicle or workplace:
> - **Registered owner vs. actual employer:** Proving that the defendant is the registered owner of the offending motor vehicle raises a disputable presumption that the driver was the owner's employee acting within the scope of assigned tasks. To overcome that presumption, the registered owner must prove either (1) absence of an employment relationship coupled with unauthorized taking, (2) that the employee was on a personal frolic outside assigned tasks (such as driving after office hours for personal socializing or on a non-working holiday in another province), or (3) due diligence in selection and supervision. When the registered owner is different from the actual operator or buyer, **both** are solidarily liable to the injured third party and the registered owner may recover full reimbursement from the actual operator via a **cross-claim, third-party complaint or separate action**.
> - **Owner absent vs. owner present in the vehicle:** If the owner is **not** inside the vehicle at the time of the mishap, **Article 2180** applies. If the owner **is** riding inside the vehicle, **Article 2184** governs and holds the owner solidarily liable only if the owner could have prevented the misfortune by the use of due diligence.
> - **Corporate managers, mayors, security agencies and hospitals:** The word "managers" in Paragraph 4 of Article 2180 means **employers**, so a corporate manager, dispatcher or municipal mayor riding as a passenger is not personally liable for a subordinate driver's negligence. Likewise, a client mall, school or landowner is not the employer of guards assigned by an independent security agency and a hospital is not the employer of accredited visiting consultants under the control test (unless ostensible agency under Articles 1431 and 1869 or corporate negligence under Article 2176 is proven), although a hospital is the direct employer of its regular staff nurses under Article 2180.

### Minors, Schools and the Family Code Overlay

> [!NOTE]
> **Reconciling Paragraphs 2 and 7 of Article 2180 with Articles 218, 219 and 221 of the Family Code.** Always check the **age** of the student or child and **who had custody** at the time of the tort:
> - **Minors living in the company of their parents:** Both the father and the mother are **jointly, primarily and directly liable** (not merely subsidiarily or alternately liable) under Article 2180 of the Civil Code and Article 221 of the Family Code for both **quasi-delicts and intentional crimes** committed by their unemancipated minor children living in their company. Actual physical custody at the time of the tort is indispensable and an adoption decree cannot be given retroactive effect to shift tort liability to adopting parents who did not yet have physical custody of the child.
> - **Minors under school supervision, instruction or custody:** Under Articles 218 and 219 of the Family Code, the school, its administrators and teachers exercise special parental authority over unemancipated minors during attendance on campus and during authorized activities inside or outside school premises, rendering them **principally and solidarily liable** while the parents or guardians are only **subsidiarily liable**.
> - **Students of legal age in school custody:** Because the Family Code applies only to unemancipated minors, torts committed by students or apprentices who have already reached the age of majority remain governed by the **seventh paragraph of Article 2180 of the Civil Code**, which applies regardless of the student's age and holds the **teacher-in-charge** liable in academic schools and the **head of the establishment** liable in schools of arts and trades under the *reddendo singula singulis* rule. Neither provision applies when the injury is inflicted by **outsiders** who are not students of the school, although the school may still be sued for breach of its academic contract.
