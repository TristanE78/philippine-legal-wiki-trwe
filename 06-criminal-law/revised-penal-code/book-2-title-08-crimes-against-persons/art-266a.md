# Explanation

## Statutory Evolution and Reclassification as a Crime Against Persons

Article 266-A of the Revised Penal Code was enacted by Republic Act No. 8353, also known as the Anti-Rape Law of 1997, which took effect on October 22, 1997.[^2] Prior to Republic Act No. 8353, the crime of rape was defined and penalized under Article 335 of the Revised Penal Code (as amended by Republic Act No. 2632, Republic Act No. 4111 and Republic Act No. 7659) within Title XI on Crimes Against Chastity.[^3] Republic Act No. 8353 repealed Article 335, transferred rape to Title VIII of Book Two on Crimes Against Persons as Chapter Three (Articles 266-A, 266-B, 266-C and 266-D) and expanded the definition of rape beyond traditional penile-vaginal intercourse by a man against a woman.[^4]

On March 4, 2022, Republic Act No. 11648 was enacted and took effect on March 22, 2022, further amending the introductory clause of paragraph 1 and subparagraph 1(d) of Article 266-A.[^5] Republic Act No. 11648 replaced the gender-specific words "a man who shall have carnal knowledge of a woman" in the lead-in of paragraph 1 with "a person who shall have carnal knowledge of another person" and raised the statutory rape age threshold in paragraph 1(d) from under 12 years of age to under 16 years of age, subject to a close-in-age proviso.[^6] Under Article 22 of the Revised Penal Code, penal laws operate prospectively unless they are favorable to the accused, so the higher statutory age threshold of under 16 years old under Republic Act No. 11648 applies only to acts committed on or after its effectivity on March 22, 2022, whereas acts committed prior to that date remain governed by the under-12 age threshold of Republic Act No. 8353, as the Supreme Court held in [*People v. XXX*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69802)[^7] and [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314)[^8].

The reclassification of rape from a crime against chastity to a crime against persons produced three major legal consequences:

1. **Public Offense Not Extinguished by Desistance or Post-Institution Pardon.** Because rape is now a crime against persons and a public offense, it can be prosecuted de oficio and is no longer a private crime.[^9] In [*People v. Bonaagua*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/34342)[^10], the Supreme Court ruled that pardon by the offended party does not extinguish the offender's criminal liability for rape. An affidavit of desistance is not a ground to dismiss criminal actions already instituted because the pardon should have been made before their institution.
2. **Recognition of Gender Autonomy and Marital Rape.** In [*People v. Jumawan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/56922)[^11], the Supreme Court explained that by removing rape from crimes against chastity and classifying it as a crime against persons, Republic Act No. 8353 repudiated patriarchal stereotypes that treated rape merely as an injury to family honor or spousal property and recognized each person's human-rights autonomy over her own body, thereby criminalizing marital rape.
3. **Creation of Dual Modes of Rape.** Republic Act No. 8353 bifurcated rape into two distinct modes: rape by sexual intercourse (carnal knowledge) under paragraph 1 of Article 266-A and rape through sexual assault (instrument or object insertion, or oral or anal penile insertion) under paragraph 2 of Article 266-A.[^12]

## Dual Modes of Commission: Sexual Intercourse vs. Sexual Assault

Article 266-A divides the felony of rape into two substantially distinct modes of commission with different elements and prescribed penalties.[^13] In [*People v. Soriano*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/51351)[^14], [*People v. Pareja*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/56553)[^15], [*People v. Caoili*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63383)[^16] and [*People v. Tulagan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/65020)[^17], the Supreme Court summarized the four structural distinctions between paragraph 1 and paragraph 2 under Republic Act No. 8353:

| Feature | Rape by Sexual Intercourse (Art. 266-A, Par. 1) | Rape Through Sexual Assault (Art. 266-A, Par. 2) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Offender** | Under R.A. No. 8353, always a man ([*People v. Caoili*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63383)[^18]), while under R.A. No. 11648, a person ([*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314)[^19]). | Any person, whether a man or a woman ([*People v. Caoili*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63383)[^20]). |
| **Offended Party** | Under R.A. No. 8353, always a woman ([*People v. Caoili*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63383)[^21]), while under R.A. No. 11648, another person ([*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314)[^22]). | Any person, whether a man or a woman ([*People v. Caoili*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63383)[^23]). |
| **Overt Anatomical Act** | Carnal knowledge, meaning penile penetration of the female vagina or vulval cleft ([*People v. Agao*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68651)[^24]). | Inserting the penis into another person's mouth or anal orifice, or inserting any instrument or object (including a finger or tongue) into the genital or anal orifice of another person ([*People v. Bonaagua*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/34342)[^25]). |
| **Basic Prescribed Penalty Under Art. 266-B** | *Reclusion perpetua* (elevated to *reclusion perpetua* to death, or death, when attended by qualifying circumstances under Art. 266-B) ([*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314)[^26]). | *Prision mayor* (elevated to *reclusion temporal* to *reclusion perpetua* when attended by qualifying circumstances under Art. 266-B, or modified by R.A. No. 7610 Sec. 5[b] for child victims) ([*People v. Tulagan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/65020)[^27]). |

### Variance Doctrine Between Paragraph 1 and Paragraph 2

Because the elements of rape through sexual intercourse under paragraph 1 and rape through sexual assault under paragraph 2 are materially different, neither mode is necessarily included in or subsumes the other under Sections 4 and 5, Rule 120 of the Rules of Criminal Procedure.[^28] Consequently, an accused who is charged in the Information with rape by carnal knowledge under Article 266-A, paragraph 1 cannot be convicted of rape through sexual assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2 even if the trial evidence proves anal penile penetration or finger insertion, without violating his constitutional right to be informed of the nature and cause of the accusation against him.[^29]

The Supreme Court has applied this variance rule across three distinct procedural scenarios:

1. **Conviction of Lesser Included Offense Under Article 336 or R.A. No. 7610 Section 5(b).** In [*People v. Pareja*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/56553)[^30], where the Information for the December 2003 incident charged carnal knowledge under Article 266-A, paragraph 1 against a 13-year-old girl but her testimony established that the accused inserted his penis into her anus, the Supreme Court held that he could not be convicted of rape by sexual assault under paragraph 2 and instead convicted him under the variance doctrine of the lesser included offense of acts of lasciviousness under Article 336 of the Revised Penal Code.[^31] Similarly, in [*People v. Caoili*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63383)[^32], where a biological father was charged with rape by carnal knowledge under paragraph 1 of his 14-year-old daughter on October 23, 2005 but the prosecution proved only that he inserted his fingers into her vagina, the Supreme Court En Banc rejected the theory that finger insertion constitutes carnal knowledge, held that he could not be convicted of rape by sexual assault under paragraph 2 and convicted him of the lesser included offense of lascivious conduct under Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610.[^33]
2. **Waiver by Failure to Object at Trial.** In [*People v. Lomaque*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55877)[^34], the Information in Criminal Case No. Q-00-96392 charged the stepfather with inserting his penis into the minor victim's vagina under paragraph 1, whereas the victim testified at trial that he put his penis into her mouth under paragraph 2. The Supreme Court sustained his conviction for rape by sexual assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2 because the accused failed to object at trial to the testimony establishing oral sexual assault and was therefore bound by the variance.
3. **Waiver of Duplicitous Information Charging Both Modes.** Under Section 13, Rule 110 and Section 3, Rule 120 of the Revised Rules of Criminal Procedure, an Information must charge only one offense, but if a single Information charges both modes of rape under Article 266-A and the accused fails to file a motion to quash before entering his plea, he waives the defect of duplicity and may be convicted of as many offenses as are charged and proved.[^35] In [*People v. Chingh*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/35870)[^36], a single Information charged the accused with inserting his index finger into a 10-year-old girl's vagina and then inserting his penis into her vagina on March 11, 2004. Because the accused did not move to quash the duplicitous Information before arraignment, the Supreme Court affirmed his dual convictions for statutory rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(d) and rape through sexual assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2.[^37]

## Rape by Sexual Intercourse (Paragraph 1): Gravamen and Anatomical Threshold

In [*People v. XXX278240*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70398)[^38], the Supreme Court listed the elements of rape through sexual intercourse:

1. the accused had carnal knowledge of the victim; and

2. said act was accompanied (a) with the use of force, threat or intimidation, (b) when the victim is deprived of reason or otherwise unconscious, (c) by means of fraudulent machination or grave abuse of authority or (d) when the victim is under 12 years of age or is demented.

The gravamen of rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1 is carnal knowledge of a woman (or another person under Republic Act No. 11648) against her will or without her consent under any of the four enumerated circumstances in subparagraphs (a), (b), (c) and (d).[^39]

### The Pudendal-Cleft Penetration Threshold (*People v. Agao*)

Full or deep penile penetration of the vaginal canal or rupture of the hymen is not required to consummate rape by sexual intercourse.[^40] Prior to 2022, two divergent lines of decisions existed on the minimum genital contact required to cross from attempted rape to consummated rape: one line required that the penis penetrate the pudendal or vulval cleft of the labia majora, while another line treated "mere touching" or "mere epidermal contact" or grazing of the external pudendum or fleshy surface of the labia majora by an erect penis as sufficient to consummate rape.[^41]

In the landmark En Banc decision in [*People v. Agao*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68651)[^42], the Supreme Court definitively clarified those "mere touching" cases and established the floor anatomical threshold for consummated rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1:

> [!NOTE]
> Under [*People v. Agao*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68651), rape by sexual intercourse reaches the consummated stage as soon as the offender's erect penis penetrates the cleft of the labia majora (also known as the vulval or pudendal cleft, or the fissure between the fleshy outer lips of the vulva) in even the slightest degree.[^43] Mere surface touching or grazing of the mons pubis or the outer skin surface of the labia majora without entering the vulval cleft constitutes only attempted rape (if accompanied by intent to lie, such as an erect penis pressing against the genitalia) or acts of lasciviousness.[^44]

The Supreme Court in [*People v. Agao*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68651)[^45] also extended this same anatomical threshold by analogy to rape through sexual assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2, holding that penetration of the vulval cleft by any instrument or object consummates rape by sexual assault.

### Five Circumstantial Guideposts for Inferring Penile Penetration

Recognizing that minor and traumatized victims frequently lack the vocabulary to articulate precise anatomical landmarks during direct examination, the Supreme Court En Banc in [*People v. Agao*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68651)[^46] enumerated five circumstantial guideposts from which trial and appellate courts may infer that the offender's penis penetrated the victim's pudendal cleft:

1. The victim testifies that she felt pain in her genitals.[^47]
2. There was bleeding in the victim's genitals.[^48]
3. The labia minora was gaping, or there was redness or discoloration thereof.[^49]
4. The hymenal tags are no longer visible.[^50]
5. The sex organ of the victim showed other signs of genital injury.[^51]

### Medical and Physical Conditions That Do Not Negate Carnal Knowledge

Consistent with the pudendal-cleft threshold, the Supreme Court has repeatedly rejected defense arguments predicated on the absence of full penetration or internal trauma:

- **Intact or Stretchable Hymen and Absence of Lacerations.** An intact hymen or the absence of fresh hymenal lacerations does not negate rape because hymenal rupture is not an element of Article 266-A, paragraph 1.[^52] In [*People v. Boromeo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46881)[^53], the Supreme Court affirmed a conviction for qualified rape of a nine-year-and-eight-month-old child where the offender's partial penile penetration left her hymen intact without external physical injury.[^54] In [*People v. Dela Paz*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44868)[^55], the examining physician's finding that the 31-year-old mentally retarded victim had a stretchable, intact hymen without injury did not bar conviction.[^56] Similarly, in [*People v. Amistoso*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55457)[^57], healed lacerations found three days after the alleged rape did not disprove it because the examining physician did not rule out healing in less than a week when there is no infection. In [*People v. Escultor*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46492)[^58], the absence of fresh lacerations when the victim was examined a week after the second rape did not negate rape.
- **Healed or Old Lacerations.** Old healed hymenal lacerations found during a subsequent medico-legal examination corroborate rather than negate prior penile penetration, as shown in [*People v. Espino*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/45874)[^59], [*People v. Cabungan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55486)[^60], [*People v. Piosang*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/56017)[^61] and [*People v. Vitero*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55835)[^62].
- **Non-Presentation of Medical Certificate or Examining Physician.** A medical examination and a medico-legal certificate are merely corroborative and are not indispensable elements to prosecute or convict an accused of rape if the victim's testimony alone is credible.[^63] In [*People v. Balonzo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44422)[^64], the prosecution's non-presentation of the medical certificate and the examining physician did not prevent the conviction of a father who raped his 15-year-old daughter.
- **Standing Position or Cramped Quarters.** In [*People v. Chingh*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/35870)[^65], the Supreme Court held that sexual intercourse committed while both the offender and the 10-year-old victim were standing in a vacant lot, while uncomfortable, is not physically impossible or improbable. Similarly, in [*People v. Canada*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/50435)[^66] and [*People v. Soriano*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/51351)[^67], the commission of rape inside a small outdoor comfort room or inside a cramped one-room dwelling where other family members were sleeping was sustained because lust is no respecter of time and place.

## Circumstances of Commission Under Paragraph 1

To constitute rape under paragraph 1 (or rape through sexual assault under paragraph 2, which incorporates "any of the circumstances mentioned in paragraph 1 hereof"), the act must be committed under at least one of the four circumstances in subparagraphs (a), (b), (c) and (d).[^68]

### Force, Threat or Intimidation (Paragraph 1[a])

In [*People v. Amistoso*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55457)[^69], the Supreme Court stated the elements of rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(a):

1. the offender had carnal knowledge of a woman; and

2. such act was accomplished through force, threat or intimidation.

It added that when the offender is the victim's father, there need not be actual force, threat or intimidation.[^70]

Under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(a), rape is committed when carnal knowledge is accomplished through force, threat or intimidation.[^71]

#### Subjective Standard of Force and Intimidation

Force in rape need not be irresistible, as it need only be present and sufficient to consummate the offender's evil design.[^72] Intimidation is likewise evaluated subjectively in light of the victim's age, size, strength, relationship to the offender and perception and judgment at the time of the commission of the crime.[^73]

- **Force Against a Minor.** In [*People v. Olpindo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67910)[^74], the accused forcibly took a 14-year-old girl to an uninhabited place, tied her hands with rope and slammed her to the floor before raping her. The Supreme Court held that he employed threat, force and intimidation and that a minor could not be expected to resist as an adult would.
- **Threats Against the Victim or Her Relatives.** In [*People v. Calongui*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/40624)[^75], death threats directed against a 13-year-old girl and her siblings by her older first cousin who lived in their household satisfied paragraph 1(a). In [*People v. Veloso*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55563)[^76], threats to drown a 12-year-old girl on a riverbank followed by boxing, kicking and choking her established two counts of rape under paragraph 1(a).
- **Use or Presence of a Deadly Weapon.** Pointing a sharp-pointed bolo (*sundang*) in [*People v. Macapanas*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/53959)[^77], a *tuba*-gatherer's scythe at the victim's neck in [*People v. Canada*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/50435)[^78], a knife in [*People v. Espino*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/45874)[^79] and [*People v. Suarez*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/42987)[^80], or a fan knife (*balisong*) in [*People v. Baldo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48968)[^81] establishes force and intimidation under paragraph 1(a). In [*People v. Baldo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48968)[^82] and [*People v. Vitero*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55835)[^83], the Supreme Court ruled that the fact that the offender laid the fan knife or 20-inch knife beside him while mounting the victim did not negate intimidation because the weapon remained within his immediate reach.

#### Moral Ascendancy in Incestuous and Intra-Familial Rape

When the offender is the victim's biological father or close ascendant exercising moral authority over a minor victim, actual physical violence or overt threat is unnecessary because the father's overpowering moral ascendancy and influence substitute for violence and intimidation under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(a).[^84] The Supreme Court applied this doctrine to biological fathers who raped their minor daughters in [*People v. Buclao*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/57176)[^85], [*People v. Amistoso*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55457)[^86], [*People v. Balonzo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44422)[^87] and [*People v. ZZZ*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69591)[^88].

#### Marital Rape and Abolition of the Implied Consent Theory (*People v. Jumawan*)

In [*People v. Jumawan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/56922)[^89], a lawful husband who forcibly had carnal knowledge of his wife on October 16 and 17, 1998 invoked Lord Matthew Hale's common-law "irrevocable implied consent" theory, arguing that a woman gives permanent consent to sexual intercourse upon marriage and cannot charge her lawful husband with rape. The Supreme Court decisively rejected the marital exemption, holding that the unqualified definition of rape in Article 266-A, paragraph 1 ("By a man who shall have carnal knowledge of a woman"), read together with the second paragraph of Article 266-C ("In case it is the legal husband who is the offender, the subsequent forgiveness by the wife as the offended party shall extinguish the criminal action or the penalty"), proves the legislative intent of Republic Act No. 8353 to penalize marital rape.[^90] A marriage license is not a license for a husband to forcibly rape his wife and a married woman retains full constitutional and statutory protection over her bodily integrity.[^91]

#### Evidentiary Presumption on Resistance in Any Degree (Article 266-D)

Article 266-D of the Revised Penal Code provides that any physical overt act manifesting resistance against the act of rape in any degree from the offended party, or where the offended party is so situated as to render her incapable of giving valid consent, may be accepted as evidence in the prosecution of the acts punished under Article 266-A.[^92]

In [*People v. XXX258643*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70301)[^93], the Supreme Court traced the legislative history of Article 266-D and reaffirmed that Republic Act No. 8353 abrogated the 1970s jurisprudence requiring that "resistance must be manifested and tenacious." Where a 14-year-old girl verbally pleaded with a 24-year-old CAFGU member to stop and tried to stand up while he pushed her back down on December 21 and 26, 2016, the Supreme Court held that refusing to heed the victim's verbal pleas and physical attempt to rise constitutes force under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(a), whether the resistance is expressed prior to, at the beginning of or in the middle of the sexual act and convicted the offender of rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(a) rather than consensual sexual abuse under Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610.[^94] Likewise, in [*People v. Sabadlab*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/29604)[^95], where a 16-year-old domestic helper was abducted at gunpoint, blindfolded, bound at the wrists and gagged with crumpled newspapers inside a car before being raped, the Supreme Court applied Article 266-D in holding that she was so situated as to render her incapable of giving valid consent.

#### Absorption of Forcible Abduction Into Rape

When an offender forcibly abducts a woman from one place and brings her to another with the primary objective of raping her, the forcible abduction under Article 342 of the Revised Penal Code is absorbed as the means of committing rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(a).[^96] In [*People v. Sabadlab*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/29604)[^97], both the trial court and the Court of Appeals convicted the accused of the complex crime of forcible abduction with rape under Article 342 and Article 48 and the Supreme Court modified the conviction to simple rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(a) because the abductors' main objective in seizing the 16-year-old victim from a street in Makati City was to ravish her inside the vehicle.

### Victim Deprived of Reason or Otherwise Unconscious (Paragraph 1[b])

In [*People v. Dela Paz*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44868)[^98], the Supreme Court held that carnal knowledge of a woman who is a mental retardate is rape and that proof of force or intimidation is not necessary. What needs to be proven are:

1. the fact of sexual congress between the accused and the victim; and

2. the mental retardation of the victim.

None of the decisions discussed on this page states a separate list of elements for a victim who is unconscious.

Under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(b), rape is committed when the offended party is deprived of reason or otherwise unconscious.[^99] In such cases, force, threat or intimidation is unnecessary because the victim lacks the rational capacity or awareness to give consent or offer resistance.[^100]

#### Clinical Distinction Among Unconscious, Deprived of Reason, Demented and Intellectually Disabled (*People v. Quintos*)

In [*People v. Quintos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/58733)[^101], the Supreme Court systematically distinguished the four mental and physical conditions of incapacity across subparagraphs 1(b) and 1(d) of Article 266-A:

1. **Unconscious (Paragraph 1[b]).** An unconscious person cannot rationally respond to stimuli or perform acts such as giving consent or offering resistance because she is unaware, asleep or in a coma.[^102] In [*People v. Guillermo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/42758)[^103], the Supreme Court applied paragraph 1(b) to affirm the conviction of a biological father on two counts of qualified rape for having carnal knowledge of his 14-year-old daughter while she was asleep in their room on November 18, 2000 and April 29, 2001.
2. **Deprived of Reason (Paragraph 1[b]).** The phrase "deprived of reason" is associated with insanity, madness or mental abnormalities that affect a person's reasoning and perception of reality and therefore impair her capacity to resist, make decisions and give consent.[^104]
3. **Demented (Paragraph 1[d]).** The term "demented" refers clinically to a person suffering from dementia, which is the deterioration or loss of previously acquired mental functions such as memory, learning, speaking and social condition, impairing independence in everyday activities.[^105]
4. **Mentally Retarded or Intellectually Disabled (Paragraph 1[b] or Paragraph 1[d]).** An intellectually disabled person is deficient in general mental abilities and has impaired conceptual, social and practical functioning relative to her age, gender and peers.[^106] While earlier decisions classified all mentally retarded victims under "deprived of reason" in paragraph 1(b), [*People v. Quintos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/58733)[^107] clarified that capacity to consent to sexual activity is a function of mental age rather than chronological age: if clinical evidence establishes that an intellectually disabled victim has a mental age below the statutory threshold (such as the 21-year-old victim in [*People v. Quintos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/58733)[^108] with an IQ of 38 and a mental age of 6 years and 2 months), carnal knowledge of her constitutes statutory rape under paragraph 1(d) as well as rape under paragraph 1(b).[^109] The Supreme Court applied the same mental-age rule to a 31-year-old victim with an IQ of 40 and a mental age of 6 years and 6 months in [*People v. Dela Paz*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44868)[^110]. Conversely, where the prosecution proves that the victim suffers from moderate to severe mental retardation without establishing a specific mental age below 12, as in [*People v. Monticalvo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55492)[^111] (or where the Information specifically charges simple rape of a mental retardate under paragraph 1[b], as in [*People v. Nieto*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44765)[^112], involving a 24-year-old victim with an IQ of 30 and a mental age of 5 years and 3 months), the conviction is properly anchored on paragraph 1(b) as rape of a woman deprived of reason.

### Fraudulent Machination or Grave Abuse of Authority (Paragraph 1[c])

The elements of rape through sexual intercourse listed in [*People v. XXX278240*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70398)[^113] apply, with the second element met by fraudulent machination or grave abuse of authority. None of the decisions discussed on this page states a separate list of elements for paragraph 1(c).

Under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(c), rape is committed when carnal knowledge (or sexual assault under paragraph 2) is accomplished by means of fraudulent machination or grave abuse of authority.[^114]

#### Fraudulent Machination by a Traditional Healer (*People v. XXX278240*)

When a victim is induced to submit to carnal knowledge or sexual assault through fraudulent machination or manipulation, such as when she is deceived into believing that the sexual act is a necessary medical treatment for a disease, she acts upon false knowledge and her apparent consent is legally void, rendering proof of force, intimidation or resistance unnecessary.[^115]

In [*People v. XXX278240*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70398)[^116], a community *albularyo* (traditional healer) deceived a 15-year-old girl on October 13 and 16, 2010 into lying naked in her room and allowing him to insert his fingers and his penis into her vagina under the false representation that it was an indispensable healing procedure to push back and close her severe hernia (*almuranas*). The Supreme Court affirmed his conviction of two counts of rape by sexual intercourse under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(c) and two counts of rape through sexual assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2.[^117] Crucially, applying *People v. Gramatica*, as cited in [*People v. XXX278240*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70398)[^118], the Supreme Court ruled that Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610 does not apply to a 15-year-old victim of fraudulent machination under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(c) and paragraph 2: the statutory phrase "indulges in sexual intercourse or lascivious conduct" in Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610 presupposes conscious participation in a sexual act (albeit with legally defective consent due to coercion or influence), whereas a child who was tricked into believing the act was a medical healing procedure did not "indulge" in sexual activity.[^119] Accordingly, the two counts of sexual assault were penalized under Article 266-A, paragraph 2 and Article 266-B by *prision mayor* (indeterminate penalty of 4 years of *prision correccional* to 9 years of *prision mayor*) rather than *reclusion temporal* under Republic Act No. 7610.[^120]

#### Archive Note on Standalone "Grave Abuse of Authority" Under Paragraph 1(c)

While ascendant and parental moral ascendancy is routinely applied under paragraph 1(a) as a substitute for force and intimidation (as in [*People v. Buclao*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/57176)[^121], [*People v. Balonzo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44422)[^122] and [*People v. Amistoso*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55457)[^123]), no Supreme Court decision in the archive convicts an offender solely under the standalone "grave abuse of authority" clause of Article 266-A, paragraph 1(c). Rather, the phrase "grave abuse of authority" appears only where the majority quotes the codal enumeration of Article 266-A, paragraph 1(c) or summarizes its text, as in [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314)[^124], [*People v. Agao*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68651)[^125], [*People v. Tulagan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/65020)[^126], [*People v. Quintos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/58733)[^127], [*People v. Jumawan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/56922)[^128] and [*People v. Sabadlab*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/29604)[^129].

### Statutory Rape and Demented Victims (Paragraph 1[d])

In [*People v. Amistoso*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55457)[^130], decided before Republic Act No. 11648 raised the statutory age, the Supreme Court stated the elements of statutory rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(d):

1. the accused had carnal knowledge of a woman; and

2. the woman is below 12 years of age or is demented.

Under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(d), statutory rape is committed when the offender has carnal knowledge of a victim who is below the statutory age threshold or is demented, even though none of the circumstances in subparagraphs (a), (b) or (c) be present.[^131]

#### Conclusive Presumption of Incapacity to Consent

In statutory rape, the law conclusively presumes that a victim below the statutory age threshold lacks discernment and is incapable of giving intelligent or rational consent to the sexual act.[^132] Consequently, proof of force, threat, intimidation, physical struggle or lack of consent is immaterial. Quoting [*People v. Arpon*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/39488), the Supreme Court in [*People v. Ronquillo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63449)[^133] stated that the only subjects of inquiry in statutory rape are the age of the woman and whether carnal knowledge took place. It reiterated that consent is immaterial because the law presumes that a child of tender years cannot discern evil from good.[^134] The Supreme Court likewise applied paragraph 1(d) to affirm statutory rape convictions involving victims under 12 years of age in [*People v. Piosang*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/56017)[^135] (three years and eleven months old), [*People v. Sia*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48935)[^136] (four years old), [*People v. Escultor*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46492)[^137] (seven and eleven years old), [*People v. Teodoro*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55618)[^138] (eight years old), [*People v. Tulagan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/65020)[^139] (nine years old), [*People v. Boromeo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46881)[^140] (nine years and eight months old), [*People v. Agao*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68651)[^141] (10 years old), [*People v. Mingming*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48272)[^142] (10 years old), [*People v. Chingh*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/35870)[^143] (10 years old) and [*People v. Soriano*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/51351)[^144] (11 years and eight months old).

By contrast, if the victim had already reached 12 years of age at the time of an offense committed prior to Republic Act No. 11648 (such as the victim in [*People v. Amistoso*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55457)[^145] who was 12 years, 1 month and 8 days old on July 10, 2000), the offense is not statutory rape under paragraph 1(d), though the accused may still be convicted under paragraph 1(a) if the body of the Information alleges force, intimidation or lack of consent.

#### Amendment by R.A. No. 11648 Raising the Threshold to Under 16 and Archive Note on the Close-in-Age Exception

Effective March 22, 2022, Section 1 of Republic Act No. 11648 amended Article 266-A, paragraph 1(d) by raising the statutory rape threshold from under 12 years of age to under 16 years of age.[^146] Republic Act No. 11648 also added a close-in-age exception providing that there shall be no criminal liability when the age difference between the parties is not more than 3 years and the sexual act is proven to be consensual, non-abusive and non-exploitative, provided further that this exception shall not apply if the victim is under 13 years of age.[^147]

In [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314)[^148], [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69910)[^149] and [*People v. XXX*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69802)[^150], the Supreme Court recognized that the term "statutory age" now means "below 12 years old" for offenses committed before the effectivity of Republic Act No. 11648 and "under 16 years old" for offenses committed on or after its effectivity on March 22, 2022. Because all decided appeals in the archive involved sexual offenses committed prior to March 22, 2022, no decision in the archive applies the Republic Act No. 11648 close-in-age exception to exonerate an accused and that proviso is only quoted in [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314)[^151].

## Rape Through Sexual Assault (Paragraph 2)

In [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314)[^152], the Supreme Court listed the elements of rape through sexual assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2:

1. the offender commits an act of sexual assault (a) by inserting his penis into another person's mouth or anal orifice or (b) by inserting any instrument or object into the genital or anal orifice of another person; and

2. the act of sexual assault is accomplished (a) by using force and intimidation, (b) when the woman is deprived of reason or otherwise unconscious, (c) by means of fraudulent machination or grave abuse of authority or (d) when the woman is under 12 years of age or demented.

Article 266-A, paragraph 2 defines rape through sexual assault as committed by any person who, under any of the circumstances mentioned in paragraph 1, commits an act of sexual assault by:

1. Inserting his penis into another person's mouth or anal orifice ([*People v. Quintos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/58733)[^153] and [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314)[^154]), or
2. Inserting any instrument or object into the genital or anal orifice of another person ([*People v. Soriano*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/51351)[^155]).

### Scope of Instrument or Object: Fingers and Tongue in Cunnilingus

The phrase "any instrument or object" in paragraph 2 includes parts of the offender's body other than his penis.[^156]

- **Finger Insertion.** Inserting a finger into the victim's vagina under any of the circumstances in paragraph 1 constitutes rape through sexual assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2, as the Supreme Court held in [*People v. Soriano*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/51351)[^157], [*People v. Flordeliz*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/53698)[^158], [*People v. Chingh*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/35870)[^159], [*People v. Bonaagua*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/34342)[^160], [*People v. Villacampa*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63854)[^161], [*People v. Tulagan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/65020)[^162], [*People v. ZZZ*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69591)[^163] and [*People v. XXX278240*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70398)[^164].
- **Cunnilingus (Tongue Insertion) vs. Unspecified Licking.** In [*People v. Bonaagua*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/34342)[^165], the Supreme Court ruled that the offender's tongue is an instrument or object within the contemplation of Article 266-A, paragraph 2, so cunnilingus in which the offender's tongue penetrates or touches the outer lip (labia majora) of the victim's vagina consummates rape through sexual assault. However, in Criminal Case No. 03-0255 of [*People v. Bonaagua*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/34342)[^166], where the 8-year-old daughter testified only that her father "touched my private part and licked it" without specifying which part of her genitalia his tongue touched and expressly denied finger insertion on that date, the Supreme Court held that reasonable doubt as to penetration of the genital orifice required modifying the conviction on that count to acts of lasciviousness under Article 336 in relation to Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610.

### Interplay Between Article 266-A(2), Article 266-B and R.A. No. 7610 Section 5(b)

In [*People v. Tulagan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/65020)[^167], the Supreme Court set out the elements of Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610:

1. the accused commits the act of sexual intercourse or lascivious conduct;

2. the said act is performed with a child exploited in prostitution or other sexual abuse; and

3. the child, whether male or female, is below 18 years of age.

The designation and penalty for acts falling under Article 266-A, paragraph 2 depend on the victim's age, the presence of qualifying circumstances under Article 266-B and whether the victim was a child subjected to sexual abuse under Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610:

1. **Adult Victims (18 Years Old or Older and Not Demented) or Deceived Child Victims Outside R.A. No. 7610.** When rape through sexual assault is committed against an adult victim of sound mind ([*People v. Tulagan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/65020)[^168] and [*People v. Quintos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/58733)[^169]), or against a minor through fraudulent machination where the victim did not consciously "indulge" in sexual activity under *People v. Gramatica*, the offense is designated as **Rape Through Sexual Assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2, in relation to Article 266-B of the Revised Penal Code** and carries the basic penalty of *prision mayor* (6 years and 1 day to 12 years), as applied in [*People v. XXX278240*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70398)[^170] and [*People v. Lomaque*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55877)[^171].
2. **Child Victims Under the Statutory Age (or Demented) Without Qualifying Circumstances: The [*Chingh*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/35870) and [*Tulagan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/65020) Rule.** Under Article 266-B, simple rape through sexual assault carries only *prision mayor*, whereas acts of lasciviousness against a child under 12 years of age under Article 336 in relation to Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610 carries the higher penalty of *reclusion temporal* in its medium period.[^172] In [*People v. Chingh*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/35870)[^173] and [*People v. Villacampa*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63854)[^174], the Supreme Court held that imposing the lighter *prision mayor* penalty when the offender actually penetrated a 10-year-old child's vagina with his finger would be unfair to the child victim and therefore imposed *reclusion temporal* in its medium period under Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610. In [*People v. Tulagan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/65020)[^175], the Supreme Court En Banc harmonized the nomenclature and penalty, holding that when sexual assault is committed against a child under 12 years of age (or demented), the offense must be designated as **"Sexual Assault under paragraph 2, Article 266-A of the Revised Penal Code, in relation to Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610"**, penalized by *reclusion temporal* in its medium period and P50,000.00 each for civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages. The Supreme Court En Banc applied this exact rule in [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314)[^176] (adding a PHP 15,000.00 fine under Section 31[f] of Republic Act No. 7610).[^177]
3. **Qualified Rape Through Sexual Assault Under Article 266-B.** When rape through sexual assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2 is attended by a pleaded and proven qualifying circumstance under Article 266-B (such as the twin circumstances of minority and biological paternity), Article 266-B itself elevates the penalty for sexual assault to *reclusion temporal* (12 years and 1 day to 20 years).[^178] In [*People v. Soriano*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/51351)[^179], [*People v. Flordeliz*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/53698)[^180], [*People v. Bonaagua*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/34342)[^181] and [*People v. ZZZ*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69591)[^182], the Supreme Court convicted biological fathers who inserted their fingers into their minor daughters' vaginas of **Rape by Sexual Assault** (or **Qualified Rape Through Sexual Assault**) under Article 266-A, paragraph 2 in relation to Article 266-B and imposed *reclusion temporal* under the Revised Penal Code (in [*People v. ZZZ*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69591)[^183], sentencing the father to 12 years and 1 day to 16 years, 5 months and 1 day of *reclusion temporal*, awarding PHP 50,000.00 each for civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages and deleting the PHP 15,000.00 fine under Republic Act No. 7610).[^184] And in [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69910)[^185], the Supreme Court En Banc emphasized that the designation "Qualified Rape of a minor" and its PHP 150,000.00 damages tier apply only to rape through carnal knowledge under paragraph 1 and not to rape through sexual assault under paragraph 2.

## Nomenclature, Penalties and Civil Liabilities

### Abandonment of "Qualified Statutory Rape" and Adoption of "Qualified Rape of a Minor" (*People v. ABC260708*)

Beginning in 2014, a string of decisions coined the hybrid designation "qualified statutory rape" whenever a victim below the statutory age threshold (or with a mental age below the statutory threshold) was raped under any of the special qualifying aggravating circumstances in Article 266-B (such as minority and relationship, age below 7 years old or the offender's knowledge of the victim's mental disability).[^186]

In the landmark En Banc Decision of January 23, 2024 in [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314)[^187] and its En Banc Resolution of April 22, 2025 in [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69910)[^188], the Supreme Court formally **abandoned** the designation "qualified statutory rape" as inconsistent with fundamental principles of criminal law.[^189] The Supreme Court explained that the victim's age below the statutory threshold (or mental retardation comparable to a child below the statutory age) is an inherent aggravating circumstance of statutory rape that merely dispenses with proof of force or intimidation, whereas the ten circumstances under Article 266-B are special qualifying aggravating circumstances that alter the nature of the offense and elevate the prescribed penalty to death.[^190] Because a single circumstance cannot be appreciated twice (both as an inherent element and as a special qualifying circumstance), the special qualifying circumstance under Article 266-B absorbs the inherent circumstance of being below the statutory age.[^191]

Under the amended guidelines in [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69910)[^192] (also applied in [*People v. XXX*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69802)[^193]):

> [!TIP]
> In [*People v. XXX257662*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70012)[^206], the Supreme Court clarified that the proper nomenclature for crimes defined and penalized under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(a) and Article 266-B without any qualifying circumstance is **"Rape"** (or **"Rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(a), penalized under Article 266-B of the Revised Penal Code"**) and **not "Simple Rape,"** because there is nothing simple about rape and trivializing it as "simple" downplays its severity as a crime against persons.[^207]

Furthermore, when two or more special qualifying aggravating circumstances under Article 266-B concur in the same rape, [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314)[^208] and [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69910)[^209] direct that only one special qualifying circumstance is used to qualify the crime, while the unutilized special qualifying circumstances (if they also fall within the exclusive list of generic aggravating circumstances under Article 14 of the Revised Penal Code, such as taking advantage of public position, disregard of respect due the offended party, ignominy or cruelty) are appreciated as generic aggravating circumstances to fix the period of a divisible penalty (for instance, when a privileged mitigating circumstance under Article 69 or attempted/accomplice graduation under Articles 50 to 57 applies) or to justify higher civil liability. In [*People v. Buclao*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/57176)[^210], the Supreme Court exercised its discretion to increase civil indemnity to P150,000.00, moral damages to P150,000.00 and exemplary damages to P100,000.00 per count in view of the extreme depravity of a biological father who repeatedly raped his minor daughter.[^211] Likewise, in [*People v. Quintos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/58733)[^212], the Supreme Court increased civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages to P100,000.00 each per count where the offender took advantage of a 21-year-old intellectually disabled neighbor with a mental age of 6 years and 2 months.

### Strict Pleading and Proof of Qualifying Circumstances Under Article 266-B

In [*People v. Vitero*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55835)[^213], the Supreme Court stated the elements of qualified rape where the victim is over 12 but under 18 years of age:

1. the victim is a female over 12 years but under 18 years of age;

2. the offender is a parent, ascendant, stepparent, guardian, relative by consanguinity or affinity within the third civil degree or the common-law spouse of the parent of the victim; and

3. the offender has carnal knowledge of the victim either through force, threat or intimidation.

For qualified rape of a minor, [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314)[^214] fixed the designation of the offense rather than a separate list of elements, as discussed above.

Because special qualifying circumstances under Article 266-B change the nature of the offense and increase the penalty from *reclusion perpetua* to death (or from *reclusion perpetua* to *reclusion perpetua* without eligibility for parole under Republic Act No. 9346), Sections 8 and 9 of Rule 110 of the Revised Rules of Criminal Procedure require every qualifying circumstance to be both **specifically alleged in the Information** and **proved beyond reasonable doubt at trial**.[^215]

1. **Biological Paternity, Direct-Line Relationship, or Third-Degree Collateral Consanguinity.**
   - When the Information alleges and the prosecution proves by the victim's Certificate of Live Birth, baptismal certificate or pre-trial stipulation that the victim was under 18 years of age and that the offender is her biological father (as in [*People v. Soriano*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/51351)[^216], [*People v. Bidoc*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/43032)[^217], [*People v. Quiachon*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44416)[^218], [*People v. Guillermo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/42758)[^219], [*People v. Balonzo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44422)[^220], [*People v. Vitero*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55835)[^221], [*People v. Amistoso*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55457)[^222], [*People v. Buclao*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/57176)[^223], [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314)[^224], [*People v. XXX*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69802)[^225] and [*People v. ZZZ*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69591)[^226]) or her uncle within the third civil degree of consanguinity (as in [*People v. Arpon*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/39488)[^227]), the offense is elevated to qualified rape (or qualified rape of a minor if she is below the statutory age).
   - If the offender is the victim's uncle within the third civil degree of consanguinity but the Information fails to allege that relationship, as in [*People v. Teodoro*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55618)[^228], he can be convicted only of statutory rape.
   - **Collateral Relatives Beyond the Third Degree.** Under Article 266-B, paragraph 1, collateral relatives are covered only within the third civil degree of consanguinity or affinity.[^229] In [*People v. Calongui*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/40624)[^230], where the offender was the 13-year-old victim's first cousin, the conviction was for rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(a). The Supreme Court also held that the first-cousin relationship was not within the concept of relationship under Article 15 and deleted the exemplary damages.
2. **Stepparent vs. Common-Law Spouse Relationship.**
   - A stepfather-stepdaughter relationship presupposes a valid legal marriage between the accused and the victim's natural mother, which must be proved by their marriage certificate.[^231] Where the Information alleges that the accused is the victim's "stepfather" (or "step-father"), the prosecution's failure to present the marriage contract between the accused and the victim's mother (or trial proof showing that he was merely her mother's common-law live-in partner in [*People v. Escultor*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46492)[^232] and [*People v. Agao*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68651)[^233], her uncle-in-law married to her mother's sister in [*People v. Cabungan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55486)[^234], or a stepfather whose marriage was supported only by oral testimony without a marriage certificate in [*People v. Lomaque*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55877)[^235]) prevents the appreciation of the qualifying circumstance and reduces the crime to simple rape (or statutory rape).
   - Conversely, when the Information accurately alleges that the offender is the **common-law spouse of the parent of the victim** and both the relationship and the victim's minority are proved, the crime is qualified rape under Article 266-B, paragraph 1, as in [*People v. Boromeo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46881)[^236].
3. **Victim Under Seven (7) Years of Age.** When the Information alleges and the victim's birth certificate proves that she was below 7 years of age at the time of the rape (such as the 4-year-old victim in [*People v. Sia*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48935)[^237]), the crime is qualified under Article 266-B, paragraph 5 (now designated as qualified rape of a minor under [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314)[^238] and [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69910)[^239]).
4. **Offender's Knowledge of the Victim's Mental Disability at the Time of Commission.** Under Article 266-B, paragraph 10, the victim's mental disability qualifies the rape only if the Information alleges and the evidence proves that the offender **knew** of the mental disability, emotional disorder and/or physical handicap at the time of the commission of the crime.[^240] In [*People v. Dela Paz*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44868)[^241], where the Information alleged and the evidence showed that the accused lived next door and knew that the 31-year-old victim was mentally retarded, the Supreme Court affirmed his conviction for qualified rape.[^242] By contrast, where the Information omitted either the victim's intellectual disability ([*People v. Quintos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/58733)[^243]) or the offender's knowledge thereof at the time of the crime ([*People v. Monticalvo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55492)[^244] and [*People v. Nieto*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44765)[^245]), the offender could be convicted only of simple rape despite his admission at trial that he knew of her condition.
5. **Use of a Deadly Weapon.** Under Article 266-B, second paragraph, when the rape is committed with the use of a deadly weapon (or by two or more persons) and that circumstance is specifically alleged in the Information and proved at trial, the prescribed penalty is elevated to *reclusion perpetua* to death (imposed as *reclusion perpetua* under Article 63, paragraph 2 of the Revised Penal Code in the absence of a generic aggravating circumstance), as in [*People v. Macapanas*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/53959)[^246] (a sharp-pointed bolo or *sundang*) and [*People v. Canada*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/50435)[^247] (a *tuba*-gatherer's scythe). If the use of a knife or firearm is proved at trial but was not alleged in the Information, it cannot qualify the offense under Article 266-B, as held in [*People v. Espino*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/45874)[^248], [*People v. Mingming*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48272)[^249] and [*People v. Sabadlab*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/29604)[^250], though in [*People v. Sabadlab*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/29604)[^251] and [*People v. Baldo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48968)[^252] the unpleaded deadly weapon or proved generic aggravating circumstance justified an award of exemplary damages under Article 2230 of the Civil Code. Similarly, in [*People v. Soriano*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/51351)[^253], the unpleaded circumstance that the father raped his daughter in full view of her younger siblings under Article 266-B, paragraph 3 could not be appreciated because it was not alleged in the Informations.

### Abolition of the Death Penalty (R.A. No. 9346), Indivisibility of *Reclusion Perpetua* and Exemplary Damages

Before the enactment of Republic Act No. 9346, the Supreme Court affirmed the death penalty on automatic review for qualified rape in [*People v. Soriano*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/51351)[^254] and [*People v. Boromeo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46881)[^255]. Upon the enactment of Republic Act No. 9346 (An Act Prohibiting the Imposition of Death Penalty in the Philippines), the Supreme Court held in [*People v. Quiachon*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44416)[^256] that Republic Act No. 9346 applies retroactively under Article 22 of the Revised Penal Code as a favorable penal law, commuting the death penalty to ***reclusion perpetua* without eligibility for parole** while maintaining the higher civil indemnity and moral damages tier fixed for offenses where the imposable penalty under Republic Act No. 7659 and Republic Act No. 8353 is death (applied across [*People v. Bidoc*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/43032)[^257], [*People v. Guillermo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/42758)[^258], [*People v. Balonzo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44422)[^259], [*People v. Dela Paz*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44868)[^260], [*People v. Sia*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48935)[^261], [*People v. Vitero*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55835)[^262], [*People v. Amistoso*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55457)[^263], [*People v. Buclao*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/57176)[^264], [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314)[^265], [*People v. XXX*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69802)[^266] and [*People v. ZZZ*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69591)[^267]).

For simple rape, the prescribed penalty of *reclusion perpetua* is an indivisible penalty with no fixed durational range in the dispositive fallo. Thus, in [*People v. Calongui*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/40624)[^268], the Supreme Court affirmed the Court of Appeals' deletion of the trial court's erroneous phrasing "twenty (20) years and one (1) day to forty (40) years of *reclusion perpetua*."

Regarding exemplary damages in older pre-*Jugueta* decisions, when no qualifying or generic aggravating circumstance was present or proved, the Supreme Court deleted exemplary damages in [*People v. Calongui*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/40624)[^269] and [*People v. Nieto*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44765)[^270], whereas where a pleaded qualifying circumstance (such as the victim's minority) failed to qualify the rape because the companion relationship was unproven, the proven minority functioned as a generic aggravating circumstance justifying P30,000.00 exemplary damages in [*People v. Cabungan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55486)[^271] and [*People v. Lomaque*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55877)[^272] and in [*People v. Veloso*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55563)[^273] the Supreme Court awarded P30,000.00 exemplary damages per count under Article 2229 of the Civil Code by way of public example against an adult who brutally mauled a 12-year-old rape victim. Under current jurisprudence synthesized in [*People v. Tulagan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/65020)[^274], [*People v. Olpindo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67910)[^275], [*People v. XXX257662*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70012)[^276], [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314)[^277] and [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69910)[^278], exemplary damages are automatically awarded alongside civil indemnity and moral damages at PHP 75,000.00 each for simple and statutory rape, PHP 100,000.00 each for qualified rape and PHP 150,000.00 each for qualified rape of a minor, all subject to legal interest of 6% per annum from finality of judgment until full payment.

### Child Offenders (R.A. No. 9344) and Supervening Death of the Accused (Article 89[1])

- **Exemption and Privileged Mitigating Circumstance of Minority Under R.A. No. 9344.** Under Sections 6, 7 and 68 of Republic Act No. 9344 (the Juvenile Justice and Welfare Act of 2006), a child in conflict with the law aged 15 years or under at the time of the commission of the rape is exempt from criminal liability (while remaining civilly liable) and a child above 15 years but below 18 years of age who acted with discernment is entitled to the privileged mitigating circumstance of minority under Article 68, paragraph 2 of the Revised Penal Code, lowering the prescribed penalty by one degree.[^279] In [*People v. Arpon*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/39488)[^280], where the accused uncle testified without contradiction that he was born on February 23, 1982, the Supreme Court applied the statutory presumption of minority in his favor, exempted him from criminal liability for the first count of rape committed in 1995 when he was 13 years old (while holding him liable for P75,000.00 civil indemnity, P75,000.00 moral damages and P30,000.00 exemplary damages), lowered the penalty for the second and third counts of qualified rape committed in July 1999 when he was 17 years old by one degree from death to *reclusion perpetua* and remanded the case for agricultural camp confinement under Section 51 of Republic Act No. 9344.[^281] Similarly, in [*People v. Monticalvo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55492)[^282], where the accused was proven by his birth certificate to be 17 years old when he raped a mentally retarded neighbor on December 9, 2002, the Supreme Court reduced his penalty for simple rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(b) by one degree from *reclusion perpetua* to *reclusion temporal* (indeterminate penalty of 10 years of *prision mayor* to 17 years and 4 months of *reclusion temporal*) and ordered his confinement in an agricultural camp under Section 51 of Republic Act No. 9344.
- **Total Extinguishment by Death Pending Appeal Under Article 89(1).** Under Article 89, paragraph 1 of the Revised Penal Code, the death of the accused pending appeal and prior to final judgment totally extinguishes both his criminal liability and his civil liability *ex delicto* (civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages).[^283] In [*People v. Amistoso y Broca*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/56168)[^284], after the Supreme Court had promulgated its January 9, 2013 Decision in [*People v. Amistoso*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55457)[^285] affirming the accused father's conviction for qualified rape, the New Bilibid Prison reported that he had died on December 11, 2012, prompting the Supreme Court to set aside its January 9, 2013 Decision and dismiss the criminal case.

## Evidentiary Principles and Common Defenses

In reviewing prosecutions under Article 266-A, the Supreme Court applies settled evidentiary principles given the intrinsic secrecy of sexual offenses:

1. **Credible Sole Testimony of the Victim and Child-Witness Rules.** Because rape is almost always committed in privacy, conviction may rest solely on the credible, natural and convincing testimony of the offended party.[^286] When the victim is an unlettered child of tender years, leading questions are permissible during direct examination under Section 10(c), Rule 132 of the Rules of Court and the Rule on Examination of a Child Witness, as held in [*People v. Escultor*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46492)[^287]. Even in an unlit bedroom at night, a 15-year-old daughter's tactile and olfactory identification of her biological father by the texture of his hair, his familiar smell and the coarse hair on his upper lip in [*People v. Balonzo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44422)[^288] (or a 19-year-old victim's hospital show-up identification of an assailant who had removed his ski mask during the rape in [*People v. Macapanas*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/53959)[^289]) is sufficient to establish identity beyond reasonable doubt. However, where multiple counts are tried jointly, each count is a separate felony requiring independent proof of the overt act: if a child witness testifies on a specific count only that the accused "raped" her a second time and explains that by "rape" she meant she was "undressed" without any follow-up questions establishing penile penetration ([*People v. Mingming*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48272)[^290]), or gives no testimony at all regarding the dates of two Informations ([*People v. Flordeliz*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/53698)[^291]), or clarifies on the stand that no sexual assault occurred on the date charged in one Information ([*People v. Soriano*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/51351)[^292]), the accused must be acquitted on those unproven counts on reasonable doubt.
2. **Non-Essentiality of Exact Date of Commission.** Under Section 11, Rule 110 of the Rules of Criminal Procedure, the precise date or hour of the commission of rape is not an essential element of Article 266-A, so an Information charging that the second rape occurred "on or about December, 1999" in [*People v. Bidoc*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/43032)[^293], or the victim's inability to recall the exact hour she went to sleep in [*People v. Suarez*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/42987)[^294], does not invalidate the charge or impair her credibility.
3. **Delay in Reporting and Post-Assault Demeanor.** Delay in reporting a rape due to death threats, physical trauma, confusion, social stigma or fear of a tyrannical parent does not equate to fabrication.[^295] The Supreme Court upheld convictions despite a 6-day delay while hospitalized for a stab wound in [*People v. Macapanas*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/53959)[^296], a 10-day delay in [*People v. Suarez*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/42987)[^297], a 2-month delay in [*People v. Cabungan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55486)[^298] and a 4-month delay by a battered wife unfamiliar with Republic Act No. 8353 in [*People v. Jumawan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/56922)[^299].
4. **Irrelevance of Victim's Moral Character or Prior Sexual History.** Under Article 266-A, the victim's moral character or prior sexual history is completely immaterial.[^300] In [*People v. Espino*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/45874)[^301], the Supreme Court held that the 14-year-old victim's alleged reputation as a habitual delinquent or employment in a beerhouse did not bar conviction, as even a prostitute or a woman of loose morals can be the victim of rape.[^302] In [*People v. Nieto*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44765)[^303], a 24-year-old mentally retarded victim's candor on cross-examination that she had previously been raped five times by another man in Nueva Ecija did not impeach her testimony against her neighbor.
5. **Rejection of the "Sweetheart Defense," Family Grudge and Subsequent Recantations.**
   - **Sweetheart Defense.** As an affirmative defense, the "sweetheart theory" or "sweetheart defense" must be established by compelling independent evidence such as love letters, mementos, tokens or photographs and even if proved, a romantic relationship is not a license to force sexual intercourse against the partner's will, as held in [*People v. Baldo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48968)[^304] and [*People v. Calongui*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/40624)[^305].
   - **Ulterior Motive or Family Grudge.** Claims that the victim's mother or relatives fabricated the rape charge out of resentment over household discipline or property disputes ([*People v. Balonzo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44422)[^306], [*People v. Quiachon*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44416)[^307], [*People v. Buclao*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/57176)[^308] and [*People v. XXX257662*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70012)[^309]) are routinely rejected because no mother or young daughter would subject herself to a public trial and genital examination merely to settle a family grievance.
   - **Subsequent Trial Recantations.** When a child victim has given a detailed, positive and medically corroborated testimony of rape during direct examination, a subsequent recantation at trial years later by the victim or her mother out of financial dependency or emotional attachment to the accused is viewed with extreme disfavor as an unreliable afterthought and does not warrant acquittal, as the Supreme Court ruled in [*People v. Boromeo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46881)[^310] and [*People v. Teodoro*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55618)[^311].

## Case Discussions

### Abandonment of "Qualified Statutory Rape" and Adoption of "Qualified Rape of a Minor"

In the January 23, 2024 En Banc Decision in [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314)[^312], biological father ABC260708 was charged in two Informations before the Regional Trial Court with qualified rape (Criminal Case No. 17006) and rape through sexual assault (Criminal Case No. 17007) for inserting his penis into the vagina and then into the mouth of his 8-year-old biological daughter AAA260708 (born February 6, 2007, alleged in the Information as 7 years old) on March 17, 2015.[^313] The Regional Trial Court convicted him of qualified rape (sentencing him to *reclusion perpetua* and ordering him to pay PHP 100,000.00 each for civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages) and rape through sexual assault (ordering him to pay PHP 30,000.00 each for civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages) and the Court of Appeals affirmed the convictions but modified the designations to "qualified statutory rape" and "sexual assault under Article 266-A(2) of the Revised Penal Code in relation to Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610" (increasing the damages on the sexual assault count to PHP 50,000.00 each).[^314] Resolving the appeal to settle conflicting jurisprudence on the taxonomy of rape when the elements of both statutory rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(d) and qualified rape under Article 266-B concur, the Supreme Court En Banc held that the victim's age below the statutory threshold is an inherent aggravating circumstance that is absorbed by the special qualifying aggravating circumstances under Article 266-B and cannot be appreciated twice, thereby abandoning the designation "qualified statutory rape" in favor of "qualified rape of a minor."[^315] The Supreme Court denied the appeal and affirmed the Court of Appeals Decision with modifications: in Criminal Case No. 17006, it found ABC260708 guilty beyond reasonable doubt of qualified rape of a minor, sentenced him to *reclusion perpetua* without eligibility for parole and ordered him to pay AAA260708 PHP 150,000.00 as civil indemnity, PHP 150,000.00 as moral damages and PHP 150,000.00 as exemplary damages, while in Criminal Case No. 17007, it found him guilty beyond reasonable doubt of sexual assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2 of the Revised Penal Code in relation to Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610, sentenced him to an indeterminate penalty of 13 years, 9 months and 11 days of *reclusion temporal* as minimum to 16 years, 5 months and 10 days of *reclusion temporal* as maximum, ordered him to pay a fine of PHP 15,000.00 and ordered him to pay AAA260708 PHP 50,000.00 as civil indemnity, PHP 50,000.00 as moral damages and PHP 50,000.00 as exemplary damages, with 6% legal interest per annum on all monetary awards from finality of judgment until fully paid.[^316]

In the April 22, 2025 En Banc Resolution in [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69910)[^317], the Supreme Court resolved ABC260708's Motion for Reconsideration and extended the designation "Qualified Rape of a minor" and its PHP 150,000.00 floor amounts for civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages to all ten special qualifying aggravating circumstances enumerated in Article 266-B whenever the victim of carnal knowledge under Article 266-A, paragraph 1 is below the statutory age (below 12 years old prior to Republic Act No. 11648, or under 16 years old under Republic Act No. 11648) or suffers from mental retardation comparable to a child below the statutory age, while confirming that the PHP 100,000.00 floor applies to "Qualified Rape" where the victim is not below the statutory age, the PHP 75,000.00 floor applies to "Statutory Rape," and the nomenclature "Qualified Rape of a minor" applies only to rape through carnal knowledge and not to rape through sexual assault.[^318] The Supreme Court denied the Motion for Reconsideration with finality and affirmed its January 23, 2024 Decision.[^319]

### Incestuous Rape by Sexual Assault, Two Counts of Qualified Rape and Acquittal on Double Jeopardy

In [*People v. ZZZ*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69591)[^320], ZZZ was charged before the Regional Trial Court of Dagupan City, Branch 43 under nine separate Informations. They included three counts of rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(a), one count of rape by sexual assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2 and a charge under Section 5(a) of Republic Act No. 9262. The sexual assault count (Criminal Case No. 2017-1045-D) alleged that in April 2009 he inserted his finger into the genital orifice of his daughter AAA, who was 10 years old according to the Information. The rape counts in Criminal Case Nos. 2017-1046-D and 2017-1048-D covered April 2011 and November 2012.[^321] In its Joint Decision dated August 28, 2018, the trial court convicted ZZZ of rape by sexual assault (12 years, 10 months and twenty-one days to 15 years, 6 months and 20 days of *reclusion temporal* and PHP 30,000.00 each as civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages). It also convicted him of two counts of rape (*reclusion perpetua* and PHP 75,000.00 as civil indemnity, PHP 75,000.00 as moral damages and PHP 30,000.00 as exemplary damages in each case) and four counts of slight physical injuries or maltreatment, including Criminal Case No. 2017-1053-D. It acquitted him in Criminal Case Nos. 2017-1047-D and 2017-1049-D. The Court of Appeals affirmed with modifications. It convicted him of sexual assault in relation to Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610 with 12 years and 1 day to 16 years, 5 months and 1 day of *reclusion temporal*. It found the two rapes qualified by minority and relationship, imposed *reclusion perpetua* without eligibility for parole and raised the damages to PHP 100,000.00 each per count. It also convicted him in Criminal Case No. 2017-1053-D of violating Section 5(a) of Republic Act No. 9262.[^322] Reviewing the appeal to correct the penalty for sexual assault and the unconstitutional reversal of an acquittal, the Supreme Court held that ZZZ's moral ascendancy as AAA's biological father substituted for violence and intimidation in the two counts of qualified rape and the one count of rape by sexual assault penalized under Article 266-A, paragraph 2 of the Revised Penal Code (which carries no fine) and ruled that the Court of Appeals violated ZZZ's constitutional right against double jeopardy when it overturned the trial court's judgment of acquittal on the Republic Act No. 9262 charge.[^323] The Supreme Court partially granted the appeal and modified the Court of Appeals Decision: (1) in Criminal Case No. 2017-1045-D, it found ZZZ guilty of Rape by Sexual Assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2, sentenced him to 12 years and 1 day to 16 years, 5 months and 1 day of *reclusion temporal* and ordered him to pay AAA PHP 50,000.00 as civil indemnity *ex delicto*, PHP 50,000.00 as moral damages and PHP 50,000.00 as exemplary damages (deleting the PHP 15,000.00 fine), (2) in Criminal Case Nos. 2017-1046-D and 2017-1048-D, it found him guilty of two counts of Qualified Rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1 in relation to Article 266-B, paragraph 1, sentenced him to *reclusion perpetua* without eligibility for parole for each count and ordered him to pay AAA PHP 100,000.00 as civil indemnity *ex delicto*, PHP 100,000.00 as moral damages and PHP 100,000.00 as exemplary damages per count, (3) in Criminal Case Nos. 2017-1050-D, 2017-1051-D and 2017-1052-D, it found him guilty of three counts of Slight Physical Injuries under Article 266, sentenced him to 20 days of *arresto menor* for each count and ordered him to pay PHP 5,000.00 moral damages each to BBB, CCC and DDD and (4) in Criminal Case No. 2017-1053-D, it acquitted him of violation of Section 5(a) of Republic Act No. 9262, with 6% interest per annum on all monetary awards from finality until fully paid and referral of AAA, BBB, CCC, DDD and EEE to the Department of Social Welfare and Development under Republic Act No. 8505.[^324]

### Rape by Fraudulent Machination by an *Albularyo* Under Paragraph 1(c) and Paragraph 2

In [*People v. XXX278240*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70398)[^325], a community *albularyo* (quack doctor), XXX278240, was charged in four Informations with two counts of rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(c) (Criminal Case Nos. U-17467 and U-17468) and two counts of rape through sexual assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2 (Criminal Case Nos. U-17469 and U-17470) for inducing 15-year-old AAA278240 on October 13 and 16, 2010 to lie naked in her bedroom and submit to the insertion of his fingers and his penis into her vagina under the fraudulent pretense that it was a medical healing procedure required to push back and close her hernia (*almuranas*).[^326] The Regional Trial Court convicted him of two counts of rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(c) (*reclusion perpetua* and PHP 75,000.00 each for civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages per count) and two counts of rape through sexual assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2 (4 years and 2 months of *prision correccional* to 8 years and 1 day of *prision mayor* and PHP 30,000.00 each for civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages per count) and on appeal, the Court of Appeals affirmed the convictions but modified the designations and penalties by applying Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610 to the sexual assault counts (imposing 12 years, 10 months and twenty-one days to 15 years, 6 months and twenty days of *reclusion temporal* and increasing damages to PHP 100,000.00 each on the rape counts and PHP 50,000.00 each on the sexual assault counts).[^327] Deciding whether fraudulent medical treatment by an *albularyo* falls under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(c) and whether Republic Act No. 7610 applies, the Supreme Court held that exploiting a healer's reputation to deceive a 15-year-old patient into believing that digital and penile penetration is a healing procedure constitutes fraudulent machination that vitiates consent without need of force or resistance and ruled under *People v. Gramatica* that Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610 does not apply because a deceived patient does not "indulge" in sexual intercourse or lascivious conduct.[^328] The Supreme Court denied the appeal and affirmed the Court of Appeals Decision with modification: in Criminal Case Nos. U-17467 and U-17468, it found XXX278240 guilty of two counts of rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1 in relation to Article 266-B, sentenced him to *reclusion perpetua* per count and ordered him to pay AAA278240 PHP 75,000.00 each for civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages per count, while in Criminal Case Nos. U-17469 and U-17470, it found him guilty of two counts of rape through sexual assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2 in relation to Article 266-B, sentenced him to 4 years of *prision correccional* as minimum to 9 years of *prision mayor* as maximum per count and ordered him to pay PHP 30,000.00 each for civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages per count, with 6% legal interest per annum and Department of Social Welfare and Development referral under Republic Act No. 8505.[^329]

### Evidentiary Presumption on Resistance in Any Degree Under Article 266-D

In [*People v. XXX258643*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70301)[^330], a 24-year-old CAFGU member assigned to Paulba Patrol Base was charged in three Informations with rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(a) (Criminal Case Nos. 8159, 8160 and 8161) for forcibly having carnal knowledge of 14-year-old AAA on December 16, 21 and 26, 2016 and in a fourth Information with sexual abuse under Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610 (Criminal Case No. 8162) for dragging AAA into a hut, removing her shorts and panty and placing her legs on his shoulders on December 28, 2016 before stopping when he heard her father's motorcycle.[^331] The Regional Trial Court convicted him of one count of simple rape in Criminal Case No. 8159 (*reclusion perpetua* and PHP 75,000.00 civil indemnity, PHP 75,000.00 [printed as PHP 75,000,00] moral damages and PHP 75,000.00 exemplary damages) and downgraded Criminal Case Nos. 8160 and 8161 (along with Criminal Case No. 8162) to three counts of sexual abuse under Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610 on the ground that AAA did not tenaciously resist on December 21 and 26, 2016 and the Court of Appeals affirmed the convictions and modified the penalty and damages on the three Republic Act No. 7610 counts to PHP 20,000.00 civil indemnity, PHP 15,000.00 moral damages, PHP 15,000.00 exemplary damages and a PHP 15,000.00 fine per count.[^332] Reviewing the appeal under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(a) and Article 266-D, the Supreme Court held that Republic Act No. 8353 abrogated the 1970s rule that "resistance must be manifested and tenacious," that AAA's verbal pleas to stop and her act of trying to stand up while the offender pushed her back down constituted resistance in any degree under Article 266-D sufficient to establish force under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(a) in Criminal Case Nos. 8160 and 8161 and that the non-consensual December 28, 2016 assault without penile contact in Criminal Case No. 8162 constituted acts of lasciviousness under Article 336 rather than consensual sexual abuse under Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610.[^333] The Supreme Court dismissed the appeal and affirmed the Court of Appeals Decision with modification: in Criminal Case Nos. 8159, 8160 and 8161, it found XXX258643 guilty beyond reasonable doubt of three counts of rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(a), sentenced him to *reclusion perpetua* for each count and ordered him to pay AAA PHP 75,000.00 as civil indemnity, PHP 75,000.00 as moral damages and PHP 75,000.00 as exemplary damages per count, while in Criminal Case No. 8162, it found him guilty of acts of lasciviousness under Article 336, sentenced him to 6 months of *arresto mayor* as minimum to 4 years and 2 months of *prision correccional* as maximum and ordered him to pay AAA PHP 75,000.00 each for civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages, with 6% legal interest per annum.[^334]

### Rejection of "Simple Rape" Nomenclature in Favor of "Rape" Under Paragraph 1(a)

In [*People v. XXX257662*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70012)[^335], XXX257662 was charged with rape in Criminal Case No. 2013-05-360 for an incident on August 25, 2012 in Leyte. AAA, then 12 years old, testified that he took her on a motorcycle, stopped at a grassy area, punched her in the abdomen, pushed her to the ground and had carnal knowledge of her. He then threatened to kill her if she told her parents.[^336] An examination on October 23, 2012 showed an incompletely healed hymenal laceration. XXX257662 claimed that AAA's father was his cousin and that AAA had motives to fabricate the charge.[^337] On June 17, 2019, the Regional Trial Court convicted him of Simple Rape, sentenced him to *reclusion perpetua* and ordered him to pay PHP 75,000.00 each as civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages. The Court of Appeals affirmed.[^338] The Supreme Court found AAA's testimony credible and held that holding her hand, punching her and pushing her to the ground showed force. It clarified that the proper nomenclature for crimes defined and penalized under Article 266-A is Rape and not Simple Rape because there is nothing simple about rape.[^339] The Supreme Court dismissed the appeal, found XXX257662 guilty of Rape, sentenced him to *reclusion perpetua* and ordered him to pay AAA PHP 75,000.00 each as civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages, with 6% interest a year from finality until full payment. ### Prospectivity of R.A. No. 11648 and Application of "Qualified Rape of a Minor"

In [*People v. XXX*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69802)[^341], biological father XXX was charged in three Informations (Criminal Case Nos. 7465, 7466 and 7467) with three counts of qualified rape for having carnal knowledge of his daughter AAA (born November 22, 2003) in October 2013 when she was 9 years old, on December 29, 2013 when she was 10 years old and on November 23, 2014 when she was 11 years old.[^342] The Regional Trial Court convicted XXX of three counts of qualified rape (*reclusion perpetua* without eligibility for parole and PHP 100,000.00 each for civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages per count) and the Court of Appeals affirmed the convictions but modified the crime designation to three counts of "qualified statutory rape."[^343] On appeal, the Supreme Court explained that while Republic Act No. 11648 (effective March 22, 2022) amended Article 266-A, paragraph 1(d) by raising the statutory rape threshold from under 12 to under 16 years of age, the 2013 and 2014 offenses remain governed by the pre-amendment text under Republic Act No. 8353 pursuant to Article 22 of the Revised Penal Code and applied [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314) to replace the abandoned designation "qualified statutory rape" with "qualified rape of a minor."[^344] The Supreme Court denied the appeal and affirmed the Court of Appeals Decision with modifications, finding XXX guilty beyond reasonable doubt of three counts of qualified rape of a minor, sentencing him to *reclusion perpetua* without eligibility for parole for each count and increasing the awards to PHP 150,000.00 as civil indemnity, PHP 150,000.00 as moral damages and PHP 150,000.00 as exemplary damages per count, with 6% legal interest per annum.[^345]

### The Pudendal-Cleft Penetration Threshold and Five Circumstantial Guideposts

In [*People v. Agao*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68651)[^346], Efren Agao y Añonuevo (common-law partner of the victim's mother, alleged in the Informations as her stepfather) was charged in two Informations with two counts of rape for inserting his erect penis into the vulval cleft of AAA when she was 10 years old (Criminal Case No. 1453-V-14) and in January 2012 when the Information alleged she was 13 years old (Criminal Case No. 1454-V-14).[^347] The Regional Trial Court convicted Agao of two counts of statutory rape and the Court of Appeals affirmed the convictions as two counts of statutory rape.[^348] Convening En Banc to reconcile conflicting decisions on what genital contact consummates rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1, the Supreme Court clarified the "mere touching" and "mere epidermal contact" cases and held that consummated rape requires the slightest penetration by the penis of the cleft of the labia majora (the vulval or pudendal cleft), laid down five circumstantial guideposts for inferring penile penetration of the pudendal cleft and held that because AAA was 10 years old during the first incident and 13 years old during the second and the stepfather marriage was unproven, the two offenses were one count of statutory rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(d) and one count of simple rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(a).[^349] The Supreme Court dismissed the appeal and affirmed the Court of Appeals Decision with modification, finding Efren Agao y Añonuevo guilty beyond reasonable doubt of one count of Statutory Rape in Criminal Case No. 1453-V-14 and one count of Simple Rape in Criminal Case No. 1454-V-14 through sexual intercourse, sentencing him to *reclusion perpetua* for each count and affirming the award of damages.[^350]

### Mode of Appeal in *Reclusion Perpetua* Cases and Rape Through Force

In [*People v. Olpindo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67910)[^351], Alexander Olpindo y Reyes was charged on October 6, 2008 with rape in relation to Republic Act No. 7610 before the Regional Trial Court of San Jose City, Branch 38. AAA, then 14 years old, testified that on February 27, 2008 he forcibly took her to an uninhabited place, tied her hands with rope, slammed her to the floor and had carnal knowledge of her.[^352] He claimed that they had been sweethearts for about five months and that the intercourse was consensual. On December 1, 2016, the trial court convicted him of rape, sentenced him to *reclusion perpetua* and ordered him to pay P50,000.00 as civil indemnity, P50,000.00 as moral damages and P30,000.00 as exemplary damages. It treated his flight for more than four years as a sign of guilt.[^353] The trial court then elevated the records on its own for automatic review. On November 22, 2019, the Court of Appeals dismissed the appeal and affirmed.[^354] The Supreme Court En Banc held that the trial court had misapplied *People v. Mateo* (as cited in [*Olpindo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/67910)) because a judgment imposing *reclusion perpetua* is not subject to automatic review. It laid down guidelines on appeals where *reclusion perpetua* or life imprisonment is imposed and declared the automatic review of death penalty cases suspended while Republic Act No. 9346 is in force.[^355] On the merits, it held that Olpindo employed threat, force and intimidation and that his sweetheart defense was unsupported. The Supreme Court affirmed the Court of Appeals Decision with modification, found Olpindo guilty of rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(a), sentenced him to *reclusion perpetua* and ordered him to pay AAA P75,000.00 each as civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages, with 6% interest a year from finality until full payment.[^356]

### Comprehensive Matrix Harmonizing Article 266-A, Article 336 and R.A. No. 7610 Section 5(b)

In [*People v. Tulagan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/65020)[^357], Salvador Tulagan was charged in two Informations with two counts of statutory rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(d) for inserting his finger into the vagina of 9-year-old AAA in September 2011 (Criminal Case No. SCC-6210) and for having carnal knowledge of her in October 2011 (Criminal Case No. SCC-6211).[^358] The Regional Trial Court convicted Tulagan of rape by sexual assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2 in Criminal Case No. SCC-6210 (6 years of *prision correccional* to 10 years of *prision mayor* and it found him guilty of Statutory Rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(d) and Article 266-B, sentenced him to *reclusion perpetua* and ordered him to pay AAA P75,000.00 each for civil indemnity, moral ### Presumed Lack of Discernment in Statutory Rape Under Paragraph 1(d)

In [*People v. Ronquillo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63449)[^367], Rommel Ronquillo was charged with statutory rape before the Regional Trial Court of Angeles City, Branch 60. On October 3, 2001, 11-year-old AAA was walking home from a friend's house after midnight when a man followed her, poked a gun at her and raped her.[^368] The trial court convicted Ronquillo of statutory rape and the Court of Appeals affirmed on November 11, 2013.[^369] The Supreme Court held that the prosecution proved carnal knowledge of a child under 12. Citing [*People v. Arpon*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/39488), it reiterated that consent is immaterial in statutory rape because the law presumes that a child of tender years cannot discern evil from good.[^370] It dismissed the appeal, affirmed the Court of Appeals Decision with modification as to damages and found Ronquillo guilty of statutory rape under Article 266-A, penalized under Article 266-B. ### Variance Doctrine Barring Sexual Assault Conviction Under a Carnal Knowledge Information

In [*People v. Caoili*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63383)[^372], biological father Noel Go Caoili alias "Boy Tagalog" was charged in the Information with rape through sexual intercourse under Article 266-A, paragraph 1 for allegedly having carnal knowledge of his daughter AAA (14 years, 1 month and 10 days old on October 23, 2005), but the trial evidence proved only that he inserted his fingers into her vagina.[^373] The Regional Trial Court convicted Caoili of rape by sexual assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2 (sentencing him to 10 years and 1 day of *prision mayor* to 17 years, 4 months and 1 day of *reclusion temporal* and ordering him to pay P50,000.00 civil indemnity, P50,000.00 moral damages and P50,000.00 exemplary damages), while the Court of Appeals set aside the conviction and remanded the case for further proceedings.[^374] Consolidating the petitions En Banc to resolve whether an accused charged under paragraph 1 may be convicted under paragraph 2, the Supreme Court held that rape through sexual assault under paragraph 2 has materially distinct elements and is not subsumed in rape by sexual intercourse under paragraph 1, but under the variance doctrine (Sections 4 and 5, Rule 120) the offender may be convicted of the lesser included offense of lascivious conduct under Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610.[^375] The Supreme Court denied both petitions and affirmed the Court of Appeals Decision and Resolution with modification, finding Noel Go Caoili guilty beyond reasonable doubt of lascivious conduct under Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610, sentencing him to *reclusion perpetua* (in view of the aggravating circumstance of relationship as biological father), ordering him to pay a fine of Php 15,000.00 and ordering him to pay AAA Php 75,000.00 each as civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages, with 6% interest per annum.[^376]

### Clinical Distinction Among Unconscious, Deprived of Reason, Demented and Intellectually Disabled Victims

In [*People v. Quintos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/58733)[^377], neighbor Enrique Quintos y Badilla was charged in two Informations (Criminal Case No. 07-0873 under Article 266-A, paragraph 2 for forcing his penis into the mouth of 21-year-old AAA on October 25, 2007 and Criminal Case No. 07-0874 under Article 266-A, paragraph 1 for having carnal knowledge of her on October 26, 2007), where clinical psychological testing proved that AAA was intellectually disabled with an IQ of 38 and a mental age of 6 years and 2 months.[^378] The Regional Trial Court convicted Quintos on both counts (sentencing him to *reclusion perpetua* on the carnal knowledge count and 6 years of *prision correccional* to 10 years and 1 day of *prision mayor* on the sexual assault count, with P50,000 civil indemnity, P50,000 moral damages and P25,000 exemplary damages per count, or P250,000.00 total) and the Court of Appeals affirmed while removing the 1-day excess from the maximum term of the sexual assault penalty and increasing exemplary damages from P25,000 to P30,000 per count.[^379] Systematically distinguishing "unconscious" and "deprived of reason" under paragraph 1(b), "demented" under paragraph 1(d) and "mentally retarded" or "intellectually disabled" (whose capacity to consent is measured by mental age rather than chronological age), the Supreme Court held that proof of AAA's 6-year-and-2-month mental age established her incapacity to consent on both counts even though her intellectual disability was not alleged in the Informations (which barred qualifying the crime under Article 266-B, paragraph 10).[^380] The Supreme Court affirmed the Court of Appeals Decision with modification, sentencing Quintos to *reclusion perpetua* without possibility for parole in Criminal Case No. 07-0874 and 6 years of *prision correccional* to 10 years of *prision mayor* in Criminal Case No. 07-0873 and increasing the awards to P100,000.00 each for moral damages, civil indemnity and exemplary damages per count (P600,000.00 total), with 6% interest per annum.[^381]

### Moral Ascendancy of Biological Father and Enhanced Damages for Multiple Incestuous Rapes

In [*People v. Buclao*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/57176)[^382], biological father Oliver Buclao was charged with two counts of qualified rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1 in relation to Article 266-B for having carnal knowledge of his minor daughter AAA on June 3, 2003 and in the third week of September 2004.[^383] The Regional Trial Court convicted him of two counts of rape (*reclusion perpetua* and P75,000.00 civil indemnity, P50,000.00 moral damages and P30,000.00 exemplary damages per count) and the Court of Appeals affirmed the convictions as qualified rape (*reclusion perpetua* without eligibility for parole).[^384] Holding that a father's moral ascendancy over his minor daughter substitutes for violence and intimidation and that the absence of hymenal lacerations does not negate rape, the Supreme Court affirmed the Court of Appeals Decision with modification by increasing civil indemnity to P150,000.00, moral damages to P150,000.00 and exemplary damages to P100,000.00 for each of the two counts in view of the depravity of multiple incestuous rapes, with 6% interest per annum.[^385]

### Criminalization of Marital Rape Under Article 266-A and Article 266-C

In [*People v. Jumawan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/56922)[^386], lawful husband Edgar Jumawan was charged in two amended Informations with two counts of rape under Republic Act No. 8353 (Article 266-A, paragraph 1[a]) for forcibly having carnal knowledge of his lawful wife KKK inside their Cagayan de Oro City residence on the nights of October 16 and 17, 1998 despite her physical illness, screams and resistance.[^387] The Regional Trial Court convicted Jumawan of two counts of rape (*reclusion perpetua* and P75,000.00 civil indemnity and P50,000.00 moral damages per count) and the Court of Appeals affirmed.[^388] Rejecting the husband's invocation of the common-law "irrevocable implied consent" theory in a landmark ruling on marital rape, the Supreme Court held that the unqualified definition of rape in Article 266-A, paragraph 1 and the spousal-forgiveness clause in the second paragraph of Article 266-C penalize a legal husband who forcibly rapes his wife.[^389] The Supreme Court affirmed the Court of Appeals Decision with modifications, finding Edgar Jumawan guilty beyond reasonable doubt of two counts of rape of his wife KKK, sentencing him to *reclusion perpetua* without eligibility for parole for each count and ordering him to pay KKK P50,000.00 civil indemnity (reduced from P75,000.00), P50,000.00 moral damages and P30,000.00 exemplary damages per count, with 6% interest per annum.[^390]

### Anal Penetration Proved Under a Carnal Knowledge Information Downgraded to Acts of Lasciviousness

In [*People v. Pareja*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/56553)[^391], common-law partner Bernabe Pareja y Cruz was charged in three Informations committed against 13-year-old AAA (two for rape by carnal knowledge in December 2003 and February 2004 in Criminal Case Nos. 04-1557 and 04-1556 and one for attempted rape in March 2004 in Criminal Case No. 04-1558).[^392] The Regional Trial Court acquitted him in Criminal Case No. 04-1558, convicted him of acts of lasciviousness in Criminal Case No. 04-1556 (where he placed himself on top of AAA and sucked her breasts) and convicted him of rape by carnal knowledge in Criminal Case No. 04-1557 (*reclusion perpetua* and P50,000.00 civil indemnity), which the Court of Appeals affirmed in toto.[^393] Because AAA testified in Criminal Case No. 04-1557 that Pareja inserted his penis into her anus whereas the Information charged carnal knowledge under Article 266-A, paragraph 1, the Supreme Court held that convicting him of rape by sexual assault under paragraph 2 would violate his constitutional right to be informed of the accusation and modified Criminal Case No. 04-1557 under the variance doctrine to a second count of acts of lasciviousness under Article 336, sentencing him on both counts to two indeterminate terms of 6 months of *arresto mayor* to 4 years and 2 months of *prision correccional* and ordering him to pay AAA P20,000.00 civil indemnity, P30,000.00 moral damages and P10,000.00 exemplary damages per count, with 6% interest per annum.[^394]

### Caption Variance and Extinguishment by Supervening Death

In the January 9, 2013 Decision in [*People v. Amistoso*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55457)[^395], biological father Anastacio Amistoso y Broca was charged with statutory rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(d) for forcibly having carnal knowledge of his daughter AAA (born June 2, 1988 and aged 12 years, 1 month and 8 days old on July 10, 2000).[^396] The Regional Trial Court convicted him of qualified rape (death and P75,000.00 civil indemnity and P50,000.00 moral damages) and the Court of Appeals reduced the penalty to *reclusion perpetua* without eligibility for parole under Republic Act No. 9346 and awarded P75,000.00 civil indemnity, P75,000.00 moral damages and P30,000.00 exemplary damages.[^397] The Supreme Court held that although AAA was already over 12 years old and the caption cited Article 266-A, paragraph 1(d), the body of the Information alleged carnal knowledge against her will and biological paternity under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(a) and Article 266-B, paragraph 1 and denied the appeal while affirming the Court of Appeals Decision with 6% interest.[^398] However, in the August 28, 2013 Resolution in [*People v. Amistoso y Broca*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/56168)[^399], after learning that Amistoso had died at the New Bilibid Prison on December 11, 2012 prior to the promulgation of the January 9, 2013 Decision (which had affirmed P75,000.00 civil indemnity, P75,000.00 moral damages and P30,000.00 exemplary damages, modifying the trial court's P50,000.00 moral damages), the Supreme Court applied Article 89, paragraph 1 of the Revised Penal Code, set aside its January 9, 2013 Decision and dismissed Criminal Case No. 10106.[^400]

### Statutory Rape of a Three-Year-and-Eleven-Month-Old Child

In [*People v. Piosang*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/56017)[^401], neighbor Ricardo Piosang was charged with statutory rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(d) for pushing three-year-and-eleven-month-old AAA (born July 21, 1994) and her eleven-year-old playmate CCC into an outdoor comfort room on July 8, 1998, pointing a fan knife at CCC and inserting his penis into AAA's vagina.[^402] The Regional Trial Court convicted Piosang of statutory rape (*reclusion perpetua* and P50,000.00 civil indemnity, P50,000.00 moral damages and P25,000.00 exemplary damages) and the Court of Appeals affirmed while increasing the awards to P75,000.00 civil indemnity, P75,000.00 moral damages and P30,000.00 exemplary damages.[^403] Holding that statutory rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(d) requires only proof of carnal knowledge and that the victim was under 12 years of age, the Supreme Court denied the appeal and affirmed the Court of Appeals Decision in toto.[^404]

### Unproven Stepfather Marriage Contract and Waiver of Variance Between Paragraph 1 and Paragraph 2

In [*People v. Lomaque*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55877)[^405], Guillermo Lomaque was charged under separate Informations with 13 counts of rape by sexual intercourse and one count of acts of lasciviousness against his stepdaughter AAA, who was born on September 15, 1985. The abuse began on May 8, 1993, when she was about eight years old.[^406] The Regional Trial Court and the Court of Appeals convicted him on seven counts of qualified rape (*reclusion perpetua* and P75,000 civil indemnity and P50,000 moral damages per count), one count of rape by sexual assault in Criminal Case No. Q-00-96392 (where the Information charged vaginal penetration but AAA testified to oral penile insertion) and one count of acts of lasciviousness in Criminal Case No. Q-00-96402.[^407] On appeal, the Supreme Court held that the prosecution's failure to offer the marriage contract between Lomaque and AAA's mother reduced the seven counts of carnal knowledge to simple rape, but sustained his conviction for rape by sexual assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2 in Criminal Case No. Q-00-96392 because he failed to object to the variance at trial.[^408] The Supreme Court affirmed the Court of Appeals Decision with modifications: (1) on the seven counts of Simple Rape, it sentenced him to *reclusion perpetua* and ordered him to pay P50,000.00 civil indemnity (reduced from P75,000), P50,000.00 moral damages and P30,000.00 exemplary damages per count, (2) in Criminal Case No. Q-00-96392, it sentenced him for Rape by Sexual Assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2 to 6 years of *prision correccional* to 12 years of *prision mayor* and P30,000.00 each for civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages and (3) in Criminal Case No. Q-00-96402, it sentenced him for Acts of Lasciviousness in relation to Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610 to 14 years and 8 months to 17 years and 4 months of *reclusion temporal* with P20,000.00 civil indemnity and P15,000.00 moral damages.[^409]

### Absorption of Forcible Abduction, Retroactivity of R.A. No. 9344, Cunnilingus Under Paragraph 2 and Duplicitous Informations

Applying Article 266-D and holding that rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(a) absorbed the forcible abduction because rape was the offenders' primary objective, the Supreme Court affirmed with modification by declaring Sabadlab guilty of simple rape (*reclusion perpetua* and P50,000.00 civil indemnity, P50,000.00 moral damages and P30,000.00 exemplary damages in view of the unpleaded generic aggravating circumstance of use of a deadly weapon, with 6% interest).[^421]
- In [*People v. Arpon*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/39488)[^422], maternal uncle Henry Arpon y Juntilla was convicted by the Regional Trial Court on eight counts of rape against his minor niece AAA (death and P50,000.00 civil indemnity and P50,000.00 moral damages per count) and the Court of Appeals reduced the penalty to *reclusion perpetua* on all eight counts and added P25,000.00 exemplary damages per count. Holding that rape is a crime against persons not extinguished by an affidavit of desistance and that unspecified licking without proof of which genital part was touched is acts of lasciviousness whereas proven finger insertion by a biological father is qualified rape through sexual assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2 and Article 266-B, paragraph 1, the Supreme Court affirmed with modifications by sentencing Bonaagua on three counts of Qualified Rape Through Sexual Assault to 10 years of *prision mayor* to 17 years and 4 months of *reclusion temporal* with P30,000.00 each for civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages (P90,000.00 per count) and on one count of Acts of Lasciviousness under Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610 to 13 years, 9 months and 11 days to 16 years, 5 months and 10 days of *reclusion temporal* with P20,000.00 civil indemnity, P15,000.00 moral damages, P15,000.00 exemplary damages and a P15,000.00 fine.[^425]

Chingh*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/35870)[^426], Armando Chingh y Parcia was charged in a single Information with both statutory rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(d) and rape through sexual assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2 for inserting his finger and then his penis into the vagina of 10-year-old VVV while standing in a vacant lot on March 11, 2004. ### Foundational 2002–2007 Rulings Under Republic Act No. 8353

## Key Cases

- [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314)[^487] (2024 En Banc Decision): Abandoned the designation "qualified statutory rape" because the victim's age below the statutory threshold is an inherent element absorbed by special qualifying circumstances under Article 266-B, adopted the nomenclature "qualified rape of a minor" with increased awards of PHP 150,000.00 each for civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages and affirmed a concurrent conviction for sexual assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2 in relation to Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610 (13 years, 9 months and 11 days to 16 years, 5 months and 10 days of *reclusion temporal*, PHP 15,000.00 fine and PHP 50,000.00 each for civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages).
- [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69910)[^488] (2025 En Banc Resolution): Denied the accused father's motion for reconsideration with finality and extended the nomenclature "Qualified Rape of a minor" and its PHP 150,000.00 floor damages to all ten special qualifying aggravating circumstances under Article 266-B whenever the victim of carnal knowledge under Article 266-A, paragraph 1 is below the statutory age or has a mental age below the statutory threshold, while confirming that those guidelines do not apply to rape through sexual assault under paragraph 2.
- [*People v. Agao*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68651)[^489] (2022 En Banc Decision): Clarified the "mere touching" and "mere epidermal contact" cases and established that consummated rape by sexual intercourse under Article 266-A, paragraph 1 (and by analogy object penetration under paragraph 2) requires at least the slightest penetration of the cleft of the labia majora (the vulval or pudendal cleft), laid down five circumstantial guideposts for inferring penile penetration and modified the common-law partner's conviction to one count of statutory rape and one count of simple rape (*reclusion perpetua* per count).
- [*People v. Tulagan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/65020)[^490] (2019 En Banc Decision): Harmonized Article 266-A, paragraphs 1 and 2 with Article 336 and Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610, holding that sexual assault of a child under 12 is designated "Sexual Assault under paragraph 2, Article 266-A of the Revised Penal Code, in relation to Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610" and penalized by *reclusion temporal* medium (12 years, 10 months and 21 days to 15 years, 6 months and 20 days of *reclusion temporal* and P50,000.00 each for civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages, modifying the trial court's Php20,000.00 awards and the Court of Appeals' P30,000.00 awards), alongside *reclusion perpetua* and P75,000.00 each for statutory rape (modifying the trial court's Php50,000.00/Php30,000.00 awards and the Court of Appeals' P100,000.00 awards).
- [*People v. Caoili*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63383)[^491] (2017 En Banc Decision): Held that because rape by sexual intercourse under paragraph 1 and rape through sexual assault under paragraph 2 have substantially distinct elements, the variance doctrine bars convicting a father charged under paragraph 1 of rape by sexual assault under paragraph 2 when the evidence proves only finger insertion and instead convicted him of the lesser included offense of lascivious conduct under Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610 (*reclusion perpetua*, Php 15,000.00 fine and Php 75,000.00 each for civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages, modifying the trial court's P50,000.00 awards).
- [*People v. Jumawan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/56922)[^492] (2014 Decision): Landmark ruling holding that Republic Act No. 8353, through the unqualified definition of rape in Article 266-A, paragraph 1 and the spousal-forgiveness proviso in Article 266-C, abolished the common-law marital exemption and criminalized marital rape, affirming the husband's conviction of two counts of rape of his wife (*reclusion perpetua* without eligibility for parole and P50,000.00 civil indemnity [reduced from P75,000.00], P50,000.00 moral damages and P30,000.00 exemplary damages per count).
- [*People v. Quintos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/58733)[^493] (2014 Decision): Systematically distinguished "unconscious" and "deprived of reason" under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(b), "demented" (dementia) under paragraph 1(d) and "mentally retarded" or "intellectually disabled" (whose capacity to consent is governed by mental age), affirming convictions for simple rape (*reclusion perpetua* without possibility for parole) and rape by sexual assault (6 years of *prision correccional* to 10 years of *prision mayor*) with P100,000.00 each for civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages per count (P600,000.00 total, modifying the trial court's P50,000/P25,000 awards totaling P250,000.00 and the Court of Appeals' P30,000 exemplary damages).
- [*People v. XXX278240*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70398)[^494] (2026 Decision): Held that a community *albularyo* who deceived a 15-year-old girl into submitting to digital and penile penetration as an ostensible medical procedure to cure her hernia committed two counts of rape by fraudulent machination under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(c) (*reclusion perpetua* and PHP 75,000.00 each for civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages per count, modifying the Court of Appeals' PHP 100,000.00 awards) and two counts of rape through sexual assault under paragraph 2 penalized under the Revised Penal Code (4 years of *prision correccional* to 9 years of *prision mayor* and PHP 30,000.00 each per count, modifying the Court of Appeals' PHP 50,000.00 awards) rather than Republic Act No. 7610 Section 5(b) because a deceived patient does not "indulge" in sexual activity.

## Suggested Answers

**Question 1:** In June 2021, X, the biological father of 10-year-old AAA, called AAA into their bedroom, inserted his index finger into her vagina and then inserted his erect penis slightly into the cleft of her labia majora without rupturing her hymen. X was charged in two separate Informations: (1) under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(d) in relation to Article 266-B, paragraph 1 for having carnal knowledge of his 10-year-old biological daughter and (2) under Article 266-A, paragraph 2 for inserting his finger into the vagina of his 10-year-old daughter. At trial, the medico-legal officer testified that AAA's hymen remained intact and the defense argued that (a) the first charge was at most attempted rape because AAA's hymen was intact and there was no deep vaginal penetration and (b) if convicted on the first charge, the proper designation is "qualified statutory rape." Rule on both defense arguments and state the proper crime designations, penalties and civil liabilities on both charges.

**Answer:** Both defense arguments are untenable. First, as the Supreme Court En Banc held in [*People v. Agao*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68651)[^497], full penile penetration or rupture of the hymen is not required to consummate rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1, because rape reaches the consummated stage as soon as the offender's erect penis penetrates the cleft of the labia majora (the vulval or pudendal cleft) in even the slightest degree and an intact hymen does not negate carnal knowledge ([*People v. Boromeo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46881)[^498]). Second, in [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314)[^499] and [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69910)[^500], the Supreme Court En Banc abandoned the designation "qualified statutory rape" because the victim's age below the statutory threshold (under 12 years old prior to Republic Act No. 11648, which took effect on March 22, 2022, as explained in [*People v. XXX*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69802)[^501]) is an inherent element of statutory rape that is absorbed by the special qualifying aggravating circumstance of minority and biological paternity under Article 266-B, paragraph 1. Accordingly, on the first Information, X must be convicted of **Qualified Rape of a minor** under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(d) in relation to Article 266-B, paragraph 1, sentenced to *reclusion perpetua* without eligibility for parole pursuant to Republic Act No. 9346 and ordered to pay AAA PHP 150,000.00 as civil indemnity, PHP 150,000.00 as moral damages and PHP 150,000.00 as exemplary damages ([*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314)[^502] and [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69910)[^503]). On the second Information for finger insertion, X is guilty of **Sexual Assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2 of the Revised Penal Code** (which, as held in [*People v. Tulagan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/65020)[^504] and [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314)[^505], carries *reclusion temporal* in its medium period, a PHP 15,000.00 fine and PHP 50,000.00 each for civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages when prosecuted in relation to Section 5[b] of Republic Act No. 7610, or *reclusion temporal* under Article 266-B, paragraph 1 with PHP 50,000.00 each for civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages without a fine when qualified by biological paternity as in [*People v. ZZZ*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69591)[^506]).[^507] As the Supreme Court En Banc clarified in [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69910)[^508], the nomenclature "Qualified Rape of a minor" and its PHP 150,000.00 damages tier apply only to rape through carnal knowledge under paragraph 1 and not to rape through sexual assault under paragraph 2.

**Question 2:** Y was charged in a single Information with rape by sexual intercourse under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(a) for allegedly having carnal knowledge of his 14-year-old biological daughter BBB through force and intimidation. During trial, BBB credibly testified that Y did not insert his penis into her vagina, but instead forcibly inserted his fingers into her vagina. Y timely objected to any conviction for a crime not charged in the Information. Can the trial court convict Y of (a) rape by sexual intercourse under Article 266-A, paragraph 1, or (b) rape through sexual assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2? What crime, if any, may Y be convicted of?

**Answer:** The trial court cannot convict Y of either rape by sexual intercourse under Article 266-A, paragraph 1 or rape through sexual assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2, but must convict him of the lesser included offense of **Lascivious Conduct under Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610**. As the Supreme Court En Banc held in [*People v. Caoili*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63383)[^509] (and similarly in [*People v. Pareja*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/56553)[^510]), paragraphs 1 and 2 of Article 266-A define two materially distinct modes of rape: paragraph 1 requires penile penetration of the female vagina (carnal knowledge), whereas paragraph 2 penalizes inserting the penis into another person's mouth or anal orifice or inserting any instrument or object (including a finger) into the genital or anal orifice of another person. Because finger insertion is not carnal knowledge, Y cannot be convicted under paragraph 1.[^511] Furthermore, because rape through sexual assault under paragraph 2 has distinct essential elements that are not subsumed in or necessarily included in a charge of carnal knowledge under paragraph 1, convicting Y of rape through sexual assault under an Information charging only carnal knowledge (where Y did not waive the variance by failing to object at trial, unlike the accused in [*People v. Lomaque*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55877)[^512]) would violate his constitutional right to be informed of the nature and cause of the accusation against him.[^513] Instead, under the variance doctrine in Sections 4 and 5, Rule 120 of the Rules of Criminal Procedure, the offense proved is included in the offense charged as **Lascivious Conduct under Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610** (since BBB was 14 years old, between 12 and under 18), for which Y is punishable by *reclusion perpetua* (in view of the aggravating circumstance of biological paternity), a fine of Php 15,000.00 and Php 75,000.00 each for civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages ([*People v. Caoili*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63383)[^514] and [*People v. Tulagan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/65020)[^515]).

**Question 3:** Evaluate the validity of the following defenses raised in three separate prosecutions under Article 266-A: (a) In a prosecution of a lawful husband for forcibly having carnal knowledge of his wife under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(a), the husband contends that marriage confers irrevocable implied consent to sexual intercourse and extinguishes any liability for marital rape. (b) In a prosecution of a 24-year-old man for raping a 14-year-old girl under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(a), the accused argues that the crime cannot be rape because the girl offered no tenacious physical struggle and merely pleaded with him to stop and tried to stand up while he pushed her back down. (c) In a prosecution of a traditional healer (*albularyo*) who tricked a 15-year-old girl into submitting to digital and penile penetration inside her room by telling her it was a medical healing procedure required to cure her hernia, the prosecutor argues on appeal that the digital penetration count should be penalized by *reclusion temporal* under Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610 rather than *prision mayor* under Article 266-A, paragraph 2 and Article 266-B.

**Answer:** All three contentions lack merit. First, in [*People v. Jumawan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/56922)[^516], the Supreme Court held that Republic Act No. 8353 abolished Lord Hale's common-law "irrevocable implied consent" marital exemption: the unqualified definition of rape in Article 266-A, paragraph 1, read together with the second paragraph of Article 266-C (which expressly refers to "the legal husband who is the offender" and provides that only the wife's subsequent forgiveness extinguishes the criminal action or penalty), criminalizes marital rape because a marriage license is not a license for a husband to forcibly rape his wife. Second, in [*People v. XXX258643*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70301)[^517] (and [*People v. Sabadlab*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/29604)[^518]), the Supreme Court applied Article 266-D of the Revised Penal Code to hold that Republic Act No. 8353 abrogated the 1970s rule that "resistance must be manifested and tenacious" and directed that any physical overt act manifesting resistance in any degree (including verbal pleas to stop and the simple act of trying to stand up prior to, at the beginning of or in the middle of the sexual act) is accepted as evidence of rape and the offender's refusal to heed that resistance constitutes force under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(a).[^519] Third, in [*People v. XXX278240*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70398)[^520], the Supreme Court held that while an *albularyo* who deceives a 15-year-old patient into submitting to digital and penile penetration as an ostensible medical cure commits rape by fraudulent machination under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(c) and rape through sexual assault under Article 266-A, paragraph 2, Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610 does not apply to elevate the penalty for the sexual assault count: under *People v. Gramatica*, the statutory term "indulges" in Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610 requires conscious participation in sexual activity, whereas a patient deceived into believing that the act was a medical healing procedure did not "indulge" in sexual intercourse or lascivious conduct, leaving the sexual assault punishable by *prision mayor* under Article 266-A, paragraph 2 and Article 266-B.

# Quotes

## Abandonment of "Qualified Statutory Rape" and Adoption of "Qualified Rape of a Minor"

> "Corollary, the victim being below the statutory age or the mental retardation of the victim comparable to the intellectual capacity of a child below the statutory age cannot be appreciated as an inherent and special qualifying aggravating circumstance at the same time. Indeed, the special qualifying aggravating circumstances of minority and relationship and the age of the victim being below 7 years old absorb the inherent circumstance of the victim being under the statutory age. Similarly, the special qualifying aggravating circumstance of the accused's knowledge of the mental disability of the victim at the time of the commission of rape absorbs the inherent circumstance of the mental retardation of the victim comparable to the intellectual capacity of a child below the statutory age. As such, the proper designation of the crime must be qualified rape and not qualified statutory rape. The Court affirms its previous rulings that the presence of special qualifying circumstances under Article 266-B of the RPC raises the crime of statutory rape to qualified rape."[^521]

> "1. The crime shall be denominated as QUALIFIED RAPE of a minor and not qualified statutory rape if any of the special qualifying aggravating circumstances mentioned in Article 266- B of the RPC is present and when the victim is below the statutory age or is suffering from mental retardation comparable to the intellectual capacity of a child below the statutory age. The floor amounts as to the civil liability of the accused shall be PHP 150,000.00 civil indemnity, PHP 150,000.00 moral damages, and PHP 150,000.00 exemplary damages without prejudice to further increase if there are other factual circumstances showing depravity of the accused in the commission of the crime."[^522]

## The Pudendal-Cleft Penetration Threshold for Consummated Rape

> "With careful and decisive reference to the anatomical illustration above, the Court clarifies that when jurisprudence refers to "mere touching," it is not sufficient that the penis grazed over the pudendum or the fleshy surface of the labia majora. Instead, what jurisprudence considers as consummated rape when it describes a penis touching the vagina is the penis penetrating the cleft of the labia maiora, however minimum or slight. Similarly, a mere grazing by the penis of the fleshy portion, not the vulval cleft of the labia majora, will also constitute only attempted rape and not consummated rape, since the same cannot be considered to have achieved the slightest level of penetration. Stated differently, the Court here elucidates that "mere touch" of the penis on the labia majora legally contemplates not mere surface touch or skin contact, but the slightest penetration of the vulval or pudendal cleft, however minimum in degree."[^523]

## Dichotomy Between Rape by Sexual Intercourse and Rape by Sexual Assault

> "The language of paragraphs 1 and 2 of Article 266-A of the RPC, as amended by R.A. No. 8353, provides the elements that substantially differentiate the two forms of rape, i.e., rape by sexual intercourse and rape by sexual assault. It is through legislative process that the dichotomy between these two modes of rape was created. To broaden the scope of rape by sexual assault, by eliminating its legal distinction from rape through sexual intercourse, calls for judicial legislation which We cannot traverse without violating the principle of separation of powers. The Court remains steadfast in confining its powers within the constitutional sphere of applying the law as enacted by the Legislature."[^524]

## Clinical Distinction Among Unconscious, Deprived of Reason, Demented and Intellectually Disabled Victims

> "Thus, a person with a chronological age of 7 years and a normal mental age is as capable of making decisions and giving consent as a person with a chronological age of 35 and a mental age of 7. Both are considered incapable of giving rational consent because both are not yet considered to have reached the level of maturity that gives them the capability to make rational decisions, especially on matters involving sexuality. Decision-making is a function of the mind. Hence, a person’s capacity to decide whether to give consent or to express resistance to an adult activity is determined not by his or her chronological age but by his or her mental age. Therefore, in determining whether a person is “twelve (12) years of age” under Article 266-A(1)(d), the interpretation should be in accordance with either the chronological age of the child if he or she is not suffering from intellectual disability, or the mental age if intellectual disability is established."[^525]

# Tips

> [!TIP]
> **1. Verify the Date of Commission Against the Statutory Age Threshold (October 22, 1997 vs. March 22, 2022).**
> Before classifying an offense as statutory rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(d), check the exact date of commission against the effectivity of Republic Act No. 11648 on March 22, 2022 ([*People v. XXX*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69802)). If the sexual intercourse occurred before March 22, 2022, the statutory age threshold under Republic Act No. 8353 is **under 12 years of age** (or a mental age below 12 years old, as in [*People v. Dela Paz*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44868) and [*People v. Quintos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/58733)). If the victim had already turned 12 (even by one month and eight days, as in [*People v. Amistoso*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55457)), a pre-2022 charge cannot prosper as statutory rape under paragraph 1(d) unless the body of the Information also alleges force, threat, intimidation or lack of consent under paragraph 1(a). For offenses committed on or after March 22, 2022, the statutory age threshold under Republic Act No. 11648 is **under 16 years of age** ([*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314) and [*People v. XXX*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69802)).

> [!NOTE]
> **2. Use the Exact Crime Nomenclature Mandated by [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314) (2024), [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69910) (2025) and [*People v. XXX257662*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70012) (2025).**
> Never use the abandoned designation "qualified statutory rape" ([*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314)). When an Information for carnal knowledge under Article 266-A, paragraph 1 alleges and the prosecution proves any of the ten special qualifying aggravating circumstances under Article 266-B alongside the victim's age below the statutory threshold (or mental retardation comparable to a child below the statutory age), designate the crime as **"Qualified Rape of a minor"** and award the PHP 150,000.00 floor for civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages ([*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69910)). When a special qualifying circumstance is present but the victim is at or above the statutory age, designate the crime as **"Qualified Rape"** with the PHP 100,000.00 damages floor ([*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69910) and [*People v. ZZZ*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69591)). When no qualifying circumstance is present and the victim is at or above the statutory age, designate the crime simply as **"Rape"** or **"Rape under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(a), penalized under Article 266-B of the Revised Penal Code"** and never as "Simple Rape" ([*People v. XXX257662*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70012)). Remember that "Qualified Rape of a minor" applies only to carnal knowledge under paragraph 1 and never to sexual assault under paragraph 2 ([*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69910)).

> [!TIP]
> **Do Not Charge Paragraph 1 Carnal Knowledge When the Evidence Shows Only Paragraph 2 Sexual Assault.** Under the variance doctrine in [*People v. Caoili*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63383) and [*People v. Pareja*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/56553), an Information that alleges only penile-vaginal carnal knowledge under Article 266-A, paragraph 1 cannot support a conviction for rape through sexual assault under paragraph 2 if the trial evidence proves only finger insertion ([*People v. Caoili*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63383)) or anal penile insertion ([*People v. Pareja*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/56553)). Unless the accused waives the variance by failing to object at trial ([*People v. Lomaque*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55877)) or fails to move to quash a duplicitous Information charging both modes before entering his plea ([*People v. Chingh*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/35870)), the court can convict him only of the lesser included offense of lascivious conduct under Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610 ([*People v. Caoili*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63383)) or acts of lasciviousness under Article 336 ([*People v. Pareja*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/56553)).

> [!TIP]
> **Distinguish "Stepfather" From "Common-Law Spouse of the Victim's Mother" in the Information and Documentary Proof.** Pleading that the accused is the victim's "stepfather" requires presenting the marriage certificate proving a valid marriage between the accused and the victim's mother ([*People v. Lomaque*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55877)). If the offender is actually the mother's live-in partner ([*People v. Escultor*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46492) and [*People v. Agao*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68651)) or an uncle-in-law married to the mother's sister ([*People v. Cabungan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55486)), an Information alleging "stepfather" will fail to qualify the rape under Article 266-B, paragraph 1. Conversely, pleading and proving that the offender is the "common-law spouse of the parent of the victim" together with the victim's minority properly qualifies the rape ([*People v. Boromeo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/46881)).

> [!TIP]
> **Remember the Third-Degree Collateral Limit for Relatives.** Uncles and aunts are third civil degree collateral relatives within Article 266-B, paragraph 1 ([*People v. Arpon*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/39488)), provided the relationship is specifically alleged in the Information ([*People v. Teodoro*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55618)). A first-cousin relationship did not qualify the rape in [*People v. Calongui*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/40624) and was held outside the concept of relationship under Article 15.

> [!TIP]
> **Plead Both the Victim's Mental Disability and the Offender's Knowledge Thereof Under Article 266-B(10).** To qualify rape under Article 266-B, paragraph 10, the Information must allege not only that the victim suffers from a mental disability, emotional disorder or physical handicap, but also that the offender **knew** of that condition at the time of the commission of the crime ([*People v. Dela Paz*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44868)). Omitting either allegation limits the conviction to simple rape even if the offender admits his knowledge on the witness stand ([*People v. Monticalvo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/55492), [*People v. Nieto*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44765) and [*People v. Quintos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/58733)).

> [!TIP]
> **Establish the Pudendal-Cleft Threshold or the Five Circumstantial Guideposts for Each Count.** When examining a child victim across multiple counts tried jointly, prosecutors must elicit specific testimony establishing at least slight penetration of the vulval or pudendal cleft (or one of the five circumstantial guideposts such as genital pain, bleeding, gaping or reddened labia minora, invisible hymenal tags or other genital injury under [*People v. Agao*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68651)) for **each separate count**. Leaving a child's statement that she was "raped" or "undressed" without follow-up questions ([*People v. Mingming*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/48272)), failing to elicit testimony on two charged dates ([*People v. Flordeliz*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/53698) and [*People v. Soriano*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/51351)) or leaving cunnilingus testimony at "touched my private part and licked it" without identifying which part of the vagina was touched ([*People v. Bonaagua*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/34342)) will result in acquittal or downgrading on those counts.

> [!TIP]
> **Check Whether a Child Victim of Paragraph 2 Sexual Assault Was Deceived by Fraudulent Machination.** While non-qualified sexual assault of a child under the statutory age is ordinarily penalized by *reclusion temporal* medium under Article 266-A, paragraph 2 in relation to Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610 ([*People v. Chingh*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/35870), [*People v. Villacampa*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63854), [*People v. Tulagan*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/65020) and [*People v. ABC260708*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69314)), Section 5(b) of Republic Act No. 7610 does not apply when an *albularyo* or offender uses fraudulent machination under Article 266-A, paragraph 1(c) and paragraph 2 to deceive a 15-year-old patient into believing the act is a medical healing procedure, because a deceived victim does not "indulge" in sexual activity ([*People v. XXX278240*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70398)).
