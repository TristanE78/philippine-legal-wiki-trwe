> Article 100. Civil liability of person guilty of felony.-Every person criminally liable for a felony is also civilly liable.

[^revised-penal-code]

# Explanation

## Civil liability arising from a felony

Article 100 attaches a civil obligation to every felony. A person criminally liable for a felony is also civilly liable.[^revised-penal-code] The provision rests on the dual nature of a crime. In [*Dy v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/62270), the Supreme Court explained that a criminal act is a breach of law against the State. The same act also injures a private person, who may recover civil damages. This private civil component is the civil liability *ex delicto*.[^2]

The criminal and civil actions serve different ends. In [*Quinto v. Andres*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/41112), the Supreme Court stated that the criminal action seeks to punish the offender, deter others and maintain social order. The civil action seeks only the restitution, reparation or indemnification of the private offended party for the damage caused by the felonious act.[^3]

### What civil liability includes

Article 104 fixes the content of the civil liability established in Articles 100 to 103. It includes restitution, reparation of the damage caused and indemnification for consequential damages, as [*Proton Pilipinas Corporation v. Republic of the Philippines*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/42038) quoted it.[^4] [*Quinto v. Andres*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/41112) stated the same three components.[^5] In criminal cases where the imposable penalty is reclusion perpetua to death, [*People v. Jugueta*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/61867) noted that the Supreme Court generally awards three kinds of damages. These are civil indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages.[^6]

Civil indemnity is a separate head of recovery. In [*People v. Ramos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64549), the Supreme Court expressly derived civil indemnity from Article 100. It awarded indemnity apart from moral and exemplary damages and increased the indemnity and moral awards below from P50,000 to P75,000 each.[^7] [*People v. Atop*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/35619) distinguished Article 100 indemnity from moral damages under Civil Code Articles 2217 and 2219. It ordered P150,000 indemnity and P50,000 moral damages for three rapes.[^8]

An existing moral award does not take the place of omitted indemnity. [*People v. Apilo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/34160) added P50,000 civil indemnity for each of two rapes while the trial court's P40,000 moral award for each count remained payable.[^9] A change in the designation of the crime does not erase the civil award either. In [*People v. Martinez and Granada*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64046), the Supreme Court corrected the designation to rape under Article 266-A(1)(b). It supplied the civil indemnity omitted below and increased exemplary damages from P25,000 to P75,000.[^10]

### Present amounts in crimes punished by reclusion perpetua

The amounts in the older decisions on this page were the awards of those judgments. They are not the present amounts. In [*People v. Jugueta*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/61867), the Supreme Court En Banc fixed the damages in criminal cases where the imposable penalty is reclusion perpetua to death. For simple or qualified rape, it set the following:[^11]

| Case | Civil indemnity | Moral damages | Exemplary damages |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Death reduced to reclusion perpetua under R.A. 9346 | P100,000 | P100,000 | P100,000 |
| Reclusion perpetua, other cases | P75,000 | P75,000 | P75,000 |

When the circumstances call for reclusion perpetua only, the Supreme Court held that the proper amounts are P75,000 each, regardless of the number of qualifying aggravating circumstances present.[^12] The 2018 judgments in [*People v. Ramos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64549)[^13] and [*People v. Martinez and Granada*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64046)[^14] awarded those amounts.

## How the civil action proceeds

### Implied institution with the criminal action

The civil action for the civil liability arising from the offense is deemed instituted with the criminal action. The exceptions are a waiver of the civil action, a reservation of the right to file it separately and the filing of the civil action before the criminal action. [*Quinto v. Andres*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/41112) stated this rule and described the two actions as merged into one composite proceeding in which the criminal action predominates.[^15] [*Budlong v. Apalisok*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1983/jun1983/gr_l-60151_1983.html) applied the same rule to a guilty plea.[^16]

The trial court must hear the civil claim even when the accused pleads guilty. In [*Budlong v. Apalisok*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1983/jun1983/gr_l-60151_1983.html), the City Court of Tagbilaran sentenced the accused at once and denied the fiscal's motion to prove civil liability because the accused had applied for probation. The Supreme Court held that the judge overlooked the rule that every person criminally liable is also civilly liable. It set aside the orders and directed hearings on the civil liability. It did not itself assess damages.[^17]

A lesser-offense plea does not shrink the civil consequences of the injury actually caused. [*Heirs of Gevero v. Guihing Agricultural & Development Corporation*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44353) concerned a death followed by a plea to reckless imprudence resulting in damage to property. The Supreme Court rejected limiting the civil liability to the value of the damaged bicycle. It reinstated the execution orders issued after a civil hearing in the criminal case.[^18]

### Participation of the private offended party

The private offended party's interest in the criminal case is limited to the civil liability. [*Banco De Oro Unibank, Inc. v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69675) held that the offended party may intervene at any stage, even on appeal, as long as the civil action has not been waived, reserved or instituted separately before the criminal action.[^19] BDO had taken part at trial and had done none of these. The Supreme Court allowed its intervention to protect its civil interest. It confined its ruling to intervention and left the Court of Appeals to determine liability, if any.[^20]

### Subsidiary liability of the employer

The employer's subsidiary liability under Article 103 rests on the employee's civil liability under Article 100. [*Carpio v. Doroja*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1989/dec1989/gr_84516_1989.html) allowed that subsidiary liability to be determined and enforced in the criminal proceeding after the employer is heard. A separate complaint was unnecessary on those facts.[^21] The conditions for subsidiary execution must still be met. [*Pangonorom v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/43505) found execution against the employer premature because the conviction was not yet final and the employee's insolvency had not been proved.[^22]

### Proof of the civil claim

The civil claim requires only a preponderance of evidence. [*Quinto v. Andres*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/41112) required the private complainant to prove her cause of action by that standard and to rely on the strength of her own evidence.[^23] The Supreme Court found that she had not proved the deliberate acts alleged in the Information. It did not find that the accused killed the child. It also did not hold that accidental death was the only possible explanation.[^24]

## Effect of acquittal and death of the accused

### Acquittal

The extinction of the penal action does not by itself extinguish the civil action. [*Dy v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/62270), quoting *Manantan v. Court of Appeals*, explained that the law recognizes two kinds of acquittal. An acquittal on the ground that the accused is not the author of the act closes the door to civil liability *ex delicto*. An acquittal based on reasonable doubt does not exempt the accused from civil liability that a preponderance of evidence proves.[^25] [*Cadungog v. Sung Ha Jung*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69921) listed the instances in which civil liability survives acquittal. These are an acquittal based on reasonable doubt, a declaration that the liability is only civil and civil liability that does not arise from the crime charged.[^26]

An acquittal for want of a crime does not allow the criminal court to award a contractual debt. In [*Dy v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/62270), the accused was acquitted of estafa because the obligation arose from a loan. The Supreme Court held that the civil action deemed instituted could not prosper because there was no delict to source it. Any contractual obligation had to be litigated in a separate civil action.[^27] [*Cadungog v. Sung Ha Jung*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69921) applied the same distinction. It annulled the contractual civil portion of a criminal judgment that the trial court had rendered after acquitting the accused.[^28]

### Death of the accused

The death of the accused pending appeal extinguishes the criminal liability and the civil liability based solely on the offense. [*Bernardo v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/61354) explained that death amounts to an acquittal because it prevents the finding of guilt needed to overcome the presumption of innocence.[^29] Independent civil liabilities survive death. They may be pursued against the estate. In B.P. Blg. 22 cases, where the criminal action includes the civil action, the Supreme Court continued to adjudicate the contractual claim against the substituted heirs.[^30]

### Release under youth provisions

Release under the youth provisions applied in a case does not necessarily extinguish its civil obligation. [*People v. Garcia and Mindaroza*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1981/jun1981/gr_45280_81_1981.html) applied Presidential Decree No. 603. The Supreme Court reviewed guilt before determining Mindaroza's civil liability and held that his discharge after rehabilitation did not erase that obligation.[^31]

## Independent sources of civil liability

The same act may also create civil liability from other sources. [*Bernardo v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/61354) stated that Article 1157 of the Civil Code lists law, contracts, quasi-contracts and quasi-delicts as sources of obligation besides crime. A person may be free of civil liability *ex delicto* and still be liable on a contract or under another law. The offended party cannot recover damages twice for the same act or omission.[^32]

The allegations of the complaint determine the source. In [*Cancio v. Isip*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/51647), the complaint to collect the value of bounced checks was based on contract. The claimant's earlier reservation of the civil action *ex delicto* did not matter. An independent civil action needs no reservation in the criminal case under the present Rules. The dismissal of the estafa cases did not bar it.[^33]

The source also decides the nature of an employer's liability. [*Safeguard Security Agency, Inc. v. Tangco*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/40427) concerned a separate action for quasi-delict. The Supreme Court held the agency primarily and solidarily liable under Civil Code Article 2180. The guard's final homicide conviction did not turn that action into one based on delict.[^34]

A statutory tax obligation is another distinct source. [*Proton Pilipinas Corporation v. Republic of the Philippines*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/42038) held that the corporation's customs duties and taxes arose from law. Their collection was not the civil aspect of the anti-graft charges against its officers.[^35]

## Case Discussions

### [*People v. Ramos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64549): Increased indemnity and moral damages

Ramos and AAA worked at a hotel in Baguio City and lived in its staff quarters. On December 27, 2007, he followed her into the recreation room, forced her onto the sofa and had carnal knowledge of her against her will. She told two co-workers right after. The RTC of La Trinidad, Benguet, Branch 62 convicted Ramos of rape by force and intimidation. The Court of Appeals affirmed. Ramos appealed to the Supreme Court. His claimed romantic relationship with AAA was not proved. The Supreme Court grounded civil indemnity on Article 100. It dismissed the appeal for lack of merit and affirmed with modification. It sustained rape and reclusion perpetua without eligibility for parole. Ramos was ordered to pay AAA P75,000 civil indemnity, P75,000 moral damages and P75,000 exemplary damages plus costs. The indemnity and moral awards replaced the P50,000 awards below. All amounts due earn six percent annual interest from finality until full payment. The judgment sustained the conviction with the modified civil awards.[^36]

### [*People v. Martinez and Granada*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64046): Correct designation and omitted indemnity

On September 13, 2000, Martinez dragged AAA out of her house in Tudela, Cebu while she was cooking and threatened her seven-year-old son. A social worker and a psychologist testified on AAA's mental condition. AAA became pregnant after the rape. The accused claimed a sweetheart relationship and an extortion scheme. The RTC, Branch 25, Danao City convicted both accused of rape. The Court of Appeals treated the offense as Article 266-A(1)(d). The appeal challenged the proof and raised a claim of consensual intercourse. The Supreme Court held that Article 266-A(1)(b) applied because AAA was deprived of reason. The Information sufficiently alleged her mental condition. It did not allege the accused's knowledge of that condition. The Supreme Court therefore sustained simple rape. It dismissed the appeal and affirmed the appellate judgment with modification. Both accused were sentenced to reclusion perpetua without eligibility for parole. They were ordered to pay AAA P75,000 civil indemnity, P75,000 moral damages and P75,000 exemplary damages. Civil indemnity had been omitted below. Exemplary damages replaced the P25,000 award below. All amounts due earn six percent annual interest from the decision date until full payment. The lower no-costs order remained unchanged. The correction to Article 266-A(1)(b) left both accused liable for the specified civil awards.[^37]

### [*People v. Atop*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/35619): Separate civil indemnity and moral damages

The complainant, an illegitimate child, lived from early childhood with her grandmother and the grandmother's live-in partner, Atop. She testified that he raped her on several occasions while her grandmother was away. The RTC of Ormoc City, Branch 12 convicted Atop of three rapes. It imposed two terms of reclusion perpetua and one death sentence. It acquitted him of attempted rape. The appeals and automatic review went directly to the Supreme Court. The statutory relationship did not cover the grandmother's common-law spouse. Deliberate recourse to nighttime was not proved. The Supreme Court distinguished Article 100 indemnity from Civil Code moral damages. It affirmed with modification to three terms of reclusion perpetua, one for each rape. Atop was ordered to pay the complainant P150,000 indemnity and P50,000 moral damages. Trial costs remained payable. The attempted-rape acquittal was undisturbed. The modified judgment awarded indemnity separately from moral damages.[^38]

### [*People v. Apilo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/34160): Added indemnity for each count

The complainant was an orphan and a Grade III pupil in Baguio City who lived with her grandmother. On October 1, 1989, afraid to go home after her grandmother lost some money, she stayed at a classmate's house. Apilo was charged with raping her on October 1 and 2, 1989. Apilo directly appealed two trial convictions for rape under former Article 335. Each carried reclusion perpetua and P40,000 moral damages. The trial orders included costs, no subsidiary imprisonment on insolvency and four-fifths credit for preventive imprisonment. There was no Court of Appeals stage. The Supreme Court affirmed and added P50,000 Article 100 indemnity for each rape, totaling P100,000. The P40,000 moral award for each count remained payable. The trial penalties and qualifiers remained. The Department of Justice was directed to investigate whether probable cause existed to charge Victor Balisi with rape. That direction did not determine his guilt. The final judgment added indemnity without replacing the moral awards.[^39]

### [*Budlong v. Apalisok*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1983/jun1983/gr_l-60151_1983.html): A civil hearing despite probation

Camilo Puyo was charged before the City Court of Tagbilaran with serious physical injuries through reckless imprudence. He pleaded guilty at the February 4, 1982 arraignment. The court sentenced him at once and imposed no civil liability. The fiscal moved the next day for a hearing to prove civil liability. The City Court of Tagbilaran, Branch II accepted a guilty plea to serious physical injuries through reckless imprudence. It sentenced the accused to thirty days of imprisonment and costs without a civil hearing. It denied the next-day motion for a hearing and reconsideration because the accused had applied for probation. On direct certiorari, the Supreme Court distinguished civil obligations under Articles 112 and 113 from the criminal sentence. It held that denial of the civil hearing violated the offended party's right to be heard. It granted the petition and set aside the orders of February 11 and 19, 1982. It ordered hearings on civil liability. It fixed no money award and did not vacate the criminal sentence. The application for probation did not justify leaving the civil claim unheard.[^40]

### [*Heirs of Gevero v. Guihing Agricultural & Development Corporation*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44353): Civil consequences of the death

On April 1, 1990, Juanito Nava, an employee of Guihing Agricultural & Development Corporation, was driving a company motorcycle in Digos, Davao del Sur. The motorcycle bumped the bicycle of Mario Gevero, who died as a result. Nava was charged with reckless imprudence resulting in homicide. In the RTC, Branch 19, Digos, the driver was charged with reckless imprudence resulting in homicide. He pleaded to damage to property under the procedural rule applied in that case. The court imposed a P200 fine. After a civil hearing within the criminal case, it awarded the heirs P153,222.15 medical, hospital and burial expenses, P8,000 lost earnings, P200,000 moral damages and P25,000 attorney's fees. It also awarded P500 per appearance as may be shown on record plus costs. Insolvency led to garnishment of P157,044.75 against the employer. The Court of Appeals quashed execution and ordered return of that amount. The Supreme Court granted the petition and reversed the appellate decision. It reinstated the RTC execution orders of December 26, 1994 and January 16, 1995. The awards and garnishment consequently remained effective. The death's civil consequences could not be pegged to the bicycle's value.[^41]

### [*Carpio v. Doroja*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1989/dec1989/gr_84516_1989.html): Hearing on subsidiary liability

On October 23, 1985, Edwin Ramirez, driving a passenger jitney owned and operated by Eduardo Toribio, bumped Dionisio Carpio as he crossed the street. Carpio suffered a fractured left clavicle. The Information charged reckless imprudence resulting in serious physical injuries. The driver pleaded guilty to reckless imprudence resulting in less serious physical injuries under an amended Information. The Municipal Trial Court of Zamboanga City, Branch IV convicted him of that lesser offense. The sentence was one month and one day to two months of arresto mayor in its minimum period. It awarded P45 for goods, P200 hospital expenses and one thousand five hundred pesos attorney's fees plus costs. On civil appeal, the RTC, Branch XVI added P5,000 moral damages. Execution against the driver was returned unsatisfied. The municipal court denied a subsidiary writ against the alleged owner-operator. On direct review, the Supreme Court set aside that denial. It directed the trial court to hear and decide subsidiary liability in the same proceeding. It assessed costs against the private respondent. It made no immediate money judgment against the employer. Articles 100 and 103 permitted enforcement in the criminal proceeding after the required hearing.[^42]

### [*Pangonorom v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/43505): Premature execution against the employer

On the night of July 10, 1989, a Metro Manila Transit Corporation bus driven by Olimpio Pangonorom bumped an Isuzu Gemini car on EDSA in Quezon City. Carlos Berba was driving the car with his mother and his aunt as passengers. Two bus passengers testified that the bus was running at 70 to 80 kph when it swerved. The Information charged reckless imprudence resulting in damage to property with multiple slight physical injuries. The RTC of Quezon City, Branch 79 convicted the driver of reckless imprudence resulting in multiple slight physical injuries. It imposed thirty days of arresto menor. It ordered P42,600 for the offended parties' car damage. Medical reimbursements were P182.50 to Carlos, P217.50 to Amelia and P45 to Mary. The Court of Appeals affirmed and denied reconsideration. The driver and employer petitioned the Supreme Court. The employer had appealed on time. Diligence in selection and supervision was not a defense to subsidiary liability under Articles 100 and 103. Nevertheless, there was no proof of insolvency. The conviction was not yet final. The Supreme Court denied the petition and affirmed the appellate decision and resolution. It made no pronouncement as to costs. Employer execution remained premature until final conviction, unsatisfied execution for insolvency and the required hearing.[^43]

### [*People v. Garcia and Mindaroza*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1981/jun1981/gr_45280_81_1981.html): Discharge without extinguishing civil liability

On June 1, 1975, a fourteen-year-old complainant was untying a cow in her stepfather's coconut plantation in San Pablo City. She testified that the two accused raped her there. Garcia claimed that they were sweethearts. The Court of First Instance of Laguna and San Pablo City, 8th Judicial District, Branch III imposed death for two rapes after suspension and commitment under Presidential Decree No. 603. On automatic review, the Supreme Court considered Mindaroza's rehabilitation and Garcia's escape. There was no Court of Appeals stage. It applied the decree favorably to the offenses before it. It reviewed and sustained the finding of guilt for purposes of civil liability. It set aside Mindaroza's death sentence and ordered his immediate discharge and release. It affirmed Garcia's convictions but reduced each of his two death sentences to reclusion perpetua. The remaining portions of the judgments were affirmed. These included joint and several liability for P12,000 moral damages in each case, no subsidiary imprisonment on insolvency and costs. Mindaroza's discharge did not obliterate the civil obligation under the decree's discharge provisions.[^44]

### [*Bernardo v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/61354): A surviving contractual claim

In June 1991, Paz Bernardo borrowed P460,000.00 from Carmencita Bumanglag. She later took back the land title given as security and issued five checks in its place. The checks were dishonored in September 1992 because the account was closed. Bernardo did not heed the notice of dishonor. The RTC, Branch 56, Makati convicted Bernardo of five B.P. Blg. 22 violations. The Court of Appeals replaced imprisonment with a P460,000 fine and retained P460,000 civil liability. Bernardo died during Rule 45 review. Her heirs were substituted. The Supreme Court rejected the claimed denial of due process. Payment had not been proved. Death extinguished criminal and purely delictual liability. The contractual claim could still be determined under the special B.P. Blg. 22 procedure. The Supreme Court affirmed with modification. It ordered the heirs to pay Bumanglag P460,000 with twelve percent annual interest from institution of the criminal charges. The total adjudged amount earns six percent annual interest on the balance and interest due from finality until full payment. The P460,000 fine was deleted. The surviving recovery rested on contract.[^45]

### [*Cadungog v. Sung Ha Jung*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69921): Contractual relief beyond the criminal court's jurisdiction

Vivien Cadungog owned and developed Sophela Tower, a condominium in Lahug, Cebu City. She and Sung Ha Jung entered into a Contract to Sell over a unit in November 2008. They later disputed the payments and the delivery of the unit. An Information for violation of Presidential Decree No. 957 followed. The RTC of Cebu City, Branch 14 acquitted the accused of a Presidential Decree No. 957 violation on reasonable doubt. It nevertheless ordered contractual prestations. The buyer could choose delivery within thirty days after paying P258,950 or refund of P3,241,050 with twelve percent interest. The Court of Appeals rejected annulment and reconsideration. On Rule 45 review, the Supreme Court held that the HLURB had exclusive jurisdiction over those remedies when the action was filed. It allowed Rule 47 annulment on the civil-jurisdiction facts, subject to laches and estoppel. It granted the petition. It reversed and set aside the appellate decision of August 20, 2019 and resolution of August 28, 2020. It declared the civil portion of the RTC judgment of September 16, 2011 null and void for lack of jurisdiction. Neither contractual alternative survived. The criminal acquittal remained untouched. Article 100 did not bring those contractual remedies within the criminal court's jurisdiction.[^46]

### [*People v. Francisco*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/49877): Simple rape and separate damages

Francisco was charged with raping his daughter and his stepdaughter, aged thirteen and sixteen, in Rosario, Cavite in July 1996. The RTC of Cavite City, Branch 16 imposed death on two rape counts. On automatic review, the Supreme Court found the age proof and qualifying step-relationship insufficient. There was no Court of Appeals stage. It confined both convictions to simple rape. It affirmed with modification to reclusion perpetua for each count. It added Article 100 indemnity. Francisco was ordered to pay each complainant P50,000 indemnity, P50,000 moral damages and P20,000 exemplary damages. The respective totals were P100,000 indemnity, P100,000 moral damages and P40,000 exemplary damages. Costs were against the accused. The two sustained simple-rape convictions carried the specified civil awards.[^47]

### [*People v. Puzon*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/35986): Deletion of exemplary damages

Puzon was charged in four Informations with raping his two daughters. The RTC, Branch 33, Bauang, La Union convicted Puzon on four counts described below as statutory rape. There were two complainants. On direct appeal, the Supreme Court held that the Informations alleged force and intimidation but omitted ages. The final designation was rape under former Article 335(1). It recognized the omitted indemnity under Articles 100 and 104. It affirmed the four convictions and reclusion perpetua sentences. It ordered P50,000 each as indemnity to the two complainants in their respective pairs of cases. Moral damages were increased to P50,000. Exemplary damages were deleted for lack of basis. Costs were against the accused. The preceding discussion suggested increasing exemplary damages, but the final order deleted them.[^48]

### [*People v. Bayya*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/53379): Civil awards after correction to simple rape

Bayya was charged with raping his daughter in Burgos, Isabela from 1994 to July 12, 1995. She first disclosed the abuse to an aunt six days after the last assault. The RTC of Ilagan, Isabela, Branch 16 imposed death for incestuous rape without awarding damages. On automatic review, the Supreme Court held that minority had not been alleged although proved. There was no Court of Appeals stage. The conviction could be sustained only for simple rape. It affirmed with modification and imposed reclusion perpetua. It ordered Bayya to pay the victim P50,000 indemnity, P50,000 moral damages and P25,000 exemplary damages plus costs. Articles 100 and 104 supported the omitted indemnity. The pleading limitation reduced the offense but did not eliminate its civil consequences.[^49]

### [*People v. Miasco*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/52468): The two civil awards in the capital judgment

The complainant was the daughter of Miasco and was born on December 14, 1983. She testified that he raped her twice at the makeshift hut on their farm in Davao City, the second time in November 1996 while armed with a knife. The RTC of Davao City imposed death for two rapes. On automatic review, the Supreme Court found minority and relationship alleged and proved. There was no Court of Appeals stage. Quoting the RTC order, the decision recorded its reference to Article 100. The Supreme Court independently increased indemnity and granted moral and exemplary damages. It affirmed death for each of the two offenses. Miasco was ordered to pay the victim P75,000 indemnity, P50,000 moral damages and P20,000 exemplary damages in each case. Upon finality, the records were to be forwarded to the Office of the President for possible executive clemency. Those were the criminal and civil orders in that judgment.[^50]

### [*People v. Bragas*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/38201): Indemnity in addition to moral damages

Bragas was charged with raping on April 30, 1995 in Davao City the thirteen-year-old daughter of his common-law wife. The RTC of Davao City, Branch 17 imposed death and P30,000 moral damages for rape. Quoting its order, the decision recorded the RTC's reference to Article 100. On automatic review, the Supreme Court found the qualifying relationship not alleged. There was no Court of Appeals stage. It affirmed with modification to simple rape under former Article 335 as amended by Republic Act No. 7659. The penalty became reclusion perpetua with accessory penalties. Bragas was ordered to pay the victim P50,000 civil indemnity and P50,000 moral damages. Costs were against him in all instances. The judgment applied the former provision before the repeal by Republic Act No. 8353 effective October 7, 1997. It separately added indemnity and increased moral damages.[^51]

### [*People v. Sayat*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1993/jun1993/gr_102773_1993.html): Increased moral award

The complainant was eight years old when her half-brother, eighteen years her senior, raped her five times. She testified at age nine. The RTC of Pasig, Metro Manila, Branch 156 convicted Sayat on five rape counts under former Article 335(3) with the aggravating circumstance of relationship. It imposed five terms of reclusion perpetua with corresponding accessory penalties and costs. It ordered P30,000 indemnity to the victim. It credited preventive imprisonment in full and stated a forty-year service ceiling. Quoting the trial order, the decision recorded the reference to Article 100. On direct appeal without a Court of Appeals stage, the Supreme Court sustained the factual findings. It increased the award to P50,000 moral damages for the complainant and her family. It otherwise affirmed the judgment. The stated moral award was not expressed as five separate P50,000 awards.[^52]

### [*People v. Dusohan*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1993/oct1993/gr_97307_1993.html): Increased indemnity

Dusohan left the family home in Marikina in October 1989 after quarreling with his wife. His daughter followed and lived with him. The trial court found that he raped her repeatedly and threatened to kill her if she told anyone. The RTC of Pasig, Metro Manila, Branch 156 convicted Dusohan of rape by force. It imposed reclusion perpetua, P30,000 indemnity and costs. Quoting the trial order, the decision recorded Article 100. On direct appeal without a Court of Appeals stage, the Supreme Court sustained the conviction. Relationship was aggravating. The circumstances justified increasing indemnity to P50,000. It affirmed with that modification. Reclusion perpetua and costs remained. The judgment did not add separate moral damages, exemplary damages or interest.[^53]

### [*People v. Cabodac*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1992/may1992/gr_93929_1992.html): Two sustained counts and one acquittal

The victim was eight years old. She told her mother on May 2, 1989 that the accused had done bad things to her. A medico-legal examination the next day found healed and healing hymenal lacerations. The eighteen-year-old accused relied on denial and alibi. The RTC of Pasig, Metro Manila, Branch 156 convicted Cabodac of three statutory rapes under former Article 335. It imposed reclusion perpetua with accessory penalties and P30,000 for each count plus costs. Quoting the trial order, the decision recorded Article 100. On direct appeal without a Court of Appeals stage, the Supreme Court found the third Information too vague. A distinct third act had not been proved beyond reasonable doubt. It affirmed in toto the statutory-rape judgments with dwelling in Criminal Cases Nos. 77766 and 77767. It set aside the judgment in No. 77768 and acquitted Cabodac on reasonable doubt. The retained awards were P30,000 for each sustained count. Costs were against the accused. The acquittal was based on reasonable doubt.[^54]

### [*Cancio v. Isip*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/51647): Collection after dismissal of estafa cases

Jose Cancio filed three B.P. Blg. 22 cases and three estafa cases against Merenciana Isip over checks that bounced for insufficient funds. The B.P. Blg. 22 cases were dismissed. On December 15, 1997, Cancio filed a collection case to recover the amount of the checks in the estafa cases. The prosecution moved to dismiss the estafa cases after failing to present its second witness. The RTC of Pampanga, Branch 49 later dismissed Cancio's collection action on res judicata and forum-shopping grounds. It denied reconsideration. Cancio sought Rule 45 review on pure questions of law without a Court of Appeals stage. The Supreme Court held that the contractual complaint was independent of the criminal proceedings. It required no prior reservation. It granted the petition and reversed and set aside the RTC orders of March 20 and June 1, 1998. It remanded the collection case for further proceedings. The prayer sought P190,000 principal plus legal interest, P30,000 attorney's fees, P1,000 per appearance and P20,000 litigation expenses. These were requested amounts, not a Supreme Court award. The collection claim remained for adjudication on remand.[^55]

### [*Safeguard Security Agency, Inc. v. Tangco*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/40427): Primary liability under the Civil Code

A guard shot a bank client who was drawing a firearm for safekeeping. The heirs reserved a civil action. The separate homicide conviction in the RTC of Quezon City, Branch 78 became final after appellate affirmance with a penalty modification. In the separate damages action, the RTC of Marikina City, Branch 273 imposed joint and several liability on the guard and agency. The Court of Appeals changed the agency's liability to subsidiary. The Supreme Court held that the complaint was based on quasi-delict. It rejected self-defense and contributory negligence. Diligence in selection was established but supervision was insufficient. It denied the petition and affirmed the appellate decision of July 16, 2004 with modification. Safeguard's liability was solidary and primary under Civil Code Article 2180. The guard and agency owed the heirs P157,430 actual damages, P50,000 death indemnity, P1,000,000 moral damages, P300,000 exemplary damages and P30,000 attorney's fees plus costs. Dismissal of the counterclaim remained effective. No interest was imposed in the Supreme Court's disposition. These awards enforced quasi-delict liability.[^56]

### [*Proton Pilipinas Corporation v. Republic of the Philippines*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/42038): Tax liability arising from statute

Cancellation of tax credit certificates led the Bureau of Customs to sue Proton for unpaid duties and taxes. Corporate officers faced Republic Act No. 3019 charges before the Sandiganbayan. The RTC of Manila denied dismissal and reconsideration of the collection case. The Court of Appeals dismissed the Rule 65 petition and denied reconsideration. On Rule 45 review, the Supreme Court held the tax obligation distinct from the alleged felony. Quoting Article 104, it reproduced the reference to Articles 100 to 103. It discussed the supplementary operation of Article 10. The parties, causes and effects required for litis pendentia differed. Collection did not have to await the criminal result. The jurisdictional ruling concerned the regime before expansion of the Court of Tax Appeals' jurisdiction. The Supreme Court denied the petition. It affirmed the appellate decision of April 29, 2004 and resolution of August 2, 2004 affirming the RTC orders. Costs were against Proton. No tax amount was fixed by the disposition. The collection action remained distinct from the criminal proceedings.[^57]

### [*Quinto v. Andres*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/41112): Failure to prove the alleged deliberate acts

A child died after entering a drainage culvert to fish. The Information alleged a deliberate attack. The RTC of Tarlac, Tarlac granted a demurrer to the homicide charge for insufficient evidence. It found no preponderant civil proof. The Court of Appeals affirmed. The mother's Rule 45 petition concerned the civil aspect only. The Supreme Court held that she had failed to prove the deliberate acts alleged by preponderance of evidence. The possibility of slipping was not foreclosed. It denied the petition for lack of merit with no costs. It made no money award and did not disturb the acquittal. The civil claim failed for insufficient proof.[^58]

### [*Banco De Oro Unibank, Inc. v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69675): Intervention without deciding the appealed merits

The RTC of Makati City, Branch 143 convicted Ruby of Estafa through Misappropriation under Article 315(1)(b). It imposed four years of prision correccional maximum as minimum to twenty years of reclusion temporal maximum as maximum plus accessory penalties. It ordered payment to BDO of PHP45,799,007.28 actual damages, PHP100,000 moral damages and PHP100,000 attorney's fees plus costs. It acquitted Bungque for insufficient evidence and ordered an alias warrant for Elizabeth. The Court of Appeals found no trial judgment concerning Elizabeth. It denied BDO intervention in Ruby's appeal and denied reconsideration. On Rule 45 review, the Supreme Court limited the issue to intervention. Quoting prior reasoning, it discussed Article 100. It recognized BDO's legal interest and participation throughout trial. The claim of delay lacked evidentiary support. It granted the petition and reversed and set aside the appellate resolutions of September 8, 2020 and January 15, 2021. It did not affirm the appealed conviction, penalty or awards. The Court of Appeals retained independent review of liability, if any. The Supreme Court's relief allowed intervention on the civil aspect.[^59]

## Key Cases

- [*People v. Ramos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64549)[^65]: After trial conviction and appellate affirmance for rape, the Supreme Court dismissed the appeal and affirmed with modified damages. The sustained sentence was reclusion perpetua without eligibility for parole. The final awards to AAA were P75,000 each for indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages plus costs. Interest runs at six percent annually from finality until full payment.
- [*People v. Martinez and Granada*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/64046)[^66]: The Supreme Court corrected the appellate designation to Article 266-A(1)(b) and supplied omitted civil indemnity. It dismissed the appeal and affirmed with modification. Both accused received reclusion perpetua without eligibility for parole. AAA was awarded P75,000 each for indemnity, moral damages and exemplary damages. Six percent annual interest runs from decision date until full payment. The no-costs order remained.
- [*Budlong v. Apalisok*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1983/jun1983/gr_l-60151_1983.html)[^67]: The city court refused a civil hearing after a guilty plea and an application for probation. The Supreme Court granted certiorari and set aside both refusal orders. It directed hearings without fixing compensation or vacating the thirty-day sentence and costs.
- [*Heirs of Gevero v. Guihing Agricultural & Development Corporation*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44353)[^68]: After a lesser-offense plea and civil hearing, the Court of Appeals quashed execution against the employer. The Supreme Court reversed and reinstated the execution orders. The restored awards were P153,222.15 expenses, P8,000 earnings, P200,000 moral damages, P25,000 attorney's fees and P500 per appearance as may be shown on record plus costs. The P157,044.75 garnishment remained effective. The P200 fine did not cap death compensation.
- [*Carpio v. Doroja*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1989/dec1989/gr_84516_1989.html)[^69]: After conviction, a civil appeal and unsatisfied execution, the municipal court denied subsidiary execution. The Supreme Court set that denial aside and required a hearing in the same proceeding. Costs were against the private respondent. It made no immediate employer money award.
- [*Bernardo v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/61354)[^70]: Death during review of five B.P. Blg. 22 convictions extinguished criminal and purely delictual liability. The Supreme Court affirmed with modification, deleted the P460,000 fine and ordered the heirs to pay Bumanglag P460,000 on the contractual claim. Twelve percent annual interest runs from institution of criminal charges. The balance and interest due earn six percent annually from finality until fully paid.
- [*Cancio v. Isip*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/51647)[^71]: The RTC dismissed collection after dismissal of the estafa cases. The Supreme Court held the contractual action independent and not barred by res judicata or forum-shopping. It reversed and set aside both RTC orders and remanded without awarding the amounts prayed for.
- [*Quinto v. Andres*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/41112)[^72]: The RTC granted the homicide demurrer and rejected civil liability. The Court of Appeals affirmed. The Supreme Court denied the mother's civil petition for failure of preponderant proof of the deliberate acts alleged. It imposed no costs and left the acquittal undisturbed.
- [*Banco De Oro Unibank, Inc. v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69675)[^73]: The Court of Appeals denied intervention after the RTC conviction and civil awards. The Supreme Court reversed and set aside the two appellate resolutions to permit intervention. It left the appealed merits, penalty and awards for independent appellate review.
- [*Cadungog v. Sung Ha Jung*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69921)[^74]: The RTC acquitted on reasonable doubt but ordered contractual delivery or refund. The Court of Appeals refused annulment. The Supreme Court granted review, reversed the appellate decision and resolution and declared the RTC civil portion null and void for lack of jurisdiction. Neither contractual alternative survived. The acquittal remained undisturbed.

## Suggested Answers

### 1. A civil claim left unheard after a guilty plea

**Question:** An accused pleads guilty to serious physical injuries through reckless imprudence. The judge sentences him without a civil hearing. The prosecutor requests a hearing the following day. The judge refuses because the accused has applied for probation. Does that application justify the refusal?

**Suggested answer:** No. [*Budlong v. Apalisok*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1983/jun1983/gr_l-60151_1983.html)[^75] distinguished the continuing civil obligation from the criminal sentence. The Supreme Court set aside the refusal orders and required hearings. It did not itself fix compensation.[^76]

### 2. Compensation after a lesser-offense plea

**Question:** Under the procedural rule applied in the case, a driver charged with reckless imprudence resulting in homicide pleads to damage to property. The fine is P200. A civil hearing establishes losses from the death. Is the bicycle's value the ceiling on compensation?

**Suggested answer:** No. [*Heirs of Gevero v. Guihing Agricultural & Development Corporation*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/44353)[^77] rejected pegging the death's civil consequences to the bicycle's value. The Supreme Court reversed the appellate decision quashing execution and reinstated the RTC execution orders. The fine did not measure the civil loss.[^78]

### 3. Moral damages already awarded

**Question:** A trial court convicts an accused of two rapes and awards moral damages for each. It omits civil indemnity. Can the Supreme Court add indemnity on direct appeal?

**Suggested answer:** Yes. [*People v. Apilo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/34160)[^79] added P50,000 indemnity for each rape, totaling P100,000. The P40,000 moral award for each offense remained payable. The awards compensated distinct consequences under that judgment.[^80]

# Quotes

> "The nature of a cause of action is determined by the facts alleged in the complaint as constituting the cause of action."[^81]

> "The civil action filed by respondents was not derived from the criminal liability of Pajarillo in the criminal case but one based on culpa aquiliana or quasi-delict which is separate and distinct from the civil liability arising from crime. The source of the obligation sought to be enforced in the civil case is a quasi-delict not an act or omission punishable by law."[^82]

> "In sum, the petitioner failed to adduce preponderance of evidence to prove a cause of action for damages based on the deliberate acts alleged in the Information."[^83]

> "The present case, although it stemmed from a criminal action, was filed solely for the purpose of seeking the annulment of the civil liabilities of the parties that were incorporated in the judgment on the criminal case. Thus, to the mind of the Court, petitioner is not precluded from availing herself of this remedy as the aspect being assailed by petitioner is purely civil in nature and one which was not deemed instituted in the criminal action pursuant to Article 100 of the Revised Penal Code. This is especially considering the allegation of lack of jurisdiction on the part of the RTC in rendering the civil aspect of the judgment which is one of the two grounds therefor."[^84]

# Tips

### Diagnostic checklist

> [!TIP]
> **Identify the obligation.** Ask whether recovery rests on the felony, contract, quasi-delict or statute. [*Cancio v. Isip*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/51647) determined the collection action's nature from the complaint's factual allegations. The claimant's description did not control. Identify the relief sought before assuming that dismissal of the prosecution also disposes of the civil claim.

> [!TIP]
> **Separate the awards.** [*People v. Apilo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/34160) added omitted civil indemnity even though the trial court had awarded moral damages. Identify each head of recovery. An existing moral award does not dispense with indemnity where the judgment requires both.

> [!TIP]
> **Check the proof.** [*Quinto v. Andres*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/41112) rejected the civil claim because the alleged deliberate acts were not proved by preponderance of evidence. An allegation in an Information is not an affirmative finding. A competing possible explanation is not necessarily a finding that the event occurred that way.

### Common traps

> [!NOTE]
> **Do not substitute the discussion for the final order.** [*People v. Puzon*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/35986) deleted exemplary damages in its disposition despite the preceding suggestion of an increase. Keep the final award and its limits. Do not multiply an award when the order does not direct that multiplication.

> [!NOTE]
> **Distinguish quoted text from the deciding reasons.** [*People v. Miasco*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/52468) recorded Article 100 while quoting the trial order. The Supreme Court independently determined the civil awards. The quoted invocation must not be described as a separate express citation by the Supreme Court in its own reasoning.

> [!NOTE]
> **Do not equate every release with acquittal.** [*People v. Garcia and Mindaroza*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1981/jun1981/gr_45280_81_1981.html) retained civil liability after discharge under the decree applied there. The Supreme Court had reviewed guilt for that purpose. The ground of release matters to the civil consequence.

### What worked and what failed

> [!TIP]
> **A hearing may be the required relief.** [*Budlong v. Apalisok*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1983/jun1983/gr_l-60151_1983.html) required the trial court to hear the civil claim. The successful petition did not itself establish the amount payable. Distinguish the opportunity to prove liability from a final assessment of damages.

> [!TIP]
> **Establish the requirements of execution.** [*Carpio v. Doroja*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1989/dec1989/gr_84516_1989.html) permitted a hearing on subsidiary liability within the criminal proceeding. The employer still had to be heard. Identify the unsatisfied execution and the factual basis for the employer's alleged liability before requesting subsidiary enforcement.

> [!NOTE]
> **Keep the Supreme Court's issue separate from the pending appeal.** [*Banco De Oro Unibank, Inc. v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69675) allowed intervention. It did not decide the appealed conviction or finally affirm damages. An order allowing participation cannot be treated as a merits judgment.

> [!TIP]
> **Identify the surviving source after death.** [*Bernardo v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/61354) distinguished extinguished criminal and purely delictual liability from a surviving contractual claim. State the source and the special procedure that permitted adjudication. Do not describe the retained recovery as a continuation of criminal liability.
