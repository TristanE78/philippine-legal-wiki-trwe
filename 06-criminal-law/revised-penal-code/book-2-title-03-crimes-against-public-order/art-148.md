> Article 148. Direct assaults.-Any person or persons who, direct assaults, without a public uprising, shall employ force or intimidation for the attainment of any of the purposes enumerated in defining the crimes of rebellion and sedition, or shall attack, employ force or seriously intimidate or resist any person in authority or any of his agents, while engaged in the performance of official duties, or on occasion of such performance, shall suffer the penalty of prision correccional in its medium and maximum periods and a fine not exceeding 1,000 pesos, when the assault is committed with a weapon or when the offender is a public officer or employee, or when the offender lays hands upon a person in authority. If none of these circumstances be present the penalty of prision correccional in its minimum period and a fine not exceeding 500 pesos shall be imposed.

[^revised-penal-code]

# Explanation

## Concept, Nature and the Two Modes

Article 148 punishes direct assault, which the Revised Penal Code places among the crimes against public order. In [*Balsamo v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69212)[^2] (2023), the Supreme Court explained that the principal object of the crime is to penalize the spirit of lawlessness or lack of respect for the rule of law.

The article describes two modes of commission. The first is committed by any person who, without a public uprising, employs force or intimidation to attain any of the purposes enumerated in defining rebellion and sedition. The second is committed by any person who, without a public uprising, attacks, employs force, seriously intimidates or resists a person in authority or an agent, while engaged in the performance of official duties or on occasion of such performance. In [*People v. Recto*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/52891)[^3] (2001), the Supreme Court described the first mode as tantamount to rebellion or sedition without the element of public uprising. It called the second mode the more common form of assault. In [*People v. Abalos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/33847)[^4] (1996), the Supreme Court went further and said that the first mode is not a true atentado, because it amounts to rebellion or sedition except for the absence of a public uprising.

Every decision on this page concerns the second mode. No decision on this page applies the first mode to a set of facts. The discussion that follows is therefore a discussion of assault upon persons in authority and their agents.

## Elements of the Second Mode

In [*Mallari v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/66238)[^5] (2020), the Supreme Court listed the elements of the second mode as follows:

1. that the offender (a) makes an attack, (b) employs force, (c) makes a serious intimidation or (d) makes a serious resistance;

2. that the person assaulted is a person in authority or his agent;

3. that at the time of the assault the person in authority or his agent (a) is engaged in the actual performance of official duties or (b) is assaulted by reason of the past performance of official duties;

4. that the offender knows that the one he is assaulting is a person in authority or his agent in the exercise of his duties; and

5. that there is no public uprising.

The same list appears in [*Gelig v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/54204)[^6] (2010). It appears again in [*Guelos v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63166)[^7] (2017). [*Pablo v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68672)[^8] (2023) adopted it expressly from *Mallari*. [*Aplacador v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70146)[^9] (2025) restated it in the same terms. The sections below take up the elements that the decisions litigated.

## Persons in Authority and Their Agents

### The definitions in Article 152

Article 148 does not define its protected class. Article 152 does. A person in authority is any person directly vested with jurisdiction, whether as an individual or as a member of a court or governmental corporation, board or commission. An agent of a person in authority is one who, by direct provision of law or by election or appointment by competent authority, is charged with the maintenance of public order and the protection and security of life and property. The distinction matters for the qualifying circumstance of laying hands upon a person in authority. It does not matter for liability itself. In [*Aplacador v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70146)[^10], the Supreme Court held that Article 148 penalizes direct assault whether it is committed against a person in authority or against his agent.

### The teacher question and its statutory resolution

The coverage of Article 152 has changed by amendment. The history of the teacher question shows how. In [*People v. Mendoza*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1933/dec1933/gr_39275_1933.html)[^11] (1933), a pupil was charged with assault upon a person in authority for slapping his teacher inside the school premises. The Supreme Court held that a teacher is not a person in authority, because he does not exercise a directly vested jurisdiction. It also held that a teacher is not an agent of authority, because agents are only those charged with the maintenance of public order and the protection of life and property. The Supreme Court accepted that a teacher is a public officer. It ruled, however, that the crime of assault cannot be committed against a public officer unless he is also a person in authority or an agent. The acts alleged were only a light felony.

That rule did not survive the amendments to Article 152. In [*Sarcepuedes v. People*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1951/oct1951/gr_l-3857_1951.html)[^12] (1951), the Supreme Court applied Article 152 as amended by Commonwealth Act No. 578. The amended text deems teachers, professors and persons charged with the supervision of public or duly recognized private schools persons in authority for the purpose of applying Articles 148 and 151. The Supreme Court therefore overruled the contention that a teacher-nurse was not a person in authority, since a teacher is expressly included in the amended article. In [*Gelig v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/54204)[^13], the Supreme Court applied the same clause as amended by Batas Pambansa Blg. 873. It held that a public school teacher belongs to the class of persons in authority expressly mentioned in Article 152. The governing rule today is the rule of *Gelig*. *Mendoza* remains useful only as the rule before the amendments.

### Barangay officials

The Supreme Court has treated the barangay captain as a person in authority in several settings. In [*People v. Rillorta*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1989/dec1989/gr_57415_1989.html)[^14] (1989), it held that under Article 152 and P.D. 299 a barangay captain is a person in authority.

In [*People v. Recto*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/52891)[^15], it treated an attack on a barangay captain who was pacifying two armed groups as qualified direct assault. In the same case, it held that a barangay chief tanod was an agent of a person in authority. It also treated a barangay kagawad shot while in the actual performance of his duties as a victim of direct assault. In [*People v. Caricungan*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/sep1991/gr_71461_1991.html)[^16] (1991), the Supreme Court found that the Chief of the Barangay Tanod was maintaining peace and order in the community when he was killed.

### Police officers, traffic enforcers and soldiers

In [*Mallari v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/66238)[^17], the Supreme Court held that a police officer is an agent of a person in authority because he is charged with the maintenance of public order and the protection and security of life and property. [*People v. Abalos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/33847)[^18] reached the same conclusion for a member of the then Integrated National Police. In [*Pablo v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68672)[^19], the accused claimed on appeal to the Regional Trial Court that the prosecution failed to show that a traffic enforcer was duly appointed. The Court of Appeals treated the appointment papers as only corroborative. The Supreme Court held that it was no longer necessary to produce the document enumerating the duties of the traffic enforcers. Traffic enforcers are duty-bound to preserve public order by enforcing traffic laws, directing traffic and apprehending erring drivers. They are therefore agents of persons in authority.

[*Aplacador v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70146)[^20] extended the same functional reasoning to members of the Armed Forces of the Philippines. The parties had stipulated at pre-trial that the victim, a military sergeant, was a person in authority. The Supreme Court held that whether a victim is a person in authority is a question of law, since Article 152 supplies the definition. It examined the functions of the Armed Forces under the Revised Administrative Code of 1987 and Republic Act No. 7898. It concluded that soldiers perform tasks that include the maintenance of public order and the protection of life and property. Both the accused and the victim should therefore have been classified as agents of a person in authority. The misclassification did not help the accused. Because Article 148 covers both persons in authority and their agents, the conviction for direct assault stood.

## Performance of Official Duty and the Occasion of the Assault

### Assault while engaged in the performance of duty

The third element is satisfied in either of two ways. The victim may be engaged in the actual performance of official duties when assaulted. The victim may also be assaulted by reason of the past performance of those duties. In the first situation, the motive of the offender does not matter. In [*Sarcepuedes v. People*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1951/oct1951/gr_l-3857_1951.html)[^21], the accused hit a teacher-nurse in the school clinic because she had closed a pathway across her land. He argued that the attack was not on occasion of her official work, since the dispute was foreign to her educational labors. The Supreme Court held that the motive becomes immaterial once it is found that the victim was hurt while performing her ordinary government tasks. She was attacked while engaged in the performance of her official duties, within the meaning of Article 148.

[*Gelig v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/54204)[^22] addressed a related defense. The Court of Appeals had held that the victim teacher descended to the level of a private person when she fought back instead of pacifying the accused. The Supreme Court rejected that reasoning. It found that the teacher was busy with paperwork while supervising her pupils when the accused entered the classroom. She tried to pacify the accused. She retaliated only after a barrage of verbal abuse, as a similarly situated person would. The Supreme Court held that no fault could be attributed to her and that she remained a person in authority engaged in her official functions.

### Assault by reason of the performance of duty

The second situation looks to the reason for the attack rather than to the activity at the moment of the attack. In [*People v. Estonilo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/58325)[^23] (2014), the trial court had found direct assault because the victim, a District Supervisor of public schools, was a person in authority. The Supreme Court corrected that reasoning. It clarified that the finding of direct assault rests on the fact that the attack was made by reason of the performance of his duty as District Supervisor. Rank alone does not convert an attack into direct assault.

### The bystander agent

The converse is also true. An official who is neither performing a duty nor attacked because of one is not within Article 148. In [*People v. Recto*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/52891)[^24], the chief tanod was on his way home from the fields when he passed the scene of the confrontation. The Supreme Court held that he was a mere bystander who was not acting and had no occasion to act in the performance of his official duties. The attack on him did not amount to direct assault. In the same case, the barangay captain was shot while attempting to pacify the accused and keep the peace. The attack on him was qualified direct assault. Official status is therefore not enough by itself.

## Knowledge of the Victim's Authority

### Knowledge as intent to defy authority

The fourth element is the mental element of the crime. In [*People v. Abalos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/33847)[^25], the Supreme Court explained that the accused must have the intention to offend, injure or assault the offended party as a person in authority or as an agent. It found that intent proven. The accused personally knew the victim to be a policeman. The victim was also in uniform. [*People v. Estonilo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/58325)[^26] restated the same requirement. In [*Balsamo v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69212)[^27], knowledge was established because the police officer introduced himself as such when he arrived. In [*Mallari v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/66238)[^28], the police officer introduced himself and was in uniform.

### Knowledge must be alleged in the Information

Knowledge is not only a matter of proof. It must also appear in the Information. In [*Guelos v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63166)[^29], two police officers were shot dead. The Informations alleged that they were members of the police performing their official duties. They did not allege that the accused knew the victims were agents of a person in authority. The evidence at trial established that knowledge, since the victims had introduced themselves as police officers. The Supreme Court held that proof of the fact cannot cure the lack of the allegation. It relied on *People v. Rodil*, as cited in *Guelos*, which treated the required knowledge like a qualifying circumstance that must be expressly averred. It grounded the rule on the constitutional right of the accused to be informed of the nature and cause of the accusation and on Sections 8 and 9 of Rule 110. The accused could be convicted only of homicide.

[*Guelos v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63166)[^30] also marks the limit of the remedy. The Supreme Court held that the knowledge could not be appreciated as a generic aggravating circumstance to increase the penalty, since it was not alleged. It nonetheless awarded exemplary damages, because the aggravating circumstance of insult to rank was proven at trial and exemplary damages do not depend on the allegations of the Information.

## Direct Assault Distinguished From Resistance and Disobedience

Articles 148 and 151 punish the same kind of defiance at different degrees of gravity. Article 151 punishes any person who, not being liable for direct assault, resists or seriously disobeys a person in authority or an agent while engaged in the performance of official duties. The second mode of direct assault requires more. The offender must attack, employ force, seriously intimidate or seriously resist. In [*Balsamo v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69212)[^31], the Supreme Court explained that the choice between the two crimes depends on the gravity of the act proved and the conditions under which it was committed. Courts must consider the circumstances surrounding the conduct, the motives prompting it and the real importance of the transgression.

### The seriousness of the force

In [*Mallari v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/66238)[^32], the Supreme Court held that the laying of hands or the use of physical force against an agent of a person in authority must be serious to constitute direct assault. It adopted the reasoning of *United States v. Gumban*, as cited in *Mallari*, that any force is not sufficient and that the circumstances of each case decide whether the force suffices. It observed that earlier convictions for direct assault against an agent involved force more severe than slapping and punching. It also adopted the rule of *People v. Breis*, as cited in *Mallari*, that non-serious force constitutes resistance or disobedience under Article 151.

The courts below had found that Mallari slapped and kicked PO2 Navarro and grabbed his shirt while he was on duty. The Supreme Court accepted those findings and held that the second to fifth elements were present. It held that the first element was absent because the force was not dangerous, grave or severe enough to warrant the penalties of direct assault. PO2 Navarro himself testified that he was not kicked hard. The Supreme Court convicted Mallari of resistance or disobedience instead.

[*Balsamo v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69212)[^33] applied the same test three years later to a punch thrown during an arrest. PO3 Adalim chased Balsamo and grabbed his right arm. Balsamo punched him in the chest to free himself and evade arrest. The Supreme Court found that the punch was not meant to assault the officer or defy his authority. It found that Balsamo slammed the gate blindly while running away, without knowing that it hit the officer's fingers. The injuries were slight abrasions and swollen fingers. The officer was still able to give chase. The Supreme Court restated the rule that the force in direct assault must be serious or more than a sudden blow, slapping or punching. It convicted Balsamo of resistance to an agent of a person in authority. It imposed a straight penalty of three months of arresto mayor and a PHP 500.00 fine, because the Indeterminate Sentence Law does not apply when the maximum term does not exceed one year.

### Serious intimidation without physical force

The seriousness test also governs intimidation. In [*Pablo v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68672)[^34], the Supreme Court noted that *Mallari* focused on force. It held that pulling out a gun and then aiming it at agents of persons in authority constitutes serious intimidation. It reasoned that the act may not be as forceful as the acts described in *Mallari* but is serious enough against an agent of persons in authority. It added that the act, if taken lightly, would cause great danger at the expense of public order and peace. The conviction of Pablo for direct assault was affirmed.

### Conviction for the lesser offense

An Information for direct assault can sustain a conviction for resistance or disobedience. In [*Mallari v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/66238)[^35], the Supreme Court applied Sections 4 and 5 of Rule 120. It held that the two offenses have similar elements, varying only in the degree of seriousness of the offender's resistance. Direct assault necessarily includes resistance or disobedience. [*Balsamo v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69212)[^36] restated the same rule.

| Point of comparison | Direct assault, second mode (Art. 148) | Resistance and disobedience (Art. 151) |
|---|---|---|
| Act of the offender | Attack, force, serious intimidation or serious resistance | Resistance or serious disobedience |
| Degree required | Serious force, more than a sudden blow, slap or punch | Force or defiance that is not serious |
| Leading decision | [*Balsamo v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69212)[^37] | [*Mallari v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/66238)[^38] |
| Penalty | Prision correccional with fine, in two degrees under the codal text | Arresto mayor and a fine not exceeding PHP 500.00 |
| Relation of the offenses | Necessarily includes resistance | Included in direct assault |

## Qualified Direct Assault and the Penalty

### The three qualifying circumstances

Article 148 prescribes two penalties. The higher penalty of prision correccional in its medium and maximum periods, with a fine, applies when the assault is committed with a weapon, when the offender is a public officer or employee or when the offender lays hands upon a person in authority. Otherwise the penalty is prision correccional in its minimum period, with a lower fine. In [*People v. Recto*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/52891)[^39], the Supreme Court described these as the circumstances that aggravate the second mode. It called the resulting offense qualified direct assault.

Each circumstance has been applied. In [*Aplacador v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70146)[^40], the use of a firearm made the direct assault qualified. In [*Gelig v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/54204)[^41], two circumstances concurred. The accused was a public officer, since she was a teacher in a public school. By slapping and pushing another teacher, she also laid hands on a person in authority. The laying of hands applies only to persons in authority. It does not apply to agents.

### Computing the penalty

[*Gelig v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/54204)[^42] also shows the computation. With no mitigating or aggravating circumstance, the Supreme Court fixed the penalty in the medium period of prision correccional in its medium and maximum periods. Under the Indeterminate Sentence Law, the minimum came from the penalty next lower in degree, which is arresto mayor in its maximum period to prision correccional in its minimum period. The Supreme Court imposed 1 year and 1 day to 3 years, 6 months and 21 days of prision correccional and a fine of PHP 1,000.00. The same indeterminate penalty and fine were affirmed for the armed intimidation in [*Pablo v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68672)[^43].

### The penalty as the measure of jurisdiction

The penalty of Article 148 has also determined which court tries the case. In [*Rilloraza v. Arciaga*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1967/oct1967/gr_l-23848_1967.html)[^44] (1967), a head teacher filed a complaint for direct assault upon a person in authority in the municipal court of a provincial capital. The Supreme Court held that jurisdiction is determined by the law in force when the action is instituted. Under that law, the municipal court could try offenses punishable by not more than prision correccional or a fine not exceeding three thousand pesos. Because Article 148 provides prision correccional in its medium and maximum periods and a fine not exceeding one thousand pesos, the municipal court had jurisdiction. A later statute did not oust the jurisdiction already acquired.

## Direct Assault in Complex Crimes

### The complex crime and the graver offense

When the assault produces another grave or less grave felony, Article 48 applies. The penalty for the most serious crime is imposed in its maximum period. In [*People v. Abalos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/33847)[^45], the Supreme Court held that when the assault results in the killing of an agent or of a person in authority, there arises the complex crime of direct assault with murder or homicide. [*People v. Estonilo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/58325)[^46] restated the rule in the same words. [*Aplacador v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70146)[^47] applied it, citing *People v. Pitulan*.

The graver offense depends on the result. If the victim dies, the killing is the graver crime. In [*Aplacador v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70146)[^48], the graver offense was homicide, punished by reclusion temporal. The penalty was reclusion temporal in its maximum period. Under the Indeterminate Sentence Law, the minimum came from prision mayor. In [*People v. Rillorta*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1989/dec1989/gr_57415_1989.html)[^49], the Supreme Court applied the same computation to homicide with assault upon a person in authority. If the victim survives an attempted homicide, the direct assault may be the graver crime. In [*People v. Ladjaalam*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/36179)[^50] (2000), the Supreme Court noted that direct assault with a weapon carries prision correccional in its medium and maximum periods, while attempted homicide carries prision correccional. Direct assault was the most serious crime and was imposed in its maximum period. In [*People v. Recto*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/52891)[^51], the Supreme Court likewise imposed the penalty for qualified direct assault in its maximum period for qualified direct assault with attempted homicide.

### Mitigating circumstances within the complex crime

The maximum period under Article 48 limits the effect of mitigating circumstances. In [*Aplacador v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70146)[^52], the Supreme Court appreciated voluntary surrender. It held that the penalty for the most serious crime in a complex crime is still applied in its maximum period irrespective of modifying circumstances. A privileged mitigating circumstance works differently. In [*People v. Cesar*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1968/mar1968/gr_l-26185_1968.html)[^53] (1968), the accused was a minor who pleaded guilty to direct assault upon a person in authority with homicide. The Supreme Court held that the proper method is to start from reclusion temporal, apply minority to reach prision mayor, then apply prision mayor in its maximum period but within its minimum range because of the plea of guilty.

### Murder, homicide and treachery

The killing is murder only if a qualifying circumstance is proven. In [*People v. Abalos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/33847)[^54], treachery was proven because the accused deliberately went behind the victim and struck him with a piece of wood. In [*Aplacador v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70146)[^55], the Court of Appeals had found treachery because the accused shot the victim at the back of the head while the victim was down. The Supreme Court held that this fact alone did not prove treachery, since no witness saw how the attack began. The crime was direct assault with homicide. In [*People v. Rillorta*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1989/dec1989/gr_57415_1989.html)[^56], treachery was absent because a heated exchange of words preceded the attack. In [*People v. Recto*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/52891)[^57], treachery was absent because the victim had every opportunity to escape and the accused was exposed to attack throughout the confrontation.

### The death penalty

For direct assault with murder, the imposable penalty was once death. The decisions record three stages. In [*People v. Caricungan*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/sep1991/gr_71461_1991.html)[^58], the Supreme Court changed death to reclusion perpetua. It did so because of the prohibition in the 1987 Constitution.

In [*People v. Abalos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/33847)[^59], it held that death could not be imposed because of the then existing proscription and that the trial court's "life imprisonment" should be denominated reclusion perpetua. In [*People v. Estonilo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/58325)[^60], applying Article 248 as amended by Republic Act No. 7659, the Supreme Court found that the imposable penalty was death, but Republic Act No. 9346 prohibits its imposition. The governing rule is the rule of *Estonilo*. The penalty for direct assault with murder is reclusion perpetua.

| Result of the assault | Graver offense | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Death, without a qualifying circumstance | Homicide, reclusion temporal in its maximum period | [*Aplacador v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70146)[^61] |
| Death, with treachery | Murder, reclusion perpetua under R.A. 9346 | [*People v. Estonilo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/58325)[^62] |
| Attempted homicide, with a weapon | Direct assault, in its maximum period | [*People v. Ladjaalam*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/36179)[^63] |

## Direct Assault and Firearms

[*People v. Ladjaalam*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/36179)[^64] settled the relation between direct assault and the illegal possession of firearms. The accused fired an unlicensed M-14 rifle at police officers who were about to serve a search warrant. The Supreme Court read Republic Act No. 8294 to allow a conviction for simple illegal possession only if no other crime was committed. Since direct assault with multiple attempted homicide was committed, the accused could not also be convicted of illegal possession. Since the crime was not homicide or murder, the illegal possession could not aggravate the direct assault either. The Supreme Court acknowledged that the result exonerated the accused of an offense that normally carries a heavier penalty. It held that the consequence arises from the language of the statute and directed that a copy of the decision be furnished to Congress.

## Open Questions

Three points have no applying decision on this page. The first mode of direct assault is only defined. The fines in the codal text have not been discussed in any decision here in light of later amendments. Indirect assault under Article 149, including assault upon a person who comes to the aid of authority, is not decided in any of the cases below.

## Case Discussions

### [*Aplacador v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70146): Soldiers as agents and the firearm qualifier

Aplacador, a member of the Military Intelligence Group 02, was charged with direct assault with murder for shooting his superior, MSgt. Jimmy P. Quilang.[^65] The Information alleged treachery and evident premeditation. The courts below found that Quilang was performing his duties at the station when he was killed.

Branch 03 of the Regional Trial Court of Tuguegarao City convicted Aplacador of the complex crime of qualified direct assault with homicide. Its fallo cites Articles 148 and 248 in relation to Article 48. The Supreme Court's own summary of that ruling cites Articles 148 and 249. The trial court imposed 12 years of prision mayor maximum to 20 years of reclusion temporal maximum. It ordered Aplacador to pay the heirs of Quilang PHP 50,000.00 as death indemnity, PHP 50,000.00 as moral damages, PHP 4,446,702.67 as lost earnings and PHP 78,000.00 as actual damages, plus costs.

The Court of Appeals found treachery and convicted Aplacador of direct assault with murder. It imposed reclusion perpetua without eligibility for parole. It added PHP 75,000.00 as civil indemnity and PHP 30,000.00 as exemplary damages with 6% interest. Aplacador filed a petition for certiorari under Rule 65.

The Supreme Court held that members of the Armed Forces are agents of a person in authority. Article 148 still applied because it covers both persons in authority and agents. The firearm made the assault qualified. Treachery and evident premeditation were not proven. The Supreme Court appreciated voluntary surrender but held that it did not change the penalty.

The Supreme Court partially granted the petition. It set aside the Court of Appeals Decision and Resolution. It reinstated the Regional Trial Court judgment. The body of the decision deleted the exemplary damages and imposed 6% interest on all damages from finality. The dispositive portion itself only reinstates the trial court judgment.

The case shows that a wrong label for the victim does not defeat a conviction for direct assault.

### [*Balsamo v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69212): A punch to evade arrest

Balsamo was charged with direct assault for punching PO3 Adalim in the chest and injuring his fingers.[^66] PO3 Adalim was in civilian clothes but had introduced himself as a police officer. He was chasing Balsamo to investigate a reported threat.

The Municipal Trial Court in Cities convicted Balsamo of direct assault. It imposed 4 months and 1 day of arresto mayor to 1 year and 1 month of prision correccional, a fine of PHP 500.00 and costs. Branch 19 of the Regional Trial Court of Pagadian City affirmed. The Court of Appeals affirmed.

The Supreme Court found that the second to fifth elements were present. It held that the first element was not. Balsamo punched the officer to free himself and evade arrest. He slammed the gate without knowing that it hit the officer's fingers. The injuries were slight abrasions and swollen fingers.

The Supreme Court partly granted the petition. It affirmed the Court of Appeals with modification. It found Balsamo guilty of resistance to an agent of a person in authority. It imposed three months of arresto mayor and a PHP 500.00 fine. It held that the Indeterminate Sentence Law did not apply.

The case shows that force must be serious for direct assault.

### [*Pablo v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68672): Aiming a gun at traffic enforcers

Pablo was charged with direct assault upon agents of a person in authority.[^67] The Information alleged that he refused to produce his driver's license and drew and pointed a .45 caliber pistol at two traffic enforcers. A second Information charged him with violating a Marikina City ordinance.

Branch 94 of the Metropolitan Trial Court of Marikina City convicted Pablo only of resistance and serious disobedience under Article 151. It imposed 1 month and 1 day of arresto mayor and a fine of PHP 100.00. It acquitted him of the ordinance violation because the prosecution did not formally offer the violation receipt.

Branch 193 of the Regional Trial Court of Marikina City reversed. It convicted Pablo of direct assault. It imposed 1 year and 1 day to 3 years, 6 months and 21 days of prision correccional and a fine of PHP 1,000.00. The Court of Appeals affirmed.

The Supreme Court held that traffic enforcers are agents of persons in authority under Article 152. It found that they were performing their duties in complete uniform. It held that pulling out and aiming a gun is serious intimidation.

The Supreme Court denied the petition. It affirmed the Court of Appeals in toto. It found Pablo guilty of direct assault and imposed 1 year and 1 day to 3 years, 6 months and 21 days of prision correccional. It ordered him to pay a fine of PHP 1,000.00.

The case shows that serious intimidation can complete direct assault without physical force.

### [*Mallari v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/66238): Slapping and kicking a police officer

The courts below found that Mallari slapped and kicked PO2 Navarro and grabbed his shirt while he was on duty.[^68] PO2 Navarro was in uniform and had introduced himself as a police officer.

The Municipal Trial Court convicted Mallari of direct assault upon an agent of a person in authority. It imposed prision correccional in its medium period of 3 years, 6 months and 21 days to 4 years, 9 months and 10 days and a fine of PHP 1,000.00 with costs. Branch 74 of the Regional Trial Court of Olongapo City affirmed. The Court of Appeals affirmed with a modified penalty of 2 months of arresto mayor to 2 years and 4 months of prision correccional and a fine of PHP 500.00.

The Supreme Court accepted the factual findings. It held that the second to fifth elements were present. It held that the force used was not dangerous, grave or severe. PO2 Navarro himself testified that he was not kicked hard.

The Supreme Court modified the Court of Appeals Decision and Resolution. It found Mallari guilty of resistance or disobedience under Article 151. It imposed arresto mayor of 1 month and 1 day as minimum to 6 months as maximum and a fine not exceeding PHP 500.00.

The case shows that a conviction for resistance may rest on an Information for direct assault.

### [*Guelos v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63166): Knowledge must be alleged

Two Informations charged the petitioners with direct assault upon an agent of a person in authority with homicide.[^69] They covered the shooting of SPO2 Estelito Andaya and P/Chief Inspector Rolando M. Camacho. The Informations did not allege that the accused knew the victims were agents of a person in authority.

Branch 83 of the Regional Trial Court of Tanauan City convicted Nestor and Gil in Criminal Case No. P-204. It convicted Nestor, Rodrigo and Alfredo in Criminal Case No. P-205. In each case, it imposed 11 years of prision correccional maximum to 18 years of reclusion temporal maximum and a fine of PHP 1,000.00. For the heirs of SPO2 Andaya, it awarded PHP 50,000.00 as indemnity, PHP 1,000,000.00 as actual damages and PHP 50,000.00 as moral damages. For the heirs of P/C Insp. Camacho, it awarded PHP 50,000.00 as indemnity, PHP 1,600,000.00 as actual damages and PHP 50,000.00 as moral damages. The Court of Appeals affirmed in toto.

The Supreme Court held that the evidence proved knowledge because the victims introduced themselves as police officers. It held that proof cannot cure the missing allegation. It also held that the knowledge could not be appreciated as an aggravating circumstance to increase the penalty. It awarded exemplary damages because the insult to rank was proven at trial.

The Supreme Court affirmed the judgment with modification. It found all four petitioners guilty of homicide. It imposed 8 years and 1 day of prision mayor to 14 years and 1 day of reclusion temporal. It deleted the fine of PHP 1,000.00. It kept the damages and civil indemnity awarded below. It ordered each petitioner to pay PHP 30,000.00 as exemplary damages to each of the victims.

The case shows that the knowledge element must appear in the Information.

### [*People v. Estonilo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/58325): A District Supervisor attacked because of his duty

The Information alleged that the accused shot Floro A. Casas, a District Supervisor of public schools, while he was performing his duty.[^70]

Branch 45 of the Regional Trial Court of Manila convicted the accused of murder with direct assault. It imposed 20 years and 1 day to 40 years of reclusion perpetua. It ordered them to pay the family of the victim PHP 50,000.00 as indemnity and PHP 100,000.00 as moral damages solidarily. The Court of Appeals affirmed with modification. It imposed reclusion perpetua without specifying a duration. It increased the civil indemnity to PHP 75,000.00.

The Supreme Court held that Floro was a person in authority. It clarified that direct assault rests on the attack being made by reason of his duty. It held that the killing gave rise to the complex crime of direct assault with murder. It held that the penalty would be death, but Republic Act No. 9346 prohibits its imposition. Reclusion perpetua was proper.

The Supreme Court affirmed with modifications. It found Reinario, Edelbrando, Eutiquiano and Calvin guilty of murder with direct assault. It increased civil indemnity and moral damages to PHP 100,000.00 each. It added PHP 100,000.00 as exemplary damages. It imposed 6% legal interest from finality. It dismissed the case against Ex-Mayor Carlos, Sr. because of his death.

The case shows that rank alone does not make an attack direct assault.

### [*Gelig v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/54204): A teacher who laid hands on another teacher

The Information charged Gelig with direct assault with unintentional abortion.[^71] Gelig slapped and pushed Gemma B. Micarsos, a public school teacher, after accusing her of calling Gelig's son a "sissy." Gemma fell and later suffered an abortion.

Branch 23 of the Regional Trial Court of Cebu City convicted Gelig of direct assault with unintentional abortion. It imposed 6 months of arresto mayor to 4 years and 2 months of prision correccional. It awarded PHP 10,000.00 as actual damages and PHP 15,000.00 as moral damages. The Court of Appeals vacated that decision. It convicted Gelig of slight physical injuries and imposed arresto menor minimum of ten days.

The Supreme Court held that Gemma was a person in authority. It held that she did not descend to the level of a private person. The prosecution did not prove that the assault was the proximate cause of the abortion. Gelig was a public officer and laid hands on a person in authority, so the higher penalty applied.

The Supreme Court reversed the Court of Appeals. It found Gelig guilty of direct assault. It imposed 1 year and 1 day to 3 years, 6 months and 21 days of prision correccional and a fine of PHP 1,000.00. The dispositive portion does not mention the damages awarded by the trial court.

The case shows that a teacher remains a person in authority while performing her functions.

### [*Abuyen v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/53130): A bolo attack on a police officer

The Information alleged that Abuyen, armed with a bolo, hacked at SPO2 Aquilino A. Fabillar.[^72] Abuyen claimed that Fabillar had a grudge against him.

Quoting the fallo of the 9th Municipal Circuit Trial Court of Giporlos-Quinapondan, Eastern Samar, the Supreme Court recounted that the trial court found him guilty of direct assault defined in Article 148. It imposed 3 years, 6 months and 21 days and a fine of PHP 500.00 with subsidiary imprisonment at P120.00 per day in case of insolvency. It forfeited the bolo. Branch 3 of the Regional Trial Court of Guiuan affirmed. The Court of Appeals imposed 4 months and 1 day of arresto mayor to 3 years, 6 months and 21 days of prision correccional.

The Supreme Court held that findings on credibility affirmed by the appellate courts bind it. It held that the inhibition issue was raised too late. It did not discuss the elements of Article 148.

The Supreme Court denied the petition. It affirmed the Court of Appeals Decision and Resolution in toto.

The case shows an affirmed conviction for an armed assault on a police officer.

### [*People v. Recto*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/52891): Bystander, barangay captain and kagawad

Recto fired a shotgun during a dispute over a rice harvest.[^73] The Branch 81 Regional Trial Court of Romblon convicted him of two counts of qualified direct assault with frustrated homicide, qualified direct assault with murder and homicide. It imposed death for the murder.

The Supreme Court held that the chief tanod Melchor Recto was an agent of a person in authority. He was a mere bystander on his way home. The attack on him did not amount to direct assault. The barangay captain Percival Orbe was attacked while he was pacifying the groups. The attack on him was qualified direct assault. The kagawad Antonio Macalipay was in the actual performance of his duties when he was shot. Treachery was not proven.

The Supreme Court modified the trial court decision.

- In Criminal Case No. 1970, it convicted Recto of attempted homicide. It imposed 4 months of arresto mayor to 4 years and 2 months of prision correccional.
- In Criminal Case No. 1971, it convicted him of qualified direct assault with attempted homicide. It imposed 6 months of arresto mayor to 6 years of prision correccional.
- In Criminal Case No. 1972, it convicted him of qualified direct assault with homicide aggravated by the use of a weapon. It imposed 12 years of prision mayor to 20 years of reclusion temporal. It affirmed the award of PHP 50,000.00 as indemnity ex delicto.
- In Criminal Case No. 1973, it affirmed the conviction for homicide and the PHP 50,000.00 indemnity. It increased the maximum penalty to 14 years, 8 months and 1 day of reclusion temporal.

The case shows that an agent who is not performing a duty is not protected by Article 148.

### [*People v. Ladjaalam*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/36179): Firing a rifle at police serving a warrant

Ladjaalam fired an M-14 rifle at police officers who were about to serve a search warrant.[^74]

Branch 16 of the Regional Trial Court of Zamboanga City convicted him of maintaining a drug den, illegal possession of firearms and direct assault with multiple attempted homicide. It acquitted him of a separate drug charge. For direct assault with multiple attempted homicide, it imposed 2 years and 4 months to 6 years of prision correccional and a fine of PHP 1,000.00.

The Supreme Court held that the conviction for direct assault with multiple attempted homicide was correct. It held that direct assault with a weapon is the most serious crime of the complex crime. It held that under R.A. 8294 there can be no separate conviction for illegal possession of firearms. It also held that the illegal possession does not aggravate direct assault.

The Supreme Court affirmed with modification. It found Ladjaalam guilty only of direct assault and multiple attempted homicide with the use of a weapon and of maintaining a drug den. It imposed 2 years and 4 months to 6 years of prision correccional for the first. It kept reclusion perpetua for the second. It ordered a copy of the decision furnished to Congress.

The case shows how Article 48 works when direct assault is the graver crime.

### [*People v. Abalos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/33847): Killing a policeman during a fiesta

The Information charged Abalos with direct assault with murder for striking Pfc. Sofronio Labine with a piece of wood.[^75] Labine was a member of the Integrated National Police.

Branch 27 of the Regional Trial Court of Catbalogan, Samar convicted Abalos as charged. It imposed life imprisonment. It awarded PHP 30,000.00 as indemnity, PHP 2,633.00 as actual and compensatory damages and PHP 15,000.00 as moral damages.

The Supreme Court held that Labine was an agent of a person in authority maintaining peace and order during the fiesta. Abalos knew that Labine was a policeman. The killing was murder qualified by treachery. The penalty should be death but had to be reduced to reclusion perpetua. Life imprisonment was the wrong designation.

The Supreme Court affirmed with modification. It imposed reclusion perpetua. It increased the death indemnity to PHP 50,000.00. It affirmed the judgment in all other respects with costs against the accused.

The case shows the complex crime of direct assault with murder.

### [*People v. Caricungan*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1991/sep1991/gr_71461_1991.html): Killing a chief of the barangay tanod

The Information charged four accused with killing Primo Milanes, the Chief of the Barangay Tanod of Tombod.[^76]

Quoting the fallo of Branch 38 of the Regional Trial Court of Lingayen, the Supreme Court recounted that the trial court convicted the four accused of direct assault upon an agent of a person in authority with murder. It imposed death. It awarded PHP 4,000.00 as actual damages and PHP 30,000.00 as moral damages jointly and severally.

The Supreme Court found that Milanes was maintaining peace and order when he was killed. It found no basis for the mitigating circumstances claimed by Anastacio. It held that the guilt of all accused was proven.

The Supreme Court affirmed with modification. It changed death to reclusion perpetua because of the 1987 Constitution. It increased moral damages to PHP 50,000.00. It affirmed the decision in all other respects with costs against the accused.

The case shows a direct assault conviction affirmed on the record.

### [*People v. Rillorta*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1989/dec1989/gr_57415_1989.html): A barangay captain who intervened

Barangay Captain Emiliano Doton was hacked to death.[^77] The Court of First Instance of Pangasinan convicted Pascual and Wesley Rillorta as principals and Wilson Rillorta as an accomplice of direct assault with murder. It ordered them to pay P12,000.00 for his death and P5,350.00 for funeral expenses.

The Supreme Court held that treachery was not present. The killing was homicide. It held that Doton was discharging his duty as barangay captain when he intervened to prevent a violent encounter. The crime was the complex crime of homicide with assault upon a person in authority.

The Supreme Court declared all three accused guilty as principals of that complex crime. It imposed 12 years of prision mayor to 20 years of reclusion temporal on each. It ordered them to pay jointly and severally P30,000.00 for the death and P5,350.00 for funeral expenses. Each was ordered to pay one-third of the costs.

The case shows that a barangay captain keeping order is protected by Article 148.

### [*People v. Cesar*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1968/mar1968/gr_l-26185_1968.html): Penalty for a minor who pleaded guilty

Cesar, a student, stabbed the Acting Principal Teacher of St. Anthony's Academy.[^78] He pleaded guilty to direct assault with homicide.

Quoting the fallo of the Court of First Instance of Bohol, the Supreme Court recounted that the trial court found him guilty of direct assault upon a person in authority with homicide. It imposed 12 years and 1 day to 14 years, 8 months and 1 day of reclusion temporal. It awarded P7,500.00 in damages and confiscated the knife.

The Supreme Court held that the penalty for the more serious crime, homicide, applies in its maximum period. It held that the privileged mitigating circumstance of minority lowers the penalty by one degree first. The plea of guilty then places the penalty in the minimum range of the maximum period.

The Supreme Court modified the personal penalty to 6 years of prision correccional to 10 years and 8 months of prision mayor. It affirmed the judgment in all other respects.

The case shows how to compute the penalty for the complex crime with mitigating circumstances.

### [*Rilloraza v. Arciaga*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1967/oct1967/gr_l-23848_1967.html): The Article 148 penalty and jurisdiction

A head teacher filed a complaint for direct assault upon a person in authority in the Municipal Court of San Fernando, La Union.[^79] The Court of First Instance of La Union declared the proceedings void.

The Supreme Court held that jurisdiction depends on the law in force when the action was instituted. The penalty of Article 148 fell within the jurisdiction of the municipal court under that law. A later statute did not oust the jurisdiction already acquired.

The Supreme Court reversed the order of the Court of First Instance. It dismissed the petition for certiorari and prohibition. It directed the Municipal Court of San Fernando to continue with Criminal Case No. 4987. The case did not decide guilt.

### [*Sarcepuedes v. People*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1951/oct1951/gr_l-3857_1951.html): Motive is immaterial

Sarcepuedes hit a teacher-nurse twice on the face with his raincoat and pushed her in the school clinic.[^80] The dispute concerned a pathway across her land. The Court of Appeals convicted him of direct assault upon a person in authority.

The Supreme Court held that a teacher is expressly included in Article 152 as amended. It held that the victim was assaulted while performing her official duties. The motive for the aggression was immaterial.

The Supreme Court affirmed the Court of Appeals. The penalty was 1 year, 8 months and 1 day to 4 years, 9 months and 11 days of prision correccional plus a fine of P500. The offense was attended by the aggravating circumstance of disregard of sex.

The case shows that the reason for the attack does not matter when the victim is performing official duties.

### [*People v. Mendoza*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1933/dec1933/gr_39275_1933.html): The old rule on teachers

The Information charged a pupil with assault upon a person in authority for slapping his teacher.[^81] The Court of First Instance of Pampanga dismissed the Information because the facts alleged were only a light felony.

The Supreme Court held that under Article 152 as then worded, a teacher was neither a person in authority nor an agent. It held that the facts alleged constituted a light felony under Article 266. The trial court lacked jurisdiction over that felony.

The Supreme Court sustained the order of dismissal.

The case shows the rule before Article 152 was amended to cover teachers.

## Key Cases

- [*Aplacador v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70146)[^94]: Soldiers are agents of a person in authority. A firearm qualifies the assault. Without treachery, the crime is direct assault with homicide.
- [*Balsamo v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69212)[^95]: A punch to evade arrest is resistance to an agent, not direct assault.
- [*Pablo v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/68672)[^96]: Aiming a gun at traffic enforcers is serious intimidation and direct assault.
- [*Mallari v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/66238)[^97]: Slapping and kicking a police officer is resistance or disobedience.
- [*Guelos v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63166)[^98]: Without an allegation of knowledge in the Information, the conviction is for homicide only.
- [*People v. Estonilo*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/58325)[^99]: Direct assault rests on an attack made by reason of the victim's duty.
- [*Gelig v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/54204)[^100]: A public school teacher is a person in authority. A public school teacher who slaps another teacher incurs the higher penalty.
- [*People v. Recto*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/52891)[^101]: A tanod who is a mere bystander is not a victim of direct assault.
- [*People v. Ladjaalam*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/36179)[^102]: Direct assault with a weapon can be the graver crime in a complex crime.
- [*People v. Abalos*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/33847)[^103]: Killing a policeman on duty is direct assault with murder.
- [*People v. Rillorta*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1989/dec1989/gr_57415_1989.html)[^104]: A barangay captain who intervenes to keep order is protected by Article 148.
- [*Sarcepuedes v. People*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1951/oct1951/gr_l-3857_1951.html)[^105]: The motive for the attack is immaterial when the victim is performing official duties.
- [*People v. Mendoza*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1933/dec1933/gr_39275_1933.html)[^106]: Under the original Article 152, a teacher was not a person in authority.

## Suggested Answers

### 1. A punch during an arrest

**Question:** A police officer in uniform grabs X's arm to arrest him. X punches the officer once in the chest and runs. The officer suffers slight abrasions. X is charged with direct assault. What crime did X commit?

**Answer:** X committed resistance to an agent of a person in authority under Article 151. The force in direct assault must be serious or more than a sudden blow, slapping or punching. In [*Balsamo v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69212)[^107], the Supreme Court convicted the accused of resistance on similar facts. Direct assault necessarily includes resistance. X may be convicted of resistance under the Information for direct assault.

### 2. An Information that omits knowledge

**Question:** An Information charges Y with direct assault upon an agent of a person in authority with homicide. It alleges that the victim was a police officer performing his duties. It does not allege that Y knew this. At trial, the prosecution proves that the victim identified himself as a police officer. What should the court convict Y of?

**Answer:** The court should convict Y of homicide only. In [*Guelos v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63166)[^108], the Supreme Court held that proof of knowledge cannot cure its absence from the Information. The knowledge element must be alleged. Exemplary damages may still be awarded if the insult to rank is proven.

### 3. A teacher attacked over a private quarrel

**Question:** Z slaps a public school teacher in her classroom while she is attending to her pupils. Z says he was angry about a private land dispute. Is Z liable for direct assault?

**Answer:** Yes. A teacher is a person in authority for Articles 148 and 151 under Article 152 as amended. In [*Sarcepuedes v. People*](https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1951/oct1951/gr_l-3857_1951.html)[^109], the Supreme Court held that the motive is immaterial when the teacher is assaulted while performing her duties. Z laid hands on a person in authority. The higher penalty of Article 148 applies.

# Quotes

> "The question of whether the crime is direct assault or resistance and disobedience to persons in authority or their agents depends on the gravity of the act proved and the particular conditions under which it is committed."[^110]

> "Again, the force involved in direct assault must be serious or more than a sudden blow, slapping, or punching."[^111]

> "Prudence dictates that one cannot simply pull out a gun and aim towards a person in a heat of argument or altercation."[^112]

> "It is essential that the accused must have knowledge that the person attacked was a person in authority or his agent in the exercise of his duties, because the accused must have the intention to offend, injure, or assault the offended party as a person in authority or agent of a person in authority."[^113]

> "Be that as it may, Article l48 of the Revised Penal Code penalizes the crime of direct assault, whether it is committed against a person in authority or his agent."[^114]

> "Here, Lydia is a public officer or employee since she is a teacher in a public school. By slapping and pushing Gemma, another teacher, she laid her hands on a person in authority."[^115]

> "However, inasmuch as we have found that Lucrecia was hurt while performing her ordinary Government tasks, the motive for the aggression becomes immaterial."[^116]

> "Moreover, since the crime committed was direct assault and not homicide or murder, illegal possession of firearms cannot be deemed an aggravating circumstance."[^117]

# Tips

> [!TIP]
> **Measure the force first.** A sudden blow, slap or punch usually points to Article 151, not Article 148. Ask whether the force was dangerous, grave or severe. [*Balsamo v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/69212) turns on this question. So does [*Mallari v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/66238).

> [!TIP]
> **Check the Information for knowledge.** If the Information does not allege that the accused knew the victim was a person in authority or an agent, the complex crime fails. [*Guelos v. People*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/63166) reduced the conviction to homicide.

> [!NOTE]
> **Classify the victim correctly.** Police officers, traffic enforcers and soldiers are agents. Teachers and barangay captains are persons in authority. A wrong label did not defeat the conviction in [*Aplacador v. Court of Appeals*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/70146).

> [!NOTE]
> **Look at the victim's activity at the time.** An agent who is only a bystander is not protected. [*People v. Recto*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/52891) turned on this point.

> [!TIP]
> **Find the graver crime under Article 48.** If the victim dies, the killing is graver. If the victim survives an attempted homicide, the direct assault may be graver, as in [*People v. Ladjaalam*](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/1/36179).
